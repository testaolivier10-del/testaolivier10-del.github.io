Details

The format: adaptive, not fixed

The cognitive exam is a computer-adaptive test delivered at a Pearson VUE test center. It starts you at a moderate difficulty and, after each answer, picks the next question to sharpen its estimate of whether you sit above or below the passing standard. Get one right and the next is a little harder; miss one and it eases off. It stops as soon as it is 95% confident either way, which is why one candidate finishes at 70 questions and another at 120.

Three things follow from that. You cannot go back to change an answer. Running long is not a sign you are failing; it means the computer is still not sure. And a run of hard questions is usually a good sign, because the test only hands them out to candidates who keep getting the easier ones right.

A handful of the questions are unscored pilot items being trialled for future exams. They are not marked, so treat every question as if it counts.

The 100-question timed exam on this site is deliberately not adaptive. It trains pacing, stamina and the six content areas at a fixed length; it does not try to imitate the algorithm.

What it covers

The exam is written to the National Registry's EMT content outline. The bulk of it is patient care across airway, respiration and ventilation; cardiology and resuscitation; trauma; medical, obstetrics and gynecology; with a smaller share on EMS operations. Every question in the bank here is tagged to one of six domains that map onto that outline, with assessment split out on its own because it runs through all of them.

Expect scenarios, not definitions. The typical stem describes a patient and asks for the next step, the most likely cause, or the finding that changes the plan. Multiple-choice is most of it; the updated exam also uses select-all-that-apply and drag-to-order items, and Practice includes both formats.

Who can sit it

  • You are 18 or older.
  • You completed a state-approved EMT course within the past two years, and your program director has verified that on the Registry's system.
  • You hold a current CPR-BLS certification for healthcare providers.
  • You have passed, or will pass, the state psychomotor (hands-on) examination. For EMT that is still a separate practical exam run through your state or program; the 2024 changes that folded skills verification into the training program applied to AEMT and Paramedic, not EMT. The skill sheets here cover the eight stations.

Create your account at nremt.org, submit the application, pay the fee, and once your course completion is verified you receive an Authorization to Test (ATT) letter. That letter is what lets you book a seat with Pearson VUE, and it is valid for a limited window, so book promptly.

Cost

The application fee is $104 per attempt, paid to the National Registry when you apply. A retake means a new application and a new fee. Your state may charge separately for its licence once you pass, and the psychomotor exam is priced by whoever runs it in your state.

If you don't pass

  • You must wait 15 days after your last attempt before testing again.
  • You get three attempts. After a third unsuccessful attempt you must complete official remedial training before you can apply again.
  • After remediation you get three more attempts, for six in total. After the sixth you must complete an entire state-approved EMT course again.
  • Your results come with a breakdown by content area (above, near or below the passing standard). Use it. On this site, the dashboard shows the same picture by domain, and the missed-question queue in Practice is built for exactly this.

Results normally appear in your National Registry account within one to two business days. The test center does not tell you on the day.

On the day

  • Bring one valid, unexpired government-issued photo ID with a signature. The name on it must match the name on your ATT exactly; a mismatch means you are turned away and lose the fee.
  • Arrive 30 minutes early. Late arrivals can be refused.
  • Nothing goes into the testing room: no phone, watch, notes, food or drink. Lockers are provided. You get an on-screen calculator if a question needs one.
  • Read every stem to the end before looking at the options, and answer the question that was asked, not the one you expected. The adaptive test rewards steady accuracy over speed; two hours is generous for 120 questions if you do not stall.
  • You can flag nothing and revisit nothing, so decide and move on. Second-guessing has no outlet here.

The week before

  1. Finish the study plan: one full timed exam, then clear the missed-question queue.
  2. Reread the frameworks until SAMPLE, OPQRST, AVPU and the primary assessment sequence come without thinking.
  3. Do a short domain drill each day rather than a marathon; the exam rewards recall that is already fluent.
  4. The night before, stop early. Sleep does more for an adaptive test than one more hour of questions.
Common questions

How many questions is the NREMT-EMT exam?

Between 70 and 120. It is computer-adaptive, so it stops as soon as it is 95% confident you sit above or below the passing standard. Finishing at 70 and finishing at 120 are both normal outcomes.

How long do you get?

Two hours, whatever length your exam turns out to be. That is generous for 120 questions if you decide and move on rather than stalling.

Does running to 120 questions mean I'm failing?

No. A long exam means the computer is still not sure, not that you are below the standard. A run of hard questions is usually a good sign, because the test only hands those out to candidates who keep getting the easier ones right.

How much does the NREMT cost?

$104 per attempt, paid to the National Registry when you apply. A retake means a new application and a new fee. Your state may charge separately for its licence, and the psychomotor exam is priced by whoever runs it in your state.

How long do I have to wait to retake it?

15 days after your last attempt. You get three attempts, then official remedial training before you can apply again; after remediation you get three more, six in total.

When do results come out?

Normally within one to two business days, in your National Registry account. The test center does not tell you on the day.
