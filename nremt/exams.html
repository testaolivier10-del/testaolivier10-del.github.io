The adaptive exam

How the adaptive exam works

The National Registry’s EMT exam is computer-adaptive. LevlPrep’s Adaptive exam copies the idea with a simple model you can check. It is our simulation, not the Registry’s algorithm, which is not public.

The exam it imitates

The Registry’s EMT exam has 70 to 120 items, plus 10 unscored pilot items, and a two-hour limit. It starts a little below the passing standard, gets harder after a right answer and easier after a wrong one, and stops once it is 95% confident you are above or below the standard, or at the time limit. Items come from five domains: Scene Size-Up and Safety 15 to 19%, Primary Assessment 39 to 43%, Secondary Assessment 5 to 9%, Patient Treatment and Transport 20 to 24%, and Operations 10 to 14%. Figures checked October 2026 on nremt.org; confirm them before you book.

LevlPrep’s model

Every question in the bank carries a difficulty tag. The model gives each tag a difficulty (easy, medium, hard and expert, one step apart) and estimates your level after every answer from all your answers so far. The next question is the one nearest that estimate, from whichever domain is furthest behind its share of the outline. It stops once your level’s 95% range lies wholly above or below the passing line, after at least 70 questions; at 120 or at the clock it decides on the best estimate.

The passing line is our estimate: someone right at it gets most medium questions and about half of the hard ones right. The Registry sets its own standard by a separate study, so read a result here as practice, not a prediction.

Reading your results

A domain is Above passing when its estimate sits more than one standard error above the line, Below when more than one below, and Near otherwise. A failed Registry attempt is reported the same three ways. Anything not Above comes with its notes and a drill.
