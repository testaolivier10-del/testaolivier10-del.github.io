Predict the change

Change one thing in the body and predict what goes up or down.

About this tool

What is inside

377 prediction scenarios, including:

  • A tube narrows: Water flows through a rubber tube from a tank.
  • Solute added to one side: Two compartments of equal volume are separated by a membrane that lets water through but not glucose.
  • Acid added with and without a buffer: Two beakers hold the same salt solution at pH 7.4.
  • A red blood cell in a hypertonic solution: A red blood cell is moved from plasma into a salt solution much more concentrated than the fluid inside the cell.
  • The sodium–potassium pump is poisoned: A poison binds the sodium–potassium pumps of a cell and stops them.
  • A cell’s oxygen supply is cut off: A blood vessel supplying a small patch of tissue is blocked, so its cells receive no oxygen.
  • Body temperature rises above its set point: You run hard on a warm day, and your body temperature climbs above its set point.
  • Potassium outside the cell doubles: The potassium concentration in the fluid around a cell rises from 4 to 8 mmol/L (hyperkalemia).
  • A toxin blocks voltage-gated sodium channels: A toxin blocks the voltage-gated sodium channels of a neuron.
  • A drug occupies a hormone’s receptor proteins: A drug binds the receptor proteins that a hormone acts on.
  • The pacemaker potential slows: A drug reduces the funny current in the SA node’s pacemaker cells, so each pacemaker potential rises toward threshold more slowly.
  • The AV node conducts more slowly: Conduction through the AV node slows, so each impulse takes longer to cross it.
  • L-type calcium channels are partly blocked: A drug partly blocks the L-type calcium channels of ventricular contractile cells.
  • A stiff, narrow aortic valve: Over many years, the aortic valve has become stiff and narrow (aortic stenosis).
  • Venous return rises: You lie down and raise your legs, and more blood returns to your heart.
  • Arterioles dilate throughout the body: A drug relaxes the smooth muscle of arterioles throughout the body.
  • Sympathetic output to the heart and vessels rises: Sympathetic nerve signals to the heart, arterioles and veins increase sharply, as in a moment of sudden fright.
  • A narrowed coronary artery on the stairs: A plaque narrows one coronary artery to half its normal radius.
  • Damaged kidneys leak albumin: Damaged kidneys leak large amounts of albumin into the urine for weeks, and the liver cannot make enough to replace it.
  • Dehydration on a hot hike: A hiker loses 3 liters of sweat over a hot day and drinks nothing.
  • Breathing through your mouth on a cold, dusty day: Your nose is blocked by a cold, so you spend a winter afternoon clearing a dusty garage while breathing through your mouth.
  • An airway narrows to half its radius: The smooth muscle around one bronchiole contracts, and its radius falls to half.
  • Fast, shallow breathing after broken ribs: Because deep breaths hurt, a patient with broken ribs takes 30 breaths a minute of 200 mL each, instead of his usual 12 breaths of 500 mL.
  • The air on a high summit: You travel from sea level, where total air pressure is 760 mm Hg, to a summit 4,300 m up, where it is about 455 mm Hg.

And 353 more prediction scenarios in the tool.
