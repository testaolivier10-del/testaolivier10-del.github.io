About this tool

What is inside

49 feedback loops, including:

  • Body temperature falls: You step outside on a winter morning at −5 °C.
  • Baroreceptor reflex: pressure falls: You stand up quickly after lying down.
  • Renin–angiotensin–aldosterone: pressure and volume fall: After a day of vomiting, a patient has lost fluid and their blood pressure is low.
  • Carbon dioxide rises in the blood: You hold your breath for 40 seconds.
  • Your calf muscles are stretched as you tip forward: You are standing on a bus when it brakes hard.
  • Bright light narrows the pupils: You step out of a dark building into bright sunshine.
  • Blood pressure dips as you stand up: You stand up quickly after lying on a couch.
  • Erythropoietin restores oxygen delivery: You donate a unit of blood.
  • Thrombin makes more thrombin: You cut your finger on a kitchen knife.
  • Blood glucose rises: insulin: You eat a bowl of rice.
  • Thyroid hormone falls: the thyroid axis: For months, a woman eats food that is low in iodine.
  • Cortisol falls: the pituitary–adrenal axis: A patient taking a drug that partly blocks cortisol synthesis has a lower cortisol level for a few days.
  • Fever: the set point is raised: You wake with a sore throat.
  • Stomach acid shuts off gastrin: An hour into a steak dinner, the parietal cells have been secreting acid steadily.
  • Secretin neutralizes acid in the duodenum: A squirt of chyme at pH 2 enters the duodenum.
  • Thermoregulation in full: the core is too cold: A hiker waits out a storm on a windy ridge at 2 °C in damp clothes.
  • Tubuloglomerular feedback: one nephron’s GFR rises: During a brisk walk, blood pressure rises for a few seconds, and filtration in one nephron starts to climb.
  • Micturition: urine flow strengthens the contraction: Once you start to urinate, the stream keeps going until the bladder is nearly empty, even though the bladder gets less stretched as it empties.
  • Pressure natriuresis: arterial pressure rises: After a salty weekend, a man’s blood volume and arterial pressure creep up.
  • Testosterone falls: the male HPG axis: A man’s blood testosterone dips below its usual range.
  • The follicular phase: estradiol and inhibin hold FSH down: As the old corpus luteum dies, its hormones fall and FSH climbs.
  • Plasma osmolality falls: ADH and thirst switch off: At a summer picnic you drink 1.5 L of water in half an hour.
  • Blood pH falls from a fixed acid: breathing responds: In untreated type 1 diabetes, ketone bodies pour into the blood faster than tissues can burn them.
  • Labor: cervical stretch and oxytocin: A woman is in the active phase of labor.

And 25 more feedback loops in the tool.
