About this tool

What is inside

76 graphs, including:

  • The pH scale is logarithmic: Blood pH looks like it barely moves, from 7.35 to 7.45.
  • Flow vs radius: the fourth-power curve: Flow through a tube depends on the fourth power of its radius.
  • Enzyme activity vs temperature: A typical human enzyme, tested across a range of temperatures.
  • A regulated variable around its set point: Your body temperature over an hour.
  • The resting potential and the equilibrium potentials: A neuron's membrane potential over 10 minutes, with the equilibrium potentials of potassium and sodium drawn in.
  • A neuron action potential: The voltage across a patch of neuron membrane.
  • Ventricular muscle action potential: One action potential in a contractile cell of the left ventricle.
  • Pacemaker potential of the SA node: The voltage of an SA node pacemaker cell over three heartbeats.
  • ECG waves and intervals: Two normal beats recorded from one lead, on standard ECG paper: each small square is 0.04 s wide and 0.1 mV tall.
  • The cardiac cycle (Wiggers diagram): One heartbeat of the left heart at 75 beats per minute, 0.8 s.
  • The Frank–Starling curve: How the stroke volume of a healthy left ventricle depends on how full it is before it contracts, with contractility held constant.
  • Pressure along the vascular tree: Blood pressure at each level of the systemic circuit, from the aorta back to the venae cavae, in a person at rest lying down.
  • Pressures along a capillary: A typical capillary in the skin of your hand.
  • Pressures during one quiet breath: One quiet breath of 4 seconds: the change in lung volume (top) and the alveolar and intrapleural pressures, written relative to the air outside (bottom).
  • Lung volumes and capacities (spirogram): A healthy young man breathes quietly, takes the deepest breath he can, blows out as far as he can, then breathes quietly again.
  • PO2 and PCO2 along the path: The partial pressures of oxygen and carbon dioxide at each stop from the air to the tissues and back, at rest at sea level.
  • The oxygen–hemoglobin dissociation curve: How saturated hemoglobin is at each PO2, for adult blood at pH 7.4, a PCO2 of 40 mm Hg and 37 °C.
  • Ventilation and arterial PCO2: How much you breathe each minute as your arterial PCO2 is raised, with oxygen held normal.
  • Neuron action potential with channel states and refractory periods: The top panel is the voltage at the trigger zone of an axon; a stimulus arrives at 0.5 ms.
  • Spatial and temporal summation at the trigger zone: The membrane potential at a neuron’s trigger zone over 100 ms, during four experiments.
  • A night of sleep (hypnogram): The sleep stages of a healthy young adult across one 8-hour night, as scored from the EEG.
  • Tonic and phasic sensory receptors: A steady press is held on the skin from 1 to 4 seconds (top).
  • Light absorbed by the three cone pigments and rhodopsin: Each curve shows how strongly one photoreceptor pigment absorbs light of each wavelength, scaled so its own peak is 1.
  • Hearing thresholds across frequencies (an audiogram): Each line shows the quietest tone one ear can hear at each frequency, in decibels hearing level (dB HL), where 0 is what an average healthy young ear can just hear.
