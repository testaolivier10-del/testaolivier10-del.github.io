Core concept

Energy and ATP

Cells capture energy from food as ATP and spend it on pumping, moving and building.

Every cell runs on ATP. Cells break down food molecules and capture part of the released energy by making ATP; they spend ATP to pump ions against their gradients, to contract, and to build large molecules. Most ATP is made in mitochondria, which need a steady supply of oxygen.

That is why tissues with a high, unstoppable demand are the most fragile when blood flow fails. Heart muscle beats every second of your life and relies almost entirely on oxygen, so a blocked coronary artery injures it within minutes. When you meet any active process, ask where its ATP comes from and what happens when the supply stops.
