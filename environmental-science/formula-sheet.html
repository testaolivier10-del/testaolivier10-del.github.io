AP® Environmental Science

Formulas you must memorize

The exam gives you no formula sheet. These are the equations its calculations use, in our own words: what each means, when to use it, and the trap that costs points.

Three habits that save points

  • Write the setup, then the answer. The calculation question gives one point for the setup and one for the answer with its units, so the setup must be on the page.
  • Brackets and units in the setup. (150−120)120×100\frac{(150 - 120)}{120} \times 100, not 150 - 120 / 120 × 100; and units that visibly cancel.
  • Read the question to the end. "In what year" means add the doubling time to the start year; "in millions" means give the scale.

Numbers and conversions to know

  • Scale words. thousand = 10310^3, million = 10610^6, billion = 10910^9, trillion = 101210^{12}. Multiply by adding exponents and divide by subtracting them. Scientific Notation and Metric Prefixes
  • Metric prefixes. kilo- = 1,000; mega- = 1,000,000; giga- = 1,000,000,000; milli- = one thousandth. 1 kWh = 1,000 Wh. Scientific Notation and Metric Prefixes

On exam day

  • No formula sheet. Bluebook shows only how to type math (fractions, powers, brackets) and a reminder to show work and units. Every formula above must be memorized.
  • Calculator. A four-function (with square root) or scientific calculator, or the Desmos scientific calculator in Bluebook. Graphing calculators are not allowed from 2027.
