Soil forms slowly, on the order of a centimeter in a century or more, but a badly farmed field can lose that much in a few years. Sustainable agriculture is the set of practices that keeps farmland productive for generations. This page sorts them by what they do: protect soil from erosion, keep it fertile, and manage water and pests.

Stopping erosion

Soil erodes when water or wind moves faster than the soil can resist. Every erosion control practice either slows the water or wind, or covers the soil.

PracticeWhat it isHow it protects soil
Contour plowingrows run across the slope, along lines of equal heighteach furrow holds back water, so it soaks in instead of rushing downhill
Strip croppingstrips of a row crop (corn) alternate with a dense crop (hay, clover)dense strips slow runoff and catch soil washed from the row-crop strips
Terracinga steep slope is cut into flat stepseach step holds water and soil, so water cannot run the whole slope
No-till farmingseeds are planted through the last crop's residue without plowingresidue shields soil from rain and wind; more water soaks in; organic matter builds
Windbreaksrows of trees or shrubs along field edgesslow the wind near the ground, so less soil is blown away
Perennial and cover cropsplants that live for years, or crops grown between main cropsroots hold soil all year; leaves cover it

Keeping soil fertile

  • Crop rotation: growing different crops in a field from year to year. It breaks the life cycles of pests that feed on one crop, and including legumes such as soybeans or clover adds nitrogen through the bacteria in their root nodules.
  • Organic fertilizer: manure, compost and plowed-in green plants. It releases nutrients slowly, so less washes away, and adds organic matter that helps soil hold water and nutrients. Its drawbacks: lower nutrient content per kilogram, so more must be spread, and less precise amounts.
  • Liming: spreading crushed limestone to raise the pH of acidic soil, so plants can take up nutrients.
  • Rotational grazing keeps pasture covered and lets it recover between grazing.

Comparing practices with numbers

A field plowed up and down a slope loses 20 metric tons of soil per hectare per year; under no-till with contour rows it loses 2. Percent change: $\frac{2 - 20}{20} \times 100 = -90\%$.

Corn yield falls from 9.6 to 9.2 metric tons per hectare under strip cropping: $\frac{9.2 - 9.6}{9.6} \times 100 \approx -4.2\%$. A 90% cut in soil loss for a 4% cut in yield is a trade most farmers can accept.

A 40-hectare field that cuts its soil loss from 18 to 3 metric tons per hectare saves $(18 - 3)\,\frac{\text{metric tons}}{\text{ha}} \times 40\,\text{ha} = 600$ metric tons of soil a year.

Water and pests

Sustainable farms also use water efficiently, with drip irrigation where it pays, and manage pests with the integrated pest management methods from the previous topic. When you write a solution, match the practice to the problem and the place: terraces for steep slopes, windbreaks for flat windy plains, strip cropping or contour plowing for rolling fields, and say how each one works.
