Water pollution

Add nutrients and follow the chain to a dead zone.

About this tool

A lake, a field and a storm. Choose how much fertilizer runs off and follow the whole chain: nutrients, an algal bloom, die-off, decomposition, falling dissolved oxygen and, if it drops below 2 mg/L, a dead zone. Then warm the water to see thermal pollution lower the oxygen by itself, and send sewage through a treatment plant stage by stage.

How the lake model works

The lake is a small model, solved day by day: nutrients feed algae, algae that run out of nutrients die and sink, aerobic decomposers break the dead algae down and use oxygen, and oxygen from the air mixes back in toward saturation. The curve is the bottom water's dissolved oxygen in mg/L. It is built to show the order and the shape of the chain, not to forecast a real lake.

Saturation (the most oxygen fresh water can hold at sea level) comes from the USGS dissolved oxygen tables, rounded to 0.1 mg/L: 14.6 mg/L at 0 °C, 11.3 at 10 °C, 9.1 at 20 °C, 7.6 at 30 °C. Warmer water holds less, so the same bloom does more harm in a warm lake. Water below 2 mg/L is called hypoxic (a dead zone). The sewage percentages are typical rounded values for each stage; real plants vary.
