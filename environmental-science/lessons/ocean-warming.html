Ocean Warming

The ocean absorbs about 90% of the extra heat.

Practice 5: Data AnalysisPractice 1: Concept Explanation

Part 1 · Hook

Why this matters

In 2016 and 2017, water off northeastern Australia stayed unusually warm for weeks. Large parts of the Great Barrier Reef turned white and many corals died. The ocean had been quietly soaking up heat for decades, and the reef showed what that heat can do.

Part 5 · Step by step

How it works, step by step

  1. Greenhouse gases trap extra heat→the ocean absorbs about 90% of it, because water stores heat well
  2. Warmer water takes up more space (thermal expansion)→sea level rises, alongside water from melting land ice
  3. Warmer water holds less oxygen and mixes less→oxygen falls and fewer nutrients reach the surface
  4. Heat stress and shifting temperatures→corals bleach, fish move toward the poles, and currents can change

Part 6 · Key ideas

Key ideas

  • The ocean has absorbed about 90% of the extra heat trapped since 1970.
  • Thermal expansion: warmer water takes up more space. With melting land ice, it causes most sea level rise. It is caused by heat, not by dissolved CO₂.
  • Warming lowers dissolved oxygen, bleaches corals, shifts species ranges and makes the surface layer mix less with nutrient-rich deep water.
  • The thermohaline circulation (ocean conveyor belt) is driven by temperature and salinity; fresh meltwater could weaken it.

Part 7 · Misconception

A common mistake

The wrong idea: Seawater expands because it absorbs carbon dioxide.

What actually happens: Thermal expansion is caused by heat: warmer water molecules move faster and spread apart. CO₂ dissolving in seawater changes its chemistry, which is a different problem covered next.

Part 8 · Check yourself

Check yourself

Exam-style questions. Anything you miss goes into your review queue.

1. What is thermal expansion of seawater?

  1. Water takes up more space as it warms
  2. Water gains mass as it absorbs CO₂
  3. Ice on land slides into the sea
  4. Warm water rises and cold water sinks
Show the answer

Thermal expansion: the same mass of water fills a larger volume when it is warmer. It is one of the two main causes of sea level rise.

  • Correct: Water takes up more space as it warms: Right: warmer water molecules move faster and spread apart, so the same mass fills more volume.
  • Water gains mass as it absorbs CO₂: Dissolved CO₂ adds little volume; thermal expansion is caused by heat.
  • Ice on land slides into the sea: That is land ice adding water, a separate cause of sea level rise.
  • Warm water rises and cold water sinks: That describes circulation, not expansion.

2. Which two processes cause most of today’s sea level rise?

  1. Melting sea ice and more rainfall
  2. Melting land ice and thermal expansion
  3. Dissolved CO₂ and melting sea ice
  4. More rivers and sinking icebergs
Show the answer

Land ice adds water; thermal expansion makes the existing water take up more room.

  • Melting sea ice and more rainfall: Sea ice already floats; rain is part of the water cycle and does not add new water.
  • Correct: Melting land ice and thermal expansion: Right: water from glaciers and ice sheets, plus warmer water expanding.
  • Dissolved CO₂ and melting sea ice: Neither raises sea level much.
  • More rivers and sinking icebergs: Rivers return water that came from the ocean; they do not add new water.

An inverse relationship: warmer seawater holds less oxygen.

7. The figure shows less mixing in a warmer ocean. Why could this lower productivity at the surface?

  1. The surface gets too dark for photosynthesis
  2. Fewer nutrients rise from deep water
  3. Warm water has too many nutrients
  4. Phytoplankton sink into the warm layer
Show the answer

A warm, light surface layer floats on cold, dense water, which blocks mixing that brings nutrients up.

  • The surface gets too dark for photosynthesis: Warming does not darken the surface layer.
  • Correct: Fewer nutrients rise from deep water: Right: phytoplankton at the surface depend on nutrients mixed up from below.
  • Warm water has too many nutrients: The problem is fewer nutrients reaching the surface.
  • Phytoplankton sink into the warm layer: Phytoplankton stay near the surface; nutrients are what is missing.

Part 9 · Summary

Summary

The ocean absorbs about 90% of the extra heat. Warmer water expands (thermal expansion), which with melting land ice raises sea level. Warm water holds less oxygen and mixes less, so oxygen and surface nutrients fall. Heat stress bleaches corals and pushes species toward the poles. The thermohaline circulation, driven by temperature and salinity, could slow as fresh meltwater enters the North Atlantic.
