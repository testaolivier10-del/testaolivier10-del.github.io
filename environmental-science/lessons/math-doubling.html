Growth Rates, Doubling Time and the Rule of 70

A population growing by a fixed percent each year doubles in a fixed time.

Practice 6: Mathematical Routines

Part 1 · Hook

Why this matters

A city growing "only" 3.5% a year doubles in 20 years: twice the schools, water and roads within one generation. Growth at a steady percent speeds up as the base gets bigger. The rule of 70 lets you turn any yearly growth rate into a doubling time in your head, and the exam asks for it every year.

Part 5 · Step by step

How it works, step by step

  1. A population grows by a fixed percent each year→each year’s increase is larger than the last
  2. Divide 70 by the percent growth rate→you get the doubling time in years
  3. Add the doubling time to the start year→you get the year the population doubles

Part 6 · Key ideas

Key ideas

  • A growth rate is the percent change per year: 2% a year means 2 more for every 100 each year.
  • Rule of 70: doubling time ≈ 70 ÷ growth rate (in %, not as a decimal).
  • Doubling time depends on the rate, not on the starting size.
  • Each doubling multiplies by 2: after n doublings, multiply by 2 n times (2, 4, 8, 16, ...).

Part 7 · Misconception

A common mistake

The wrong idea: A question asking "in what year" wants the doubling time.

What actually happens: The doubling time is a number of years. "In what year" asks you to add it to the starting year: 70 ÷ 2.5 = 28 years, so 2024 + 28 = 2052.

Part 9 · Summary

Summary

A population growing by a fixed percent each year doubles in a fixed time. By the rule of 70, doubling time ≈ 70 ÷ growth rate in percent. Add the doubling time to the start year for "in what year"; multiply by 2 for each doubling to project the size. Doubling time depends only on the rate: twice the rate, half the time.
