Unit 4 · Topic 4.4

Earth's Atmosphere

The atmosphere is about 78% nitrogen and 21% oxygen, layered by how temperature changes with height.

Practice 1: Concept ExplanationPractice 2: Visual Representations

Part 1 · Hook

Why this matters

A jet cruising at 11 km is above nearly all the weather, in air colder than −50 °C and too thin to breathe. A little higher, a thin band of ozone soaks up the ultraviolet light that would otherwise burn skin and damage the DNA of every living thing on the surface. The air above you is not one blanket but a stack of layers, each with its own job.

Part 4 · Step by step

How it works, step by step

  1. Sunlight warms the ground, and the ground warms the air above it→the troposphere is warmest at the bottom and cools with height
  2. Warm air near the ground rises, expands and cools→water vapor condenses into clouds, and weather forms in the troposphere
  3. Ozone in the stratosphere absorbs ultraviolet light→the stratosphere warms with height, and most harmful UV never reaches the surface
  4. Warmer air sits on top of colder air at the bottom of the stratosphere→rising air stops near the top of the troposphere, so weather stays below about 10 to 16 km

Part 5 · Key ideas

Key ideas

  • Dry air is about 78% nitrogen, 21% oxygen and 0.9% argon; carbon dioxide is about 0.04%. Water vapor varies from about 0% to 4%.
  • The layers are defined by how temperature changes with height: the troposphere cools upward, the stratosphere warms upward, the mesosphere cools upward, the thermosphere warms upward.
  • The troposphere (ground to about 10-16 km, highest over the equator) holds about 75% of the air's mass and nearly all the weather.
  • The ozone layer, about 15 to 35 km up in the stratosphere, absorbs most of the Sun's ultraviolet (UV) radiation, which causes skin cancer and cataracts and harms plants and plankton.
  • Air gets thinner with height: about half of the air's mass is below 5.5 km.

Part 6 · Misconception

A common mistake

The wrong idea: The higher you go, the colder it gets, all the way up.

What actually happens: Temperature falls with height only in the troposphere and mesosphere. In the stratosphere, ozone absorbing UV light makes it warmer with height, and the thin thermosphere is heated strongly by the Sun.

Part 7 · Check yourself

Check yourself

Exam-style questions. Anything you miss goes into your review queue.

1. In which layer of the atmosphere does almost all weather happen?

  1. Troposphere
  2. Stratosphere
  3. Mesosphere
  4. Thermosphere
Show the answer

The troposphere, the lowest layer, holds about three quarters of the air's mass and nearly all its water vapor, so clouds, rain and storms form there.

  • Correct: Troposphere: Right: weather happens in the troposphere.
  • Stratosphere: The stratosphere is dry and stable; it holds the ozone layer.
  • Mesosphere: The mesosphere is far above the clouds.
  • Thermosphere: The thermosphere is extremely thin air, far above the weather.

2. What does the ozone layer do for life on Earth's surface?

  1. Absorbs most harmful ultraviolet light
  2. Traps heat near the ground to warm Earth
  3. Supplies the oxygen that animals breathe
  4. Reflects sunlight to keep the surface cool
Show the answer

Ozone in the stratosphere absorbs most UV-B and nearly all UV-C, which would otherwise damage DNA, cause skin cancer and cataracts, and harm plants and plankton.

  • Correct: Absorbs most harmful ultraviolet light: Right: the ozone layer filters out most harmful UV radiation.
  • Traps heat near the ground to warm Earth: Keeping Earth warm is a different job, done mostly by gases such as water vapor and carbon dioxide in the troposphere.
  • Supplies the oxygen that animals breathe: The oxygen animals breathe is O2 made by photosynthesis, not ozone.
  • Reflects sunlight to keep the surface cool: Ozone absorbs UV light; it does not act as a mirror for sunlight.

3. Why does air get colder as you climb a mountain?

  1. The air is heated from the ground, and rising air cools
  2. Air higher up sits closer to the cold ozone layer
  3. Mountains block sunlight from reaching their peaks
  4. Air higher up holds more oxygen, which absorbs heat
Show the answer

Sunlight warms the ground, which warms the air above it. Air that rises meets lower pressure, expands and cools, so the troposphere cools by about 6.5 °C per kilometer on average.

  • Correct: The air is heated from the ground, and rising air cools: Right: heating from below plus cooling of rising air makes the troposphere colder with height.
  • Air higher up sits closer to the cold ozone layer: The ozone layer is where the air starts to warm, not a cold source.
  • Mountains block sunlight from reaching their peaks: Mountain peaks get plenty of sunlight.
  • Air higher up holds more oxygen, which absorbs heat: The share of oxygen stays about 21% with height; the air just gets thinner.

Part 8 · Summary

Summary

The atmosphere is about 78% nitrogen and 21% oxygen, layered by how temperature changes with height. The troposphere, heated from the ground, cools upward and holds the weather. The stratosphere warms upward because its ozone layer absorbs ultraviolet light, shielding life below. The mesosphere cools upward again, and the thin thermosphere heats sharply.
