Rates and Per-Capita Values

A rate divides one quantity by another: liters per day, people per km², kilograms per person.

Practice 6: Mathematical Routines

Part 1 · Hook

Why this matters

Which city wastes more water: one that uses 200 million liters a day, or one that uses 20 million? You cannot tell until you know how many people live in each. Dividing a total by the number of people, the area or the time turns raw totals into rates you can compare fairly, and the exam asks for them constantly.

Part 4 · Step by step

How it works, step by step

  1. Divide a total by the number of people→you get a per-capita value
  2. Divide by an area or a time instead→you get a density or a rate per unit of time
  3. Keep the units in the setup→the answer’s unit tells you what you found: L per person, people per km²

Part 5 · Key ideas

Key ideas

  • A rate compares two quantities with different units: liters per day, people per km², kg per person.
  • A unit rate is a rate for one unit of the second quantity: 150 L per 1 person.
  • Per capita means per person: the total ÷ the population.
  • The word after "per" goes on the bottom: liters per km = L ÷ km.

Part 6 · Misconception

A common mistake

The wrong idea: The place with the biggest total uses resources the most heavily.

What actually happens: Big totals often just mean more people. Divide by the population (per capita) before you decide who uses more.

Part 7 · Check yourself

Check yourself

Exam-style questions. Anything you miss goes into your review queue.

1. A town of 30,000 people uses 4,500,000 liters of water a day. How many liters is that per person per day?

Type a number, then its unit (L/person/day). Scale words (2.5 million) and powers of ten (2.5 × 10^6) are read.

Show the answer

4,500,000L30,000people\frac{4{,}500{,}000\,\text{L}}{30{,}000\,\text{people}}=150= 150 L per person per day.

  • Answer: 150 L/person/day

2. A family’s car used 1,200 liters of gasoline in 15,000 km of driving. Write the setup for the fuel used per km, with units, then give the answer.

Type the setup as on the exam, with brackets and units, then the answer.

Show the answer

1,200L15,000km\frac{1{,}200\,\text{L}}{15{,}000\,\text{km}}=0.08= 0.08 L per km.

  • Setup: 1200L15000km\frac{1200\,\text{L}}{15000\,\text{km}}; answer 0.08 L/km

Type a number, then its unit (people/km^2). Scale words (2.5 million) and powers of ten (2.5 × 10^6) are read.

Show the answer

7. A newspaper headline says "Birch is the most wasteful town." What does the per-person data show?

  1. Birch has the largest total but the smallest per-person amount.
  2. Birch is the most wasteful by both measures.
  3. Birch has the most trash per person.
  4. The towns are too different in size to compare.
Show the answer

Birch’s total (20,000 t) is largest because it has the most people; per person (0.4 t) it is the least wasteful of the three.

  • Correct: Birch has the largest total but the smallest per-person amount.: Right: totals reflect size; per person shows behavior.
  • Birch is the most wasteful by both measures.: Per person, Birch is the lowest.
  • Birch has the most trash per person.: Cedar has the most per person (0.65 t).
  • The towns are too different in size to compare.: Per-person values make a fair comparison across sizes.

Part 8 · Summary

Summary

A rate divides one quantity by another: liters per day, people per km², kilograms per person. A per-capita value divides a total by the number of people, so places of different sizes can be compared fairly. Write the setup with units: the word after "per" goes on the bottom, and the units of the answer tell you what you found.
