Percents and Percent Change

A percent is a part out of 100: part ÷ whole × 100.

Practice 6: Mathematical Routines

Part 1 · Hook

Why this matters

A news story says a fish population "crashed 50% and then bounced back 50%". Is it back where it started? No: it is 25% lower. Percents are the most common calculation on the exam and the easiest place to lose points, by dividing by the wrong number, forgetting × 100 or leaving out the brackets.

Part 4 · Step by step

How it works, step by step

  1. Subtract old from new, in brackets→you get the change, with its sign
  2. Divide the change by the old value→you get the change as a fraction of where you started
  3. Multiply by 100→you get the percent change: positive for a rise, negative for a fall

Part 5 · Key ideas

Key ideas

  • A percent is a number out of 100: part ÷ whole × 100.
  • Percent of a quantity: (percent ÷ 100) × quantity. 18% of 4.0 million = 0.18 × 4,000,000 = 720,000.
  • Percent change = (new - old) / old × 100. Brackets first, divide by the old value, then × 100.
  • Percent changes from different starting values do not add: +25% then -10% is +12.5%, not +15%.

Part 6 · Misconception

A common mistake

The wrong idea: Percent change divides by the new value, or by whichever number is bigger.

What actually happens: Always divide by the old (starting) value. A rise from 120 to 150 is 30 / 120 = 25%; a fall from 150 to 120 is -30 / 150 = -20%.

Check yourself

Exam-style questions. Anything you miss goes into your review queue.

1. A town’s water use rose from 4,000 to 5,000 liters per day. Write the setup for the percent change, then give the answer.

Type the setup as on the exam, with brackets and units, then the answer.

Show the answer

5,000−4,0004,000×100\frac{5{,}000 - 4{,}000}{4{,}000} \times 100=25%= 25\%.

  • Setup: 5000−40004000⋅100\frac{5000 - 4000}{4000} \cdot 100; answer 25 %

2. Coal provides 18% of a country’s 4.0 million GWh of electricity. How many GWh is that?

Type a number, then its unit (GWh). Scale words (2.5 million) and powers of ten (2.5 × 10^6) are read.

Show the answer

18100×4,000,000\frac{18}{100} \times 4{,}000{,}000=720,000= 720{,}000 GWh.

  • Answer: 720,000 GWh

3. Which setup gives the percent change from 80 to 60?

  1. (60 - 80) / 80 × 100
  2. 60 - 80 / 80 × 100
  3. (80 - 60) / 60 × 100
  4. (60 - 80) / 60 × 100
Show the answer

New minus old, in brackets, divided by the old value, times 100: −25%-25\%.

  • Correct: (60 - 80) / 80 × 100: Right: brackets, old value on the bottom, × 100.
  • 60 - 80 / 80 × 100: Without brackets the division happens first, so it is not the change.
  • (80 - 60) / 60 × 100: That divides by the new value and reverses the sign.
  • (60 - 80) / 60 × 100: That divides by the new value.

7. Which statement describes the frog counts?

  1. They rose 25%, then fell 10%, ending 12.5% above 2020.
  2. They rose 25% and kept rising.
  3. They fell over the whole period.
  4. They rose 15% overall, since 25 - 10 = 15.
Show the answer

120 → 150 is +25%; 150 → 135 is -10%; 120 → 135 is +12.5% overall.

  • Correct: They rose 25%, then fell 10%, ending 12.5% above 2020.: Right: each change computed from its own start.
  • They rose 25% and kept rising.: The count fell from 2022 to 2024.
  • They fell over the whole period.: 135 is above 120.
  • They rose 15% overall, since 25 - 10 = 15.: Percent changes from different starting values do not simply add.

Part 8 · Summary

Summary

A percent is a part out of 100: part ÷ whole × 100. Percent of a quantity is (percent ÷ 100) × quantity. Percent change is (new - old) / old × 100: subtract inside brackets, divide by the starting value, multiply by 100, and keep the sign. Show this setup exactly; readers give a point for it.
