Unit 1 · Topic 1.10

Energy Flow and the 10% Rule

Energy enters ecosystems through producers and flows one way, up the trophic levels.

Practice 2: Visual RepresentationsPractice 6: Mathematical Routines

Part 1 · Hook

Why this matters

A field of grass can feed a herd of grazers, the grazers a few predators, and the predators perhaps one large hunter. Every step up loses most of the energy as heat. That single fact explains why big predators are rare, why there are so few trophic levels, and why eating plants feeds more people per acre than eating meat.

Part 5 · Step by step

How it works, step by step

  1. Organisms at each level use most of their food energy for respiration and body heat→that energy leaves as heat and cannot be passed on
  2. Some of each level is never eaten, or is lost in waste→that energy goes to decomposers instead
  3. Only about 10% is stored as new tissue→about 10% is available to the next level up, so energy shrinks at every step

Part 6 · Key ideas

Key ideas

  • The 10% rule: about 10% of the energy at one trophic level reaches the next. Multiply by 0.10 for each step up.
  • The first law of thermodynamics: energy is not created or destroyed. The second law: every transfer turns some into heat, so no transfer is 100% efficient.
  • An energy pyramid always narrows going up; biomass pyramids usually do too.
  • Transfer efficiency = energy at the higher level ÷ energy at the lower level × 100.

Part 7 · Misconception

A common mistake

The wrong idea: Energy is used up or destroyed as it moves up the trophic levels.

What actually happens: Energy is not destroyed (first law). It changes form, and at each step most of it becomes heat released to the surroundings (second law), which organisms cannot use as food.

Part 9 · Summary

Summary

Energy enters ecosystems through producers and flows one way, up the trophic levels. At each step about 90% is used for life processes and lost as heat, so only about 10% passes on: multiply by 0.10 for each step. This explains energy pyramids, the small number of trophic levels and why eating at a lower level feeds more people. Energy is not destroyed; it leaves as heat.
