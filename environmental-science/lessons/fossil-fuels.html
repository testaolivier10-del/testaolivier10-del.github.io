Fossil Fuels

A fossil fuel power plant burns fuel to make steam, which spins a turbine and generator.

Practice 2: Visual RepresentationsPractice 7: Environmental Solutions

Part 1 · Hook

Why this matters

Flip a light switch and, in much of the world, a power plant somewhere burns a little more coal or natural gas. Only about a third of that fuel's energy reaches your outlet; the rest warms a river or rises up a smokestack. Following the energy from fuel to outlet shows why fossil fuels are so useful, and why they cost so much.

Part 5 · Step by step

How it works, step by step

  1. Fuel burns in the boiler→its chemical energy heats water into high-pressure steam
  2. Steam pushes on the turbine blades→the turbine spins a shaft connected to the generator
  3. The generator spins magnets past coils of wire→electricity flows out to the power lines
  4. Used steam must be cooled back to water to keep the cycle going→about two thirds of the fuel's energy is lost as waste heat, and burning releases carbon dioxide

Part 6 · Key ideas

Key ideas

  • A power plant converts energy in steps: chemical (fuel) to heat (steam) to motion (turbine) to electricity (generator). Each step loses some energy as heat.
  • Overall efficiency is the product of the step efficiencies: 0.88×0.37×0.98×0.940.88 \times 0.37 \times 0.98 \times 0.94≈0.30\approx 0.30, about 30 percent. To find the input needed, divide the output by the efficiency.
  • Hydraulic fracturing cracks shale with high-pressure water, sand and chemicals to free natural gas and oil; it can contaminate groundwater and release volatile chemicals and methane.
  • Fossil fuels are energy-dense, storable and reliable, but they release carbon dioxide and air pollutants, their extraction damages land and water, and their supply is fixed.

Part 7 · Misconception

A common mistake

The wrong idea: The warm water a power plant returns to a river is harmful because it carries chemicals or metals from the fuel.

What actually happens: Cooling water never touches the fuel. It carries only heat. The harm is thermal: warmer water holds less dissolved oxygen, which stresses fish.

Part 8 · Check yourself

Check yourself

Exam-style questions. Anything you miss goes into your review queue.

1. In a power plant, what does the turbine do?

  1. It cools the used steam back into liquid water for reuse
  2. It turns electricity into heat for nearby homes and offices
  3. It turns the energy of moving steam into a spinning shaft
  4. It burns the fuel to release heat inside the boiler walls
Show the answer

Steam pushes on the turbine blades and spins them. The spinning shaft drives the generator.

  • It cools the used steam back into liquid water for reuse: Cooling the steam is the condenser's job.
  • It turns electricity into heat for nearby homes and offices: The plant makes electricity; it does not turn electricity into heat.
  • Correct: It turns the energy of moving steam into a spinning shaft: Right: the turbine changes the steam's energy into rotation.
  • It burns the fuel to release heat inside the boiler walls: Burning happens in the boiler, before the turbine.

2. What is hydraulic fracturing (fracking)?

  1. Cracking shale with high-pressure fluid to free gas or oil
  2. Burning coal seams underground to release gas up through wells
  3. Using explosives on mountaintops to uncover the coal seams below
  4. Heating tar sands with steam so the bitumen flows up to the surface
Show the answer

Fracking injects a high-pressure mix of water, sand and chemicals into shale rock. The cracks let trapped natural gas or oil flow to the well; the sand holds the cracks open.

  • Correct: Cracking shale with high-pressure fluid to free gas or oil: Right: high-pressure fluid cracks the shale.
  • Burning coal seams underground to release gas up through wells: Underground coal burning is a different, rare process.
  • Using explosives on mountaintops to uncover the coal seams below: That describes surface mining of coal.
  • Heating tar sands with steam so the bitumen flows up to the surface: That describes steam extraction of bitumen from tar sands.

Part 9 · Summary

Summary

A fossil fuel power plant burns fuel to make steam, which spins a turbine and generator. Each step loses energy as heat, so only about a third of the fuel's energy becomes electricity; overall efficiency is the product of the step efficiencies. Fossil fuels are dense and reliable but release carbon dioxide and pollutants. Fracking has unlocked shale gas and oil, with risks to groundwater and air.
