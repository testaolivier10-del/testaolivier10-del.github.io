Ocean Acidification

The ocean absorbs part of the CO₂ people add to the air.

Practice 2: Visual RepresentationsPractice 6: Mathematical Routines

Part 1 · Hook

Why this matters

Around 2007, oyster hatcheries in Oregon and Washington began losing most of their baby oysters. The larvae could not build their first shells. The culprit was not disease or pollution from a pipe, but seawater that had absorbed so much carbon dioxide from the air that it was eating away at shell-building.

Part 5 · Step by step

How it works, step by step

  1. Burning fossil fuels raises CO₂ in the air→the ocean absorbs about a quarter of it
  2. Dissolved CO₂ reacts with water to form carbonic acid→it releases hydrogen ions, so pH falls
  3. Hydrogen ions combine with carbonate ions→less carbonate is left for building calcium carbonate
  4. Shell-builders such as corals, oysters and some plankton struggle→reefs weaken and food webs that depend on them suffer

Part 6 · Key ideas

Key ideas

  • Ocean acidification: seawater pH falls as it absorbs CO₂. Average surface pH has dropped from about 8.2 to about 8.1 since the 1700s and could reach about 7.8 by 2100 if emissions stay high.
  • Chemistry: CO2+H2O→H2CO3\text{CO}_2 + \text{H}_2\text{O} \rightarrow \text{H}_2\text{CO}_3 (carbonic acid) →H++HCO3−\rightarrow \text{H}^+ + \text{HCO}_3^-; then H++CO32−→HCO3−\text{H}^+ + \text{CO}_3^{2-} \rightarrow \text{HCO}_3^- removes carbonate.
  • The pH scale is logarithmic: hydrogen ions multiply by 10drop10^{\text{drop}}. A drop of 0.1 is about 26% more H⁺; a drop of 0.3 is about twice as much.
  • The ocean is still basic (pH above 7); "acidification" names the direction of change.

Part 7 · Misconception

A common mistake

The wrong idea: Ocean acidification is caused by acid rain, and it means the ocean is now an acid.

What actually happens: It is caused by CO₂ dissolving straight from the air, not by the SO₂ and NOx of acid rain. Seawater is still basic, around pH 8.1; it is becoming less basic.

Part 8 · Check yourself

Check yourself

Exam-style questions. Anything you miss goes into your review queue.

1. When CO₂ dissolves in seawater, which product makes the water more acidic?

  1. Oxygen gas released by the reaction
  2. Hydrogen ions from carbonic acid
  3. Calcium carbonate dissolving out of the water
  4. Nitrogen gas from the air
Show the answer

pH measures hydrogen ions: more H⁺ means lower pH and more acidic water.

  • Oxygen gas released by the reaction: The reaction does not release oxygen.
  • Correct: Hydrogen ions from carbonic acid: Right: CO₂ + H₂O → H₂CO₃, which releases H⁺. More H⁺ means lower pH.
  • Calcium carbonate dissolving out of the water: Calcium carbonate is what shells are made of; acidification makes it harder to build.
  • Nitrogen gas from the air: Nitrogen is not part of the reaction.

2. Why does ocean acidification make it harder for corals, clams and some plankton to build shells and skeletons?

  1. Acid water is too cold for shells to form
  2. Dissolved CO₂ blocks sunlight from reaching the reef
  3. Hydrogen ions bind carbonate, the shell material
  4. Acid water has too much carbonate for shells to use
Show the answer

Fewer carbonate ions mean shell-builders spend more energy making calcium carbonate, and existing shells can start to dissolve.

  • Acid water is too cold for shells to form: Acidification is a chemical change, not a temperature change.
  • Dissolved CO₂ blocks sunlight from reaching the reef: CO₂ in water does not block light.
  • Correct: Hydrogen ions bind carbonate, the shell material: Right: H⁺ + CO₃²⁻ → HCO₃⁻ removes the carbonate these animals need.
  • Acid water has too much carbonate for shells to use: It has less carbonate, not more.

7. Which action would do the most to slow ocean acidification worldwide?

  1. Adding fertilizer to the open ocean
  2. Cutting CO₂ emissions from burning fossil fuels
  3. Phasing out CFCs
  4. Banning sulfur in gasoline
Show the answer

Acidification is driven by CO₂ itself, so the global fix is cutting CO₂ emissions.

  • Adding fertilizer to the open ocean: Fertilizer causes algal blooms and dead zones.
  • Correct: Cutting CO₂ emissions from burning fossil fuels: Right: less CO₂ in the air means less dissolving into the ocean.
  • Phasing out CFCs: CFCs destroy ozone; they are not the cause of acidification.
  • Banning sulfur in gasoline: That reduces acid rain, not ocean acidification.

Part 9 · Summary

Summary

The ocean absorbs part of the CO₂ people add to the air. Dissolved CO₂ forms carbonic acid, which releases hydrogen ions and lowers pH; those ions also bind carbonate, so corals, oysters and some plankton struggle to build calcium carbonate. pH has fallen from about 8.2 to 8.1; because the scale is logarithmic, that is about 26% more hydrogen ions. Cutting CO₂ emissions is the fix.
