Unit 8 · Topic 8.8

Bioaccumulation and Biomagnification

Bioaccumulation is the build-up of a persistent, fat-soluble pollutant in one organism over its life.

Practice 2: Visual RepresentationsPractice 6: Mathematical Routines

Part 1 · Hook

Why this matters

In a lake, a pesticide is so dilute in the water that a test can barely find it: 0.00005 ppm, or 5 parts per hundred billion. In the ospreys that fish there, the same pesticide is a million times more concentrated, enough to make their eggs crack under the parents' weight. Nobody sprayed the ospreys. The food chain concentrated the chemical for them.

Part 5 · Step by step

How it works, step by step

  1. A persistent, fat-soluble pollutant (DDT, PCBs, methylmercury) enters the water at a very low level→producers and small organisms absorb it and store it in fat
  2. The organism keeps taking it in but cannot break it down or excrete it→its body concentration rises over its life: bioaccumulation
  3. Each consumer eats many organisms from the level below→it takes in all their stored pollutant, while much of the food's mass is used for energy
  4. This repeats at every trophic level→the concentration multiplies up the chain: biomagnification
  5. Top predators carry the highest levels→they suffer harm, such as thin eggshells in birds of prey, and people who eat top predator fish are exposed

Part 6 · Key ideas

Key ideas

  • Bioaccumulation: a substance builds up in one organism over its life, because it is taken in faster than it is removed.
  • Biomagnification: the concentration rises at each higher trophic level of a food chain. Top predators carry the most.
  • Substances that do this are persistent and fat-soluble: DDT, PCBs and methylmercury, which bacteria make from mercury released by sources such as burning coal and small-scale gold mining.
  • Effects: DDT thinned eggshells of eagles, ospreys and pelicans; mercury harms the developing nervous system, so fish advisories limit meals of large predatory fish.

Part 7 · Misconception

A common mistake

The wrong idea: Bioaccumulation and biomagnification are two names for the same thing.

What actually happens: Bioaccumulation is the build-up inside one organism over time. Biomagnification is the increase from one trophic level to the next.

Part 8 · Check yourself

Check yourself

Exam-style questions. Anything you miss goes into your review queue.

1. What is biomagnification?

  1. The build-up of a pollutant in one organism over its life
  2. The growth of algae when nutrients are added
  3. The breakdown of a pollutant by bacteria
  4. A pollutant rising at each higher trophic level
Show the answer

Biomagnification is the increase in concentration of a persistent, fat-soluble substance at each higher trophic level of a food chain.

  • The build-up of a pollutant in one organism over its life: That is bioaccumulation.
  • The growth of algae when nutrients are added: That describes an algal bloom.
  • The breakdown of a pollutant by bacteria: That is biodegradation, the opposite of building up.
  • Correct: A pollutant rising at each higher trophic level: Right: it climbs up the food chain.

4. Why does the concentration rise at each step of the food chain?

  1. Larger animals make the pesticide in their livers
  2. Each level keeps the pesticide in what it eats
  3. Predators drink more lake water than their prey
  4. The pesticide breaks down faster in small organisms
Show the answer

Each level eats many organisms from the level below. The pesticide is stored in fat and not broken down or excreted, while much of the food's energy is used and lost, so the pesticide is concentrated.

  • Larger animals make the pesticide in their livers: Animals do not make the pesticide.
  • Correct: Each level keeps the pesticide in what it eats: Right: the chemical is retained while the food's mass is used up.
  • Predators drink more lake water than their prey: Most of the pesticide comes from food, not water.
  • The pesticide breaks down faster in small organisms: It is persistent at every level; that is why it builds up.

5. The osprey population in the region is falling. Based on the table, which effect is the most likely cause?

  1. The pesticide thins their eggshells
  2. The pesticide kills the fish, so ospreys starve
  3. Ospreys lay more eggs as pesticide levels rise
  4. The lake water itself is too toxic for ospreys to drink
Show the answer

Ospreys are at the top of the chain, with the highest concentration. Organochlorine pesticides like DDT thinned the eggshells of fish-eating birds of prey, so fewer chicks hatched.

  • Correct: The pesticide thins their eggshells: Right: top predators carry the highest load.
  • The pesticide kills the fish, so ospreys starve: The table shows fish alive and carrying the pesticide, not gone.
  • Ospreys lay more eggs as pesticide levels rise: High levels reduce, not increase, breeding success.
  • The lake water itself is too toxic for ospreys to drink: The water has the lowest concentration.

6. Between which two adjacent levels does the concentration rise by the largest factor?

  1. Large fish to osprey
  2. Small fish to large fish
  3. Zooplankton to small fish
  4. Water to phytoplankton
Show the answer

Water to phytoplankton rises 800 times (0.04 / 0.00005). The later steps are about 7.5, 6.7, 6 and 4.2 times. Producers take up the pesticide from water and store it in fat.

  • Large fish to osprey: 50 / 12 is about 4.2 times, the smallest step.
  • Small fish to large fish: 12 / 2 = 6 times.
  • Zooplankton to small fish: 2 / 0.3 is about 6.7 times.
  • Correct: Water to phytoplankton: Right: 0.04 / 0.00005 = 800 times.

Part 9 · Summary

Summary

Bioaccumulation is the build-up of a persistent, fat-soluble pollutant in one organism over its life. Biomagnification is the rise in its concentration at each higher trophic level, because each consumer eats many organisms and keeps their stored pollutant. DDT, PCBs and methylmercury reach their highest levels in top predators, thinning eggshells in birds of prey and leading to fish advisories for people.
