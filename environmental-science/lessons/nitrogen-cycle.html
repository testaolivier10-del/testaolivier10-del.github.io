Unit 1 · Topic 1.5

The Nitrogen Cycle

Nitrogen’s largest reservoir is N2 gas in the air, which most organisms cannot use.

Practice 1: Concept ExplanationPractice 2: Visual Representations

Part 1 · Hook

Why this matters

Every protein in your body contains nitrogen, and you are swimming in it: 78% of each breath is nitrogen gas. Yet plants can starve for nitrogen while surrounded by it, because almost nothing can break N2 apart. Bacteria in soil and in bean roots do it for the whole planet, and farmers spend billions on fertilizer to do it faster.

Part 5 · Step by step

How it works, step by step

  1. Bacteria fix N2 from the air (nitrogen fixation)→ammonium enters the soil
  2. Other bacteria turn ammonium into nitrate (nitrification)→plants take up nitrate and build proteins (assimilation)
  3. Decomposers break down wastes and remains (ammonification)→ammonium returns to the soil, and denitrifying bacteria send some nitrate back to the air as N2

Part 6 · Key ideas

Key ideas

  • The atmosphere (78% N2) is the main reservoir, but most living things cannot use N2.
  • Nitrogen fixation (bacteria, lightning, industry) makes ammonium; nitrification makes nitrate; assimilation brings it into living things; ammonification returns ammonium; denitrification returns N2 to the air.
  • Nitrogen is often the limiting nutrient on land, which is why fertilizer raises crop yields.
  • Nitrate dissolves and is not held by soil, so extra nitrate is lost by leaching into streams and wells.

Part 7 · Misconception

A common mistake

The wrong idea: Plants take nitrogen straight from the air, since the air is mostly nitrogen.

What actually happens: Most plants cannot break N2 apart. They take up nitrate and ammonium from the soil, made by nitrogen-fixing and nitrifying bacteria (or added as fertilizer). Legumes get help from bacteria in their root nodules.

Part 8 · Check yourself

Check yourself

Exam-style questions. Anything you miss goes into your review queue.

1. A soil stays soaked with water for weeks, so it has very little oxygen. Which process speeds up there?

  1. Denitrification
  2. Nitrification
  3. Nitrogen fixation by lightning
  4. Assimilation by animals
Show the answer

Denitrifying bacteria work where oxygen is scarce and turn nitrate into N2 gas, which escapes to the air.

  • Correct: Denitrification: Right: low oxygen favors denitrification.
  • Nitrification: Nitrification needs oxygen, so it slows in soaked soil.
  • Nitrogen fixation by lightning: Lightning is not affected by soil oxygen.
  • Assimilation by animals: Animals assimilate nitrogen by eating; soil oxygen does not drive it.

2. Nitrogen is often the limiting nutrient for plant growth on land. What does that mean?

  1. Adding nitrogen raises growth more than adding other nutrients.
  2. Nitrogen in the soil stops plants from growing taller.
  3. Plants contain more nitrogen than any other element.
  4. Too much nitrogen in the soil poisons the roots of plants.
Show the answer

A limiting nutrient is the one in shortest supply compared with need; adding it raises growth until something else becomes limiting.

  • Correct: Adding nitrogen raises growth more than adding other nutrients.: Right: the scarcest needed nutrient limits growth.
  • Nitrogen in the soil stops plants from growing taller.: Nitrogen is needed for growth; it does not stop it.
  • Plants contain more nitrogen than any other element.: Plants contain far more carbon, oxygen and hydrogen than nitrogen.
  • Too much nitrogen in the soil poisons the roots of plants.: Excess nitrogen causes other problems, but that is not what limiting means.

5. What best explains the April peak below the fields?

  1. April fertilizer was leached into the stream.
  2. Denitrification added nitrate to the stream in April.
  3. Fish in the stream released extra nitrate in April.
  4. Nitrate fell from the air in the April rain.
Show the answer

The peak matches the month fertilizer was spread, and it appears below the fields but not above them.

  • Correct: April fertilizer was leached into the stream.: Right: timing and location both point to fertilizer washing off the fields.
  • Denitrification added nitrate to the stream in April.: Denitrification removes nitrate; it does not add it.
  • Fish in the stream released extra nitrate in April.: Fish would raise nitrate at both sites, and not mostly in April.
  • Nitrate fell from the air in the April rain.: Rain falls on both sites, but only the lower one peaks.

7. Which process explains how the soybeans "put nitrogen back in the ground"?

  1. Bacteria in the nodules fix nitrogen.
  2. Soybean leaves absorb nitrate from the air.
  3. Soybean roots carry out denitrification.
  4. The nodules turn other minerals into nitrogen.
Show the answer

Nitrogen-fixing bacteria living in legume root nodules turn N2 into ammonium, which the plant uses; leftover roots and stems release it to the soil.

  • Correct: Bacteria in the nodules fix nitrogen.: Right: nitrogen fixation by bacteria in the nodules.
  • Soybean leaves absorb nitrate from the air.: Leaves do not take nitrate from air.
  • Soybean roots carry out denitrification.: Denitrification removes nitrogen from soil.
  • The nodules turn other minerals into nitrogen.: One element cannot become another.

Part 9 · Summary

Summary

Nitrogen’s largest reservoir is N2 gas in the air, which most organisms cannot use. Bacteria fix it into ammonium, nitrifying bacteria turn ammonium into nitrate, plants assimilate nitrate, decomposers return ammonium by ammonification, and denitrifying bacteria in low-oxygen soil return N2 to the air. Nitrogen often limits plant growth, and excess fertilizer nitrate leaches into water.
