Unit 8 · Topic 8.15

Pathogens and Infectious Diseases

Pathogens are bacteria, viruses, protozoa, fungi and worms that cause infectious diseases.

Practice 1: Concept ExplanationPractice 7: Environmental Solutions

Part 1 · Hook

Why this matters

In the 1990s, West Nile virus was unknown in the Americas. In 1999 it appeared in New York City; within a few years mosquitoes had carried it across the United States. A tiny virus, a common insect and a warm summer were enough. Infectious diseases depend on the environment: on water, crowding, vectors and climate.

Part 4 · Step by step

How it works, step by step

  1. A pathogen (a bacterium, virus, protozoan, fungus or worm) infects a person or animal→it multiplies and causes an infectious disease
  2. Contaminated water, the air, food, contact or a vector carries it on→the disease spreads to new hosts
  3. Poor sanitation, crowding and little health care→make spread easier, so developing countries carry much of the burden
  4. A warming climate→lets vectors such as mosquitoes survive in new places, widening the disease’s range

Part 5 · Key ideas

Key ideas

  • A pathogen causes disease: bacteria, viruses, protozoa, fungi and parasitic worms. An infectious disease is one that can spread to new hosts.
  • A disease vector is a living thing, often an insect, that carries a pathogen between hosts: mosquitoes (malaria, West Nile virus, Zika virus), fleas (plague).
  • Routes to know: waterborne (cholera, dysentery), airborne (tuberculosis; SARS and MERS are respiratory viruses that jumped from animals), vector-borne (malaria, plague, West Nile, Zika).
  • Climate change can spread vectors to new latitudes and elevations; floods can push sewage into drinking water.

Part 6 · Misconception

A common mistake

The wrong idea: The mosquito is the cause of malaria.

What actually happens: Malaria is caused by a protozoan parasite. The mosquito is the vector: it carries the parasite from one person to the next. Controlling the vector breaks the chain.

Part 7 · Check yourself

Check yourself

Exam-style questions. Anything you miss goes into your review queue.

1. What is a pathogen?

  1. A bacterium, virus or other agent that causes disease
  2. An animal, such as a mosquito, that carries disease to people
  3. A chemical pollutant that builds up in body fat
  4. An illness that does not pass from person to person
Show the answer

A pathogen is a disease-causing agent: a bacterium, virus, protozoan, fungus or parasitic worm.

  • Correct: A bacterium, virus or other agent that causes disease: Right: pathogens include bacteria, viruses, protozoa, fungi and parasitic worms.
  • An animal, such as a mosquito, that carries disease to people: An animal that carries a pathogen is a vector, not the pathogen itself.
  • A chemical pollutant that builds up in body fat: That describes a persistent organic pollutant, not a pathogen.
  • An illness that does not pass from person to person: Pathogens cause infectious diseases, which can spread.

2. In the spread of malaria, what is the role of the Anopheles mosquito?

  1. It is the pathogen that infects people’s red blood cells
  2. It is the vector: it carries the parasite between people
  3. It is the host that becomes sick with malaria symptoms
  4. It kills the parasite before it can reach people
Show the answer

Malaria is caused by a protozoan parasite; Anopheles mosquitoes are the vector that carries it between people.

  • It is the pathogen that infects people’s red blood cells: The pathogen is a protozoan parasite (Plasmodium); the mosquito carries it.
  • Correct: It is the vector: it carries the parasite between people: Right: the mosquito picks up the parasite in one bite and passes it on in the next.
  • It is the host that becomes sick with malaria symptoms: People get sick with malaria; the mosquito carries the parasite.
  • It kills the parasite before it can reach people: The mosquito spreads the parasite; it does not kill it.

4. Which two diseases in the table are spread by the same kind of vector?

  2. Plague and cholera, both spread by fleas
  3. Tuberculosis and plague, both spread by drinking water

Malaria, West Nile virus and Zika virus all use mosquitoes as vectors.

5. For which disease in the table would building sewage treatment plants and safe water supplies do the most to cut cases?

  1. Cholera
  2. Plague
  3. West Nile virus
  4. Tuberculosis
Show the answer

Match the solution to the route: for a waterborne disease, keep sewage out of drinking water.

  • Correct: Cholera: Right: cholera spreads through water contaminated with feces, so clean water and sewage treatment stop it.
  • Plague: Plague is spread by fleas on rodents; water treatment does not stop it.

  • Tuberculosis: Tuberculosis spreads through the air; ventilation and treatment matter more.

7. Which action would break the cycle in the diagram at the vector?

  1. Draining standing water where mosquitoes lay their eggs
  2. Treating the town’s drinking water with chlorine
  3. Asking sick people to cover their mouths when they cough
  4. Cooking meat to a higher temperature
Show the answer

Mosquito control (removing standing water, bed nets, repellents) targets the vector step of the cycle.

  • Correct: Draining standing water where mosquitoes lay their eggs: Right: fewer breeding sites means fewer mosquitoes to carry the pathogen.
  • Treating the town’s drinking water with chlorine: Chlorine stops waterborne diseases, but this pathogen is carried by mosquitoes.
  • Asking sick people to cover their mouths when they cough: Covering coughs slows airborne diseases, not mosquito-borne ones.
  • Cooking meat to a higher temperature: Cooking stops foodborne pathogens; this one is spread by bites.

Part 8 · Summary

Summary

Pathogens are bacteria, viruses, protozoa, fungi and worms that cause infectious diseases. They spread through water, air, food, contact and vectors such as mosquitoes and fleas. Poor sanitation and crowding speed the spread, which is why clean water and sewage treatment prevent cholera. A warming climate can widen the range of vectors and the diseases they carry.
