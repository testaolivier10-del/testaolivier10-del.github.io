Geothermal Energy

Geothermal energy is heat from Earth's interior.

Practice 1: Concept ExplanationPractice 7: Environmental Solutions

Part 1 · Hook

Why this matters

In Iceland, sidewalks are kept free of ice by hot water piped from underground, and most homes are heated the same way. In Minnesota, a school stays warm in January using pipes buried in its playing field. Both tap the same source, Earth's internal heat, in very different ways.

Part 5 · Step by step

How it works, step by step

  1. Radioactive decay and leftover heat warm Earth's interior→heat flows outward and is close to the surface near plate boundaries and volcanoes
  2. Wells bring up underground steam and hot water→the steam spins a turbine and generator, day and night, with no fuel burned
  3. Cooled water is pumped back down→the reservoir is refilled so the heat source is not depleted
  4. A few meters down, the ground stays about the same temperature all year→a ground source heat pump can heat a building in winter and cool it in summer almost anywhere

Part 6 · Key ideas

Key ideas

  • Geothermal energy is heat from Earth's interior, from radioactive decay and heat left from Earth's formation. It is renewable when managed.
  • Power plants need very hot water or steam near the surface, found near plate boundaries and hot spots (Iceland, New Zealand, California). They run day and night.
  • A ground source heat pump uses electricity to move heat between a building and shallow ground, which stays at a steady temperature, so it works almost anywhere.
  • Drawbacks: few suitable sites for power plants, high drilling costs, release of hydrogen sulfide and dissolved minerals, and depletion if water is withdrawn faster than it is replaced.

Part 7 · Misconception

A common mistake

The wrong idea: Geothermal energy is the sun's heat stored in the ground, so it stops working on cloudy winter days.

What actually happens: Geothermal power comes from Earth's interior heat, so it runs day and night in any weather. Heat pumps use shallow ground, which stays at a steady temperature year-round.

Part 8 · Check yourself

Check yourself

Exam-style questions. Anything you miss goes into your review queue.

1. Where does geothermal energy come from?

  1. Sunlight that warms the soil
  2. Natural gas burned underground
  3. Friction from flowing water
  4. Heat from Earth's interior
Show the answer

Geothermal heat comes from Earth's interior: roughly half from radioactive decay deep in Earth, the rest heat left over from Earth's formation.

  • Sunlight that warms the soil: Sunlight warms only the top few meters; geothermal plants tap heat from far deeper.
  • Natural gas burned underground: No fuel is burned in a geothermal plant.
  • Friction from flowing water: Water flow does not make meaningful heat.
  • Correct: Heat from Earth's interior: Right: heat from Earth's interior.

2. Why are geothermal power plants built in places like Iceland, New Zealand and California but rarely in the middle of continents?

  1. Geothermal plants have to be built next to an ocean for cooling
  2. The middle of a continent has no heat at any depth beneath it
  3. Hot rock is close to the surface near plate boundaries
  4. Those places get more sunlight to heat the ground than other places
Show the answer

Making electricity needs very hot water or steam at depths that can be drilled. That happens near plate boundaries, hot spots and volcanic areas.

  • Geothermal plants have to be built next to an ocean for cooling: Coastal cooling is not the reason.
  • The middle of a continent has no heat at any depth beneath it: Heat exists everywhere at depth; it is just too deep to reach cheaply in most places.
  • Correct: Hot rock is close to the surface near plate boundaries: Right: plate boundaries bring heat near the surface.
  • Those places get more sunlight to heat the ground than other places: Geothermal heat comes from below, not from sunlight.

3. How does a ground source heat pump heat a house in winter?

  1. It burns natural gas that is stored in the soil
  2. It uses sunlight that is stored in the roof shingles
  3. It makes electricity from hot steam under the house
  4. It moves heat from the ground into the house
Show the answer

A few meters down, the ground stays about 10 to 16 °C all year. Fluid in buried pipes picks up that heat, and the heat pump concentrates it into the house.

  • It burns natural gas that is stored in the soil: No fuel is burned.
  • It uses sunlight that is stored in the roof shingles: Roof sunlight is solar energy, not a heat pump.
  • It makes electricity from hot steam under the house: Heat pumps work with mild ground temperatures, not steam.
  • Correct: It moves heat from the ground into the house: Right: the pump moves heat from the ground into the house.

Part 9 · Summary

Summary

Geothermal energy is heat from Earth's interior. Where hot rock is near the surface, at plate boundaries and hot spots, wells bring up steam to spin turbines day and night, and cooled water is reinjected so the resource lasts. Ground source heat pumps move heat between buildings and the steady-temperature shallow ground almost anywhere. Limits are few good power plant sites, drilling costs, some released gases and the risk of depleting a field.
