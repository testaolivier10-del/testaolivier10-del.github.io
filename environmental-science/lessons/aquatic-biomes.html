Unit 1 · Topic 1.3

Aquatic Biomes

Aquatic biomes differ mainly in salinity, depth, light, temperature and dissolved oxygen.

Practice 1: Concept Explanation

Part 1 · Hook

Why this matters

Follow a single river from a mountain to the sea. It starts cold, fast and full of oxygen, slows and warms as it crosses farmland, mixes with seawater in a muddy, food-rich estuary, and finally reaches an ocean so deep that most of it never sees sunlight. Each stretch is a different aquatic biome, set by salt, light, depth and oxygen.

Part 5 · Step by step

How it works, step by step

  1. Light fades with depth→plants and algae grow only in the sunlit photic zone
  2. Rivers carry nutrients to coasts and estuaries→shallow, sunlit coastal water is very productive
  3. Warm or still water holds less dissolved oxygen→fish and other animals there have less oxygen to use

Part 6 · Key ideas

Key ideas

  • Aquatic biomes are sorted by salinity, depth, light, temperature and dissolved oxygen: the freshwater biome (lakes, rivers, wetlands) and marine biomes (estuary, intertidal zone, coral reef, open ocean).
  • A lake has a littoral zone (shallow edge), limnetic zone (open sunlit water), profundal zone (deep, dark) and benthic zone (bottom).
  • Only the photic zone has enough light for photosynthesis; below it is the aphotic zone.
  • Estuaries, salt marshes and mangrove forests grow more life per square meter than almost anywhere on Earth: nutrients plus light plus shelter.

Part 7 · Misconception

A common mistake

The wrong idea: The open ocean grows the most life per square meter because it is so large.

What actually happens: The open ocean grows a lot in total because it is huge, but each square meter grows little: nutrients are scarce in the sunlit surface. Coral reefs, estuaries and wetlands grow far more per square meter.

Part 8 · Check yourself

Check yourself

Exam-style questions. Anything you miss goes into your review queue.

1. Coral reefs grow in clear, warm, shallow water that is low in nutrients. Why does coral need clear, shallow water?

  1. The algae living inside the coral need sunlight for photosynthesis.
  2. Coral needs to be near the surface to breathe air.
  3. Waves carry more food into shallow water.
  4. Cold, deep water has too much oxygen for coral.
Show the answer

Corals and the algae inside them are a mutualism: the algae make food from light and share it. Murky or deep water blocks the light.

  • Correct: The algae living inside the coral need sunlight for photosynthesis.: Right: the coral’s partner algae need light.
  • Coral needs to be near the surface to breathe air.: Coral animals take oxygen from water; they do not breathe air.
  • Waves carry more food into shallow water.: Coral gets most of its food from its algae, not from waves.
  • Cold, deep water has too much oxygen for coral.: Cold water holds more oxygen, but that is not why coral needs shallow water.

6. Algae need at least about 1% of surface light to make more food by photosynthesis than they use. Where does the photic zone of this lake end?

  1. At about 4 m
  2. At about 8 m
  3. At about 15 m
  4. At the lake surface
Show the answer

Light falls to 1.5% at 14 m and 0.8% at 16 m, so the 1% level is near 15 m. Below it is the aphotic zone.

  • At about 4 m: At 4 m, 30% of the light is left, well above 1%.
  • At about 8 m: At 8 m, 9% is left, still above 1%.
  • Correct: At about 15 m: Right: between 14 m (1.5%) and 16 m (0.8%).
  • At the lake surface: The surface has all of the light.

7. Rain after a storm washes mud into the lake and makes the water cloudy. How would the graph change?

  1. Light would fall off faster, so the photic zone would be shallower.
  2. Light would fall off more slowly, so the photic zone would be deeper.
  3. The curve would not change, because mud does not block light.
  4. The surface value would rise above 100%.
Show the answer

Particles absorb and scatter light, so less reaches each depth. The 1% level moves up, and fewer algae and rooted plants can grow deep down.

  • Correct: Light would fall off faster, so the photic zone would be shallower.: Right: cloudy water blocks light, so the curve drops faster.
  • Light would fall off more slowly, so the photic zone would be deeper.: Mud blocks light; it does not let more through.
  • The curve would not change, because mud does not block light.: Suspended particles do block light.
  • The surface value would rise above 100%.: The surface value is 100% by definition.

Part 9 · Summary

Summary

Aquatic biomes differ mainly in salinity, depth, light, temperature and dissolved oxygen. Freshwater biomes include lakes (with littoral, limnetic, profundal and benthic zones), rivers and wetlands; marine biomes include estuaries, the intertidal zone, coral reefs and the open ocean. Plants and algae need the photic zone, and colder, moving water holds more oxygen.
