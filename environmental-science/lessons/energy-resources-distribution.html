Distribution of Natural Energy Resources

Energy resources are spread unevenly because they depend on geology and climate.

Practice 5: Data AnalysisPractice 1: Concept Explanation

Part 1 · Hook

Why this matters

One country sits on vast oil fields, its neighbor has none. Iceland heats its homes with water from hot rock, while a desert nation covers its land with sun-powered panels. Energy resources are not spread evenly: where they are depends on ancient geology and today's climate, and that shapes what every country burns, buys and builds.

Part 5 · Step by step

How it works, step by step

  1. Ancient swamps and seas buried organic matter in some basins→coal, oil and natural gas are concentrated in those sedimentary rocks
  2. Plate boundaries bring hot rock near the surface→heat from underground is practical in places like Iceland, but not everywhere
  3. Latitude, clouds and land shape decide sunlight, wind and falling water→renewable resources also vary from place to place
  4. A country lacks the resources it uses→it must import energy, so its supply and prices depend on other countries

Part 6 · Key ideas

Key ideas

  • Fossil fuels lie where ancient swamps and seas buried organic matter: a few regions hold most of the world's oil and gas, and a few countries most of the coal.
  • A proven reserve is the part of a deposit that is known and can be recovered profitably with today's technology; it rises with higher prices or new techniques, not only new discoveries.
  • Renewables depend on place too: sunlight is strongest in dry, clear, lower-latitude regions; wind on coasts, plains and offshore; falling water on steep rivers with steady flow; Earth's internal heat at plate boundaries.
  • Years a reserve lasts = reserve ÷ production per year, if production stays the same.

Part 7 · Misconception

A common mistake

The wrong idea: A country's proven reserves are all the oil it has, so the number can only go down.

What actually happens: Proven reserves count only the oil that is known and worth recovering now. They can rise when prices go up or technology improves, even with no new discovery.

Part 8 · Check yourself

Check yourself

Exam-style questions. Anything you miss goes into your review queue.

1. What is a proven reserve of oil?

  1. Oil stored in large tanks by a government for emergencies
  2. Oil that has already been pumped, refined and sold to buyers
  3. Known oil that can be recovered at a profit right now
  4. The total amount of oil that exists anywhere in Earth's crust
Show the answer

Proven reserves are the part of a deposit that has been located and can be extracted profitably now. The number changes as prices and technology change.

  • Oil stored in large tanks by a government for emergencies: Emergency stocks are stored oil, not reserves in the ground.
  • Oil that has already been pumped, refined and sold to buyers: Oil already produced is no longer a reserve.
  • Correct: Known oil that can be recovered at a profit right now: Right: known, recoverable and economic now.
  • The total amount of oil that exists anywhere in Earth's crust: Much oil is not found yet or is too costly to recover; that is not counted as proven.

2. Which place is best suited for large solar farms?

  1. A polar region with long, dark winters and low sun angles in summer
  2. A dry region near 25 degrees latitude with clear skies
  3. A cloudy coastal region near 55 degrees latitude with frequent fog
  4. A rainy tropical forest near the equator with heavy cloud cover most days
Show the answer

Solar output depends on insolation. Dry subtropical regions get strong, direct sunlight and few clouds.

  • A polar region with long, dark winters and low sun angles in summer: Low sun angles and long dark winters give little solar energy.
  • Correct: A dry region near 25 degrees latitude with clear skies: Right: high insolation and clear skies.
  • A cloudy coastal region near 55 degrees latitude with frequent fog: Clouds and lower sun angles at high latitude cut output.
  • A rainy tropical forest near the equator with heavy cloud cover most days: Heavy cloud cover in rainforests blocks much of the sunlight.

Part 9 · Summary

Summary

Energy resources are spread unevenly because they depend on geology and climate. Fossil fuels sit in sedimentary basins where ancient organisms were buried; Earth's internal heat is near the surface at plate boundaries; sunlight, wind and falling water vary with latitude, clouds and terrain. A country without the resources it uses must import energy. Proven reserves count what can be recovered profitably now, and reserve ÷ yearly production estimates how long they last.
