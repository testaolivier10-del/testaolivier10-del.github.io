Energy Conservation

Energy conservation means using less energy; energy efficiency means using less for the same service.

Practice 6: Mathematical RoutinesPractice 7: Environmental Solutions

Part 1 · Hook

Why this matters

The cheapest, cleanest unit of energy is the one you never use. Swapping twenty light bulbs, sealing a drafty attic or riding the bus can cut a household's energy bills and carbon dioxide more than you might expect, and the exam asks you to prove it with numbers: kWh, dollars and kilograms.

Part 5 · Step by step

How it works, step by step

  1. A family replaces bulbs, adds insulation or lowers the thermostat→the home uses fewer kWh of electricity and less fuel for the same comfort and light
  2. Fewer kWh are needed→power plants burn less fuel to meet demand
  3. Less fuel is burned→less carbon dioxide and other pollution is released, and the family pays less
  4. Governments set efficiency standards and invest in public transportation→savings are multiplied across millions of homes and vehicles

Part 6 · Key ideas

Key ideas

  • Energy conservation is using less energy (turning things off, lowering the thermostat, driving less). Energy efficiency is getting the same service with less energy (LEDs, insulation, efficient appliances).
  • Energy (kWh) = power (kW) × time (h). Set up chains so units cancel: W × h/day × day/yr × 1 kWh/1,000 Wh.
  • Then convert: cost = kWh × price per kWh; carbon dioxide = kWh × kg per kWh; payback = extra cost ÷ yearly savings.
  • At home: insulation, programmable thermostats, LEDs, efficient appliances, shade trees and windbreaks. Large scale: fuel economy standards, hybrid vehicles and electric vehicles, public transportation, green building design.

Part 7 · Misconception

A common mistake

The wrong idea: To find the savings from switching bulbs, multiply the old bulb's watts by the hours.

What actually happens: Savings come from the difference: (old watts - new watts) × hours, then convert Wh to kWh. Using only the old bulb's watts gives what it used, not what you saved.

1. What is the difference between energy conservation and energy efficiency?

  1. Conservation means making more energy; efficiency means storing it
  2. They mean the same thing in environmental science and on the exam
  3. Conservation applies to homes; efficiency applies to power plants
  4. Conservation is using less; efficiency is the same service for less
Show the answer

Turning off lights you are not using is conservation. Replacing a bulb with an LED that gives the same light with less electricity is efficiency. Both cut energy use.

  • Conservation means making more energy; efficiency means storing it: Neither term means making or storing energy.
  • They mean the same thing in environmental science and on the exam: They are related but different ideas.
  • Conservation applies to homes; efficiency applies to power plants: Efficiency applies to any device, from bulbs to cars to power plants.
  • Correct: Conservation is using less; efficiency is the same service for less: Right: using less versus doing the same with less.

6. A student wants the yearly kWh used by a 1,500 W space heater run 3 hours a day. Which calculation is set up correctly?

  1. 1,500 W × 3 h/day × 365 day/yr × 1 kWh/1,000 Wh
  2. 1,500 W × 3 h/day ÷ 365 day/yr
  3. 1,500 W ÷ 3 h/day × 365 day/yr × 1,000
  4. 1,500 W × 365 day/yr ÷ 1,000
Show the answer

Energy = power × time. Multiply watts by hours per day and days per year, then convert Wh to kWh: 1,500×3×365÷1,0001{,}500 \times 3 \times 365 \div 1{,}000=1,642.5= 1{,}642.5 kWh.

  • Correct: 1,500 W × 3 h/day × 365 day/yr × 1 kWh/1,000 Wh: Right: every unit cancels except kWh.
  • 1,500 W × 3 h/day ÷ 365 day/yr: Dividing by days per year does not give a yearly total.
  • 1,500 W ÷ 3 h/day × 365 day/yr × 1,000: Dividing watts by hours does not give energy, and the 1,000 should divide.
  • 1,500 W × 365 day/yr ÷ 1,000: This leaves out the 3 hours a day.

Part 9 · Summary

Summary

Energy conservation means using less energy; energy efficiency means using less for the same service. Savings are calculated by chains whose units cancel: the change in watts times hours, converted to kWh, then times the price for dollars and times the emission factor for carbon dioxide. At home, insulation, thermostats, LEDs and efficient appliances save energy; at large scale, fuel economy standards, hybrid and electric vehicles, public transportation and green building design do.
