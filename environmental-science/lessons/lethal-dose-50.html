Unit 8 · Topic 8.12

Lethal Dose 50% (LD50)

LD50 is the dose, in milligrams per kilogram of body mass, that kills half of a test population; it is read off a graph of percent killed against dose at the 50% line.

Practice 5: Data AnalysisPractice 6: Mathematical Routines

Part 1 · Hook

Why this matters

A farm store sells two rat poisons in nearly identical bags. One label says DANGER and the other says CAUTION. The difference comes from one number measured in the lab: how many milligrams of each product, per kilogram of animal, it takes to kill half of a test group. That number is the LD50, and a smaller one means a more dangerous chemical.

Part 4 · Step by step

How it works, step by step

  1. Researchers give groups of test animals larger and larger doses (mg per kg of body mass)→a larger percent of each group dies
  2. They plot percent killed against dose and read across from 50%→the dose under that point is the LD50
  3. A substance with a lower LD50 kills half the animals at a smaller dose→it is more toxic and gets a stronger warning label
  4. Multiplying the LD50 by a body mass, with kilograms cancelling→gives the whole-body dose in milligrams for an animal of that size

Part 5 · Key ideas

Key ideas

  • Toxicity is how harmful a substance is. The dose is how much enters the body, usually in milligrams per kilogram of body mass (mg/kg).
  • LD50 is the dose that kills 50% of a test population. A lower LD50 means a more toxic substance.
  • Whole-body dose: LD50×body mass\text{LD}_{50} \times \text{body mass}, for example 40mgkg×0.25kg40\,\frac{\text{mg}}{\text{kg}} \times 0.25\,\text{kg}=10mg= 10\,\text{mg}. Write the units so kilograms visibly cancel.
  • LD50s come from animal tests, so they only estimate the effect on people, and they say nothing about harm that does not kill.

Part 6 · Misconception

A common mistake

The wrong idea: A chemical with an LD50 of 5,000 mg/kg is more toxic than one with an LD50 of 5 mg/kg, because 5,000 is bigger.

What actually happens: It is the reverse. The LD50 is the dose needed to kill half the animals, so the chemical that needs only 5 mg/kg is 1,000 times as toxic.

Part 7 · Check yourself

Check yourself

Exam-style questions. Anything you miss goes into your review queue.

1. In toxicology, what does LD50 mean?

  1. The dose that kills half of a test population
  2. The dose at which half the test animals first show any effect
  3. The dose that kills 50 animals out of a test group
  4. The largest dose that causes no deaths in the test
Show the answer

LD50 is the lethal dose for 50% of a test population, usually in milligrams of substance per kilogram of body mass.

  • Correct: The dose that kills half of a test population: Right: LD stands for lethal dose, and 50 for the 50% of test animals it kills.
  • The dose at which half the test animals first show any effect: A dose that causes any effect in half the animals is a different measure; LD50 counts deaths only.
  • The dose that kills 50 animals out of a test group: The 50 is a percent of the group, not a count of animals, so it works for a group of any size.
  • The largest dose that causes no deaths in the test: That would be a safe-looking dose with zero deaths, not the dose that kills half the animals.

2. Pesticide P has an LD50 of 20 mg/kg in rats. Pesticide Q has an LD50 of 2,000 mg/kg in rats. Which statement is correct?

  1. Q is more toxic, because its LD50 is the larger number
  2. P is more toxic: less of it per kilogram kills half the rats
  3. P and Q are equally toxic, since both were tested on rats
  4. Q is safe for people, since its LD50 is above 1,000 mg/kg
Show the answer

LD50 and toxicity run in opposite directions: the lower the LD50, the more toxic the substance. P is 2,00020=100\frac{2{,}000}{20} = 100 times as toxic as Q per kilogram.

  • Q is more toxic, because its LD50 is the larger number: A larger LD50 means more of the substance is needed to kill half the rats, so Q is less toxic.
  • Correct: P is more toxic: less of it per kilogram kills half the rats: Right: a lower LD50 means less of the substance is needed to kill, so it is more toxic.
  • P and Q are equally toxic, since both were tested on rats: Testing in the same species makes them comparable, but the LD50s differ a hundredfold.
  • Q is safe for people, since its LD50 is above 1,000 mg/kg: A high LD50 means low toxicity in rats, not that a substance is safe; large doses can still kill.

3. The LD50 of a pesticide in rats is 60 mg/kg. What dose of pesticide would be expected to kill half of a group of rats that each have a mass of 0.40 kg? Give the dose for one rat in milligrams.

Type a number, then its unit (mg). Scale words (2.5 million) and powers of ten (2.5 × 10^6) are read.

Show the answer

The setup multiplies the dose per kilogram by the body mass, so kilograms cancel: 60mgkg×0.40kg60\,\frac{\text{mg}}{\text{kg}} \times 0.40\,\text{kg}=24mg= 24\,\text{mg}.

  • Answer: 24 mg

4. A cleaning chemical has an LD50 of 25 mg/kg, measured in dogs. Write the setup, with units, for the dose expected to kill half of a group of 12 kg dogs, then give the answer in milligrams.

Type the setup as on the exam, with brackets and units, then the answer.

Show the answer

Multiply the LD50 by the body mass so kilograms cancel: 25mgkg×12kg=300mg25\,\frac{\text{mg}}{\text{kg}} \times 12\,\text{kg} = 300\,\text{mg}.

  • Setup: 25mgkg⋅12kg\frac{25\,\text{mg}}{\text{kg}} \cdot 12\,\text{kg}; answer 300 mg

6. What percent of the mice died at a dose of 600 mg/kg?

  1. 84%
  2. 68%
  3. 96%
  4. 50%
Show the answer

Read straight up from 600 mg/kg to the line, then across: 84% of the mice died.

  • Correct: 84%: Right: the point at 600 mg/kg sits at 84% of mice killed.
  • 68%: 68% is the value at 500 mg/kg. Read straight up from 600 mg/kg.
  • 96%: 96% is the value at 800 mg/kg, the highest dose tested.
  • 50%: 50% is the response at the LD50 (400 mg/kg), not at 600 mg/kg.

7. A mouse has a mass of 0.025 kg. Using the LD50 you read from the graph, what dose of the solvent, in milligrams, would be expected to kill half of a group of such mice?

Type a number, then its unit (mg). Scale words (2.5 million) and powers of ten (2.5 × 10^6) are read.

Show the answer

400mgkg×0.025kg400\,\frac{\text{mg}}{\text{kg}} \times 0.025\,\text{kg}=10mg= 10\,\text{mg}.

  • Answer: 10 mg

Part 8 · Summary

Summary

LD50 is the dose, in milligrams per kilogram of body mass, that kills half of a test population; it is read off a graph of percent killed against dose at the 50% line. The lower the LD50, the more toxic the substance. Multiply the LD50 by a body mass to get a whole-body dose, writing the units so kilograms cancel, and convert milligrams to grams when asked.
