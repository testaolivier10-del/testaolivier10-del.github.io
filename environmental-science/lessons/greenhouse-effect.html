The Greenhouse Effect

Sunlight warms the surface, which gives off infrared; greenhouse gases (water vapor, CO₂, methane, nitrous oxide, CFCs) absorb it and send part back down, keeping Earth about 33 °C warmer.

Practice 2: Visual RepresentationsPractice 1: Concept Explanation

Part 1 · Hook

Why this matters

The Moon is about as far from the Sun as Earth, yet its surface swings from about 120 °C in sunlight to below −170 °C at night. Earth’s average stays near 15 °C. The difference is a blanket of gases a few kilometers thick that lets sunlight in but slows heat on its way out.

Part 5 · Step by step

How it works, step by step

  1. Sunlight, mostly visible light, passes through the air→it warms Earth’s surface
  2. The warm surface gives off infrared radiation→greenhouse gases in the troposphere absorb much of it
  3. The gases re-emit that energy in all directions, part of it back down→the lower atmosphere and surface stay warmer than they would be
  4. Adding more greenhouse gases→traps more heat, strengthening the effect and raising temperatures

Part 6 · Key ideas

Key ideas

  • The greenhouse effect: greenhouse gases absorb infrared radiation given off by the surface and re-emit part of it downward, warming the lower atmosphere.
  • Greenhouse gases: water vapor (most abundant), carbon dioxide, methane, nitrous oxide (N₂O) and CFCs and HFCs. SO₂ and particulates are not greenhouse gases.
  • Global warming potential (GWP) compares the heat trapped by a gas with the same mass of CO₂ over 100 years: CO₂ 1; methane roughly 25 to 30 (the IPCC value depends on the report); N₂O roughly 265 to 300; CFCs thousands. A calculation question gives you the GWP to use.
  • The natural greenhouse effect keeps Earth about 33 °C warmer than it would be. Ozone depletion is a separate problem: UV and the stratosphere.

Part 7 · Misconception

A common mistake

The wrong idea: The ozone hole lets heat in, and that is the cause of global warming.

What actually happens: The ozone hole lets in more UV, which raises skin cancer. Warming comes from greenhouse gases trapping outgoing infrared in the lower atmosphere. CFCs are the one link: they do both.

Part 8 · Check yourself

Check yourself

Exam-style questions. Anything you miss goes into your review queue.

1. Which statement describes the greenhouse effect?

  1. Low-level gases absorb infrared from the surface
  2. Ozone in the stratosphere absorbs ultraviolet light from the Sun
  3. Chlorine from CFCs destroys ozone and lets more UV reach the ground
  4. Clouds reflect sunlight back to space and cool the planet
Show the answer

The greenhouse effect: sunlight warms the surface, the surface gives off infrared, and greenhouse gases absorb and re-emit it, warming the lower atmosphere.

  • Correct: Low-level gases absorb infrared from the surface: Right: greenhouse gases absorb outgoing infrared and send part of it back down.
  • Ozone in the stratosphere absorbs ultraviolet light from the Sun: That is the ozone layer’s job, a different process in a different layer.
  • Chlorine from CFCs destroys ozone and lets more UV reach the ground: That is ozone depletion, not the greenhouse effect.
  • Clouds reflect sunlight back to space and cool the planet: Reflecting sunlight is part of albedo; the greenhouse effect traps outgoing infrared.

2. Which gas is NOT a greenhouse gas?

  1. Methane (CH₄)
  2. Nitrous oxide (N₂O)
  3. Sulfur dioxide (SO₂)
  4. Water vapor (H₂O)
Show the answer

The main greenhouse gases are water vapor, carbon dioxide, methane, nitrous oxide and CFCs. SO₂ and particulates are not greenhouse gases.

  • Methane (CH₄): Methane absorbs infrared strongly; it is a greenhouse gas.
  • Nitrous oxide (N₂O): Nitrous oxide is a long-lived greenhouse gas.
  • Correct: Sulfur dioxide (SO₂): Right: sulfur dioxide causes acid rain and forms particles that reflect sunlight; it is not a greenhouse gas.
  • Water vapor (H₂O): Water vapor is the most abundant greenhouse gas.

4. According to the figure, which statement correctly contrasts the two processes?

  1. Both processes take place in the stratosphere
  2. Gases warm low air; ozone blocks UV high up
  3. Both processes involve ultraviolet light from the Sun
  4. Ozone traps infrared, and greenhouse gases absorb UV
Show the answer

Two separate stories: infrared and the troposphere for warming, UV and the stratosphere for the ozone layer.

  • Both processes take place in the stratosphere: The greenhouse effect warms the troposphere, the lowest layer.
  • Correct: Gases warm low air; ozone blocks UV high up: Right: different gases, radiation and layers.
  • Both processes involve ultraviolet light from the Sun: The greenhouse effect involves outgoing infrared.
  • Ozone traps infrared, and greenhouse gases absorb UV: This swaps the two: ozone absorbs UV; greenhouse gases absorb infrared.

5. A student says, "The ozone hole lets more heat in, and that is what causes global warming." Using the figure, why is this wrong?

  1. The ozone hole makes Earth colder, which cancels global warming
  2. Global warming is caused by UV, so the student is right
  3. The hole admits UV; warming is trapped infrared
  4. The ozone hole is over the equator, where there is no warming
Show the answer

Ozone depletion raises UV at the surface; the greenhouse effect traps infrared. Mixing them up is a common exam error.

  • The ozone hole makes Earth colder, which cancels global warming: Ozone loss is not a main cause of cooling or warming.
  • Global warming is caused by UV, so the student is right: Warming is from trapped infrared, not UV.
  • Correct: The hole admits UV; warming is trapped infrared: Right: they involve different radiation and different gases.
  • The ozone hole is over the equator, where there is no warming: The ozone hole is over Antarctica, and warming is global.

Part 9 · Summary

Summary

Sunlight warms the surface, which gives off infrared; greenhouse gases (water vapor, CO₂, methane, nitrous oxide, CFCs) absorb it and send part back down, keeping Earth about 33 °C warmer. More gas traps more heat. Global warming potential compares gases with CO₂: multiply a mass by its GWP for CO₂ equivalent. The greenhouse effect is not ozone depletion.
