Global Climate Change

More greenhouse gases warm Earth.

Practice 5: Data AnalysisPractice 7: Environmental Solutions

Part 1 · Hook

Why this matters

Glacier National Park in Montana had about 150 glaciers in 1850. Today only a few dozen remain large enough to count. Far to the north, Arctic sea ice in late summer now covers much less area than it did in 1980. Both are signs of the same change, but only one of them is raising the oceans.

Part 5 · Step by step

How it works, step by step

  1. More greenhouse gases trap more heat→global average temperature rises (about 1.1 °C since the late 1800s)
  2. Warming melts glaciers and the ice on Greenland and Antarctica→water flows into the ocean and sea level rises
  3. Warming melts sea ice and thaws permafrost→albedo falls and stored carbon is released: positive feedback loops amplify warming
  4. A warmer climate shifts heat, rainfall and storms→heat waves, droughts, heavy rain and species range shifts become more common

Part 6 · Key ideas

Key ideas

  • Climate change: long-term change in Earth’s climate, now mainly warming caused by added greenhouse gases.
  • Sea level rise comes from melting land ice (glaciers, ice sheets) and from warming water taking up more space. Melting sea ice does not raise sea level, because it already floats.
  • A positive feedback loop amplifies a change: melting ice lowers albedo; thawing permafrost releases methane and CO₂.
  • Effects: heat waves, shifts in rainfall, stronger storms, coastal flooding, coral bleaching, species moving toward the poles.

Part 7 · Misconception

A common mistake

The wrong idea: Melting Arctic sea ice is the main reason sea level is rising.

What actually happens: Sea ice floats, so it already displaces its own weight of water. Melting it barely changes sea level. Sea level rises from melting land ice and from ocean water expanding as it warms. Sea ice matters because losing it lowers albedo.

Part 8 · Check yourself

Check yourself

Exam-style questions. Anything you miss goes into your review queue.

1. Which change would raise global sea level?

  1. Melting of land ice on Greenland
  2. Melting of floating Arctic sea ice
  3. More snowfall staying on Antarctica
  4. Thicker sea ice around Antarctica
Show the answer

Land ice (glaciers and the ice on Greenland and Antarctica) adds water to the ocean when it melts; floating sea ice does not.

  • Correct: Melting of land ice on Greenland: Right: water from land ice flows into the ocean and adds to its volume.
  • Melting of floating Arctic sea ice: Sea ice already floats and displaces its own weight of water, so melting it barely changes sea level.
  • More snowfall staying on Antarctica: Snow piling up on land holds water out of the ocean, so it lowers sea level slightly.
  • Thicker sea ice around Antarctica: Floating ice does not change sea level as it grows or shrinks.

2. An ice cube floats in a full glass of water. When it melts, what happens to the water level?

  1. It rises and overflows
  2. It stays about the same
  3. It falls below the rim
  4. It rises by the ice’s volume
Show the answer

This is why melting sea ice does not raise sea level, while melting land ice does.

  • It rises and overflows: The ice already pushed aside its weight of water; melting fills that same space.
  • Correct: It stays about the same: Right: floating ice displaces its own weight of water, which is what it adds when it melts.
  • It falls below the rim: The meltwater exactly fills the space the floating ice displaced.
  • It rises by the ice’s volume: Only the underwater part of the ice displaced water, and that is what the meltwater fills.

  2. It fell by about 1 °C
  3. It stayed about the same
  4. It rose, then fell back to 1960 levels
Show the answer

Read the endpoints and give the change with its unit: about 1.0 °C warmer.

  • Correct: It rose by about 1 °C: Right: from −0.03 to 1.01 °C, a rise of about 1.04 °C.
  • It fell by about 1 °C: The values increase from 1960 to 2020.
  • It stayed about the same: A change from −0.03 to 1.01 °C is large for a global average.
  • It rose, then fell back to 1960 levels: The last value, in 2020, is the highest.

5. What does a value of −0.27 °C in 1920 mean?

  1. The average temperature was below freezing
  2. Temperatures fell by 0.27 °C that year
  3. That year was 0.27 °C cooler than the baseline
  4. The data for 1920 are missing
Show the answer

An anomaly is a difference from a baseline average; negative means cooler than that average.

  • The average temperature was below freezing: The value is a difference from an average, not the temperature itself.
  • Temperatures fell by 0.27 °C that year: It compares the year with a fixed baseline, not with the year before.
  • Correct: That year was 0.27 °C cooler than the baseline: Right: the values are differences from a 1951 to 1980 average.
  • The data for 1920 are missing: A negative number is a measured difference, not missing data.

6. Which statement about the data is supported?

  1. Global temperature rose in each 20-year step since 1900
  2. Temperature will reach 2 °C above the baseline by 2030
  3. The data show that the Sun caused the warming
  4. Warming since 1980 is faster than in earlier decades
Show the answer

Use the data for claims they support: a faster rise after 1980. Do not claim a cause the table does not show.

  • Temperature will reach 2 °C above the baseline by 2030: The data cannot support that exact prediction.
  • The data show that the Sun caused the warming: The table shows temperature, not its cause.
  • Correct: Warming since 1980 is faster than in earlier decades: Right: 0.75 °C in 40 years after 1980, compared with smaller and uneven changes before.

7. Why do low-lying island nations such as the Maldives face especially high risk from climate change?

  1. They release the most greenhouse gases
  2. Their sea ice is melting fastest
  3. Most of their land is close to sea level
  4. They lie under the ozone hole
Show the answer

Much of the Maldives is less than 1 meter above sea level, so rising seas and storm surges threaten homes and fresh water.

  • They release the most greenhouse gases: Small island nations release very little.
  • Their sea ice is melting fastest: Tropical islands do not have sea ice.
  • Correct: Most of their land is close to sea level: Right: a small rise in sea level floods a large share of their land.
  • They lie under the ozone hole: The ozone hole is over Antarctica.

Part 9 · Summary

Summary

More greenhouse gases warm Earth. Warming melts land ice, which raises sea level, and melts sea ice, which does not raise sea level but lowers albedo. Positive feedback loops such as melting ice and thawing permafrost amplify warming. Effects include heat waves, droughts, heavy rain, coastal flooding and species shifting toward the poles.
