Unit 5 · Topic 5.9

Impacts of Mining

Mining extracts ore and coal by surface methods (strip, open-pit, mountaintop removal), which remove overburden over large areas, or by subsurface methods, which are more dangerous for miners.

Practice 7: Environmental Solutions

Part 1 · Hook

Why this matters

Every smartphone holds small amounts of copper, gold, cobalt and more than a dozen other metals, all dug out of the ground. To get about one kilogram of copper from a typical modern ore, a mine moves more than a hundred kilograms of rock and leaves most of it as waste piled nearby. What happens to that waste, and to the hole, decides how a mine affects the land and water for decades.

Part 5 · Step by step

How it works, step by step

  1. Mining exposes sulfide minerals in rock and waste piles to air and rain→the minerals react to form sulfuric acid
  2. Acid water flows out of the mine and waste piles→the pH of nearby streams falls
  3. Acidic water dissolves metals such as lead, mercury and arsenic from the rock→toxic metals enter streams and groundwater
  4. Low pH and metals reach aquatic life→fish and insects die and the stream can stay polluted for decades

Part 6 · Key ideas

Key ideas

  • Ore is rock with enough valuable mineral to mine at a profit. As rich ores run out, lower-grade ore means more rock moved and more waste per kilogram of metal.
  • Surface mining (strip, open-pit, mountaintop removal) removes the overburden to reach shallow ore: cheaper and safer for miners, but it destroys habitat and leaves huge waste piles.
  • Subsurface mining uses shafts and tunnels for deep ore: less land disturbed, but more dangerous for miners and the ground above can sink.
  • Mine waste causes acid mine drainage, metal pollution, sediment and dust. Land reclamation regrades the land, replaces topsoil and replants it.

Part 7 · Misconception

A common mistake

The wrong idea: Once a mine closes, its pollution stops.

What actually happens: Waste rock and tailings keep reacting with air and rain for decades or centuries. Acid mine drainage from mines closed long ago still pollutes thousands of kilometers of streams.

Part 8 · Check yourself

Check yourself

Exam-style questions. Anything you miss goes into your review queue.

1. What is overburden?

  1. The soil and rock removed from on top of a deposit
  2. The valuable mineral that is left after the ore is processed
  3. The water that is pumped out of a deep mine shaft
  4. The machine that lifts ore to the top of a mine shaft
Show the answer

Overburden lies above the ore and is stripped away in surface mining.

  • Correct: The soil and rock removed from on top of a deposit: Right: the material above the ore.
  • The valuable mineral that is left after the ore is processed: The waste left after processing is tailings, not the mineral itself.
  • The water that is pumped out of a deep mine shaft: Pumped water is mine water, not overburden.
  • The machine that lifts ore to the top of a mine shaft: The structure over a shaft is a headframe.

2. How does acid mine drainage form?

  1. Sulfide minerals in exposed rock react with air and water
  2. Acidic rainwater falls on the mine and collects in the pit as acid
  3. Miners pour acid into the mine to dissolve the ore and leave it there
  4. Coal burned at the mine releases acid that drips into nearby streams
Show the answer

Mining exposes sulfide minerals such as pyrite to oxygen and water, which form sulfuric acid.

  • Correct: Sulfide minerals in exposed rock react with air and water: Right: sulfides plus air and water make sulfuric acid.
  • Acidic rainwater falls on the mine and collects in the pit as acid: Rain is only slightly acidic; acid mine drainage comes from the exposed rock itself.
  • Miners pour acid into the mine to dissolve the ore and leave it there: Acid drainage forms naturally from the rock; it is not poured in.
  • Coal burned at the mine releases acid that drips into nearby streams: Coal is not burned at the mine to make drainage.

7. In the United States, a federal law requires coal mine operators to post money before mining that is used to restore the land if they do not. Why require the money in advance?

  1. So reclamation is paid for if the company goes out of business
  2. So the mine can dig deeper without a permit later on
  3. So the price of coal falls for people who use electricity
  4. So the miners are paid if the mine closes early
Show the answer

A bond in advance means taxpayers are not left with the cost of an abandoned mine.

  • Correct: So reclamation is paid for if the company goes out of business: Right: the money is there even if the company is gone.
  • So the mine can dig deeper without a permit later on: The money is for reclamation, not for permits to dig deeper.
  • So the price of coal falls for people who use electricity: The aim is to restore land, not change coal prices.
  • So the miners are paid if the mine closes early: The money is for land, not wages.

Part 9 · Summary

Summary

Mining extracts ore and coal by surface methods (strip, open-pit, mountaintop removal), which remove overburden over large areas, or by subsurface methods, which are more dangerous for miners. Waste rock and tailings release acid mine drainage and toxic metals; mining also causes erosion, sediment and dust, and clears habitat. Land reclamation restores mined land, and lower-grade ores mean more waste per kilogram of metal.
