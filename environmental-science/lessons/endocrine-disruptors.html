Unit 8 · Topic 8.3

Endocrine Disruptors

Endocrine disruptors are chemicals that interfere with hormones by mimicking them, blocking their receptors or changing how much is made.

Practice 1: Concept ExplanationPractice 5: Data Analysis

Part 1 · Hook

Why this matters

Biologists sampling a river below a city's wastewater pipe found something strange: more than half of the male bass had egg cells growing in their testes. Upstream, almost none did. The water looked clean. The culprit was a set of chemicals at a few parts per trillion that the fish's bodies mistook for their own hormones.

Part 4 · Step by step

How it works, step by step

  1. Hormones from glands travel in the blood and bind to receptors on cells→the cells switch on growth, development or reproduction
  2. An endocrine disruptor has a shape similar to a hormone→it binds the receptor and sends a false signal, or blocks the real hormone
  3. Exposure happens during development, even at very low doses→reproductive organs, growth and behavior develop abnormally
  4. Many such chemicals pass through city wastewater plants and persist→fish, frogs and other wildlife downstream show intersex and reproductive effects

Part 5 · Key ideas

Key ideas

  • An endocrine disruptor interferes with hormones: it mimics a hormone, blocks its receptor, or changes how much is made.
  • Examples: the herbicide atrazine, BPA and phthalates in some plastics, and synthetic estrogen from birth control pills that passes into wastewater.
  • Effects: intersex fish, frogs with abnormal sex organs, thinner eggshells, and in people reproductive, developmental and some cancer risks. Effects can appear at very low doses.
  • Reduce them by limiting their use, extra cleaning steps at wastewater plants, proper disposal of medicines, and safer substitutes.

Part 6 · Misconception

A common mistake

The wrong idea: A chemical at a few parts per billion is too dilute to matter.

What actually happens: Hormones themselves work at tiny concentrations, so chemicals that mimic them can change development at very low doses.

Part 7 · Check yourself

Check yourself

Exam-style questions. Anything you miss goes into your review queue.

1. What is an endocrine disruptor?

  1. A germ that infects the glands that make hormones
  2. A gas that blocks oxygen from reaching the blood
  3. A chemical that breaks down quickly in water
  4. A chemical that interferes with hormones
Show the answer

An endocrine disruptor is a chemical that interferes with the body's hormones, by mimicking them, blocking them or changing how much is made.

  • A germ that infects the glands that make hormones: Endocrine disruptors are chemicals, not germs.
  • A gas that blocks oxygen from reaching the blood: That describes carbon monoxide.
  • A chemical that breaks down quickly in water: Many endocrine disruptors are persistent, not quick to break down.
  • Correct: A chemical that interferes with hormones: Right: it interferes with the endocrine (hormone) system.

4. Which claim is best supported by these data alone?

  1. The outfall is linked to more intersex fish downstream
  2. Estrogen-like chemicals have no effect on fish
  3. The intersex fish will recover once they swim upstream
  4. People drinking river water will become intersex
Show the answer

The data show a strong relationship along one river. They support that the outfall is linked to intersex fish; a controlled experiment would be needed to show that the chemicals cause it.

  • Correct: The outfall is linked to more intersex fish downstream: Right: a strong pattern, but these field data show a link, not proof of cause.
  • Estrogen-like chemicals have no effect on fish: The pattern points to an effect, not to no effect.
  • The intersex fish will recover once they swim upstream: Nothing in the data shows recovery of individual fish.
  • People drinking river water will become intersex: The data are about fish, not people, and give no human doses.

6. Why are low-dose effects like these a special concern for endocrine disruptors?

  1. Endocrine disruptors are harmless at high doses
  2. Hormones act at tiny levels, and so do mimics
  3. Low doses make the chemical turn into a hormone
  4. Frogs absorb less of a chemical at high doses
Show the answer

Hormones work at tiny concentrations, so chemicals that mimic or block them can change development at very low doses, below levels once assumed safe.

  • Endocrine disruptors are harmless at high doses: The graph shows effects rising with dose, not vanishing.
  • Correct: Hormones act at tiny levels, and so do mimics: Right: the body's signals are naturally very dilute.
  • Low doses make the chemical turn into a hormone: The chemical mimics or blocks a hormone; it does not turn into one.
  • Frogs absorb less of a chemical at high doses: Nothing in the data suggests less absorption at high doses.

7. How can a chemical that mimics estrogen cause male fish to develop egg cells?

  1. It kills the male cells so female cells grow in their place
  2. It binds estrogen receptors and turns on female development
  3. It lowers dissolved oxygen so males develop poorly
  4. It changes the fish's genes into female genes
Show the answer

The chemical binds to estrogen receptors and switches on female development signals, so males exposed while developing grow female tissue.

  • It kills the male cells so female cells grow in their place: It acts as a signal, not as a poison that kills cells.
  • Correct: It binds estrogen receptors and turns on female development: Right: the body reads it as an estrogen signal.
  • It lowers dissolved oxygen so males develop poorly: Low oxygen does not turn males female.
  • It changes the fish's genes into female genes: It changes hormone signals, not the fish's sex genes.

Part 8 · Summary

Summary

Endocrine disruptors are chemicals that interfere with hormones by mimicking them, blocking their receptors or changing how much is made. Atrazine, BPA, phthalates and synthetic estrogens are examples. Even at very low doses they can cause intersex fish, abnormal frog development and human reproductive and developmental problems. Limits on use, extra cleaning steps at wastewater plants and safe medicine disposal reduce them.
