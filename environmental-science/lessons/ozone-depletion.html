Stratospheric Ozone Depletion

The stratospheric ozone layer absorbs most of the Sun’s UV.

Practice 2: Visual RepresentationsPractice 5: Data Analysis

Part 1 · Hook

Why this matters

In 1985, British scientists in Antarctica published a startling result: every spring, the ozone above them was dropping far below normal. Satellites confirmed a "hole" larger than the continent. The cause was not a volcano or the Sun, but chemicals in refrigerators and spray cans, released tens of thousands of kilometers away.

Part 5 · Step by step

How it works, step by step

  1. CFCs from refrigerants, air conditioners and spray cans are stable near the ground→they last long enough to drift up into the stratosphere
  2. Ultraviolet light in the stratosphere breaks CFC molecules→chlorine atoms are set free
  3. Each chlorine atom destroys an ozone molecule and is released again→one atom can destroy many thousands of ozone molecules
  4. A thinner ozone layer absorbs less UV→more UV-B reaches the surface, raising skin cancer and cataracts and harming phytoplankton

Part 6 · Key ideas

Key ideas

  • Chlorofluorocarbons (CFCs) contain chlorine, fluorine and carbon. They were used in refrigerators, air conditioners, foams and spray cans because they are stable and nontoxic near the ground.
  • In the stratosphere, UV frees chlorine; Cl+O3→ClO+O2\text{Cl} + \text{O}_3 \rightarrow \text{ClO} + \text{O}_2, then ClO+O→Cl+O2\text{ClO} + \text{O} \rightarrow \text{Cl} + \text{O}_2. The chlorine is reused.
  • Ozone depletion is worst over Antarctica each spring (the ozone hole), where extreme cold forms clouds that speed the reactions.
  • More UV-B at the surface means more skin cancer and cataracts and damage to phytoplankton and crops.

Part 7 · Misconception

A common mistake

The wrong idea: Carbon dioxide from burning fossil fuels is what makes the ozone hole.

What actually happens: Carbon dioxide traps heat but does not destroy ozone. The ozone hole is caused by chlorine and bromine from CFCs and halons.

Part 8 · Check yourself

Check yourself

Exam-style questions. Anything you miss goes into your review queue.

1. Which substances are the main cause of stratospheric ozone depletion?

  1. Carbon dioxide from burning fossil fuels
  2. CFCs from old refrigerants and spray cans
  3. Sulfur dioxide from coal-burning power plants
  4. Methane from cattle and landfills
Show the answer

CFCs, and halons with bromine, are the ozone-depleting substances; their chlorine and bromine destroy ozone in the stratosphere.

  • Carbon dioxide from burning fossil fuels: Carbon dioxide traps heat near the surface; it does not destroy stratospheric ozone.
  • Correct: CFCs from old refrigerants and spray cans: Right: chlorine released from CFCs destroys ozone.
  • Sulfur dioxide from coal-burning power plants: Sulfur dioxide causes acid rain; it is not the cause of the ozone hole.
  • Methane from cattle and landfills: Methane traps heat, but it is not the main ozone-depleting substance.

2. Why can one chlorine atom destroy many thousands of ozone molecules?

  1. Each chlorine atom splits into thousands of smaller atoms
  2. It absorbs ultraviolet light, which then destroys the ozone
  3. It traps heat from the ground, which breaks the ozone
  4. It is released after each cycle and reacts again
Show the answer

In Cl + O₃ → ClO + O₂ and then ClO + O → Cl + O₂, the chlorine comes out unchanged and starts again.

  • Each chlorine atom splits into thousands of smaller atoms: Chlorine atoms do not split; the same atom is reused.
  • It absorbs ultraviolet light, which then destroys the ozone: Ultraviolet frees chlorine from CFCs; the chlorine reacts with ozone directly.
  • It traps heat from the ground, which breaks the ozone: Trapping heat is a separate problem from how chlorine destroys ozone.
  • Correct: It is released after each cycle and reacts again: Right: chlorine is a catalyst; it is not used up by the reactions.

5. The hole stopped growing after about 2000, but did not shrink much by 2020. What best explains this?

  1. CFC production rose sharply after 2000
  2. Carbon dioxide began to rebuild the ozone layer after 2000
  3. The ozone hole moved from Antarctica to the Arctic after 2000
  4. Long-lived CFCs make recovery slow after cuts
Show the answer

The slow response fits long-lived CFCs already in the stratosphere; recovery is expected around the 2060s.

  • CFC production rose sharply after 2000: CFC production was being phased out by then.
  • Carbon dioxide began to rebuild the ozone layer after 2000: Carbon dioxide does not rebuild ozone.
  • The ozone hole moved from Antarctica to the Arctic after 2000: The data track the Antarctic hole each year.
  • Correct: Long-lived CFCs make recovery slow after cuts: Right: CFC molecules stay in the air for 50 to 100 years or more, so recovery lags behind the cuts.

6. Why does the ozone hole form over Antarctica each spring?

  1. Polar clouds activate chlorine; spring sun starts the loss
  2. Antarctica produces more CFCs than any other continent
  3. The Antarctic summer sun is strong enough to burn ozone away
  4. Antarctic ice releases chlorine as it melts each spring
Show the answer

Extreme cold over Antarctica forms polar stratospheric clouds, where chlorine becomes reactive; when sunlight returns in spring, ozone loss is rapid.

  • Correct: Polar clouds activate chlorine; spring sun starts the loss: Right: polar stratospheric clouds form in the extreme cold and turn chlorine into active forms; spring sunlight starts the destruction.
  • Antarctica produces more CFCs than any other continent: Few CFCs are released in Antarctica; they mix through the whole atmosphere.
  • The Antarctic summer sun is strong enough to burn ozone away: The hole forms in spring (September to October), and sunlight alone does not remove ozone.
  • Antarctic ice releases chlorine as it melts each spring: The chlorine comes from CFCs, not from ice.

7. Which effects are expected from more UV-B radiation reaching Earth’s surface? Select all that apply.

  1. More cases of skin cancer
  2. More cataracts, a clouding of the eye’s lens
  3. Damage to phytoplankton at the ocean surface
  4. Faster loss of sea ice in the Arctic Ocean
  5. A rise in global average air temperature
  6. More acid rain in the eastern United States
Show the answer

More UV-B harms skin, eyes and some organisms, including phytoplankton; it is not the cause of global warming or acid rain.

  • Correct: More cases of skin cancer: Right: UV-B damages DNA in skin cells.
  • Correct: More cataracts, a clouding of the eye’s lens: Right: UV-B damages the eye.
  • Correct: Damage to phytoplankton at the ocean surface: Right: UV-B harms phytoplankton, the base of ocean food webs.
  • Faster loss of sea ice in the Arctic Ocean: Sea ice melts with warming, not because of more UV.
  • A rise in global average air temperature: Warming is driven by heat-trapping gases, not by more UV.
  • More acid rain in the eastern United States: Acid rain comes from NOx and SOx emissions.

Part 9 · Summary

Summary

The stratospheric ozone layer absorbs most of the Sun’s UV. CFCs drift up to the stratosphere, where UV frees chlorine, and each chlorine atom destroys many thousands of ozone molecules because it is released again after each cycle. The ozone hole forms over Antarctica each spring. Thinner ozone lets more UV-B through, raising skin cancer and cataracts. Carbon dioxide is not an ozone-depleting gas.
