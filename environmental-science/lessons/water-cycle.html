Unit 1 · Topic 1.7

The Hydrologic (Water) Cycle

The water cycle moves water among the ocean, air, land, ice and groundwater.

Practice 1: Concept ExplanationPractice 2: Visual Representations

Part 1 · Hook

Why this matters

The water in your glass may have fallen on a mountain last month, been pumped from rock where it sat for a thousand years, or risen from a tree leaf yesterday. Water never runs out globally, but where it is, how fast it moves, and how much of it is fresh and reachable decide whether a town has enough.

Part 5 · Step by step

How it works, step by step

  1. The sun heats water and plants transpire→water vapor rises into the air
  2. Rising vapor cools and condenses→clouds form and precipitation falls
  3. On land, water either flows over the surface or soaks in→runoff feeds rivers; infiltration refills groundwater in aquifers

Part 6 · Key ideas

Key ideas

  • The hydrologic cycle is driven by the sun (evaporation, transpiration) and gravity (precipitation, runoff, infiltration).
  • About 96.5% of Earth’s water is in the ocean; most freshwater is ice or groundwater, and lakes and rivers hold a tiny share.
  • An aquifer is porous rock or sediment that holds groundwater; deep aquifers refill very slowly.
  • Pavement and cleared land mean less infiltration and more runoff: more floods and less groundwater recharge.

Part 7 · Misconception

A common mistake

The wrong idea: Groundwater is an underground lake or river.

What actually happens: Most groundwater fills the tiny spaces between grains of sand, gravel and rock in an aquifer, like water in a sponge. It moves slowly, often centimeters to meters a day.

Check yourself

Exam-style questions. Anything you miss goes into your review queue.

1. What is an aquifer?

  1. Porous rock that holds groundwater
  2. The area of land that drains into one river
  3. A large lake formed behind a dam on a river
  4. A low cloud that is full of water droplets
Show the answer

An aquifer is porous rock or sediment that stores and transmits groundwater, which can be pumped from wells.

  • Correct: Porous rock that holds groundwater: Right: an underground layer that holds and moves groundwater.
  • The area of land that drains into one river: That describes the land that drains to one river, which is a different idea from an aquifer.
  • A large lake formed behind a dam on a river: A lake behind a dam is a surface reservoir.
  • A low cloud that is full of water droplets: Clouds are in the air, not underground.

Part 9 · Summary

Summary

The water cycle moves water among the ocean, air, land, ice and groundwater. The sun drives evaporation and transpiration; vapor condenses into clouds and falls as precipitation; on land it runs off to rivers or infiltrates to recharge aquifers. Almost all water is salty ocean water, and most freshwater is ice or groundwater. Paving increases runoff and reduces recharge.
