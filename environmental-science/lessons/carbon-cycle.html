Unit 1 · Topic 1.4

The Carbon Cycle

Carbon cycles among the atmosphere, living things, soil, the ocean, sediments and rock, and fossil fuels.

Practice 1: Concept ExplanationPractice 2: Visual Representations

Part 1 · Hook

Why this matters

The carbon in your body was, not long ago, carbon dioxide in the air. A plant pulled it in, you ate the plant, and you breathe some of it back out every few seconds. Other carbon has stayed locked in rock for a hundred million years. Burning coal, oil and natural gas takes that ancient carbon and puts it into the air in an instant.

Part 5 · Step by step

How it works, step by step

  1. Photosynthesis takes CO2 from the air→carbon is stored in plants and the animals that eat them
  2. Respiration and decomposition break those sugars down→CO2 returns to the air
  3. Combustion of fossil fuels releases carbon buried for millions of years→CO2 builds up in the atmosphere faster than sinks can take it back

Part 6 · Key ideas

Key ideas

  • Carbon moves between reservoirs (air, living things, soil, ocean, rock, fossil fuels); each movement is a flux.
  • Photosynthesis removes CO2; cellular respiration, decomposition and combustion release it.
  • A carbon sink takes in more carbon than it releases (forests, the ocean); a carbon source releases more than it takes in.
  • The fast cycle (air, life, ocean surface) turns over in days to centuries; the slow cycle (sedimentation, rock, fossil fuels) takes millions of years.

Part 7 · Misconception

A common mistake

The wrong idea: Trees store CO2.

What actually happens: Trees take in CO2 but store the carbon, built into sugars, wood and leaves; the oxygen is released. Say "trees store carbon".

Part 8 · Check yourself

Check yourself

Exam-style questions. Anything you miss goes into your review queue.

1. A forest takes in more carbon by photosynthesis each year than it releases. What is the forest?

  1. A carbon sink
  2. A carbon source
  3. A fossil fuel reservoir
  4. A flux of zero
Show the answer

A sink takes in more carbon than it releases, so carbon builds up in it.

  • Correct: A carbon sink: Right: net uptake makes it a sink.
  • A carbon source: A source releases more than it takes in.
  • A fossil fuel reservoir: Living forests are not fossil fuel reservoirs.
  • A flux of zero: The flux into the forest is larger than the flux out, so the net flux is not zero.

6. Why does CO2 fall from about May to September at a northern station?

  1. Northern plants photosynthesize more in summer.
  2. People burn more fossil fuels in the summer months.
  3. Decomposition of dead leaves speeds up in summer.
  4. The ocean releases more CO2 during the summer.
Show the answer

Most land is in the Northern Hemisphere. In its growing season, photosynthesis takes in more CO2 than respiration and decomposition release, so CO2 falls.

  • Correct: Northern plants photosynthesize more in summer.: Right: summer photosynthesis draws CO2 down.
  • People burn more fossil fuels in the summer months.: More burning would raise CO2, not lower it.
  • Decomposition of dead leaves speeds up in summer.: More decomposition would raise CO2.
  • The ocean releases more CO2 during the summer.: Releasing CO2 would raise it.

Part 9 · Summary

Summary

Carbon cycles among the atmosphere, living things, soil, the ocean, sediments and rock, and fossil fuels. Photosynthesis removes CO2 from the air; respiration, decomposition and combustion return it. The ocean and growing forests act as carbon sinks. Burning fossil fuels moves slow-cycle carbon into the air far faster than sinks remove it, so atmospheric CO2 rises.
