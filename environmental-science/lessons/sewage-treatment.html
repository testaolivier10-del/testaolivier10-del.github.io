Sewage Treatment

Sewage treatment cleans wastewater in stages.

Practice 2: Visual RepresentationsPractice 7: Environmental Solutions

Part 1 · Hook

Why this matters

Before modern sewage treatment, many rivers below cities had stretches where almost nothing could live: the water smelled, fish were gone, and swimmers got sick. Today a city of a million people can return its wastewater to a river clean enough for fish. The trick is a series of steps that copy what nature does, only faster and in a small space.

Part 4 · Step by step

How it works, step by step

  1. Screens and settling tanks remove debris and let solids sink (primary)→large objects, grit and much of the solid matter are removed as sludge
  2. Microbes in aerated tanks eat the dissolved organic matter (secondary)→BOD falls, so the waste will not use up the river's oxygen
  3. Filters and chemicals remove nitrogen and phosphorus (tertiary)→nutrients that would feed algal blooms are kept out of the river
  4. Chlorine, ozone or UV light treats the water (disinfection)→germs are killed before the water is released
  5. Sludge is digested, dried and disposed of or reused→methane can be captured for energy and solids are landfilled, burned or spread as fertilizer

Part 5 · Key ideas

Key ideas

  • Primary treatment: physical. Screens, grit chambers and settling tanks remove solids as sludge.
  • Secondary treatment: biological. Microbes in aerated tanks break down dissolved organic matter, cutting BOD so the river keeps its oxygen.
  • Tertiary treatment: removes nitrogen and phosphorus (preventing eutrophication) and other chemicals. Then disinfection with chlorine, ozone or UV kills germs.
  • Homes without sewers use a septic system: a tank where solids settle and a drain field where soil filters the liquid. Combined sewers can overflow untreated sewage in heavy rain.

Part 6 · Misconception

A common mistake

The wrong idea: Sewage treatment just filters the water.

What actually happens: Filtering and settling are only the first step. Secondary treatment uses living microbes to eat the dissolved waste, and tertiary treatment and disinfection remove nutrients and kill germs.

5. Why does secondary treatment remove so much BOD?

  1. Microbes in aerated tanks eat it
  2. Screens catch the large objects in the sewage
  3. Chlorine dissolves the organic matter
  4. Settling tanks let the dissolved matter sink
Show the answer

In secondary treatment, aerated tanks let bacteria and other microbes eat the dissolved organic matter, so it is broken down in the plant instead of in the river.

  • Correct: Microbes in aerated tanks eat it: Right: the plant uses oxygen so the river does not have to.
  • Screens catch the large objects in the sewage: Screens act at the start and remove little BOD.
  • Chlorine dissolves the organic matter: Chlorine kills germs; it is not the main BOD step.
  • Settling tanks let the dissolved matter sink: Settling is primary treatment; dissolved organics do not sink.

6. Phosphorus falls from 4.5 to 0.5 mg/L only after tertiary treatment. Why does that step matter for the lake downstream?

  1. It raises the water temperature for fish
  2. It adds oxygen directly to the lake
  3. It kills the bacteria in the sewage
  4. It keeps out a nutrient algae need
Show the answer

Primary and secondary treatment leave most phosphorus. Removing it in tertiary treatment keeps the limiting nutrient out of the lake, which prevents algal blooms and the oxygen drop that follows.

  • It raises the water temperature for fish: Phosphorus removal does not change temperature.
  • It adds oxygen directly to the lake: Removing phosphorus prevents the oxygen drop; it does not add oxygen.
  • It kills the bacteria in the sewage: Disinfection kills bacteria; phosphorus removal targets a nutrient.
  • Correct: It keeps out a nutrient algae need: Right: less phosphorus, less eutrophication.

Part 8 · Summary

Summary

Sewage treatment cleans wastewater in stages. Primary treatment physically removes solids as sludge; secondary treatment uses microbes in aerated tanks to break down dissolved organic matter and cut BOD; tertiary treatment removes nitrogen and phosphorus; disinfection kills germs. Sludge is digested and disposed of or reused. Septic systems treat wastewater for single homes, and combined sewers can overflow in storms.
