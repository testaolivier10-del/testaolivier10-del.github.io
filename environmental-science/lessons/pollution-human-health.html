Pollution and Human Health

Linking a pollutant to a disease is hard because people face many exposures over long times.

Practice 1: Concept ExplanationPractice 7: Environmental Solutions

Part 1 · Hook

Why this matters

Asthma visits at a town’s clinic have climbed for five years. Neighbors blame the factory on the hill. But the town also sits beside a busy highway, many homes burn wood in winter, and summer smog has grown worse. Which one is the cause? Probably several at once, and some may make each other worse. That is why pollution and health are so hard to untangle.

Part 5 · Step by step

How it works, step by step

  1. People are exposed to many pollutants, at different doses, over many years→it is hard to link one illness to one pollutant
  2. Two pollutants that interact inside the body→can cause more harm together than their separate effects add up to (synergism)
  3. A short, high exposure→causes acute effects soon after, such as dizziness or an asthma attack
  4. A long, low exposure, such as asbestos fibers or lead→causes chronic effects that may appear years later, such as mesothelioma

Part 6 · Key ideas

Key ideas

  • Cause and effect are hard to prove in people: exposures are mixed and long, other factors vary, and many diseases take years to appear.
  • Synergism: two or more substances together cause more harm than the sum of their separate effects.
  • Acute effects follow a short, high exposure quickly. Chronic effects follow long or repeated exposure and may take years to appear.
  • Examples to know: ground-level ozone lowers lung function and triggers asthma; asbestos causes mesothelioma; untreated sewage in drinking water spreads dysentery.

Part 7 · Misconception

A common mistake

The wrong idea: If pollutant A alone damages 10% of lungs and pollutant B alone damages 8%, together they must damage 18%.

What actually happens: Effects do not always add. With synergism, the combined harm can be much larger: 40% in the mouse study, more than twice the sum.

Part 8 · Check yourself

Check yourself

Exam-style questions. Anything you miss goes into your review queue.

1. A worker breathes in a large amount of a solvent in one afternoon and gets dizzy within minutes. Another worker breathes small amounts of the same solvent every day for 20 years and develops liver disease. Which statement is correct?

  1. The first is a chronic effect and the second an acute effect
  2. The first is an acute effect and the second a chronic effect
  3. Both are acute effects, since the same solvent caused them
  4. Both are chronic effects, since each harmed a body organ
Show the answer

Acute: a short exposure, effects soon after. Chronic: long or repeated exposure, effects that appear over months or years.

  • The first is a chronic effect and the second an acute effect: Reversed: the quick reaction to one large exposure is acute, and harm after years of exposure is chronic.
  • Correct: The first is an acute effect and the second a chronic effect: Right: acute effects come soon after a short, high exposure; chronic effects follow long, repeated exposure.
  • Both are acute effects, since the same solvent caused them: Acute and chronic describe timing and length of exposure, not which chemical is involved.
  • Both are chronic effects, since each harmed a body organ: Harm to an organ can be acute or chronic; the dizziness came within minutes of one exposure.

4. Which conclusion do the data support?

  1. Pollutant A is harmless, since it damaged the lungs of 10% of mice
  2. A and B are synergistic: over twice the expected damage
  3. A and B cancel each other out, since the effects are not added
  4. The control mice had lung damage, so the study shows nothing
Show the answer

The combined bar (40%) towers over the sum of the separate effects (18%), which is synergism.

  • Pollutant A is harmless, since it damaged the lungs of 10% of mice: 10% is five times the control’s 2%, so A does cause damage.
  • Correct: A and B are synergistic: over twice the expected damage: Right: 40% observed is more than twice the 18% that adding their effects would give.
  • A and B cancel each other out, since the effects are not added: Cancelling would give less damage than either alone; the result is far more.
  • The control mice had lung damage, so the study shows nothing: 2% damage in the control is the baseline every group is compared with.

5. What is the purpose of the group of mice that breathed clean air?

  1. To prove that clean air causes 2% of the mice to have lung damage
  2. To give the researchers more mice in the study for less money
  3. A baseline: the lung damage with neither pollutant
  4. To expose the mice to a smaller dose of pollutant A and B together
Show the answer

A control group is a baseline: it shows what happens without the treatment, so the effect of each pollutant can be measured against it.

  • To prove that clean air causes 2% of the mice to have lung damage: The control is not a test of clean air; it shows the background level of damage.
  • To give the researchers more mice in the study for less money: Saving money is not the reason; the group is there for comparison.
  • Correct: A baseline: the lung damage with neither pollutant: Right: the 2% in clean air shows damage from other causes, so effects can be measured against it.
  • To expose the mice to a smaller dose of pollutant A and B together: The control gets neither pollutant; that is what makes it a baseline.

6. Untreated sewage flows into a river that a village uses for drinking water. Which illness is the village most likely to see more of?

  1. Dysentery, spread by microbes in feces
  2. Mesothelioma, a cancer of the lining of the lungs
  3. Skin cancer from more ultraviolet light
  4. Asthma from ground-level ozone
Show the answer

Sewage carries microbes from human waste. Drinking that water spreads dysentery, a leading cause of illness where sanitation is poor.

  • Correct: Dysentery, spread by microbes in feces: Right: dysentery spreads through water contaminated with untreated sewage.
  • Mesothelioma, a cancer of the lining of the lungs: Mesothelioma is caused mainly by breathing asbestos fibers, not by sewage.
  • Skin cancer from more ultraviolet light: Sewage in water does not change ultraviolet light.
  • Asthma from ground-level ozone: Ozone is an air pollutant; sewage contaminates water.

7. Which are examples of chronic exposure? Select all that apply.

  1. A farmworker handles small amounts of a pesticide every season for 15 years
  2. A worker breathes chlorine gas from one spill and coughs for a day
  3. A driver breathes thick smoke for ten minutes during a single fire
  4. A hiker is stung once by a bee
Show the answer

Chronic exposure is long or repeated; acute exposure is short, often a single event. Only the farmworker’s 15 years of exposure is chronic.

  • Correct: A farmworker handles small amounts of a pesticide every season for 15 years: Right: repeated, long-term exposure is chronic.
  • A worker breathes chlorine gas from one spill and coughs for a day: One short, high exposure with effects within hours is acute.
  • A driver breathes thick smoke for ten minutes during a single fire: One short, high exposure is acute.
  • A hiker is stung once by a bee: A single short exposure is acute, not chronic.

Part 9 · Summary

Summary

Linking a pollutant to a disease is hard because people face many exposures over long times. Synergism means pollutants together can do more harm than their effects added. Acute effects follow short, high exposures; chronic effects follow long ones. Know ground-level ozone and lung function, asbestos and mesothelioma, and sewage-contaminated water and dysentery.
