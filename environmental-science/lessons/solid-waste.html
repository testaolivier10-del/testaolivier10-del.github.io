Solid Waste Disposal

Municipal solid waste in the United States is mostly landfilled (about half in 2018), with the rest recycled, composted or burned.

Practice 7: Environmental Solutions

Part 1 · Hook

Why this matters

Each person in the United States threw away about 4.9 pounds of trash a day in 2018, according to the EPA. Across the country that came to 292 million tons in a year, and half of it went into landfills. A modern landfill is not a dump. It is a lined, engineered structure designed to keep trash, liquid and gas from escaping for decades.

Part 4 · Step by step

How it works, step by step

  1. Households and businesses throw away municipal solid waste→about half goes to landfills in the United States
  2. Rain filters through the buried waste→it dissolves chemicals and becomes leachate, which a liner and pipes must catch
  3. Bacteria break down food, yard waste and paper with no oxygen→methane and CO2 form; gas wells collect the methane to burn or use for power
  4. Tightly packed, dry trash gets little oxygen and water→it decomposes very slowly, so landfills fill up and must be managed for decades

Part 5 · Key ideas

Key ideas

  • Municipal solid waste is everyday trash from homes and businesses. In 2018 (EPA) about 50% was landfilled, 32% recycled or composted, and 12% burned for energy.
  • A sanitary landfill has a clay and plastic liner, leachate collection pipes, daily soil cover, gas wells and a final cap. Leachate is liquid that seeps through trash and picks up pollutants.
  • Buried organic waste decomposes slowly without oxygen and makes methane. Incineration cuts volume about 90% and can make power, but releases air pollutants and toxic ash.
  • E-waste holds lead, mercury and cadmium and needs special handling. Ocean dumping of trash is banned in the United States.

Part 6 · Misconception

A common mistake

The wrong idea: Trash in a landfill rots away within a few years.

What actually happens: A landfill is packed tight and kept dry, so little oxygen and water reach the trash. Decomposition is very slow, and some newspapers and food stay recognizable for decades.

Part 7 · Check yourself

Check yourself

Exam-style questions. Anything you miss goes into your review queue.

1. Which feature of a sanitary landfill keeps leachate out of groundwater?

  1. A daily cover of soil over the trash
  2. A liner with collection pipes
  3. Gas wells that collect methane
  4. A fence around the whole site
Show the answer

A sanitary landfill sits on a clay and plastic liner, with pipes that collect leachate for treatment.

  • A daily cover of soil over the trash: Daily soil cover controls pests and odors; the liner protects groundwater.
  • Correct: A liner with collection pipes: Right: the liner blocks it and the pipes collect it.
  • Gas wells that collect methane: Gas wells handle methane, not leachate.
  • A fence around the whole site: A fence keeps people and animals out.

6. Why do newspapers and food buried in a landfill decades ago often look almost unchanged when dug up?

  1. Little oxygen or water reaches them
  2. Landfills are too cold for any bacteria to live
  3. Newspapers and food are unable to decompose
  4. Methane preserves everything in the landfill
Show the answer

Landfills are packed tight and kept dry, with little oxygen or water, so decomposition is very slow.

  • Correct: Little oxygen or water reaches them: Right: tight, dry conditions slow decay.
  • Landfills are too cold for any bacteria to live: Landfills are often warm inside; the limit is oxygen and water.
  • Newspapers and food are unable to decompose: They decompose readily where oxygen and moisture are present.
  • Methane preserves everything in the landfill: Methane is a product of decay, not a preservative.

Part 8 · Summary

Summary

Municipal solid waste in the United States is mostly landfilled (about half in 2018), with the rest recycled, composted or burned. Sanitary landfills use liners, leachate collection, soil cover, gas wells and caps to contain trash; buried organic waste decomposes slowly without oxygen and makes methane. Incineration cuts volume but releases air pollutants and ash. E-waste needs special handling, and ocean dumping is banned.
