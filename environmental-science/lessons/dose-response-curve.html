Dose Response Curve

A dose-response curve shows the percent of a test group responding at each dose.

Practice 5: Data Analysis

Part 1 · Hook

Why this matters

Two pesticides are both "toxic to fish" on their labels. A pond downhill from the fields will only ever see a few milligrams per kilogram of either one in its runoff. Which one is safer for the fish? The answer is not in one number but in the shape of a curve: where it leaves zero, and where it crosses 50%.

Part 5 · Step by step

How it works, step by step

  1. Below the threshold dose→the test group shows no measurable response, so the curve sits at zero
  2. Above the threshold, each larger dose→affects a larger percent of the group, and the curve rises steeply through 50%
  3. Reading across from 50% to the curve and down→gives the LD50 for deaths or the ED50 for another effect
  4. A curve that rises at lower doses→belongs to the more toxic substance

Part 6 · Key ideas

Key ideas

  • A dose-response curve plots dose (mg/kg) on the horizontal axis against the percent of a test group responding on the vertical axis. It is usually S-shaped.
  • The threshold dose is where the curve leaves zero: the highest dose with no observed response.
  • The LD50 is read at 50% on a curve of deaths. The ED50 (effective dose 50%) is read at 50% on a curve of a nonlethal effect, such as illness or sleep.
  • To compare substances, compare where their curves rise: the curve to the left belongs to the more toxic substance.

Part 7 · Misconception

A common mistake

The wrong idea: If a dose is below a substance’s LD50, it is safe.

What actually happens: Below the LD50, up to half of the test animals can still die, and nonlethal harm can start at much lower doses. Only the threshold dose marks where no effect was seen, and some substances have no threshold at all.

Part 8 · Check yourself

Check yourself

Exam-style questions. Anything you miss goes into your review queue.

1. On a dose-response curve, what is the threshold dose?

  1. The dose at which half of the test animals die
  2. The highest dose at which no response is seen
  3. The largest dose that was tested in the study
  4. The dose at which about 99% of test animals die
Show the answer

The threshold dose is where the curve leaves zero: below it, no effect is observed in the test group.

  • The dose at which half of the test animals die: That is the LD50, read at 50% on the response axis.
  • Correct: The highest dose at which no response is seen: Right: below the threshold, the test group shows no measurable effect.
  • The largest dose that was tested in the study: The largest dose tested is the end of the curve, not its threshold.
  • The dose at which about 99% of test animals die: At the top of the curve nearly all animals respond; the threshold is at the bottom.

4. Which statement compares the two pesticides correctly?

  1. X is twice as toxic as Y, since its LD50 is twice as large
  2. They are equally toxic, since both kill about 98% or more of the fish at 160 mg/kg
  3. Y is twice as toxic as X: its LD50 is half of X’s
  4. X is more toxic, since its curve keeps rising to a higher dose
Show the answer

Compare LD50s by division: 80mg/kg40mg/kg\frac{80\,\text{mg/kg}}{40\,\text{mg/kg}}=2= 2, so Y is twice as toxic.

  • X is twice as toxic as Y, since its LD50 is twice as large: A larger LD50 means more pesticide is needed to kill, so X is less toxic.
  • They are equally toxic, since both kill about 98% or more of the fish at 160 mg/kg: Toxicity is compared at the 50% point, where Y needs half the dose X does.
  • Correct: Y is twice as toxic as X: its LD50 is half of X’s: Right: the LD50 of Y is 40 mg/kg and of X is 80 mg/kg, and 8040=2\frac{80}{40} = 2.
  • X is more toxic, since its curve keeps rising to a higher dose: X rises later because it is less toxic; more of it is needed for the same effect.

6. A town must choose one of the two pesticides for fields that drain into a fish pond. Based only on these data, which choice is better for the fish, and why?

  1. X, because a larger dose of it is needed before fish start to die
  2. Y, because its curve reaches 100% faster, so it breaks down sooner
  3. Y, because it kills fish at lower doses, so less of it reaches the pond
  4. Either one, because both kill about 98% or more of the fish at high doses
Show the answer

At every dose from 20 to 80 mg/kg, Y kills a larger percent of fish than X, and its threshold and LD50 are both lower.

  • Correct: X, because a larger dose of it is needed before fish start to die: Right: X has the higher threshold (about 20 mg/kg) and the higher LD50 (80 mg/kg).
  • Y, because its curve reaches 100% faster, so it breaks down sooner: How fast a curve rises with dose says nothing about how fast the pesticide breaks down.
  • Y, because it kills fish at lower doses, so less of it reaches the pond: Killing fish at lower doses makes Y more dangerous to them, not less.
  • Either one, because both kill about 98% or more of the fish at high doses: At the doses most likely in runoff, the low end of the curves, Y kills far more fish.

  2. Go straight across to the curve
  3. Go straight down from that point to the dose axis
  4. Read the dose and write it with its unit, mg/kg
Show the answer

Across from 50%, down to the dose axis, then read the value with its unit.

Part 9 · Summary

Summary

A dose-response curve shows the percent of a test group responding at each dose. It is flat at zero below the threshold dose, rises steeply through 50% and levels off near 100%. Read across from 50% and down to find the LD50 (deaths) or ED50 (another effect). The substance whose curve rises at lower doses is more toxic, and harm can begin far below the LD50.
