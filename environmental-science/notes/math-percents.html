Percent calculations show up on almost every free-response calculation question: the percent of energy from coal, the percent decrease in a fish catch, the percent change in a population. Chief Reader Reports name the same slips year after year: no brackets around the subtraction, dividing by the wrong number, and forgetting to multiply by 100. This chapter fixes all three.

What a percent is

A percent is a number out of 100. "26%" means 26 out of every 100. There are three common jobs.

JobSetupExample
What percent is a part of a whole?part / whole × 100650 kg recycled of 2,500 kg: (650 / 2,500) × 100 = 26%
What is a percent of a quantity?(percent / 100) × quantity18% of 4.0 million GWh: 0.18 × 4,000,000 = 720,000 GWh
By what percent did it change?(new - old) / old × 100120 to 150 frogs: (150 - 120) / 120 × 100 = 25%

A percent of a quantity keeps the quantity's unit and scale: 18% of 4.0 million GWh is 0.72 million GWh, or 720,000 GWh. Do not drop the "million".

Percent change, step by step

percent change=new−oldold×100\text{percent change} = \frac{\text{new} - \text{old}}{\text{old}} \times 100

  1. Subtract, in brackets: (new - old). The sign tells you the direction: positive is an increase, negative a decrease.
  2. Divide by the old value, the starting point you are comparing with.
  3. Multiply by 100 to turn the fraction into a percent.

Worked example. A town's water use rose from 4,000 to 5,000 liters per day. Find the percent change.

Setup, as you would type it: (5000 - 4000) / 4000 * 100

Answer: 25% (an increase).

Without brackets, 5000 - 4000 / 4000 * 100 means 5,000−100=4,9005{,}000 - 100 = 4{,}900, because a calculator divides before it subtracts. Dividing by the new value instead, 1,000 / 5,000, gives 20%, which is wrong.

Decreases

Worked example. Better insulation and new appliances cut a home's electricity use from 9,600 to 6,000 kWh a year.

Setup: (6000 - 9600) / 9600 * 100

Answer: −37.5%-37.5\%, a 37.5% decrease. If the question asks for "the percent decrease", give 37.5%; the word "decrease" already carries the sign.

Percents of different starting values do not add

A fish population falls 50% and then rises 50%. Start at 100: it falls to 50, then rises by half of 50, to 75. It ends 25% lower, not back at 100, because the second percent is of a smaller number. In the same way, frogs that rise 25% (120 to 150) and then fall 10% (150 to 135) end up 135−120120×100\frac{135 - 120}{120} \times 100=12.5%= 12.5\% above the start, not 15%. For an overall change, always go back to the first and last values.

Exam habits

  • Type the setup with brackets and × 100 exactly as above; the setup is its own point.
  • Give the answer with the % sign and say "increase" or "decrease" if asked.
  • Check the size: a change from 120 to 150 cannot be 2,500% or 0.25%.
