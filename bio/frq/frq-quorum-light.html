Spent medium and light from sparse bacteria

Researchers grew the bacterium Vibrio fischeri to a high density (8 × 10⁸ cells/mL), then filtered out the cells to obtain cell-free "spent" medium. They placed fresh cells at a low density (1 × 10⁷ cells/mL) in mixtures of fresh medium and spent medium and measured light per cell after 1 hour; the cells did not multiply noticeably in that time. In a last treatment they used spent medium from a strain that cannot make the autoinducer. Values are means ± standard error of 3 cultures, in relative units.

  1. (a) Describe how light per cell changes as the percentage of spent medium from the normal strain increases. [1 point]

  2. (b) Calculate how many times greater the light per cell is with 20% spent medium than with 10% spent medium. [1 point]

  3. (c) Identify what the treatment with spent medium from the strain that cannot make autoinducer rules out. [1 point]

  4. (d) A student claims that the bacteria switch on their light in response to the concentration of a signal molecule, not to the number of cells around them. Support the claim with evidence from the data. [1 point]
