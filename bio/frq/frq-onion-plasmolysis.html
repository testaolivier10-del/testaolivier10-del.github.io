Plasmolysis in onion cells

Students peeled thin layers of epidermis from two groups of red onions: onions grown with normal watering and onions grown under drought. They placed pieces in sucrose solutions for 20 minutes and, under a microscope, counted 100 cells per piece, recording how many were plasmolyzed. Each value is the mean of four pieces.

  1. (a) Describe the relationship between sucrose concentration and the percent of plasmolyzed cells in the normally watered onions. [1 point]

  2. (b) Using the data, calculate the difference, in percentage points, between the two groups at 0.4 mol/L sucrose, and identify the sucrose concentration at which about half of the drought-grown cells are plasmolyzed. [1 point]

  3. (c) Explain, in terms of water potential, why cells become plasmolyzed in the stronger sucrose solutions. [1 point]

  4. (d) A student claims that drought-grown onion cells contain a higher concentration of dissolved solutes than normally watered cells. Justify the claim using the data, and explain how this difference could help a plant growing in dry soil. [1 point]
