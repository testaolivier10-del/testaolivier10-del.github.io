Three chemicals that act on mitochondria

In the inner mitochondrial membrane, an electron transport chain passes electrons from NADH and FADH₂ to O₂ and pumps H⁺ into the intermembrane space; ATP synthase uses the H⁺ gradient to make ATP. Three chemicals affect this system. Rotenone, a pesticide, blocks the first protein of the chain, which accepts electrons from NADH. Oligomycin blocks the H⁺ channel of ATP synthase. 2,4-Dinitrophenol (DNP) carries H⁺ across membranes; in the 1930s it was sold as a diet pill, and some people who took it died of dangerously high body temperature.

  1. (a) Describe the role of O₂ in the electron transport chain. [1 point]

  2. (b) Explain why adding oligomycin to isolated mitochondria reduces their O₂ consumption, even though oligomycin does not act on the electron transport chain. [1 point]

  3. (c) Predict the effect of DNP on the amount of ATP a cell makes and on body temperature. Justify your prediction. [1 point]

  4. (d) Cells treated with rotenone produce much more lactate than untreated cells. Explain this observation. [1 point]
