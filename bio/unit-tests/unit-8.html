PCB concentrations in a lake food web

PCBs are industrial chemicals that are fat-soluble and break down very slowly. Researchers measured PCBs in the water of a large lake and in organisms from its food web. Gulls eat mainly large fish. Values are means; ppm is parts per million by mass.

1. Which explanation best accounts for the pattern in the table?

  1. PCBs are made inside the bodies of predators from chemicals in their prey, so more PCBs form at each higher level.
  2. Larger animals drink more lake water than smaller ones, so they take in more of the PCBs dissolved in the water each day.
  3. PCBs stay in body fat instead of being excreted, and each consumer eats many times its own mass, so they concentrate.
  4. PCBs break down slowly in small organisms but speed up as they move into larger ones, which makes them more concentrated.
Show the answer

This is biomagnification. Persistent, fat-soluble PCBs stay in the body; since only about 10% of food becomes new tissue, each predator eats far more prey than its own mass and keeps the PCBs from all of it.

  • PCBs are made inside the bodies of predators from chemicals in their prey, so more PCBs form at each higher level.: PCBs are industrial chemicals; animals do not make them.
  • Larger animals drink more lake water than smaller ones, so they take in more of the PCBs dissolved in the water each day.: The water holds only 0.000004 ppm; drinking cannot explain 4.5 ppm in trout, which take in PCBs mainly in food.
  • Correct: PCBs stay in body fat instead of being excreted, and each consumer eats many times its own mass, so they concentrate.: Correct: storage plus the large amount of food eaten.
  • PCBs break down slowly in small organisms but speed up as they move into larger ones, which makes them more concentrated.: PCBs break down slowly everywhere; faster breakdown would lower concentrations, not raise them.

2. A student claims that because PCB levels in the lake water are tiny, the PCBs pose no risk to wildlife. Which response is best supported by the data?

  1. The claim is supported, because 0.000004 ppm is far too low to harm any organism.
  2. The claim is not supported, because gull eggs carry 90 ppm, over 20 million times the water level.
  3. The claim is supported, because PCB levels in phytoplankton are below those in fish.
  4. The claim is not supported, because the water level is higher than in the phytoplankton.
Show the answer

Biomagnification turns a tiny concentration in water into a high one in top predators; harm happens where the concentration is high, such as in gull eggs.

  • The claim is supported, because 0.000004 ppm is far too low to harm any organism.: The water level is low, but animals at the top of the food web are exposed to far more.
  • Correct: The claim is not supported, because gull eggs carry 90 ppm, over 20 million times the water level.: Correct: the risk is at the top of the food web.
  • The claim is supported, because PCB levels in phytoplankton are below those in fish.: Low levels in producers fit biomagnification; they do not show the top predators are safe.
  • The claim is not supported, because the water level is higher than in the phytoplankton.: The water level (0.000004 ppm) is lower than in phytoplankton (0.02 ppm).

3. PCB releases into the lake stop, and PCB levels in the water fall within a few years. Predict which samples will take longest to return to low PCB levels.

  1. Phytoplankton, because they are closest to the water and absorb PCBs directly
  2. Zooplankton, because they reproduce faster than the other consumers in the lake
  3. Lake trout and gulls, because they are long-lived and keep stored PCBs in their fat
  4. Lake water, because PCBs dissolve more easily in water than in living tissue
Show the answer

Long-lived top predators hold PCBs accumulated over years in their fat and keep eating prey that still carry them, so their levels fall slowly. Short-lived plankton are replaced quickly by new, cleaner individuals.

  • Phytoplankton, because they are closest to the water and absorb PCBs directly: Phytoplankton live days to weeks, so new cells soon reflect the cleaner water.
  • Zooplankton, because they reproduce faster than the other consumers in the lake: Fast reproduction replaces zooplankton quickly, so their levels drop sooner.
  • Correct: Lake trout and gulls, because they are long-lived and keep stored PCBs in their fat: Correct: long life and fat storage keep PCBs in top predators.
  • Lake water, because PCBs dissolve more easily in water than in living tissue: The stem says the water levels fall within a few years; PCBs are fat-soluble, not water-soluble.

4. The phytoplankton store 10,200 kJ in new biomass, but the zooplankton take in only 4,100 kJ. Which best accounts for most of the other 6,100 kJ?

  1. It is released as heat by the phytoplankton's respiration after the cells have grown.
  2. It is destroyed by the zooplankton's digestive enzymes as they break down the algae cells.
  3. Algae that die uneaten, and eaten algae lost in zooplankton feces, pass to decomposers.
  4. It is stored as fat by the zooplankton, a store that the table does not include.
Show the answer

Energy stored in phytoplankton biomass that zooplankton do not take in either is never eaten or is eaten but not absorbed. Both end up as detritus, the food of decomposers.

  • It is released as heat by the phytoplankton's respiration after the cells have grown.: Phytoplankton respiration is already subtracted: 13,800 kJ was released as heat, leaving 10,200 stored.
  • It is destroyed by the zooplankton's digestive enzymes as they break down the algae cells.: Energy is not destroyed; digestion breaks molecules down so they can be absorbed.
  • Correct: Algae that die uneaten, and eaten algae lost in zooplankton feces, pass to decomposers.: Correct: uneaten algae and undigested food feed the decomposers.
  • It is stored as fat by the zooplankton, a store that the table does not include.: Energy taken in already includes everything the zooplankton absorb, fat stores included.

5. The small fish store 20% of the energy they take in (82 of 410 kJ), but the birds store 2% (0.8 of 40 kJ). Which best explains the difference?

  1. The birds are endotherms, so they burn much of their food to make heat that keeps the body warm, and that energy leaves the food chain.
  2. The birds are at a higher trophic level, and the second law of thermodynamics destroys more energy at each higher level of a chain.
  3. The birds digest fish less fully than the fish digest zooplankton, so less of the fish they eat is ever absorbed into the body.
  4. The birds store most of their energy as fat, and the table counts the energy in fat as heat released by cellular respiration.
Show the answer

Endotherms spend most absorbed energy on respiration that produces body heat (39.2 of 40 kJ here), leaving little for growth. Ectotherms like the fish do not pay that cost.

  • Correct: The birds are endotherms, so they burn much of their food to make heat that keeps the body warm, and that energy leaves the food chain.: Correct: the cost of endothermy shows up as heat released by respiration.
  • The birds are at a higher trophic level, and the second law of thermodynamics destroys more energy at each higher level of a chain.: Energy is never destroyed, and trophic level alone does not explain why one animal stores 20% and another 2%.
  • The birds digest fish less fully than the fish digest zooplankton, so less of the fish they eat is ever absorbed into the body.: The comparison already uses energy taken in (absorbed), so differences in digestion are not part of it.
  • The birds store most of their energy as fat, and the table counts the energy in fat as heat released by cellular respiration.: Fat is stored biomass and would be counted in the last column, not as heat.

6. Suppose the birds were replaced by a large predatory fish (an ectotherm) that also takes in 40 kJ per m² per year. Predict the energy it would store in new biomass.

  1. About 0.8 kJ, the same as the birds, because both feed at the same trophic level
  2. Much more than 0.8 kJ, because an ectotherm does not spend energy making body heat
  3. Less than 0.8 kJ, because large fish need more energy to swim than birds need to fly
  4. Zero, because a fourth trophic level has no energy left to store as new biomass in a lake
Show the answer

If it stored a share like the small fish (about 20%), it would store about 8 kJ, ten times as much. An ectotherm's body temperature follows the water, so less of its food is burned for heat.

  • About 0.8 kJ, the same as the birds, because both feed at the same trophic level: Trophic level sets how much energy is available (40 kJ), not what share an animal stores.
  • Correct: Much more than 0.8 kJ, because an ectotherm does not spend energy making body heat: Correct: ectotherms store a larger share of what they take in.
  • Less than 0.8 kJ, because large fish need more energy to swim than birds need to fly: The data show fish storing 20% of their intake; nothing suggests a fish's swimming costs exceed an endotherm's heat production.
  • Zero, because a fourth trophic level has no energy left to store as new biomass in a lake: The birds at this level already store 0.8 kJ, so a fourth level can store energy.

7. A hamster kept in constant darkness for three weeks started running on its wheel 18 minutes later each day than the day before. What is the period of its circadian rhythm, in hours? Give your answer to one decimal place.

Type a number in h.

Show the answer

Each cycle lasts 24 hours plus 18 minutes. 18 ÷ 60 = 0.3 hour, so the period is 24.3 hours. In constant conditions the internal clock runs at its own pace; daily light would normally reset it to 24 hours.

  • Answer: 24.3 h

8. Creosote bushes in a desert are spaced almost evenly, far apart. Seedlings that sprout near an adult bush usually die within a year. Which explanation best accounts for this dispersion?

  1. Adult roots take up the scarce water from a wide area, so seedlings near them dry out and die.
  2. Seeds fall straight down from the parent, so the young grow in tight groups around each adult.
  3. Wind spreads the seeds at random, so the bushes end up scattered with no pattern.
  4. Bushes grow best close together, where they shade each other's roots from the desert sun.
Show the answer

Competition for water spaces the survivors out: wherever a seedling is too close to an adult, it loses. Uniform dispersion comes from neighbors pushing each other apart.

  • Correct: Adult roots take up the scarce water from a wide area, so seedlings near them dry out and die.: Correct: competition between neighbors produces uniform spacing.
  • Seeds fall straight down from the parent, so the young grow in tight groups around each adult.: Seeds falling near the parent would give a clumped pattern, but the seedlings there die.
  • Wind spreads the seeds at random, so the bushes end up scattered with no pattern.: The bushes are evenly spaced, not randomly scattered.
  • Bushes grow best close together, where they shade each other's roots from the desert sun.: Seedlings close to adults die, so being close together does not help them.

9. A population of rabbits has r_max = 0.4 per year and a carrying capacity of 1,000. Use dN/dt = r_max N (K − N)/K to calculate its growth rate when N = 900, in rabbits per year. Give a whole number.

Type a number in rabbits per year.

Show the answer

dN/dt = 0.4 × 900 × (1,000 − 900)/1,000 = 0.4 × 900 × 0.1 = 36 rabbits per year. Near K the growth rate is small even though N is large.

  • Answer: 36 rabbits per year

10. Hoverflies cannot sting, but they have black and yellow stripes like stinging wasps. Birds that have been stung by a wasp tend to avoid hoverflies too. What is this an example of?

  1. Müllerian mimicry, because both insects share a warning pattern
  2. Camouflage, because the stripes help the hoverfly blend into flowers
  3. Batesian mimicry, because a harmless species resembles a harmful one
  4. Warning coloration, because the hoverfly is toxic and signals it
Show the answer

In Batesian mimicry a harmless mimic gains protection by resembling a harmful model that predators have learned to avoid.

  • Müllerian mimicry, because both insects share a warning pattern: Müllerian mimicry needs both species to be harmful; the hoverfly is harmless.
  • Camouflage, because the stripes help the hoverfly blend into flowers: Bright stripes stand out rather than blend in; they work by resemblance to wasps.
  • Correct: Batesian mimicry, because a harmless species resembles a harmful one: Correct: harmless mimic, harmful model.
  • Warning coloration, because the hoverfly is toxic and signals it: The hoverfly is harmless; its stripes are a bluff, not an honest signal.

11. Beavers cut trees and build dams that flood a stream valley, creating a pond with marshy edges where frogs, ducks and water plants live. Which term best describes the beaver's role?

  1. Foundation species, because beavers are the most abundant animals there
  2. Producer, because beavers supply the energy that the pond relies on
  3. Keystone predator, because beavers eat the strongest competitor present
  4. Ecosystem engineer, because beavers physically reshape the environment
Show the answer

Beavers change the physical environment, turning a stream into a pond and wetland that many other species then use.

  • Foundation species, because beavers are the most abundant animals there: Beavers are few; their effect comes from building, not from being abundant.
  • Producer, because beavers supply the energy that the pond relies on: Beavers are consumers; the pond's energy comes from its plants and algae.
  • Keystone predator, because beavers eat the strongest competitor present: Beavers eat bark and plants; their effect is not through predation on a competitor.
  • Correct: Ecosystem engineer, because beavers physically reshape the environment: Correct: a species that creates habitat by changing the physical environment.

12. Ants travel in a line between a sugar bait and their nest. A student wipes a 3 cm strip across the line with a cloth soaked in alcohol. Ants arriving at the strip stop and wander, then pick up the line again on the far side. Which best explains this?

  1. The ants follow the sight of the ants ahead of them, and the alcohol strip hides those ants from view.
  2. The ants follow a chemical trail laid by earlier ants, and wiping removed the signal over that strip.
  3. The alcohol smell is a warning signal that makes the ants turn back toward the nest at the strip.
  4. The ants navigate by the sun, and the wet strip changes how the sunlight reflects off the ground.
Show the answer

Foraging ants lay a trail pheromone, a chemical signal, and nestmates follow it. Wiping erased the chemical over the strip only, so the trail resumes beyond it.

  • The ants follow the sight of the ants ahead of them, and the alcohol strip hides those ants from view.: Ants on either side of the strip are still visible, yet the ants lose the trail only on the wiped part.
  • Correct: The ants follow a chemical trail laid by earlier ants, and wiping removed the signal over that strip.: Correct: a pheromone trail explains why only the wiped strip confuses them.
  • The alcohol smell is a warning signal that makes the ants turn back toward the nest at the strip.: The ants wander and then cross the strip; they do not turn back to the nest.
  • The ants navigate by the sun, and the wet strip changes how the sunlight reflects off the ground.: Ants lose the trail only where the chemical was wiped, which a sun compass does not explain.
