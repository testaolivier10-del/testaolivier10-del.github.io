Experimental design and argumentation

Practice the experiment questions: variables, controls, hypotheses and claims with evidence.

About this tool

Practice the points free-response graders most often see missed: naming the variables, choosing the control that isolates the variable and saying what it rules out, writing null and alternative hypotheses, telling claim from evidence from reasoning, and predicting a result with the mechanism behind it. Each scenario gives real-looking data. The choice drills are checked part by part; the written answers are scored by you against a rubric, with a model answer.

How it works

  • Independent variable: what the investigator changes on purpose. Dependent variable: what is measured in response. Controlled variables: everything kept the same. A confounding variable differs between groups by accident and could explain the result.
  • A control is the group or step that removes one other explanation. Several controls can be sensible; the best one matches the test groups in everything except the factor you need to rule out. Saying what a control rules out earns its own point.
  • Null hypothesis: the independent variable has no effect, so there is no difference in the dependent variable among the groups. Alternative hypothesis: there is an effect (with or without a direction). Data support or fail to support a hypothesis; they never prove it.
  • Claim answers the question. Evidence is data: values, comparisons, error bars, a test statistic. Reasoning is the biology that explains why the evidence supports the claim.
  • Prediction says what will happen; the mechanism says why, in biological terms. Each is scored separately.
  • Write it yourself: write your answer first, then open the rubric, tick each point you earned and compare with the model answer.
