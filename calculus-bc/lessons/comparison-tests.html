Unit 10 · Topic 10.6

Comparison Tests

For a series with positive terms, compare it with a known geometric series or p -series.

Practice 3: JustificationPractice 4: Communication and Notation

Part 1 · Hook

Why this matters

You cannot add infinitely many terms, but you can often tell whether their sum settles by setting the series beside one you already know. If every term of a positive series is smaller than the matching term of a convergent geometric series or pp-series, its partial sums are trapped under a ceiling, and the series converges.

Part 5 · Step by step

How it works, step by step

  1. Every term of ∑an\sum a_n is positive→its partial sums only increase, so they converge exactly when they stay below some ceiling
  2. 0≤an≤bn0 \le a_n \le b_n for every nn and ∑bn\sum b_n converges→each partial sum of ∑an\sum a_n is below the sum of ∑bn\sum b_n, so ∑an\sum a_n converges (direct comparison)
  3. an≥bn≥0a_n \ge b_n \ge 0 and ∑bn\sum b_n diverges→the partial sums of ∑an\sum a_n grow past every bound, so ∑an\sum a_n diverges
  4. limn→∞anbn\lim_{n \to \infty} \frac{a_n}{b_n}=L= L with 0<L<∞0 < L < \infty→for large nn, ana_n is about LbnL\,b_n, so the two series converge together or diverge together (limit comparison)

Part 6 · Key ideas

Key ideas

  • Compare positive terms with a series whose behavior is known and named: geometric with its ratio, or a pp-series with its pp.
  • Direct comparison needs the inequality in the useful direction: smaller than convergent, or larger than divergent. Larger than convergent, or smaller than divergent, proves nothing.
  • Choose bnb_n by keeping the highest powers on top and bottom; use limit comparison when the inequality points the wrong way.
  • Write the limit in nn: limn→∞anbn\lim_{n \to \infty} \frac{a_n}{b_n}, and say it is finite and positive.

Part 7 · Misconception

A common mistake

The wrong idea: 1n2+n≤1n\frac{1}{n^2 + n} \le \frac{1}{n} and the harmonic series diverges, so ∑1n2+n\sum \frac{1}{n^2 + n} diverges.

What actually happens: Being smaller than a divergent series proves nothing. Compare with 1n2\frac{1}{n^2} instead: 1n2+n<1n2\frac{1}{n^2 + n} < \frac{1}{n^2} and p=2>1p = 2 > 1, so the series converges.

3. Why is bn=1n2b_n = \frac{1}{n^2} a good series to compare with an=n2+1n4+3na_n = \frac{n^2 + 1}{n^4 + 3n}?

  1. Keeping the constant terms gives 1n2\frac{1}{n^2} after cancelling
  2. Keeping the highest powers gives n2n4=1n2\frac{n^2}{n^4} = \frac{1}{n^2}
  3. Both terms are equal at n=1n = 1
  4. 1n2\frac{1}{n^2} is smaller than ana_n for large nn, as the test needs
Show the answer

For large nn the numerator behaves like n2n^2 and the denominator like n4n^4, so ana_n behaves like n2n4=1n2\frac{n^2}{n^4} = \frac{1}{n^2}.

  • Keeping the constant terms gives 1n2\frac{1}{n^2} after cancelling: The constants matter least for large nn; the highest powers dominate.
  • Correct: Keeping the highest powers gives n2n4=1n2\frac{n^2}{n^4} = \frac{1}{n^2}: Right: the dominant terms decide how the terms behave for large nn.
  • Both terms are equal at n=1n = 1: At n=1n = 1, a1=24a_1 = \frac{2}{4} and b1=1b_1 = 1: they are not equal, and one value decides nothing.
  • 1n2\frac{1}{n^2} is smaller than ana_n for large nn, as the test needs: A limit comparison needs no inequality; the choice comes from the dominant powers.

4. What does the limit comparison test conclude about ∑n=1∞n2+1n4+3n\sum_{n=1}^{\infty} \frac{n^2 + 1}{n^4 + 3n}?

  1. It diverges: the limit is 1, which is not less than 1 as the test needs
  2. Nothing: the ratio is 0.5 at n=1n = 1, so the two series differ
  3. It converges: the terms approach 0 and the ratios approach 1
  4. It converges, like the pp-series ∑1n2\sum \frac{1}{n^2}
Show the answer

Both series have positive terms and limn→∞anbn\lim_{n \to \infty} \frac{a_n}{b_n}=1= 1, a finite positive number. ∑1n2\sum \frac{1}{n^2} is a pp-series with p=2>1p = 2 > 1, so it converges, and by the limit comparison test ∑an\sum a_n converges.

  • It diverges: the limit is 1, which is not less than 1 as the test needs: The limit comparison test does not compare the limit with 1; any finite positive limit links the two series.
  • Nothing: the ratio is 0.5 at n=1n = 1, so the two series differ: Only the limit as n→∞n \to \infty matters, not early values.
  • It converges: the terms approach 0 and the ratios approach 1: Terms approaching 0 are not enough; the comparison with p=2p = 2 is the reason.
  • Correct: It converges, like the pp-series ∑1n2\sum \frac{1}{n^2}: Right: a finite positive limit means both series do the same thing.

5. A student writes: "1n+1<1n\frac{1}{n+1} < \frac{1}{n} and the harmonic series diverges, so ∑n=1∞1n+1\sum_{n=1}^{\infty} \frac{1}{n+1} diverges by the direct comparison test." What is wrong?

  1. The series converges, because its terms are smaller than those of the harmonic series
  2. The inequality is false: 1n+1>1n\frac{1}{n+1} > \frac{1}{n}, so the comparison is reversed
  3. Nothing: the argument is correct, since both series have positive terms
  4. Smaller than a divergent series proves nothing
Show the answer

To show divergence by direct comparison, the terms must be at least as large as those of a divergent series. Here they are smaller, so no conclusion follows. The limit comparison with 1n\frac{1}{n} works: limn→∞nn+1\lim_{n \to \infty} \frac{n}{n+1}=1= 1, so the series diverges.

  • The series converges, because its terms are smaller than those of the harmonic series: The series does diverge (its limit comparison with 1n\frac{1}{n} gives 1); only the reason is wrong.
  • The inequality is false: 1n+1>1n\frac{1}{n+1} > \frac{1}{n}, so the comparison is reversed: The inequality is true; the problem is which way it points.
  • Nothing: the argument is correct, since both series have positive terms: A divergent series that is larger shows nothing about a smaller one.
  • Correct: Smaller than a divergent series proves nothing: Right: the inequality points the wrong way for divergence.

6. Which argument shows that ∑n=1∞12n−1\sum_{n=1}^{\infty} \frac{1}{2^n - 1} converges?

  1. Limit comparison with 12n\frac{1}{2^n}: the limit is 1 and ∑12n\sum \frac{1}{2^n} converges
  2. Direct comparison with 12n\frac{1}{2^n}, since 12n−1<12n\frac{1}{2^n - 1} < \frac{1}{2^n} for each nn
  3. Direct comparison with 1n\frac{1}{n}, since 12n−1<1n\frac{1}{2^n - 1} < \frac{1}{n}
  4. Limit comparison with 1n\frac{1}{n}: the limit is 0 and ∑1n\sum \frac{1}{n} diverges
Show the answer

The terms 12n−1\frac{1}{2^n - 1} are larger than 12n\frac{1}{2^n}, so a direct comparison goes the wrong way. With limit comparison, limn→∞1/(2n−1)1/2n\lim_{n \to \infty} \frac{1/(2^n - 1)}{1/2^n}=limn→∞2n2n−1= \lim_{n \to \infty} \frac{2^n}{2^n - 1}=1= 1, and ∑12n\sum \frac{1}{2^n} is geometric with r=12r = \frac{1}{2}, so the series converges.

  • Correct: Limit comparison with 12n\frac{1}{2^n}: the limit is 1 and ∑12n\sum \frac{1}{2^n} converges: Right: the limit lim2n2n−1\lim \frac{2^n}{2^n - 1} is 1.
  • Direct comparison with 12n\frac{1}{2^n}, since 12n−1<12n\frac{1}{2^n - 1} < \frac{1}{2^n} for each nn: The inequality is backwards: 2n−1<2n2^n - 1 < 2^n makes 12n−1\frac{1}{2^n - 1} the larger term.
  • Direct comparison with 1n\frac{1}{n}, since 12n−1<1n\frac{1}{2^n - 1} < \frac{1}{n}: Smaller than a divergent series proves nothing.
  • Limit comparison with 1n\frac{1}{n}: the limit is 0 and ∑1n\sum \frac{1}{n} diverges: The limit comparison test needs a finite positive limit; 0 is not one.

Part 9 · Summary

Summary

For a series with positive terms, compare it with a known geometric series or pp-series. Direct comparison: smaller than a convergent series converges, larger than a divergent series diverges. Limit comparison: if limn→∞anbn\lim_{n \to \infty} \frac{a_n}{b_n} is finite and positive, both series do the same thing.
