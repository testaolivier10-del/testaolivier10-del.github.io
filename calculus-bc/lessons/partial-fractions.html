Unit 6 · Topic 6.12

Linear Partial Fractions

When a denominator factors into distinct linear factors, split the fraction into A/(x − a) + B/(x − b) .

Practice 1: Implementing Mathematical ProcessesPractice 2: Connecting Representations

Part 1 · Hook

Why this matters

Adding 2x−1−1x+3\frac{2}{x - 1} - \frac{1}{x + 3} gives x+7(x−1)(x+3)\frac{x + 7}{(x - 1)(x + 3)}, a fraction no basic rule can integrate. Running the addition backward splits it into two pieces that each integrate to a natural log. That backward step is the method of partial fractions.

Part 5 · Step by step

How it works, step by step

  1. The denominator factors into distinct linear factors (x−a)(x−b)(x - a)(x - b)→the fraction can be written as Ax−a+Bx−b\frac{A}{x - a} + \frac{B}{x - b}
  2. Multiply both sides by (x−a)(x−b)(x - a)(x - b)→a polynomial identity that holds for every xx
  3. Substitute x=ax = a, then x=bx = b→one term vanishes each time, giving AA and then BB
  4. Each piece has the form Ax−a\frac{A}{x - a}→it integrates to Aln⁡|x−a|A\ln|x - a|
  5. The top's degree is at least the bottom's→do long division first, then split the remainder

Part 6 · Key ideas

Key ideas

  • Factor the denominator into distinct linear factors; write one fraction Ax−a\frac{A}{x - a} per factor.
  • Clear the denominator and substitute each zero to find the constants. Check by adding the pieces back.
  • ∫Ax−adx\int \frac{A}{x - a}\,dx=Aln⁡|x−a|+C= A\ln|x - a| + C; combine logs only when it helps.
  • Top degree at least the bottom's: divide first. Top equal to the derivative of the bottom: substitute instead.

Part 7 · Misconception

A common mistake

The wrong idea: ∫x+7(x−1)(x+3)dx\int \frac{x + 7}{(x - 1)(x + 3)}\,dx=ln⁡|(x−1)(x+3)|= \ln|(x - 1)(x + 3)|+C+ C: the log of the denominator.

What actually happens: The log of the denominator is right only when the top is the derivative of the bottom (2x+22x + 2 here). Split first: 2x−1−1x+3\frac{2}{x - 1} - \frac{1}{x + 3}, so the integral is 2ln⁡|x−1|−ln⁡|x+3|+C2\ln|x - 1| - \ln|x + 3| + C.

1. Why does step 3 substitute x=1x = 1 and x=−3x = -3?

  1. They are the values where x+7x + 7 equals zero
  2. They are the special inputs where the identity in step 2 holds
  3. They make the original fraction equal to zero
  4. Each value makes one term of A(x+3)+B(x−1)A(x + 3) + B(x - 1) zero, leaving one unknown
Show the answer

The identity x+7x + 7=A(x+3)+B(x−1)= A(x + 3) + B(x - 1) holds for every xx. Choosing the zeros of the factors makes one term disappear each time.

  • They are the values where x+7x + 7 equals zero: x+7=0x + 7 = 0 at x=−7x = -7. The useful values are the zeros of the denominator's factors, which knock out one term each.
  • They are the special inputs where the identity in step 2 holds: Step 2 holds for every xx (it is an identity), which is why any xx may be substituted. These two are just the most convenient.
  • They make the original fraction equal to zero: At x=1x = 1 and x=−3x = -3 the original fraction is undefined. Step 2 has no denominator, so those inputs are fine there.
  • Correct: Each value makes one term of A(x+3)+B(x−1)A(x + 3) + B(x - 1) zero, leaving one unknown: Right: at x=1x = 1 the BB term vanishes (8=4A8 = 4A); at x=−3x = -3 the AA term vanishes (4=−4B4 = -4B).

5. Which integral needs long division before linear partial fractions?

  1. ∫5x2−1dx\int \frac{5}{x^2 - 1}\,dx
  2. ∫2xx2−1dx\int \frac{2x}{x^2 - 1}\,dx
  3. ∫x2+3x2−1dx\int \frac{x^2 + 3}{x^2 - 1}\,dx
  4. ∫3x+1x2−1dx\int \frac{3x + 1}{x^2 - 1}\,dx
Show the answer

Partial fractions need the top's degree below the bottom's. When it is not, long division (6.10) comes first.

  • ∫5x2−1dx\int \frac{5}{x^2 - 1}\,dx: A constant top has degree 0, below the bottom's 2: split directly.
  • ∫2xx2−1dx\int \frac{2x}{x^2 - 1}\,dx: The top is the derivative of the bottom, so substitution gives ln⁡|x2−1|+C\ln|x^2 - 1| + C with no division at all.
  • Correct: ∫x2+3x2−1dx\int \frac{x^2 + 3}{x^2 - 1}\,dx: Right: the top and bottom have the same degree, so divide first: x2+3x2−1\frac{x^2 + 3}{x^2 - 1}=1+4x2−1= 1 + \frac{4}{x^2 - 1}.
  • ∫3x+1x2−1dx\int \frac{3x + 1}{x^2 - 1}\,dx: The top has degree 1, below the bottom's 2, so partial fractions apply at once.

6. ∫1(x−2)(x+2)dx\int \frac{1}{(x - 2)(x + 2)}\,dx==

  1. 14ln⁡|x−2x+2|+C\frac{1}{4}\ln\left|\frac{x - 2}{x + 2}\right| + C
  2. ln⁡|(x−2)(x+2)|+C\ln\left|(x - 2)(x + 2)\right| + C
  3. 14ln⁡|x+2x−2|+C\frac{1}{4}\ln\left|\frac{x + 2}{x - 2}\right| + C
  4. ln⁡|x−2|−ln⁡|x+2|+C\ln|x - 2| - \ln|x + 2| + C
Show the answer

1=A(x+2)+B(x−2)1 = A(x + 2) + B(x - 2): x=2x = 2 gives A=14A = \frac{1}{4}, x=−2x = -2 gives B=−14B = -\frac{1}{4}.

  • Correct: 14ln⁡|x−2x+2|+C\frac{1}{4}\ln\left|\frac{x - 2}{x + 2}\right| + C: Right: 1(x−2)(x+2)\frac{1}{(x - 2)(x + 2)}=1/4x−2−1/4x+2= \frac{1/4}{x - 2} - \frac{1/4}{x + 2}, and a difference of logs is the log of a quotient.
  • ln⁡|(x−2)(x+2)|+C\ln\left|(x - 2)(x + 2)\right| + C: That would need the top to be the derivative of the bottom, 2x2x. The top is 1, so split the fraction.
  • 14ln⁡|x+2x−2|+C\frac{1}{4}\ln\left|\frac{x + 2}{x - 2}\right| + C: The quotient is upside down: A=14A = \frac{1}{4} goes with x−2x - 2, so x−2x - 2 is on top.
  • ln⁡|x−2|−ln⁡|x+2|+C\ln|x - 2| - \ln|x + 2| + C: The pieces carry 14\frac{1}{4}: at x=2x = 2, 1=4A1 = 4A.

Part 9 · Summary

Summary

When a denominator factors into distinct linear factors, split the fraction into Ax−a+Bx−b\frac{A}{x - a} + \frac{B}{x - b}. Clear the denominator, substitute the zeros to find AA and BB, and integrate each piece to a natural log. Divide first if the top's degree is not below the bottom's.
