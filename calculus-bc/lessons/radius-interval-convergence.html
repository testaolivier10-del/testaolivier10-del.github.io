Unit 10 · Topic 10.13

Radius and Interval of Convergence

For a power series centered at a , the ratio test gives |x − a| < R : the radius R and an open interval.

Practice 1: Implementing Mathematical ProcessesPractice 3: JustificationPractice 4: Communication and Notation

Part 1 · Hook

Why this matters

Put an xx into a series, as in ∑(x−1)nn⋅4n\sum \frac{(x - 1)^n}{n \cdot 4^n}, and every value of xx gives a different series of numbers. Some converge and some do not. The values that converge always form one interval around a center, and finding it, endpoints included, is the most tested skill of the unit.

Part 5 · Step by step

How it works, step by step

  1. Apply the ratio test to ∑an(x−a)n\sum a_n(x - a)^n, keeping xx as a letter→the limit is a multiple of |x−a||x - a|
  2. Set that limit below 1 and solve→the radius RR and the open interval (a−R,a+R)(a - R, a + R), where the series converges absolutely
  3. At x=a−Rx = a - R and x=a+Rx = a + R the ratio test limit is exactly 1→substitute each endpoint, name the series you get, and test it with another test
  4. Each endpoint converges or diverges on its own→write the interval with a bracket for an included endpoint and a parenthesis for one left out

Part 6 · Key ideas

Key ideas

  • A power series ∑an(x−a)n\sum a_n (x - a)^n converges only at its center, for every real xx, or on an interval of radius RR around the center.
  • The ratio test gives the radius; it never decides the endpoints.
  • Test each endpoint separately: substitute, simplify, and use the pp-series, alternating series or nth term test.
  • Radius and interval are different answers: R=4R = 4, but the interval is [−3,5)[-3, 5).

Part 7 · Misconception

A common mistake

The wrong idea: The interval of convergence of ∑(x−1)nn⋅4n\sum \frac{(x - 1)^n}{n \cdot 4^n} is (−3,5)(-3, 5), from the ratio test.

What actually happens: The ratio test says nothing at x=−3x = -3 and x=5x = 5. Tested on its own, x=−3x = -3 gives ∑(−1)nn\sum \frac{(-1)^n}{n}, which converges, so the interval is [−3,5)[-3, 5).

1. What is the radius of convergence of the series?

No calculator. Type the exact value (a fraction, π, e or a root is fine).

Show the answer

2. On which open interval does the ratio test show the series converges?

  1. (−1,5)(-1, 5)
  2. (−3,3)(-3, 3)
  3. (−5,1)(-5, 1)
  4. (−73,−53)\left(-\frac{7}{3}, -\frac{5}{3}\right)
Show the answer

|x+2|<3|x + 2| < 3 means −3<x+2<3-3 < x + 2 < 3, so −5<x<1-5 < x < 1. The endpoints still have to be tested.

  • (−1,5)(-1, 5): The center is x=−2x = -2, not 22: x+2=x−(−2)x + 2 = x - (-2).
  • (−3,3)(-3, 3): That is centered at 0; this series is centered at −2-2.
  • Correct: (−5,1)(-5, 1): Right: |x+2|<3|x + 2| < 3 means −5<x<1-5 < x < 1.
  • (−73,−53)\left(-\frac{7}{3}, -\frac{5}{3}\right): That uses 13\frac{1}{3} as the radius; the radius is 3.

3. At the right endpoint, x=1x = 1, what does the series become, and does it converge?

  1. ∑1n\sum \frac{1}{n}, which diverges as the harmonic series
  2. A series whose ratio test limit is 1, so it converges
  3. ∑1\sum 1, which diverges by the nth term test
  4. ∑1n2\sum \frac{1}{n^2}: a convergent pp-series
Show the answer

At x=1x = 1, x+2=3x + 2 = 3 and each term is 3nn23n=1n2\frac{3^n}{n^2\,3^n} = \frac{1}{n^2}. The pp-series with p=2>1p = 2 > 1 converges, so x=1x = 1 is included.

  • ∑1n\sum \frac{1}{n}, which diverges as the harmonic series: The denominator has n2n^2, so the terms are 1n2\frac{1}{n^2}.
  • A series whose ratio test limit is 1, so it converges: A ratio test limit of 1 decides nothing; another test is needed.
  • ∑1\sum 1, which diverges by the nth term test: The 3n3^n cancels but the n2n^2 stays: the terms are 1n2\frac{1}{n^2}.
  • Correct: ∑1n2\sum \frac{1}{n^2}: a convergent pp-series: Right: 3nn23n=1n2\frac{3^n}{n^2\,3^n} = \frac{1}{n^2}.

4. At the left endpoint, x=−5x = -5, what happens?

  1. ∑(−1)nn2\sum \frac{(-1)^n}{n^2}, which converges absolutely
  2. ∑(−1)nn2\sum \frac{(-1)^n}{n^2} diverges, since the signs alternate
  3. ∑(−3)nn2\sum \frac{(-3)^n}{n^2} diverges, since its terms grow
  4. ∑1n2\sum \frac{1}{n^2} converges, the same as at x=1x = 1
Show the answer

At x=−5x = -5, x+2=−3x + 2 = -3 and each term is (−3)nn23n\frac{(-3)^n}{n^2\,3^n}=(−1)nn2= \frac{(-1)^n}{n^2}. Its absolute values form ∑1n2\sum \frac{1}{n^2}, which converges, so the series converges (absolutely) and x=−5x = -5 is included.

  • Correct: ∑(−1)nn2\sum \frac{(-1)^n}{n^2}, which converges absolutely: Right: the absolute values form a convergent pp-series.
  • ∑(−1)nn2\sum \frac{(-1)^n}{n^2} diverges, since the signs alternate: Alternating signs never cause divergence on their own; here the sizes 1n2\frac{1}{n^2} even converge.
  • ∑(−3)nn2\sum \frac{(-3)^n}{n^2} diverges, since its terms grow: The 3n3^n in the denominator cancels: the terms are (−1)nn2\frac{(-1)^n}{n^2}.
  • ∑1n2\sum \frac{1}{n^2} converges, the same as at x=1x = 1: At x=−5x = -5 the signs alternate: (x+2)n=(−3)n(x + 2)^n = (-3)^n.

5. Give the interval of convergence of ∑n=1∞(x+2)nn23n\sum_{n=1}^{\infty} \frac{(x + 2)^n}{n^2\,3^n}, with both endpoints tested.

Type the interval, in interval notation such as [1, 5) or as an inequality such as 1 ≤ x < 5.

Show the answer

The ratio test gives −5<x<1-5 < x < 1. At x=1x = 1 the series is ∑1n2\sum \frac{1}{n^2} (converges); at x=−5x = -5 it is ∑(−1)nn2\sum \frac{(-1)^n}{n^2} (converges absolutely). The interval of convergence is [−5,1][-5, 1].

  • Answer: [−5,1][-5, 1]

6. Give the interval of convergence of ∑n=0∞x2n5n\sum_{n=0}^{\infty} \frac{x^{2n}}{5^n}.

Type the interval, in interval notation such as [1, 5) or as an inequality such as 1 ≤ x < 5.

Show the answer

The series is geometric with ratio x25\frac{x^2}{5}, so it converges when x25<1\frac{x^2}{5} < 1, that is |x|<5|x| < \sqrt{5}. At x=±5x = \pm\sqrt{5} every term is 1, so both endpoints diverge: (−5,5)\left(-\sqrt{5}, \sqrt{5}\right).

  • Answer: (−5,5)(-\sqrt{5}, \sqrt{5})

Part 9 · Summary

Summary

For a power series centered at aa, the ratio test gives |x−a|<R|x - a| < R: the radius RR and an open interval. The ratio test limit is 1 at both endpoints, so test each one separately and include it only if the series converges there.
