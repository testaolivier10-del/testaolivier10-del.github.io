Unit 9 · Topic 9.7

Polar Coordinates and Polar Derivatives

Treat r = f(θ) as the parametric curve x = rcos θ , y = rsin θ .

Practice 1: Implementing Mathematical ProcessesPractice 2: Connecting RepresentationsPractice 3: Justification

Part 1 · Hook

Why this matters

A polar curve is drawn by a beam that turns: θ\theta goes up and the point slides along the curve. So it is a parametric curve, with θ\theta as the parameter, and its slope comes from the rule you already know. The new question polar adds is whether the point is moving toward the pole or away from it, and that lives in drdθ\frac{dr}{d\theta}.

Part 5 · Step by step

How it works, step by step

  1. Write the curve as x=rcos⁡θx = r\cos\theta, y=rsin⁡θy = r\sin\theta with r=f(θ)r = f(\theta)→it is a parametric curve with parameter θ\theta
  2. Differentiate each with the product rule→dxdθ\frac{dx}{d\theta}=drdθcos⁡θ−rsin⁡θ= \frac{dr}{d\theta}\cos\theta - r\sin\theta and dydθ\frac{dy}{d\theta}=drdθsin⁡θ+rcos⁡θ= \frac{dr}{d\theta}\sin\theta + r\cos\theta
  3. Divide, where dxdθ≠0\frac{dx}{d\theta} \ne 0→the slope dydx=dy/dθdx/dθ\frac{dy}{dx} = \frac{dy/d\theta}{dx/d\theta}
  4. Read the signs of rr and drdθ\frac{dr}{d\theta} together→the same sign: moving away from the pole; opposite signs: moving toward it

Part 6 · Key ideas

Key ideas

  • Slope: dydx\frac{dy}{dx}=dr/dθsin⁡θ+rcos⁡θdr/dθcos⁡θ−rsin⁡θ= \frac{dr/d\theta\,\sin\theta + r\cos\theta}{dr/d\theta\,\cos\theta - r\sin\theta}. Horizontal tangent: dydθ=0\frac{dy}{d\theta} = 0 with dxdθ≠0\frac{dx}{d\theta} \ne 0; vertical tangent: dxdθ=0\frac{dx}{d\theta} = 0 with dydθ≠0\frac{dy}{d\theta} \ne 0.
  • drdθ\frac{dr}{d\theta} is not the slope. It is the rate of change of the signed distance from the pole.
  • Distance from the pole is |r||r|. It increases when rr and drdθ\frac{dr}{d\theta} have the same sign and decreases when they have opposite signs.
  • dxdθ\frac{dx}{d\theta} and dydθ\frac{dy}{d\theta} say whether the point moves right or left, up or down. Distance from the yy-axis is |x|=|rcos⁡θ||x| = |r\cos\theta|.

Part 7 · Misconception

A common mistake

The wrong idea: The slope of a polar curve is drdθ\frac{dr}{d\theta}.

What actually happens: drdθ\frac{dr}{d\theta} tells how fast the distance from the pole changes. The slope needs xx and yy: dydx=dy/dθdx/dθ\frac{dy}{dx} = \frac{dy/d\theta}{dx/d\theta}.

1. Find the slope of the tangent line to r=3sin⁡θr = 3\sin\theta at θ=π6\theta = \frac{\pi}{6}.

No calculator. Type the exact value (a fraction, π, e or a root is fine).

Show the answer

r=32r = \frac{3}{2} and drdθ=3cos⁡θ=332\frac{dr}{d\theta} = 3\cos\theta = \frac{3\sqrt{3}}{2}. Then dydθ\frac{dy}{d\theta}=drdθsin⁡θ= \frac{dr}{d\theta}\sin\theta+rcos⁡θ+ r\cos\theta=334= \frac{3\sqrt{3}}{4}+334+ \frac{3\sqrt{3}}{4}=332= \frac{3\sqrt{3}}{2} and dxdθ\frac{dx}{d\theta}=drdθcos⁡θ−rsin⁡θ= \frac{dr}{d\theta}\cos\theta - r\sin\theta=94−34= \frac{9}{4} - \frac{3}{4}=32= \frac{3}{2}, so dydx\frac{dy}{dx}=33/23/2= \frac{3\sqrt{3}/2}{3/2}=3= \sqrt{3}.

  • Answer: 3\sqrt{3} ≈ 1.732

2. At θ=π2\theta = \frac{\pi}{2}, is the point on the curve moving toward the pole or away from it, as θ\theta increases?

  1. Away from the pole: r=4r = 4 is large
  2. Toward the pole: r>0r > 0 and drdθ<0\frac{dr}{d\theta} < 0
  3. Away from the pole: dxdθ<0\frac{dx}{d\theta} < 0
  4. Neither: cos⁡π2=0\cos\frac{\pi}{2} = 0 makes drdθ=0\frac{dr}{d\theta} = 0
Show the answer

The distance from the pole is |r|=r|r| = r here, and drdθ=−2<0\frac{dr}{d\theta} = -2 < 0: the distance is decreasing, so the point moves toward the pole.

  • Away from the pole: r=4r = 4 is large: The size of rr says where the point is; the sign of drdθ\frac{dr}{d\theta}, with the sign of rr, says how the distance changes.
  • Correct: Toward the pole: r>0r > 0 and drdθ<0\frac{dr}{d\theta} < 0: Right: a positive distance that is decreasing.
  • Away from the pole: dxdθ<0\frac{dx}{d\theta} < 0: dxdθ\frac{dx}{d\theta} tracks the horizontal position. The distance from the pole is |r||r|, read from rr and drdθ\frac{dr}{d\theta}.
  • Neither: cos⁡π2=0\cos\frac{\pi}{2} = 0 makes drdθ=0\frac{dr}{d\theta} = 0: drdθ=−2\frac{dr}{d\theta} = -2 is read from the table; it is not multiplied by cos⁡θ\cos\theta.

3. At θ=π\theta = \pi, is the point on the curve moving toward the pole or away from it, as θ\theta increases?

  1. Away: r<0r < 0 and drdθ<0\frac{dr}{d\theta} < 0, so |r||r| grows
  2. Toward: drdθ<0\frac{dr}{d\theta} < 0, so the value of rr is decreasing
  3. Toward: r<0r < 0 puts the point on the opposite ray
  4. Neither: the point is at the pole
Show the answer

The distance from the pole is |r||r|. With r=−1r = -1 and drdθ=−3\frac{dr}{d\theta} = -3, rr is becoming more negative, so |r||r| is increasing: the point moves away from the pole. The rule: |r||r| increases when rr and drdθ\frac{dr}{d\theta} have the same sign.

  • Correct: Away: r<0r < 0 and drdθ<0\frac{dr}{d\theta} < 0, so |r||r| grows: Right: rr and drdθ\frac{dr}{d\theta} have the same sign, so the distance |r||r| increases.
  • Toward: drdθ<0\frac{dr}{d\theta} < 0, so the value of rr is decreasing: A negative drdθ\frac{dr}{d\theta} means toward the pole when r>0r > 0. Here r=−1r = -1, and rr becoming more negative moves it away.
  • Toward: r<0r < 0 puts the point on the opposite ray: A negative rr puts the point on the opposite ray; it says nothing about how the distance is changing.
  • Neither: the point is at the pole: The point is at the pole when r=0r = 0; here r=−1r = -1, one unit away.

4. Find the slope dydx\frac{dy}{dx} of the curve at θ=π2\theta = \frac{\pi}{2}.

No calculator. Type the exact value (a fraction, π, e or a root is fine).

Show the answer

dxdθ\frac{dx}{d\theta}=drdθcos⁡θ−rsin⁡θ= \frac{dr}{d\theta}\cos\theta - r\sin\theta=(−2)(0)−4(1)= (-2)(0) - 4(1)=−4= -4 and dydθ\frac{dy}{d\theta}=drdθsin⁡θ= \frac{dr}{d\theta}\sin\theta+rcos⁡θ+ r\cos\theta=(−2)(1)= (-2)(1)+4(0)+ 4(0)=−2= -2, so dydx=−2−4=12\frac{dy}{dx} = \frac{-2}{-4} = \frac{1}{2}.

  • Answer: 12\frac{1}{2} ≈ 0.500

5. At which points does a polar curve r=f(θ)r = f(\theta) have a horizontal tangent line?

  1. Where dydθ=0\frac{dy}{d\theta} = 0 and dxdθ≠0\frac{dx}{d\theta} \ne 0
  2. Where drdθ=0\frac{dr}{d\theta} = 0
  3. Where dxdθ=0\frac{dx}{d\theta} = 0 and dydθ≠0\frac{dy}{d\theta} \ne 0
  4. Where r=0r = 0
Show the answer

dydx=dy/dθdx/dθ\frac{dy}{dx} = \frac{dy/d\theta}{dx/d\theta} is 0 when the numerator is 0 and the denominator is not.

  • Correct: Where dydθ=0\frac{dy}{d\theta} = 0 and dxdθ≠0\frac{dx}{d\theta} \ne 0: Right: the slope dy/dθdx/dθ\frac{dy/d\theta}{dx/d\theta} is 0 there.
  • Where drdθ=0\frac{dr}{d\theta} = 0: drdθ=0\frac{dr}{d\theta} = 0 is where the distance from the pole stops changing, such as the tip of a petal; the tangent there is perpendicular to the ray, not horizontal in general.
  • Where dxdθ=0\frac{dx}{d\theta} = 0 and dydθ≠0\frac{dy}{d\theta} \ne 0: That makes the slope's denominator 0: a vertical tangent.
  • Where r=0r = 0: r=0r = 0 is the pole. A curve through the pole has tangent along the ray θ\theta there, horizontal only if that ray is horizontal.

6. For a polar curve r=f(θ)r = f(\theta), f(1)=2f(1) = 2 and f′(1)=−3f'(1) = -3. What does f′(1)=−3f'(1) = -3 mean?

  1. At θ=1\theta = 1, the slope of the curve is −3-3
  2. The distance from the pole shrinks at 3 units per radian
  3. At θ=1\theta = 1, the point is moving downward at 3 units per radian
  4. At θ=1\theta = 1, the point is 3 units from the pole
Show the answer

f′(θ)=drdθf'(\theta) = \frac{dr}{d\theta} is the rate of change of rr with respect to θ\theta. With r=2>0r = 2 > 0, a negative rate means the distance from the pole is decreasing, at 3 units per radian.

  • At θ=1\theta = 1, the slope of the curve is −3-3: The slope is dydx\frac{dy}{dx}, which needs both dxdθ\frac{dx}{d\theta} and dydθ\frac{dy}{d\theta}.
  • Correct: The distance from the pole shrinks at 3 units per radian: Right: r=2>0r = 2 > 0 is the distance, and drdθ<0\frac{dr}{d\theta} < 0 means it is shrinking.
  • At θ=1\theta = 1, the point is moving downward at 3 units per radian: Moving down is the sign of dydθ\frac{dy}{d\theta}=f′(1)sin⁡1+f(1)cos⁡1= f'(1)\sin 1 + f(1)\cos 1, not of f′(1)f'(1).
  • At θ=1\theta = 1, the point is 3 units from the pole: The distance is |f(1)|=2|f(1)| = 2; f′(1)f'(1) is its rate of change.

Part 9 · Summary

Summary

Treat r=f(θ)r = f(\theta) as the parametric curve x=rcos⁡θx = r\cos\theta, y=rsin⁡θy = r\sin\theta. The product rule gives dxdθ\frac{dx}{d\theta} and dydθ\frac{dy}{d\theta}; their quotient is the slope. Separately, the signs of rr and drdθ\frac{dr}{d\theta} say whether the point moves toward the pole (opposite signs) or away from it (the same sign).
