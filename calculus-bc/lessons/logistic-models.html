Unit 7 · Topic 7.9

Logistic Models

A logistic model, dP/dt = kP(K − P) , has a rate jointly proportional to the quantity and to the room left.

Practice 2: Connecting RepresentationsPractice 3: Justification

Part 1 · Hook

Why this matters

An exponential model says a population grows faster and faster forever. Real populations run out of food and space: growth is fast at first, then slows, and the population levels off. The logistic model builds that limit into the differential equation, and you can read the limit, and the moment of fastest growth, straight from the equation.

Part 5 · Step by step

How it works, step by step

  1. The growth rate is jointly proportional to PP and to the room left, K−PK - P→dPdt=kP(K−P)\frac{dP}{dt} = kP(K - P), a logistic differential equation
  2. When PP is small, K−P≈KK - P \approx K→dPdt≈kKP\frac{dP}{dt} \approx kKP: growth is nearly exponential
  3. As PP approaches KK, the factor K−PK - P approaches 0→growth slows and PP levels off: limt→∞P(t)=K\lim_{t \to \infty} P(t) = K, the carrying capacity
  4. The rate kP(K−P)kP(K - P) is a downward parabola in PP→it is largest at P=K2P = \frac{K}{2}, where the graph of P(t)P(t) has a point of inflection

Part 6 · Key ideas

Key ideas

  • Logistic: dPdt=kP(K−P)\frac{dP}{dt} = kP(K - P) or dPdt=rP(1−PK)\frac{dP}{dt} = rP\left(1 - \frac{P}{K}\right). The carrying capacity KK is the positive value of PP that makes the rate 0.
  • For 0<P(0)<K0 < P(0) < K, PP increases toward KK; for P(0)>KP(0) > K, PP decreases toward KK. Either way limt→∞P(t)=K\lim_{t \to \infty} P(t) = K.
  • Growth is fastest at P=K2P = \frac{K}{2}: below it the graph of PP is concave up, above it concave down.
  • Read these from the equation; no formula for P(t)P(t) is needed.

Part 7 · Misconception

A common mistake

The wrong idea: For dPdt\frac{dP}{dt}=0.0006P(1200−P)= 0.0006P(1200 - P), the population grows fastest when it is largest, near 1200.

What actually happens: Near 1200 the factor 1200−P1200 - P is almost 0, so growth has nearly stopped. The rate 0.0006P(1200−P)0.0006P(1200 - P) is largest halfway, at P=600P = 600.

5. Which differential equation is a logistic model?

  1. dydt=3y2\frac{dy}{dt} = 3y^2
  2. dydt=3y(10−y)\frac{dy}{dt} = 3y(10 - y)
  3. dydt=3y\frac{dy}{dt} = 3y
  4. dydt=3(10−y)\frac{dy}{dt} = 3(10 - y)
Show the answer

A logistic model has the form dydt=ky(K−y)\frac{dy}{dt} = ky(K - y), or ry(1−yK)ry\left(1 - \frac{y}{K}\right).

  • dydt=3y2\frac{dy}{dt} = 3y^2: The rate grows with yy without limit; nothing slows it down.
  • Correct: dydt=3y(10−y)\frac{dy}{dt} = 3y(10 - y): Right: the rate is jointly proportional to yy and to 10−y10 - y.
  • dydt=3y\frac{dy}{dt} = 3y: The rate is proportional to yy alone: an exponential model, with no carrying capacity.
  • dydt=3(10−y)\frac{dy}{dt} = 3(10 - y): The rate is proportional to 10−y10 - y alone; it is largest at y=0y = 0, not halfway.

Part 9 · Summary

Summary

A logistic model, dPdt=kP(K−P)\frac{dP}{dt} = kP(K - P), has a rate jointly proportional to the quantity and to the room left. Any positive starting value approaches the carrying capacity KK (from below it rises, from above it falls), and growth is fastest at P=K2P = \frac{K}{2}, the point of inflection of P(t)P(t). All of this is read from the equation, without solving it.
