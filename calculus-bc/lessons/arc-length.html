Unit 8 · Topic 8.13

Arc Length and Distance Traveled

The length of the graph of y = f(x) from x = a to x = b is ∫ from a to b of √(1 + (f′(x))²) dx : the limit of sums of chord lengths, each a hypotenuse.

Practice 1: Implementing Mathematical ProcessesPractice 2: Connecting Representations

Part 1 · Hook

Why this matters

Lay a string along a curve, straighten it, and measure it: that is the curve's length. Calculus gets the same number without a string. Approximate the curve by short straight chords, find each chord's length with the Pythagorean theorem, and let the chords shrink. The sum becomes an integral.

Part 5 · Step by step

How it works, step by step

  1. Replace a short piece of the curve by its chord, with run Δx\Delta x and rise Δy\Delta y→the chord's length is (Δx)2+(Δy)2\sqrt{(\Delta x)^2 + (\Delta y)^2}=1+(ΔyΔx)2Δx= \sqrt{1 + \left(\frac{\Delta y}{\Delta x}\right)^2}\,\Delta x
  2. By the Mean Value Theorem, ΔyΔx=f′(c)\frac{\Delta y}{\Delta x} = f'(c) for some cc in the piece→the chord total is a Riemann sum of 1+(f′(x))2\sqrt{1 + (f'(x))^2}
  3. Let the pieces shrink→the sum becomes ∫ab1+(f′(x))2dx\int_a^b \sqrt{1 + (f'(x))^2}\,dx, the arc length
  4. The curve is given as x=g(y)x = g(y)→swap roles: ∫cd1+(g′(y))2dy\int_c^d \sqrt{1 + (g'(y))^2}\,dy with yy-limits

Part 6 · Key ideas

Key ideas

  • Arc length of y=f(x)y = f(x) from x=ax = a to x=bx = b: ∫ab1+(f′(x))2dx\int_a^b \sqrt{1 + (f'(x))^2}\,dx, for f′f' continuous on [a,b][a, b].
  • Square the derivative, not the function; keep the square root and the 1.
  • Few arc length integrals have exact values; most are calculator questions. Write the setup, then the value to three decimal places.
  • From a table of f′f', estimate the length with a Riemann or trapezoidal sum of 1+(f′)2\sqrt{1 + (f')^2}.

Part 7 · Misconception

A common mistake

The wrong idea: The length of y=x3y = x^3 from 0 to 1 is ∫011+x6dx\int_0^1 \sqrt{1 + x^6}\,dx.

What actually happens: x6x^6 squares the function. The formula squares the derivative: y′=3x2y' = 3x^2, so the length is ∫011+9x4dx\int_0^1 \sqrt{1 + 9x^4}\,dx≈1.548\approx 1.548.

Chords along y=x22y = \frac{x^2}{2}

The graph of f(x)=x22f(x) = \frac{x^2}{2} for 0≤x≤20 \le x \le 2 is shown with two chords, from (0,0)(0, 0) to (1,12)\left(1, \frac{1}{2}\right) and from (1,12)\left(1, \frac{1}{2}\right) to (2,2)(2, 2).

1. Find the length of the chord from (0,0)(0, 0) to (1,12)\left(1, \frac{1}{2}\right).

No calculator. Type the exact value (a fraction, π, e or a root is fine).

Show the answer

By the Pythagorean theorem: (Δx)2+(Δy)2\sqrt{(\Delta x)^2 + (\Delta y)^2}=1+14= \sqrt{1 + \frac{1}{4}}=52= \frac{\sqrt{5}}{2}.

  • Answer: 52\frac{\sqrt{5}}{2} ≈ 1.118

2. Find the total length of the two chords.

No calculator. Type the exact value (a fraction, π, e or a root is fine).

Show the answer

1+14\sqrt{1 + \frac{1}{4}}+1+94+ \sqrt{1 + \frac{9}{4}}=52= \frac{\sqrt{5}}{2}+132+ \frac{\sqrt{13}}{2}≈2.921\approx 2.921.

  • Answer: 5+132\frac{\sqrt{5} + \sqrt{13}}{2} ≈ 2.921

3. Which integral gives the exact length of the graph of ff from x=0x = 0 to x=2x = 2?

  1. ∫021+x44dx\int_0^2 \sqrt{1 + \frac{x^4}{4}}\,dx
  2. ∫02(1+x2)dx\int_0^2 \left(1 + x^2\right)dx
  3. ∫021+xdx\int_0^2 \sqrt{1 + x}\,dx
  4. ∫021+x2dx\int_0^2 \sqrt{1 + x^2}\,dx
Show the answer

Arc length =∫ab1+(f′(x))2dx= \int_a^b \sqrt{1 + (f'(x))^2}\,dx with f′(x)=xf'(x) = x.

  • ∫021+x44dx\int_0^2 \sqrt{1 + \frac{x^4}{4}}\,dx: That squares f(x)f(x) itself. The formula uses the derivative: f′(x)=xf'(x) = x.
  • ∫02(1+x2)dx\int_0^2 \left(1 + x^2\right)dx: The square root is missing: the integrand comes from a hypotenuse, 1+(f′)2\sqrt{1 + (f')^2}.
  • ∫021+xdx\int_0^2 \sqrt{1 + x}\,dx: The derivative must be squared: 1+(f′(x))2\sqrt{1 + (f'(x))^2}=1+x2= \sqrt{1 + x^2}.
  • Correct: ∫021+x2dx\int_0^2 \sqrt{1 + x^2}\,dx: Right: f′(x)=xf'(x) = x, so the integrand is 1+(f′(x))2\sqrt{1 + (f'(x))^2}=1+x2= \sqrt{1 + x^2}.

4. How does the two-chord total compare with the length of the graph?

  1. It is less: a chord is the shortest path between its endpoints
  2. It is greater, because ff is concave up
  3. It is equal, because the chords meet the curve at its endpoints
  4. No comparison is possible without evaluating the integral
Show the answer

Each chord is the straight path between two points of the curve, so the chord total, about 2.921, is less than the arc length, about 2.958. More chords raise the total toward it.

  • Correct: It is less: a chord is the shortest path between its endpoints: Right: each piece of the curve is at least as long as its chord, and here strictly longer, since the curve bends.
  • It is greater, because ff is concave up: Concavity decides whether chords lie above or below the curve, not whether they are shorter. A straight segment is never longer than a curved path.
  • It is equal, because the chords meet the curve at its endpoints: Sharing endpoints does not make the lengths equal: the curve bends between them.
  • No comparison is possible without evaluating the integral: The comparison follows from geometry alone: the straight path is the shortest.

No calculator. Type the exact value (a fraction, π, e or a root is fine).

Show the answer

y′=x1/2y' = x^{1/2}, so the length is ∫031+xdx\int_0^3 \sqrt{1 + x}\,dx=23(1+x)3/2|03= \frac{2}{3}(1 + x)^{3/2}\Big|_0^3=23(8−1)= \frac{2}{3}(8 - 1)=143= \frac{14}{3}.

6. A cable hangs between two poles 5 meters apart; h(x)h(x) is its height, in meters, above the point xx meters from the first pole. What does ∫051+(h′(x))2dx\int_0^5 \sqrt{1 + (h'(x))^2}\,dx=6.2= 6.2 mean?

  1. The cable's height changes by 6.2 meters between the poles
  2. The area between the cable and the ground is 6.2 square meters
  3. The average height of the cable is 6.2 meters
  4. The cable is 6.2 meters long between the two poles
Show the answer

∫ab1+(h′(x))2dx\int_a^b \sqrt{1 + (h'(x))^2}\,dx is the length of the graph of hh on [a,b][a, b], here the cable from one pole to the other, in meters.

  • The cable's height changes by 6.2 meters between the poles: A change in height is ∫05h′(x)dx\int_0^5 h'(x)\,dx=h(5)−h(0)= h(5) - h(0), without the root.
  • The area between the cable and the ground is 6.2 square meters: An area would be ∫05h(x)dx\int_0^5 h(x)\,dx, in square meters.
  • The average height of the cable is 6.2 meters: An average height would divide ∫05h(x)dx\int_0^5 h(x)\,dx by 5.
  • Correct: The cable is 6.2 meters long between the two poles: Right: the arc length integral of the graph of hh from x=0x = 0 to x=5x = 5 is the cable's length, in meters.

Part 9 · Summary

Summary

The length of the graph of y=f(x)y = f(x) from x=ax = a to x=bx = b is ∫ab1+(f′(x))2dx\int_a^b \sqrt{1 + (f'(x))^2}\,dx: the limit of sums of chord lengths, each a hypotenuse. Square the derivative, keep the root, use the right limits, and expect to need a calculator for most values.
