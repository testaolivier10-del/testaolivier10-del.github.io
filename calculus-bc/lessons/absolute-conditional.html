Unit 10 · Topic 10.9

Absolute and Conditional Convergence

Every series is absolutely convergent ( ∑ |a_n| converges), conditionally convergent ( ∑ a_n converges but ∑ |a_n| diverges), or divergent.

Practice 3: JustificationPractice 4: Communication and Notation

Part 1 · Hook

Why this matters

The alternating harmonic series 1−12+13−⋯1 - \frac{1}{2} + \frac{1}{3} - \cdots converges, but strip the signs and you get the harmonic series, which diverges. Some series converge because their terms are small; others converge only because their signs keep cancelling. Telling the two apart is what absolute and conditional convergence are about.

Part 5 · Step by step

How it works, step by step

  1. Take the absolute value of every term→∑|an|\sum |a_n| is a series of positive terms, which the comparison, integral and ratio tests can decide
  2. ∑|an|\sum |a_n| converges→∑an\sum a_n converges too (absolutely): signs can only cancel, never add
  3. ∑|an|\sum |a_n| diverges but ∑an\sum a_n converges→the convergence comes from the signs cancelling: conditional convergence
  4. ∑an\sum a_n converges absolutely→its terms can be rearranged in any order without changing the sum

Part 6 · Key ideas

Key ideas

  • Absolutely convergent: ∑|an|\sum |a_n| converges. Conditionally convergent: ∑an\sum a_n converges but ∑|an|\sum |a_n| diverges.
  • Absolute convergence implies convergence.
  • To classify: test ∑|an|\sum |a_n| first. If it converges, you are done (absolute). If not, test ∑an\sum a_n itself, usually with the alternating series test.
  • A divergent ∑|an|\sum |a_n| alone decides nothing about ∑an\sum a_n.

Part 7 · Misconception

A common mistake

The wrong idea: ∑1n\sum \frac{1}{\sqrt{n}} diverges, so ∑(−1)nn\sum \frac{(-1)^n}{\sqrt{n}} diverges too.

What actually happens: A divergent series of absolute values only rules out absolute convergence. The signed series converges by the alternating series test, so it converges conditionally.

1. How does series I, ∑(−1)nn2\sum \frac{(-1)^n}{n^2}, behave?

  1. It converges conditionally: it alternates and 1n2→0\frac{1}{n^2} \to 0
  2. It converges absolutely: ∑1n2\sum \frac{1}{n^2} converges
  3. It diverges: 1n2\frac{1}{n^2} shrinks too slowly
  4. It converges absolutely: its terms alternate in sign
Show the answer

∑|(−1)nn2|=∑1n2\sum \left|\frac{(-1)^n}{n^2}\right| = \sum \frac{1}{n^2} is a pp-series with p=2>1p = 2 > 1, so it converges. The series converges absolutely (and so it converges).

  • It converges conditionally: it alternates and 1n2→0\frac{1}{n^2} \to 0: Conditional means ∑|an|\sum |a_n| diverges, but ∑1n2\sum \frac{1}{n^2} converges.
  • Correct: It converges absolutely: ∑1n2\sum \frac{1}{n^2} converges: Right: the series of absolute values is a pp-series with p=2p = 2.
  • It diverges: 1n2\frac{1}{n^2} shrinks too slowly: 1n2\frac{1}{n^2} shrinks fast enough: p=2>1p = 2 > 1.
  • It converges absolutely: its terms alternate in sign: Alternating signs do not make convergence absolute; the absolute values must form a convergent series.

2. How does series II, ∑(−1)nn\sum \frac{(-1)^n}{\sqrt{n}}, behave?

  1. It converges absolutely
  2. It diverges, since ∑1n\sum \frac{1}{\sqrt{n}} diverges
  3. It converges conditionally
  4. It diverges by the nth term test
Show the answer

The signs alternate and 1n\frac{1}{\sqrt{n}} decreases to 0, so the series converges by the alternating series test. But ∑1n\sum \frac{1}{\sqrt{n}} diverges (p=12≤1p = \frac{1}{2} \le 1). It converges conditionally.

  • It converges absolutely: ∑1n\sum \frac{1}{\sqrt{n}} is a pp-series with p=12p = \frac{1}{2}, which diverges.
  • It diverges, since ∑1n\sum \frac{1}{\sqrt{n}} diverges: The series of absolute values diverging does not make the signed series diverge.
  • Correct: It converges conditionally: Right: it converges by the alternating series test, but ∑1n\sum \frac{1}{\sqrt{n}} diverges.
  • It diverges by the nth term test: 1n→0\frac{1}{\sqrt{n}} \to 0, so the nth term test says nothing.

3. How does series III, ∑(−1)nnn+1\sum (-1)^n \frac{n}{n + 1}, behave?

  1. It converges conditionally, by the alternating series test
  2. It converges absolutely, since nn+1<1\frac{n}{n+1} < 1
  3. It converges conditionally, since ∑nn+1\sum \frac{n}{n+1} diverges
  4. It diverges: its terms do not approach 0
Show the answer

nn+1→1\frac{n}{n + 1} \to 1, so the terms swing near ±1\pm 1 and do not approach 0. The series diverges by the nth term test.

  • It converges conditionally, by the alternating series test: The sizes approach 1, not 0, so that test does not apply.
  • It converges absolutely, since nn+1<1\frac{n}{n+1} < 1: ∑nn+1\sum \frac{n}{n + 1} diverges; each term near 1 adds about 1.
  • It converges conditionally, since ∑nn+1\sum \frac{n}{n+1} diverges: A divergent series of absolute values is half the story; the series itself must converge, and it does not.
  • Correct: It diverges: its terms do not approach 0: Right: the sizes approach 1.

4. How should ∑n=1∞(−1)n+12n+1\sum_{n=1}^{\infty} \frac{(-1)^{n+1}}{2n + 1} be classified?

  1. Conditionally convergent
  2. Absolutely convergent
  3. Divergent
  4. Both absolutely and conditionally convergent
Show the answer

The series converges (alternating series test) and ∑|an|=∑12n+1\sum |a_n| = \sum \frac{1}{2n + 1} diverges (limit comparison with 1n\frac{1}{n}), so it is conditionally convergent.

  • Correct: Conditionally convergent: Right: it converges, but its absolute values do not.
  • Absolutely convergent: ∑12n+1\sum \frac{1}{2n + 1} diverges, so the convergence is not absolute.
  • Divergent: The alternating series test shows it converges.
  • Both absolutely and conditionally convergent: The two labels exclude each other: absolute means ∑|an|\sum |a_n| converges, conditional means it diverges.

5. The signs of sin⁡n\sin n follow no regular pattern. What can you conclude about ∑n=1∞sin⁡nn2\sum_{n=1}^{\infty} \frac{\sin n}{n^2}?

  1. It converges by the alternating series test
  2. Nothing, since the signs are irregular
  3. It diverges, since sin⁡n\sin n does not approach 0
  4. It converges absolutely, since |sin⁡nn2|≤1n2\left|\frac{\sin n}{n^2}\right| \le \frac{1}{n^2}
Show the answer

0≤|sin⁡nn2|≤1n20 \le \left|\frac{\sin n}{n^2}\right| \le \frac{1}{n^2} and ∑1n2\sum \frac{1}{n^2} converges, so ∑|sin⁡nn2|\sum \left|\frac{\sin n}{n^2}\right| converges by direct comparison. The series converges absolutely, so it converges.

  • It converges by the alternating series test: The signs do not alternate in a regular pattern, so the test does not apply.
  • Nothing, since the signs are irregular: Absolute convergence handles any pattern of signs.
  • It diverges, since sin⁡n\sin n does not approach 0: The term is sin⁡nn2\frac{\sin n}{n^2}, which does approach 0.
  • Correct: It converges absolutely, since |sin⁡nn2|≤1n2\left|\frac{\sin n}{n^2}\right| \le \frac{1}{n^2}: Right: compare the absolute values with a convergent pp-series.

6. You know that ∑|an|\sum |a_n| diverges. What can you conclude about ∑an\sum a_n?

  1. It diverges
  2. Nothing yet: it may converge conditionally or diverge
  3. It converges conditionally
  4. It converges absolutely
Show the answer

A divergent ∑|an|\sum |a_n| rules out absolute convergence and nothing more. ∑(−1)nn\sum \frac{(-1)^n}{n} converges and ∑(−1)n\sum (-1)^n diverges, though both have divergent absolute values.

  • It diverges: The alternating harmonic series converges though ∑1n\sum \frac{1}{n} diverges.
  • Correct: Nothing yet: it may converge conditionally or diverge: Right: both happen, so test ∑an\sum a_n itself.
  • It converges conditionally: ∑(−1)n\sum (-1)^n has ∑|an|\sum |a_n| divergent and itself diverges.
  • It converges absolutely: Absolute convergence needs ∑|an|\sum |a_n| to converge.

Part 9 · Summary

Summary

Every series is absolutely convergent (∑|an|\sum |a_n| converges), conditionally convergent (∑an\sum a_n converges but ∑|an|\sum |a_n| diverges), or divergent. Absolute convergence implies convergence, and an absolutely convergent series keeps its sum when its terms are rearranged.
