Unit 9 · Topic 9.2

Second Derivatives of Parametric Equations

The second derivative of a parametric curve is the derivative of dy/dx with respect to x : differentiate dy/dx with respect to t , then divide by dx/dt .

Practice 1: Implementing Mathematical ProcessesPractice 4: Communication and Notation

Part 1 · Hook

Why this matters

A roller coaster designer cares about more than the slope of the track: whether the track bends up into a valley or down over a hill decides whether riders are pressed into their seats or lifted out of them. For a track drawn by parametric equations, that bend is d2ydx2\frac{d^2y}{dx^2}, and finding it takes one extra division that most students forget.

Part 5 · Step by step

How it works, step by step

  1. dydx=dy/dtdx/dt\frac{dy}{dx} = \frac{dy/dt}{dx/dt} is itself a function of tt→its rate of change with respect to xx needs the same slope rule again
  2. Apply the rule to the function dydx\frac{dy}{dx}→d2ydx2\frac{d^2y}{dx^2}=ddt(dydx)dx/dt= \frac{\frac{d}{dt}\left(\frac{dy}{dx}\right)}{dx/dt}
  3. The sign of d2ydx2\frac{d^2y}{dx^2} at a time→gives the concavity of the curve there: positive is concave up, negative concave down
  4. dydx=0\frac{dy}{dx} = 0 and d2ydx2\frac{d^2y}{dx^2} has a sign→the second derivative test classifies the point as a relative minimum or maximum

Part 6 · Key ideas

Key ideas

  • Step 1: find dydx=dy/dtdx/dt\frac{dy}{dx} = \frac{dy/dt}{dx/dt} and simplify it as a function of tt.
  • Step 2: differentiate dydx\frac{dy}{dx} with respect to tt. Step 3: divide by dxdt\frac{dx}{dt}. Skipping step 3 is the classic slip.
  • d2ydx2\frac{d^2y}{dx^2} is not d2y/dt2d2x/dt2\frac{d^2y/dt^2}{d^2x/dt^2}: second derivatives do not divide that way.
  • Concavity, inflection points and the second derivative test work as in Unit 5, with every derivative found from tt.

Part 7 · Misconception

A common mistake

The wrong idea: For x=2tx = 2t, y=t3y = t^3: dydx=3t22\frac{dy}{dx} = \frac{3t^2}{2}, so d2ydx2=3t\frac{d^2y}{dx^2} = 3t.

What actually happens: 3t3t is ddt(dydx)\frac{d}{dt}\left(\frac{dy}{dx}\right), a rate per unit of tt. Divide by dxdt=2\frac{dx}{dt} = 2 to make it a rate per unit of xx: d2ydx2=3t2\frac{d^2y}{dx^2} = \frac{3t}{2}. Check: y=(x2)3=x38y = \left(\frac{x}{2}\right)^3 = \frac{x^3}{8}, whose second derivative is 6x8=3t2\frac{6x}{8} = \frac{3t}{2}.

5. The circle x=cos⁡tx = \cos t, y=sin⁡ty = \sin t has dydx=−cos⁡tsin⁡t\frac{dy}{dx} = -\frac{\cos t}{\sin t}. Find d2ydx2\frac{d^2y}{dx^2} at t=π6t = \frac{\pi}{6}.

Show the answer

ddt(−cot⁡t)\frac{d}{dt}(-\cot t)=csc2⁡t= \csc^2 t=4= 4 at t=π6t = \frac{\pi}{6}, and dxdt=−sin⁡π6=−12\frac{dx}{dt} = -\sin\frac{\pi}{6} = -\frac{1}{2}. So d2ydx2=4−1/2=−8\frac{d^2y}{dx^2} = \frac{4}{-1/2} = -8: concave down, as the top half of a circle should be.

6. For a curve x=x(t)x = x(t), y=y(t)y = y(t) with dxdt≠0\frac{dx}{dt} \ne 0, which expression equals d2ydx2\frac{d^2y}{dx^2}?

  1. d2y/dt2d2x/dt2\frac{d^2y/dt^2}{d^2x/dt^2}
  2. ddt(dydx)\frac{d}{dt}\left(\frac{dy}{dx}\right)
  3. ddt(dydx)dx/dt\frac{\frac{d}{dt}\left(\frac{dy}{dx}\right)}{dx/dt}
  4. dy/dxdx/dt\frac{dy/dx}{dx/dt}
Show the answer

d2ydx2\frac{d^2y}{dx^2} is the derivative of dydx\frac{dy}{dx} with respect to xx. Apply the slope rule to the function dydx\frac{dy}{dx}: its tt-rate divided by the tt-rate of xx.

  • d2y/dt2d2x/dt2\frac{d^2y/dt^2}{d^2x/dt^2}: Second derivatives do not divide like this; differentiate dydx\frac{dy}{dx} with respect to tt first.
  • ddt(dydx)\frac{d}{dt}\left(\frac{dy}{dx}\right): This is a rate per unit of tt; one more division by dxdt\frac{dx}{dt} makes it per unit of xx.
  • Correct: ddt(dydx)dx/dt\frac{\frac{d}{dt}\left(\frac{dy}{dx}\right)}{dx/dt}: Right: ddx(dydx)\frac{d}{dx}\left(\frac{dy}{dx}\right)=ddt(dydx)dx/dt= \frac{\frac{d}{dt}\left(\frac{dy}{dx}\right)}{dx/dt}.
  • dy/dxdx/dt\frac{dy/dx}{dx/dt}: The numerator must be differentiated before dividing.

Part 9 · Summary

Summary

The second derivative of a parametric curve is the derivative of dydx\frac{dy}{dx} with respect to xx: differentiate dydx\frac{dy}{dx} with respect to tt, then divide by dxdt\frac{dx}{dt}. Its sign gives the concavity of the curve, and with dydx=0\frac{dy}{dx} = 0 it classifies a horizontal tangent as a relative minimum or maximum. Never stop after differentiating, and never divide d2ydt2\frac{d^2y}{dt^2} by d2xdt2\frac{d^2x}{dt^2}.
