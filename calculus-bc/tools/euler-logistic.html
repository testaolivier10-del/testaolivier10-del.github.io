Euler and logistic

Step along a slope field, then model logistic growth.

About this tool

Euler's method walks along a slope field: from a known point, follow the tangent segment for one step Δx\Delta x, recompute the slope where you land, and repeat. Drag the starting point, change the step size and watch the Euler polygon pull away from the true solution, above it or below it depending on the concavity. Then switch to logistic growth: a population that levels off at its carrying capacity and grows fastest at half of it.

How it works

Each Euler step uses new yy = old y+dydxΔxy + \frac{dy}{dx}\,\Delta x, with the slope taken at the point you are at, never the one you are heading to. The table under the picture lists xx, yy, dydx\frac{dy}{dx} and Δy\Delta y for every step, as an FRQ answer should. The true solution is drawn dashed from its exact formula, so you can see the error, and the second derivative along it says why the polygon runs low (concave up) or high (concave down).

The logistic model dPdt=kP(1−PL)\frac{dP}{dt} = kP\left(1 - \frac{P}{L}\right) is read without solving it: the rate is 0 at P=0P = 0 and at the carrying capacity LL, every solution with P(0)>0P(0) > 0 approaches LL, and the rate, a parabola in PP, is largest at P=L2P = \frac{L}{2}, where the graph of PP changes concavity. The same equation is often written cP(L−P)cP(L - P) or aP−bP2aP - bP^2; factor it to find LL.

Test yourself has four kinds: Euler steps from an equation in xx and yy, Euler steps in a context, one step then over or under from concavity, and reading a logistic model. Every step is checked; a wrong answer gets the name of the slip it matches (the step size dropped, the old slope reused, one step too many, LL read from the wrong factor).
