Taylor lab

Add terms and watch the polynomial wrap the function.

About this tool

A Taylor polynomial matches a function at one point, its center: the same value, the same slope, the same concavity, and so on up to its degree. Add terms and watch the polynomial wrap around the function farther and farther from the center; drag the center along the curve. The shaded band is an error bound: the Lagrange bound, or the alternating series bound where the series alternates. The function always stays inside it.

How the Taylor lab works

The polynomial is Pn(x)P_n(x)=∑k=0nf(k)(c)k!(x−c)k= \sum_{k=0}^{n} \frac{f^{(k)}(c)}{k!}(x - c)^k, computed from the exact derivatives of the function (for sin⁡x\sin x, cos⁡x\cos x, exe^x, ln⁡(1+x)\ln(1 + x), 11−x\frac{1}{1 - x} and arctan⁡x\arctan x, each has a formula for every order). It is written out term by term in Why: a polynomial ends, so it never has a "+⋯+ \cdots".

The Lagrange band at each xx is M(n+1)!|x−c|n+1\frac{M}{(n+1)!}|x - c|^{n+1}, with MM the largest value of |f(n+1)||f^{(n+1)}| between cc and that xx, found exactly (the end of the interval for exe^x, ln⁡(1+x)\ln(1 + x) and 11−x\frac{1}{1 - x}; 1 once the interval reaches a peak of sin⁡\sin or cos⁡\cos). The alternating band is the size of the first nonzero term left out; it is drawn only where the series at xx alternates and its terms decrease from there on, which is when that bound applies.

Test yourself works the AP® Calculus BC skills: coefficients from a table of derivatives, a polynomial from a known series, derivatives read back from a polynomial, which bound applies and its value, and the wording "the error is at most".
