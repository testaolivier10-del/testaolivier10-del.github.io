Series lab

Choose a test, justify it, and test both endpoints.

About this tool

A series converges when its partial sums settle on a number. Pick a series and add terms one at a time: the dots are the partial sums SnS_n, the bars the terms ana_n, and the dashed line the sum when there is one. Below the picture, the six tests the AP® Calculus BC exam assesses, each marked for this series: which one settles it, and why the others cannot. Switch to Power series to drag xx along a number line and see where the series converges, endpoints included.

How the series lab works

Every partial sum is added up term by term in order, the way the definition says (SnS_n=a1+a2+⋯+an= a_1 + a_2 + \cdots + a_n). A picture of partial sums can suggest convergence but never prove it: ∑1nln⁡n\sum \frac{1}{n \ln n} diverges, yet its partial sums stay under 3 for the first 500 million terms. That is why each series comes with the test that decides it, the condition the test needs, and its conclusion.

For a power series, the ratio test gives the open interval, lim|an+1an|<1\lim \left|\frac{a_{n+1}}{a_n}\right| < 1. At an endpoint that limit is exactly 1, so the ratio test cannot decide: each endpoint is a series of its own, tested with another test. In Test yourself the interval you type is graded in four parts (each endpoint and each bracket), so a missed endpoint is named.

The tests are the six in the course framework: the nth term test (divergence only), the integral test, direct and limit comparison, the alternating series test and the ratio test, with geometric and p-series as the series you compare with.
