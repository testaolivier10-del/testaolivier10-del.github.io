Parametric motion

Trace a particle and read its velocity, speed and distance.

About this tool

A particle traced by x(t)x(t) and y(t)y(t), with its velocity and acceleration vectors drawn as it moves. Press Play or drag the particle along its path. The readouts give the speed, the slope dydx\frac{dy}{dx} and the concavity; the graph under the path is the speed, and the area under it is the distance traveled. With velocity given (9.5), watch the position build from the starting point.

How the motion is computed

  • Velocity is the vector ⟨x′(t),y′(t)⟩\left\langle x'(t), y'(t) \right\rangle, drawn from the particle and always tangent to the path. Acceleration is ⟨x″(t),y″(t)⟩\left\langle x''(t), y''(t) \right\rangle.
  • Speed is the length of the velocity vector, (x′(t))2+(y′(t))2\sqrt{(x'(t))^2 + (y'(t))^2}, never |dydx|\left|\frac{dy}{dx}\right|.
  • Slope: dydx=dy/dtdx/dt\frac{dy}{dx} = \frac{dy/dt}{dx/dt} where dxdt≠0\frac{dx}{dt} \ne 0. Where dxdt=0\frac{dx}{dt} = 0 and dydt≠0\frac{dy}{dt} \ne 0 the tangent is vertical; where both are 0 the particle stops.
  • Concavity: d2ydx2\frac{d^2y}{dx^2}=ddt(dydx)dx/dt= \frac{\frac{d}{dt}\left(\frac{dy}{dx}\right)}{dx/dt}, divided by dxdt\frac{dx}{dt} once more.
  • Distance traveled is ∫ab(x′(t))2+(y′(t))2dt\int_a^b \sqrt{(x'(t))^2 + (y'(t))^2}\,dt, the area under the speed graph, computed numerically (adaptive Simpson). With velocity given, x(t)x(t)=x(a)= x(a)+∫atx′(s)ds+ \int_a^t x'(s)\,ds, and the same for yy.
  • The arrows are scaled to each path's largest velocity and acceleration, so compare lengths along one path, not between paths.
