Polar lab

Sweep theta and watch the curve and its area build.

About this tool

A polar curve r=f(θ)r = f(\theta) drawn as θ\theta sweeps, with the area swept so far shaded and the setup 12∫r2dθ\frac{1}{2}\int r^2\,d\theta built beside it. Press Play or drag around the curve. The graph of rr against θ\theta shows when the point moves toward or away from the origin; with two curves, the region between them is swept the same way.

How the curves and areas are computed

  • Points: x=rcos⁡θx = r\cos\theta and y=rsin⁡θy = r\sin\theta. Where r<0r < 0 the point is plotted on the opposite ray, at distance |r||r| from the origin.
  • Toward or away: the distance from the origin is |r||r|, so the point moves away when rr and drdθ\frac{dr}{d\theta} have the same sign and toward when their signs differ.
  • Slope: dydx=dy/dθdx/dθ\frac{dy}{dx} = \frac{dy/d\theta}{dx/d\theta} with dydθ\frac{dy}{d\theta}=drdθsin⁡θ+rcos⁡θ= \frac{dr}{d\theta}\sin\theta + r\cos\theta and dxdθ\frac{dx}{d\theta}=drdθcos⁡θ−rsin⁡θ= \frac{dr}{d\theta}\cos\theta - r\sin\theta (the product rule on each).
  • Area: a thin slice is a sector of area 12r2Δθ\frac{1}{2}r^2\,\Delta\theta, so the area swept from α\alpha to β\beta is 12∫αβr2dθ\frac{1}{2}\int_\alpha^\beta r^2\,d\theta. Between two curves, 12∫αβ(R2−r2)dθ\frac{1}{2}\int_\alpha^\beta (R^2 - r^2)\,d\theta, outer RR minus inner rr, with α\alpha and β\beta where they meet. Values are integrated numerically (adaptive Simpson).
  • A sweep that traces part of the curve twice counts that area twice: one petal or loop runs between consecutive zeros of rr.
