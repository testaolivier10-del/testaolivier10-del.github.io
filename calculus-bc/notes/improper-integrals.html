Every definite integral so far had finite limits and a continuous integrand on a closed interval. An improper integral breaks one of those rules: a limit of integration is infinite, as in ∫1∞1x2dx\int_1^\infty \frac{1}{x^2}\,dx, or the integrand is unbounded on the interval, as in ∫011xdx\int_0^1 \frac{1}{\sqrt{x}}\,dx. Calculus handles both the same way: integrate on a safe interval, then take a limit.

Infinite limits of integration

∫a∞f(x)dx=limb→∞∫abf(x)dx.\int_a^\infty f(x)\,dx = \lim_{b \to \infty} \int_a^b f(x)\,dx.

If the limit is a finite number, the improper integral converges to that number. If the limit is infinite or does not exist, the integral diverges. The same idea works at the other end: ∫−∞bf(x)dx\int_{-\infty}^{b} f(x)\,dx=lima→−∞∫abf(x)dx= \lim_{a \to -\infty} \int_a^b f(x)\,dx.

Worked example 1. Evaluate ∫1∞1x2dx\int_1^\infty \frac{1}{x^2}\,dx or show that it diverges.

∫1∞1x2dx\int_1^\infty \frac{1}{x^2}\,dx=limb→∞∫1bx−2dx= \lim_{b \to \infty} \int_1^b x^{-2}\,dx=limb→∞(−1x)|1b= \lim_{b \to \infty}\left(-\frac{1}{x}\right)\Big|_1^b=limb→∞(1−1b)= \lim_{b \to \infty}\left(1 - \frac{1}{b}\right)=1.= 1.

The limit is a finite number, so the integral converges to 1.

Worked example 2. Evaluate ∫1∞1xdx\int_1^\infty \frac{1}{x}\,dx or show that it diverges.

limb→∞∫1b1xdx\lim_{b \to \infty} \int_1^b \frac{1}{x}\,dx=limb→∞ln⁡b= \lim_{b \to \infty} \ln b=∞= \infty, so the integral diverges.

The figure puts the two side by side. Both graphs approach 0, yet one area levels off and the other grows without bound. A height that goes to 0 is not enough to make the area finite: only the limit of the integrals decides.

Integrands that blow up

If ff is unbounded at an endpoint, put the limit at that endpoint. For an upper endpoint cc: ∫acf(x)dx\int_a^c f(x)\,dx=limb→c−∫abf(x)dx= \lim_{b \to c^-} \int_a^b f(x)\,dx.

Worked example 3. Evaluate ∫0313−xdx\int_0^3 \frac{1}{\sqrt{3 - x}}\,dx.

The integrand is unbounded as x→3−x \to 3^-. With ∫(3−x)−1/2dx\int (3 - x)^{-1/2}\,dx=−23−x= -2\sqrt{3 - x}:

limb→3−(−23−x)|0b\lim_{b \to 3^-}\left(-2\sqrt{3 - x}\right)\Big|_0^b=limb→3−(−23−b+23)= \lim_{b \to 3^-}\left(-2\sqrt{3 - b} + 2\sqrt{3}\right)=23.= 2\sqrt{3}. The integral converges.

The dangerous case is a blow-up inside the interval. ∫−111x2dx\int_{-1}^1 \frac{1}{x^2}\,dx looks like −1x|−11=−2-\frac{1}{x}\Big|_{-1}^1 = -2, a negative answer for a positive integrand. The Fundamental Theorem does not apply, because 1x2\frac{1}{x^2} is not continuous at 0. Look at the piece from 0 to 1: ∫011x2dx\int_0^1 \frac{1}{x^2}\,dx=lima→0+(1a−1)= \lim_{a \to 0^+}\left(\frac{1}{a} - 1\right)=∞= \infty, so the integral diverges. Before integrating, always check the integrand for vertical asymptotes on the interval.

Using parts and L'Hospital's rule

Worked example 4. Evaluate ∫0∞xe−xdx\int_0^\infty xe^{-x}\,dx.

Parts with u=xu = x, dv=e−xdxdv = e^{-x}\,dx: ∫xe−xdx\int xe^{-x}\,dx=−xe−x−e−x= -xe^{-x} - e^{-x}+C+ C.

limb→∞(−xe−x−e−x)|0b\lim_{b \to \infty}\left(-xe^{-x} - e^{-x}\right)\Big|_0^b=limb→∞(−beb−1eb)−(0−1).= \lim_{b \to \infty}\left(-\frac{b}{e^b} - \frac{1}{e^b}\right) - (0 - 1).

beb\frac{b}{e^b} has the form ∞∞\frac{\infty}{\infty}, so by L'Hospital's rule limb→∞beb\lim_{b \to \infty}\frac{b}{e^b}=limb→∞1eb= \lim_{b \to \infty}\frac{1}{e^b}=0= 0. The integral converges to 0−0+1=10 - 0 + 1 = 1.

Writing it so it earns the point

  • Write limb→∞\lim_{b \to \infty} in front of every expression until the step where you take the limit. Dropping it in the middle makes a false statement such as ∫1bdxx2\int_1^b \frac{dx}{x^2}=1= 1.
  • Never substitute ∞\infty: −1∞-\frac{1}{\infty} and e−∞e^{-\infty} are not numbers. Recent scoring guidelines give the point only when the notation is right all the way.
  • State the conclusion in words: "the integral converges to 1" or "the integral diverges".
