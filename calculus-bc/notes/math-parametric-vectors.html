Unit 9 follows points that move in the plane. To describe them you need two tools from precalculus: parametric equations, which give the position at each time, and vectors, which describe a change in position. This page rebuilds both, one step at a time.

A point for each value of t

A graph y=f(x)y = f(x) gives one yy for each xx. Parametric equations instead give both coordinates from a third variable, the parameter tt:

x=x(t),y=y(t).x = x(t), \qquad y = y(t).

Think of tt as time. At each time the point is at (x(t),y(t))(x(t), y(t)), and as tt increases the point moves, tracing a path. The order of the points matters, so a parametric path has a direction (its orientation). Draw it with arrows.

Worked example 1. Sketch the path x=t2−2tx = t^2 - 2t, y=3−ty = 3 - t for −1≤t≤3-1 \le t \le 3.

Eliminating the parameter

To see the shape of a path, remove tt and get one equation in xx and yy. Two methods cover almost every case.

Solve and substitute. When one equation is linear in tt, solve it for tt and put the result into the other. In worked example 1, y=3−ty = 3 - t gives t=3−yt = 3 - y, so

x=(3−y)2−2(3−y)=y2−4y+3,x = (3 - y)^2 - 2(3 - y) = y^2 - 4y + 3,

a parabola that opens to the right. Check a point: y=2y = 2 gives x=4−8+3=−1x = 4 - 8 + 3 = -1, as in the table at t=1t = 1.

Use an identity. When the equations hold cos⁡t\cos t and sin⁡t\sin t, square and add: cos2⁡t+sin2⁡t=1\cos^2 t + \sin^2 t = 1 removes tt.

Worked example 2. Describe x=3cos⁡tx = 3\cos t, y=3sin⁡ty = 3\sin t for 0≤t≤2π0 \le t \le 2\pi.

x2x^2+y2+ y^2=9cos2⁡t= 9\cos^2 t+9sin2⁡t+ 9\sin^2 t=9(cos2⁡t+sin2⁡t)= 9(\cos^2 t + \sin^2 t)=9= 9: a circle of radius 3 centered at the origin.

Direction: t=0t = 0 gives (3,0)(3, 0) and t=π2t = \frac{\pi}{2} gives (0,3)(0, 3). From the right side to the top is counterclockwise, and tt from 0 to 2π2\pi is one full turn.

Elimination keeps the shape and throws away the rest. x=3sin⁡tx = 3\sin t, y=3cos⁡ty = 3\cos t satisfies the same equation x2+y2=9x^2 + y^2 = 9, yet it starts at the top, (0,3)(0, 3), and goes clockwise. And on 0≤t≤4π0 \le t \le 4\pi either path goes around twice. Always find the start and the direction from the parametric equations themselves.

Vectors: a change in position

A vector has a size and a direction. In the plane it is written by its components,

⟨a,b⟩:a across (right if positive),b up (if positive).\left\langle a, b \right\rangle: \quad a \text{ across (right if positive)}, \; b \text{ up (if positive)}.

The vector from P(x1,y1)P(x_1, y_1) to Q(x2,y2)Q(x_2, y_2) is end minus start: ⟨x2−x1,y2−y1⟩\left\langle x_2 - x_1, y_2 - y_1 \right\rangle. The vector from the origin to a point is that point's position; the position of Q(4,5)Q(4, 5) is ⟨4,5⟩\left\langle 4, 5 \right\rangle.

The magnitude (length) of ⟨a,b⟩\left\langle a, b \right\rangle is the hypotenuse of a right triangle with legs |a||a| and |b||b|:

‖⟨a,b⟩‖=a2+b2.\left\| \left\langle a, b \right\rangle \right\| = \sqrt{a^2 + b^2}.

Add vectors and multiply them by numbers one component at a time:

⟨1,−3⟩+⟨4,5⟩=⟨5,2⟩,2⟨1,−3⟩=⟨2,−6⟩.\left\langle 1, -3 \right\rangle + \left\langle 4, 5 \right\rangle = \left\langle 5, 2 \right\rangle, \qquad 2\left\langle 1, -3 \right\rangle = \left\langle 2, -6 \right\rangle.

Worked example 3. A drone flies from P(1,1)P(1, 1) to Q(4,5)Q(4, 5), in meters. Find the vector of the trip and its length.

End minus start: ⟨4−1,5−1⟩=⟨3,4⟩\left\langle 4 - 1, 5 - 1 \right\rangle = \left\langle 3, 4 \right\rangle, so 3 meters east and 4 meters north.

Length: 32+42=25=5\sqrt{3^2 + 4^2} = \sqrt{25} = 5 meters. Not 3+4=73 + 4 = 7: the drone flies the diagonal, not the two legs.
