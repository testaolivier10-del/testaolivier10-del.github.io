SMath you need

Every later test in Unit 10 takes some work. The nth term test takes almost none, so it is the first thing to check. It looks only at the terms ana_n and asks one question: do they approach 0?

The test

nth term test for divergence. If limn→∞an≠0\lim_{n \to \infty} a_n \ne 0, or the limit does not exist, then ∑an\sum a_n diverges.

Why it works: if a series converges to SS, then its partial sums SnS_n and Sn−1S_{n-1} both approach SS, so

an=Sn−Sn−1→S−S=0.a_n = S_n - S_{n-1} \to S - S = 0.

So every convergent series has terms that approach 0. A series whose terms do not approach 0 cannot converge.

The left panel shows why: when the terms level off at 12\frac{1}{2}, each new term adds about 12\frac{1}{2}, and the partial sums climb without bound.

What it cannot do

The test only ever proves divergence. If liman=0\lim a_n = 0, it is inconclusive: the series might converge or diverge. The right panel shows terms 1n\frac{1}{n} that approach 0 while the partial sums keep growing. In 10.1 you saw another: ∑ln⁡(n+1n)\sum \ln\left(\frac{n + 1}{n}\right) has terms approaching 0 and partial sums ln⁡(n+1)\ln(n + 1) that grow without bound. On the other hand ∑(12)n\sum \left(\frac{1}{2}\right)^n also has terms approaching 0, and it converges.

limn→∞an\lim_{n \to \infty} a_nWhat the nth term test says
a number other than 0the series diverges
does not exist (including ±∞\pm\infty)the series diverges
00nothing: use another test

Using it

Worked example 1. Does ∑n=1∞3n2−15n2+n\sum_{n=1}^{\infty} \frac{3n^2 - 1}{5n^2 + n} converge?

Limit of the terms: divide the top and bottom by n2n^2: 3−1/n25+1/n→35\frac{3 - 1/n^2}{5 + 1/n} \to \frac{3}{5}.

Conclusion: limn→∞3n2−15n2+n\lim_{n \to \infty} \frac{3n^2 - 1}{5n^2 + n}=35≠0= \frac{3}{5} \ne 0, so the series diverges by the nth term test.

Worked example 2. What does the nth term test say about ∑n=1∞(−1)n2nn+3\sum_{n=1}^{\infty} (-1)^n \frac{2n}{n + 3} and ∑n=1∞ln⁡nn\sum_{n=1}^{\infty} \frac{\ln n}{n}?

First: the sizes 2nn+3\frac{2n}{n + 3} approach 2, so the terms jump between values near 2 and near −2-2. The limit does not exist, so it is not 0: the series diverges by the nth term test.

Second: ln⁡xx\frac{\ln x}{x} is an ∞∞\frac{\infty}{\infty} form, and L'Hospital's rule gives 1/x1→0\frac{1/x}{1} \to 0. The terms approach 0, so the nth term test says nothing. (Topic 10.4 settles it.)

Saying it right

A justification names the limit, compares it with 0, names the test and concludes about the series:

"limn→∞3n2−15n2+n\lim_{n \to \infty} \frac{3n^2 - 1}{5n^2 + n}=35≠0= \frac{3}{5} \ne 0, so ∑3n2−15n2+n\sum \frac{3n^2 - 1}{5n^2 + n} diverges by the nth term test."

Three phrases cost points: "the terms diverge" when they approach 35\frac{3}{5}; "the series converges to 35\frac{3}{5}", which mixes up the terms with the sum; and "the terms go to 0, so the series converges", which uses the test backward.

Limits in this unit are in nn: write limn→∞an\lim_{n \to \infty} a_n. To use L'Hospital's rule or a derivative, switch to a function f(x)f(x) with f(n)=anf(n) = a_n, find limx→∞f(x)\lim_{x \to \infty} f(x), and then state the result for nn.
