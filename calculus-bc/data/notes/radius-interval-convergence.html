Every series so far was a list of numbers. A power series has an $x$ in it, $\sum_{n=0}^{\infty} a_n (x - a)^n$, so it is a whole family of series, one for each value of $x$. For some values the series converges, for others it diverges. The set where it converges is the interval of convergence, and finding it is asked on the free-response section almost every year.

What a power series is

A power series centered at $x = a$ is

$$\sum_{n=0}^{\infty} a_n (x - a)^n = a_0 + a_1(x - a) + a_2(x - a)^2 + \cdots$$

The numbers $a_n$ are its coefficients. Read the center from the powers: $(x + 2)^n = (x - (-2))^n$ is centered at $-2$, and $(2x - 1)^n = 2^n\left(x - \frac{1}{2}\right)^n$ is centered at $\frac{1}{2}$. At the center every term after the first is 0, so a power series always converges there.

Beyond the center there are exactly three possibilities:

  • it converges only at $x = a$ (radius $0$);
  • it converges for every real $x$ (infinite radius);
  • there is a radius $R > 0$: it converges when $|x - a| < R$ and diverges when $|x - a| > R$. At the two endpoints $x = a \pm R$ anything can happen.

Step 1: the radius from the ratio test

Apply the ratio test to the terms, treating $x$ as a fixed number. Keep the absolute value bars: they turn the limit into a distance from the center.

Worked example. Find the interval of convergence of $\sum_{n=1}^{\infty} \frac{(x - 1)^n}{n \cdot 4^n}$.

Ratio: $\left|\frac{a_{n+1}}{a_n}\right| = \frac{|x - 1|^{n+1}}{(n + 1)4^{n+1}} \cdot \frac{n \cdot 4^n}{|x - 1|^n} = \frac{|x - 1|}{4} \cdot \frac{n}{n + 1}$.

Limit: $\lim_{n \to \infty} \frac{|x - 1|}{4} \cdot \frac{n}{n + 1} = \frac{|x - 1|}{4}$.

Converges when $\frac{|x - 1|}{4} < 1$, that is $|x - 1| < 4$. Radius $R = 4$; open interval $-3 < x < 5$.

Step 2, right endpoint. At $x = 5$: $\frac{4^n}{n \cdot 4^n} = \frac{1}{n}$. The harmonic series diverges, so $x = 5$ is left out.

Step 2, left endpoint. At $x = -3$: $\frac{(-4)^n}{n \cdot 4^n} = \frac{(-1)^n}{n}$. It alternates and $\frac{1}{n}$ decreases to 0, so it converges by the alternating series test: $x = -3$ is included.

Answer. The interval of convergence is $[-3, 5)$.

Inside the open interval the series converges absolutely, which the ratio test shows. At an endpoint it may converge absolutely (like $\sum \frac{(-1)^n}{n^2}$), conditionally (like $\sum \frac{(-1)^n}{n}$), or diverge.

Radius traps

  • A coefficient on $x$: $|2x - 1| < 1$ means $\left|x - \frac{1}{2}\right| < \frac{1}{2}$. Factor it out before reading the radius.
  • Even powers: for $\sum \frac{x^{2n}}{5^n}$ the condition is $x^2 < 5$, so $R = \sqrt{5}$, not 5.
  • Factorials: $\sum \frac{x^n}{n!}$ has ratio limit $\frac{|x|}{n + 1} \to 0$ for every $x$, so it converges everywhere; $\sum n!\,x^n$ has ratio limit $(n + 1)|x| \to \infty$ unless $x = 0$, so it converges only at its center.

Geometric power series

When the power series is geometric, its sum has a formula. $\sum_{n=0}^{\infty} \left(\frac{x - 2}{3}\right)^n$ has ratio $\frac{x - 2}{3}$, so for $|x - 2| < 3$ its sum is $\frac{1}{1 - \frac{x - 2}{3}} = \frac{3}{5 - x}$. At both endpoints every term has size 1, so both diverge, and the interval is $(-1, 5)$. Inside its interval, a power series is a function of $x$; the next topics build power series for familiar functions.

Writing it the way graders want

Show the ratio with absolute values and $a_{n+1}$ on top, take the limit in $n$, solve the inequality, then write a sentence for each endpoint that names the series and the test. Give the interval with brackets and parentheses. If the question asks for the radius, give a number; if it asks for the interval, give an interval.
