Substitution undoes the chain rule. Integration by parts undoes the product rule, and it handles integrals that substitution cannot: $\int x\cos x\,dx$, $\int xe^{3x}\,dx$, $\int \ln x\,dx$. It is a BC-only topic, and it often appears on the free-response section as one part of a question that mixes techniques.

The product rule, run backward

For differentiable functions $u$ and $v$ of $x$, the product rule says $\frac{d}{dx}(uv) = u\frac{dv}{dx} + v\frac{du}{dx}$. Integrate both sides:

$$uv = \int u\,dv + \int v\,du,$$

so

$$\int u\,dv = uv - \int v\,du.$$

The formula trades one integral, $\int u\,dv$, for another, $\int v\,du$. It helps only when the new integral is easier. The figure shows the trade as two areas that together fill a rectangle of area $uv$.

Choosing u and dv

Split the integrand into $u$ (to be differentiated) and $dv$ (to be integrated, and it must include $dx$). Two tests decide the split: $u$ should get simpler when you differentiate it, and you must be able to integrate $dv$.

A useful rule of thumb: logarithms and inverse trigonometric functions go in $u$; exponentials, sines and cosines go in $dv$; a power of $x$ goes in $u$ unless a logarithm or an inverse trigonometric function is present.

Worked example 1. Find $\int x\cos x\,dx$.

Label the parts: $u = x$, $dv = \cos x\,dx$, so $du = dx$ and $v = \sin x$.

Apply the formula: $\int x\cos x\,dx = x\sin x - \int \sin x\,dx = x\sin x + \cos x + C$.

Check: $\frac{d}{dx}(x\sin x + \cos x) = \sin x + x\cos x - \sin x = x\cos x$.

Writing $u$, $dv$, $du$ and $v$ before applying the formula is the habit the scoring guidelines reward. Most lost points come from a wrong $v$ (a sign slip on $\int \sin x\,dx$) or from multiplying two antiderivatives, which is never right.

Definite integrals: uv has bounds too

$$\int_a^b u\,dv = uv\Big|_a^b - \int_a^b v\,du.$$

Worked example 2. Evaluate $\int_1^e x\ln x\,dx$.

$u = \ln x$, $dv = x\,dx$, so $du = \frac{1}{x}\,dx$ and $v = \frac{x^2}{2}$.

$\int_1^e x\ln x\,dx = \frac{x^2}{2}\ln x\Big|_1^e - \int_1^e \frac{x^2}{2}\cdot\frac{1}{x}\,dx = \frac{e^2}{2} - \frac{x^2}{4}\Big|_1^e = \frac{e^2}{2} - \frac{e^2}{4} + \frac{1}{4} = \frac{e^2 + 1}{4}.$

Writing $x\ln x - \int_1^e \ldots$ with no bounds on the first term mixes a function with a number; the answer must be a number.

Repeated parts and the tabular method

For $x^2e^{2x}$, one round of parts leaves $\int xe^{2x}\,dx$, which needs a second round. The tabular method does every round at once. List $u$ and its derivatives down to 0 in one column, $dv$ and its repeated antiderivatives in the other, and multiply along the diagonals with signs $+, -, +, \ldots$

Worked example 3. Find $\int x^2e^{2x}\,dx$.

Left column: $x^2$, $2x$, $2$, $0$. Right column: $e^{2x}$, $\frac{1}{2}e^{2x}$, $\frac{1}{4}e^{2x}$, $\frac{1}{8}e^{2x}$.

Diagonals: $+x^2\cdot\frac{1}{2}e^{2x} - 2x\cdot\frac{1}{4}e^{2x} + 2\cdot\frac{1}{8}e^{2x}$, so

$$\int x^2e^{2x}\,dx = e^{2x}\left(\frac{x^2}{2} - \frac{x}{2} + \frac{1}{4}\right) + C.$$

The table works only when the $u$ column reaches 0, so it suits a power of $x$ times $e^{kx}$, $\sin kx$ or $\cos kx$.

When the original integral comes back

For $\int e^x\sin x\,dx$, two rounds of parts give $\int e^x\sin x\,dx = e^x\sin x - e^x\cos x - \int e^x\sin x\,dx$. Do not go around a third time. Treat the integral as an unknown $I$: $2I = e^x(\sin x - \cos x)$, so $I = \frac{1}{2}e^x(\sin x - \cos x) + C$.

Parts with functions given by tables

Parts also works when $f$ is known only through values. With $u = x$ and $dv = f'(x)\,dx$, $\int_a^b xf'(x)\,dx = xf(x)\Big|_a^b - \int_a^b f(x)\,dx$. You need $f(a)$, $f(b)$ and the value of $\int_a^b f(x)\,dx$, which a question gives or lets you find.
