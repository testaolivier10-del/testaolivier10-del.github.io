Every later test in Unit 10 takes some work. The nth term test takes almost none, so it is the first thing to check. It looks only at the terms $a_n$ and asks one question: do they approach 0?

The test

nth term test for divergence. If $\lim_{n \to \infty} a_n \ne 0$, or the limit does not exist, then $\sum a_n$ diverges.

Why it works: if a series converges to $S$, then its partial sums $S_n$ and $S_{n-1}$ both approach $S$, so

$$a_n = S_n - S_{n-1} \to S - S = 0.$$

So every convergent series has terms that approach 0. A series whose terms do not approach 0 cannot converge.

The left panel shows why: when the terms level off at $\frac{1}{2}$, each new term adds about $\frac{1}{2}$, and the partial sums climb without bound.

What it cannot do

The test only ever proves divergence. If $\lim a_n = 0$, it is inconclusive: the series might converge or diverge. The right panel shows terms $\frac{1}{n}$ that approach 0 while the partial sums keep growing. In 10.1 you saw another: $\sum \ln\left(\frac{n + 1}{n}\right)$ has terms approaching 0 and partial sums $\ln(n + 1)$ that grow without bound. On the other hand $\sum \left(\frac{1}{2}\right)^n$ also has terms approaching 0, and it converges.

Using it

Worked example 1. Does $\sum_{n=1}^{\infty} \frac{3n^2 - 1}{5n^2 + n}$ converge?

Limit of the terms: divide the top and bottom by $n^2$: $\frac{3 - 1/n^2}{5 + 1/n} \to \frac{3}{5}$.

Conclusion: $\lim_{n \to \infty} \frac{3n^2 - 1}{5n^2 + n} = \frac{3}{5} \ne 0$, so the series diverges by the nth term test.

Worked example 2. What does the nth term test say about $\sum_{n=1}^{\infty} (-1)^n \frac{2n}{n + 3}$ and $\sum_{n=1}^{\infty} \frac{\ln n}{n}$?

First: the sizes $\frac{2n}{n + 3}$ approach 2, so the terms jump between values near 2 and near $-2$. The limit does not exist, so it is not 0: the series diverges by the nth term test.

Second: $\frac{\ln x}{x}$ is an $\frac{\infty}{\infty}$ form, and L'Hospital's rule gives $\frac{1/x}{1} \to 0$. The terms approach 0, so the nth term test says nothing. (Topic 10.4 settles it.)

Saying it right

A justification names the limit, compares it with 0, names the test and concludes about the series:

"$\lim_{n \to \infty} \frac{3n^2 - 1}{5n^2 + n} = \frac{3}{5} \ne 0$, so $\sum \frac{3n^2 - 1}{5n^2 + n}$ diverges by the nth term test."

Three phrases cost points: "the terms diverge" when they approach $\frac{3}{5}$; "the series converges to $\frac{3}{5}$", which mixes up the terms with the sum; and "the terms go to 0, so the series converges", which uses the test backward.

Limits in this unit are in $n$: write $\lim_{n \to \infty} a_n$. To use L'Hospital's rule or a derivative, switch to a function $f(x)$ with $f(n) = a_n$, find $\lim_{x \to \infty} f(x)$, and then state the result for $n$.
