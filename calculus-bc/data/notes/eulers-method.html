A slope field shows the direction a solution curve takes at every point, but not a formula for the curve. Euler's method turns the slope field into numbers: it estimates the value of a solution by walking along short tangent segments, reading a new slope from the differential equation at the start of each one. It works for any differential equation $\frac{dy}{dx} = F(x, y)$, including ones you cannot solve.

One step is a tangent line

Suppose a solution passes through $(x_0, y_0)$. The equation gives its slope there, $F(x_0, y_0)$. For a short run $\Delta x$, the tangent line rises by about $\Delta y = F(x_0, y_0)\,\Delta x$, so

$$y_1 = y_0 + F(x_0, y_0)\,\Delta x \quad \text{at} \quad x_1 = x_0 + \Delta x.$$

This is the local linearization of topic 4.6, with the slope supplied by the differential equation. Euler's method simply repeats it: from $(x_1, y_1)$, read the slope $F(x_1, y_1)$ and step again.

Too big or too small?

Each Euler step follows a tangent line, so the shape of the solution decides the error. The figure shows two steps on $\frac{dy}{dx} = y$ from $(0, 1)$. The solution through that point is $y = e^x$ (check: its derivative is itself), which is concave up, so the steps fall below it: Euler gives $2.25$ for a true value of $e \approx 2.718$.

To find the concavity without solving, differentiate the equation implicitly. For $\frac{dy}{dx} = x + y$:

$$\frac{d^2y}{dx^2} = 1 + \frac{dy}{dx} = 1 + x + y,$$

which is positive for $x \ge 0$ and $y > 0$. The solution curves there are concave up, so the estimate 2.5 in worked example 1 is an underestimate. Concave down curves lie below their tangent lines, so Euler's method would give an overestimate. Name the concavity and say why it decides the answer; "the solution is increasing" is not a reason.

Euler's method in context

Worked example 2. The volume $V$ of water in a tank, in liters, satisfies $\frac{dV}{dt} = 20 - 2\sqrt{V}$, with $t$ in minutes, and $V = 25$ at $t = 0$. Estimate $V$ at $t = 1$ with two steps of size $0.5$.

At $t = 0$: $\frac{dV}{dt} = 20 - 2(5) = 10$, so $V \approx 25 + 10(0.5) = 30$ liters at $t = 0.5$.

At $t = 0.5$: $\frac{dV}{dt} = 20 - 2\sqrt{30}$, so $V \approx 30 + \left(20 - 2\sqrt{30}\right)(0.5) = 40 - \sqrt{30} \approx 34.5$ liters at $t = 1$.

The rate has units of liters per minute and $\Delta t$ has units of minutes, so each $\Delta V$ is in liters, the units of the estimate.
