Unit 1 · Topic 1.7

Summary Statistics for One Quantitative Variable

The mean and standard deviation use every value, so outliers and skew pull them; the median and IQR are resistant.

Practice 3: Analyze DataPractice 4: Interpret Results

Part 1 · Hook

Why this matters

A reviewer tests 12 phones of one model. Eleven last between 7 and 11 hours; one lasts 14.9. Report the mean and the typical phone seems to last 9.68 hours. Report the median and it is 9.4. Both are correct. Which one to report depends on what each summary does with that one long-lasting phone, and that is the heart of this topic.

Part 5 · Step by step

How it works, step by step

  1. The mean adds every value and divides by n→one extreme value pulls it toward the tail
  2. The median and quartiles depend only on the order of the values→they barely move when an extreme value changes: they are resistant
  3. The standard deviation squares each distance from the mean→it measures typical distance from the mean, and an outlier inflates it
  4. So for skewed data or outliers→report the median and IQR; for roughly symmetric data, the mean and standard deviation

Part 6 · Key ideas

Key ideas

  • Mean x¯=∑xin\bar{x} = \frac{\sum x_i}{n}; median: the middle of the ordered values (the average of the two middle ones when n is even).
  • Quartiles: Q1Q_1 and Q3Q_3 are the medians of the lower and upper halves (leave out the median when n is odd). IQR=Q3−Q1\text{IQR} = Q_3 - Q_1; range = max − min.
  • Standard deviation ss=∑(xi−x¯)2n−1= \sqrt{\frac{\sum (x_i - \bar{x})^2}{n - 1}}, the typical distance from the mean; variance is s2s^2. Population versions: μ\mu and σ\sigma.
  • Outliers: beyond Q1−1.5×IQRQ_1 - 1.5 \times \text{IQR} or Q3+1.5×IQRQ_3 + 1.5 \times \text{IQR}, or more than 2 standard deviations from the mean.
  • Adding a constant shifts the center but not the spread; multiplying by a constant multiplies both.

Part 7 · Misconception

A common mistake

The wrong idea: The mean is always the best measure of center, because it uses every value.

What actually happens: Using every value is what makes the mean sensitive to extreme values. With strong skew or outliers, the resistant median describes a typical value better.

5. Which battery lives are outliers by the 1.5×IQR1.5 \times \text{IQR} rule?

  1. 7.2 and 14.9 hours
  2. 14.9 hours alone
  3. No phone, since 14.9 is below twice the mean of 9.68 hours
  4. 10.6 and 14.9 hours
Show the answer

Fences: Q1−1.5×IQR=6.25Q_1 - 1.5 \times \text{IQR} = 6.25 and Q3+1.5×IQR=12.65Q_3 + 1.5 \times \text{IQR} = 12.65. Only 14.9 lies outside.

  • 7.2 and 14.9 hours: 7.2 is above the lower fence of 6.25, so it is not an outlier.
  • Correct: 14.9 hours alone: Right: the fences are 8.65−1.5(1.6)=6.258.65 - 1.5(1.6) = 6.25 and 10.25+1.5(1.6)=12.6510.25 + 1.5(1.6) = 12.65; only 14.9 is outside.
  • No phone, since 14.9 is below twice the mean of 9.68 hours: Twice the mean is not an outlier rule. Use the fences: 14.9 is above 12.65.
  • 10.6 and 14.9 hours: Values above Q3Q_3 are common; an outlier must be beyond the fence, 12.65.

6. A student’s score is at the 80th percentile of a test. What does that tell you?

  1. 80% of the scores are at or below this score
  2. The student answered 80% of the questions correctly
  3. The score was 80 points
  4. 80 students scored higher than this student on the test
Show the answer

The pth percentile is the value with p% of the data at or below it.

  • Correct: 80% of the scores are at or below this score: Right: a percentile tells the percent of the data at or below a value.
  • The student answered 80% of the questions correctly: A percentile is about position among scores, not the percent correct.
  • The score was 80 points: A percentile is not a score; it tells where a score stands.
  • 80 students scored higher than this student on the test: A percentile is a percent of the data below, not a count above.

7. Which summaries are resistant to an extreme outlier? Select all that apply.

  1. The median
  2. The IQR
  3. The mean
  4. The standard deviation
  5. The range
Show the answer

The median and IQR are resistant; the mean, standard deviation and range are not.

  • Correct: The median: Right: it depends on the middle of the ordered data.
  • Correct: The IQR: Right: it depends on the quartiles, not the extremes.
  • The mean: The mean adds in the outlier, so it moves toward it.
  • The standard deviation: An outlier far from the mean makes one squared distance huge.
  • The range: The range uses the maximum or minimum, which may be the outlier.

Part 9 · Summary

Summary

The mean and standard deviation use every value, so outliers and skew pull them; the median and IQR are resistant. Quartiles are the medians of the two halves, and the 1.5×IQR1.5 \times \text{IQR} fences or the 2 standard deviation rule flag outliers. Adding a constant shifts center but not spread; multiplying by a constant scales both.
