Constructing a Confidence Interval for a Population Proportion

A confidence interval for p is p-hat ± z*√(p-hat(1 − p-hat)/n) : the point estimate plus or minus a margin of error.

Practice 2: Collect DataPractice 3: Analyze DataPractice 4: Interpret Results

Part 1 · Hook

Why this matters

A dietitian surveys 400 randomly chosen students and finds that 62% regularly skip breakfast. She could report "62%" and stop, but another sample would give 59% or 65%. A confidence interval reports a range instead, built by a method that captures the true campus proportion a known share of the time.

Part 5 · Step by step

How it works, step by step

  1. p^\hat{p} is approximately normal, centered at pp→in 95% of random samples, p^\hat{p} lands within 1.96 standard deviations of pp
  2. Turn that around→in 95% of samples, pp is within 1.96 standard deviations of p^\hat{p}
  3. We do not know pp, so the standard deviation cannot be computed→we use the standard error p^(1−p^)/n\sqrt{\hat{p}(1 - \hat{p})/n} in its place
  4. Go z∗z^* standard errors out on each side of p^\hat{p}→p^±z∗p^(1−p^)n\hat{p} \pm z^*\sqrt{\frac{\hat{p}(1 - \hat{p})}{n}} captures pp for the stated share of samples

Part 6 · Key ideas

Key ideas

  • Interval = statistic ± (critical value)(standard error). For one proportion: p^±z∗p^(1−p^)n\hat{p} \pm z^*\sqrt{\frac{\hat{p}(1 - \hat{p})}{n}}.
  • z∗z^*: 1.645 for 90%, 1.96 for 95%, 2.576 for 99%. Area below z∗z^* is C+1−C2C + \frac{1 - C}{2}.
  • Conditions in words: random sample; nn at most 10% of the population; at least 10 successes and 10 failures in the sample.
  • Margin of error =z∗⋅SE= z^* \cdot \text{SE}. It shrinks with a larger nn (4 times nn halves it) and grows with the confidence level.
  • Name the procedure in full: one-sample z interval for a proportion.

Part 7 · Misconception

A common mistake

The wrong idea: For a 90% interval, find the zz with 0.90 of the area below it, so z∗=1.28z^* = 1.28.

What actually happens: A 90% interval keeps 90% in the middle and leaves 5% in each tail, so z∗z^* has 0.95 below it: z∗=1.645z^* = 1.645.

1. Which statement correctly checks the large counts condition for a confidence interval?

  1. 248 skip and 152 do not, and both are at least 10
  2. n=400n = 400 is at least 30
  3. 400 is less than 10% of 28,000
  4. 400(0.5)=200400(0.5) = 200 is at least 10
Show the answer

For an interval, pp is unknown, so check the observed successes and failures: np^=248n\hat{p} = 248 and n(1−p^)=152n(1 - \hat{p}) = 152, both at least 10.

  • Correct: 248 skip and 152 do not, and both are at least 10: Right: for an interval, check the sample's successes and failures.
  • n=400n = 400 is at least 30: The rule of 30 is about means. For a proportion, check successes and failures.
  • 400 is less than 10% of 28,000: That is the 10% condition, which is about independence, not the normal shape.
  • 400(0.5)=200400(0.5) = 200 is at least 10: No value of 0.5 is claimed here. For an interval, use the sample's counts, 248 and 152.

Part 9 · Summary

Summary

A confidence interval for pp is p^±z∗p^(1−p^)/n\hat{p} \pm z^*\sqrt{\hat{p}(1 - \hat{p})/n}: the point estimate plus or minus a margin of error. Check random, 10% and large counts (sample successes and failures at least 10) in words first. Larger samples shrink the margin of error; higher confidence levels stretch it.
