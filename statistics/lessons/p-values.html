Unit 3 · Topic 3.6

p-Values

A p-value measures how surprising the data are if H_0 is true.

Practice 4: Interpret Results

Part 1 · Hook

Why this matters

Head office says 40% of customers buy a pastry with their coffee. At one store, only 105 of 300 sampled orders include one: 35%. Is that store really different, or would a gap that size show up by luck at a store that matches head office? The p-value puts a number on "by luck".

Part 5 · Step by step

How it works, step by step

  1. Assume H0H_0 is true, so p=p0p = p_0→p^\hat{p} is approximately normal with mean p0p_0 and standard deviation p0(1−p0)/n\sqrt{p_0(1 - p_0)/n}
  2. Standardize the observed p^\hat{p}→the test statistic zz=p^−p0p0(1−p0)/n= \frac{\hat{p} - p_0}{\sqrt{p_0(1 - p_0)/n}} says how many standard deviations it is from p0p_0
  3. Find the area beyond zz in the direction of HaH_a (both tails if two-sided)→that area is the p-value: the chance of a result at least this extreme if H0H_0 is true
  4. The p-value is small→the result would be rare under H0H_0, which is evidence against H0H_0 and for HaH_a (but never proof)

Part 6 · Key ideas

Key ideas

  • Test statistic: zz=p^−p0p0(1−p0)/n= \frac{\hat{p} - p_0}{\sqrt{p_0(1 - p_0)/n}}, with p0p_0 in the square root.
  • p-value: the probability, assuming H0H_0 is true, of a statistic at least as extreme as observed, in the direction of HaH_a. Two-sided: double one tail.
  • Interpret: "Assuming the true proportion of all __ is p0p_0, there is a __ probability of getting a sample proportion of __ or less (more) by chance in a random sample of __."
  • A simulation estimates a p-value: the share of simulated results, made assuming H0H_0, at least as extreme as the real one.
  • Not the probability that H0H_0 is true. A large p-value does not prove H0H_0.

Part 7 · Misconception

A common mistake

The wrong idea: A p-value of 0.0385 means there is a 3.85% chance that the null hypothesis is true.

What actually happens: The p-value is computed assuming H0H_0 is true. It is the probability of data at least this extreme if H0H_0 holds, not the probability that H0H_0 holds.

5. What does the small p-value, 0.0385, suggest?

  1. This result is unusual if p=0.40p = 0.40, which is evidence that p<0.40p < 0.40
  2. There is a 3.85% chance that the head office's figure of 40% is correct
  3. About 3.85% of the store's orders include a pastry
  4. The manager has proven that fewer than 40% of orders include a pastry
Show the answer

If the true proportion were 0.40, a sample proportion as low as 0.35 would happen only about 4% of the time. That makes 0.40 hard to believe, which is evidence for Ha:p<0.40H_a: p < 0.40.

  • Correct: This result is unusual if p=0.40p = 0.40, which is evidence that p<0.40p < 0.40: Right: a small p-value counts against H0H_0 and for HaH_a.
  • There is a 3.85% chance that the head office's figure of 40% is correct: The p-value assumes the figure is correct; it is not the probability that it is.
  • About 3.85% of the store's orders include a pastry: The p-value is a probability about samples, not a proportion of orders.
  • The manager has proven that fewer than 40% of orders include a pastry: A small p-value is evidence, never proof; a sample this low can still happen when p=0.40p = 0.40.

7. A student writes: "The p-value is 0.02, so there is a 2% chance that the null hypothesis is true." What is the error?

  1. There is no error
  2. It should say 98%, the complement, is the chance that HaH_a is true
  3. The p-value is computed assuming H0H_0 is true, so it is not the chance H0H_0 is true
  4. A p-value has to be compared with 0.5 before it means anything
Show the answer

The p-value is computed by assuming H0H_0 is true. It is the probability of data this extreme or more under H0H_0, not the probability that H0H_0 holds.

  • There is no error: This is the most common misreading of a p-value.
  • It should say 98%, the complement, is the chance that HaH_a is true: The complement is not the probability that HaH_a is true either.
  • Correct: The p-value is computed assuming H0H_0 is true, so it is not the chance H0H_0 is true: Right: the condition is "if H0H_0 is true", not "that H0H_0 is true".
  • A p-value has to be compared with 0.5 before it means anything: A p-value is compared with α\alpha, chosen in advance, not with 0.5.

Part 9 · Summary

Summary

A p-value measures how surprising the data are if H0H_0 is true. Assuming H0H_0 is true, standardize the sample proportion with zz=p^−p0p0(1−p0)/n= \frac{\hat{p} - p_0}{\sqrt{p_0(1 - p_0)/n}} and find the tail area in the direction of HaH_a (both tails for ≠\ne). That area is the p-value: the chance of a result at least as extreme as the one observed if H0H_0 is true. Small p-values are evidence against H0H_0; they are never the probability that H0H_0 is true.
