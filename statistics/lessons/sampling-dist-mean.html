Sampling Distributions for Sample Means

The sample mean x-bar has a sampling distribution centered at μ with standard deviation (σ)/(√n) (10% condition).

Practice 3: Analyze DataPractice 4: Interpret Results

Part 1 · Hook

Why this matters

A coffee chain times every car at its drive-through. Most cars wait 2 or 3 minutes, a few wait 12. If a manager averages a random sample of 40 cars, how far can that average land from the true mean of 4.2 minutes? The answer is a whole distribution, and it is much narrower and much more normal than the wait times themselves.

Part 5 · Step by step

How it works, step by step

  1. Each sample mean averages nn values→high and low values partly cancel, so means vary less than single values
  2. On average over many samples, x¯\bar{x} hits the population mean→μx¯=μ\mu_{\bar{x}} = \mu: the sample mean is unbiased
  3. The variance of an average of nn independent values is σ2n\frac{\sigma^2}{n}→the standard deviation of x¯\bar{x} is σx¯=σn\sigma_{\bar{x}} = \frac{\sigma}{\sqrt{n}} (when the sample is under 10% of the population)
  4. Averaging many values smooths away skew→for n≥30n \ge 30 the sampling distribution is approximately normal (the central limit theorem); for a normal population it is normal at any nn
  5. With a normal model, center and spread→a z-score x¯−μσ/n\frac{\bar{x} - \mu}{\sigma/\sqrt{n}} gives probabilities about sample means

Part 6 · Key ideas

Key ideas

  • Center: μx¯=μ\mu_{\bar{x}} = \mu for every sample size.
  • Spread: σx¯=σn\sigma_{\bar{x}} = \frac{\sigma}{\sqrt{n}}, valid when the sample is less than 10% of the population. Four times the sample size halves the spread.
  • Shape: normal if the population is normal; approximately normal if n≥30n \ge 30 (central limit theorem); otherwise it keeps some of the population's skew.
  • The central limit theorem is about the distribution of many sample means, not about the data in one sample.

Part 7 · Misconception

A common mistake

The wrong idea: With a large sample, the data become normal, so a skewed population gives a normal sample.

What actually happens: A large random sample looks like the population, skew and all. What becomes approximately normal is the sampling distribution of x¯\bar{x}, the pattern of the means over many samples.

1. What are the mean and the standard deviation of the sampling distribution of x¯\bar{x} for samples of 40 cars?

  1. Mean 4.2 minutes, standard deviation about 0.44 minutes
  2. Mean 4.2 minutes, standard deviation 2.8 minutes
  3. Mean 4.2 minutes, standard deviation 0.07 minutes
  4. Mean 0.44 minutes, standard deviation 2.8 minutes
Show the answer

μx¯=μ=4.2\mu_{\bar{x}} = \mu = 4.2 minutes and σx¯\sigma_{\bar{x}}=σn= \frac{\sigma}{\sqrt{n}}=2.840= \frac{2.8}{\sqrt{40}}=0.443= 0.443 minutes.

  • Correct: Mean 4.2 minutes, standard deviation about 0.44 minutes: The mean of x¯\bar{x} equals μ=4.2\mu = 4.2, and its standard deviation is 2.840≈0.44\frac{2.8}{\sqrt{40}} \approx 0.44 minutes.
  • Mean 4.2 minutes, standard deviation 2.8 minutes: 2.8 minutes is the spread of single wait times. Averages of 40 cars vary much less: divide by 40\sqrt{40}.
  • Mean 4.2 minutes, standard deviation 0.07 minutes: That divides 2.8 by 40. The standard deviation of x¯\bar{x} divides by the square root of nn, not by nn.
  • Mean 0.44 minutes, standard deviation 2.8 minutes: The center does not shrink. The sample mean is an unbiased estimator, so its mean is μ=4.2\mu = 4.2.

2. The wait times are strongly skewed. Why is a normal model still reasonable for the sampling distribution of x¯\bar{x}?

  1. The population of wait times is approximately normal
  2. 40 cars is less than 10% of the cars served
  3. The sample size of 40 is at least 30, so the central limit theorem applies
  4. The cars were chosen at random from last month
Show the answer

The central limit theorem: for a random sample with n≥30n \ge 30, the sampling distribution of x¯\bar{x} is approximately normal even when the population is skewed.

  • The population of wait times is approximately normal: The stimulus says the population is strongly skewed right. The normal shape comes from the large sample, not the population.
  • 40 cars is less than 10% of the cars served: The 10% condition justifies the standard deviation formula. It says nothing about shape.
  • Correct: The sample size of 40 is at least 30, so the central limit theorem applies: With n=40≥30n = 40 \ge 30, the central limit theorem says the sampling distribution of x¯\bar{x} is approximately normal whatever the population shape.
  • The cars were chosen at random from last month: Random selection is needed, but it does not by itself make the shape normal. The sample size does that.

5. A random sample of 50 is taken from a large, strongly skewed population with mean μ\mu and standard deviation σ\sigma. Which statements are true? Select all that apply.

  1. The sampling distribution of x¯\bar{x} has mean μ\mu
  2. The sampling distribution of x¯\bar{x} has standard deviation σ50\frac{\sigma}{\sqrt{50}}
  3. The sampling distribution of x¯\bar{x} is approximately normal
  4. The 50 values in the sample are approximately normal
  5. The standard deviation of x¯\bar{x} is σ\sigma
  6. The sampling distribution of x¯\bar{x} is skewed right, like the population
Show the answer

Center μ\mu, spread σn\frac{\sigma}{\sqrt{n}} and an approximately normal shape describe the sampling distribution of x¯\bar{x}, not the sample itself.

  • Correct: The sampling distribution of x¯\bar{x} has mean μ\mu: Right: x¯\bar{x} is unbiased for μ\mu.
  • Correct: The sampling distribution of x¯\bar{x} has standard deviation σ50\frac{\sigma}{\sqrt{50}}: Right: the population is large, so the 10% condition holds.
  • Correct: The sampling distribution of x¯\bar{x} is approximately normal: Right: n=50≥30n = 50 \ge 30, so the central limit theorem applies.
  • The 50 values in the sample are approximately normal: The sample looks like the population, so it is skewed too. The central limit theorem is about the means of many samples.
  • The standard deviation of x¯\bar{x} is σ\sigma: Averages vary less than single values; divide σ\sigma by 50\sqrt{50}.
  • The sampling distribution of x¯\bar{x} is skewed right, like the population: With 50 values in each mean, the skew is smoothed away; the central limit theorem gives an approximately normal shape.

Part 9 · Summary

Summary

The sample mean x¯\bar{x} has a sampling distribution centered at μ\mu with standard deviation σn\frac{\sigma}{\sqrt{n}} (10% condition). It is normal for a normal population and approximately normal for n≥30n \ge 30 by the central limit theorem. Standardize with z=x¯−μσ/nz = \frac{\bar{x} - \mu}{\sigma/\sqrt{n}} to find probabilities about a sample mean.
