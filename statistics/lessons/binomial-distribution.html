Unit 2 · Topic 2.10

The Binomial Distribution

When a yes-or-no trial repeats n times independently with the same success probability p, the number of successes is binomial: P(X = k) = (nCk)p^k(1 − p)^(n − k) , with mean np and standard deviation √(np(1 − p)) .

Practice 3: Analyze DataPractice 4: Interpret Results

Part 1 · Hook

Why this matters

A coffee shop says 30% of its customers order with its app. You watch 10 random customers and 7 of them use it. Is the shop wrong, or did you see a fluke? When a chance process repeats the same yes-or-no trial a fixed number of times, one formula gives every probability you need to decide.

Part 5 · Step by step

How it works, step by step

  1. A trial has two outcomes and repeats a fixed number of times, independently, with the same p→the count of successes X is binomial with parameters n and p
  2. One order of k successes and n − k failures has probability pk(1−p)n−kp^k(1 - p)^{n - k}→and there are (nk)\binom{n}{k} such orders, so P(X=k)P(X = k)=(nk)pk(1−p)n−k= \binom{n}{k}p^k(1 - p)^{n - k}
  3. An event like "at least 4" covers several values→add them, or use the complement: P(X≥4)=1−P(X≤3)P(X \ge 4) = 1 - P(X \le 3)
  4. The probability of a result as extreme as the one seen is very small under a claim→the result gives reason to doubt the claim

Part 6 · Key ideas

Key ideas

  • A binomial setting: two outcomes per trial, independent trials, a fixed number n of trials, the same success probability p on every trial.
  • The binomial distribution: P(X=k)P(X = k)=(nk)pk(1−p)n−k= \binom{n}{k}p^k(1 - p)^{n - k} for k=0,1,…,nk = 0, 1, \dots, n.
  • Show work: name the distribution and parameters, the boundary and the direction: "X is binomial, n=10n = 10, p=0.3p = 0.3; P(X≥4)P(X \ge 4)=1−P(X≤3)= 1 - P(X \le 3)=0.350= 0.350."
  • Mean μX=np\mu_X = np and standard deviation σX=np(1−p)\sigma_X = \sqrt{np(1 - p)}.
  • Translate carefully: "fewer than 3" is P(X≤2)P(X \le 2), "at least 4" is P(X≥4)P(X \ge 4).

Part 7 · Misconception

A common mistake

The wrong idea: P(X≥4)P(X \ge 4) is 1−P(X≤4)1 - P(X \le 4).

What actually happens: The complement of "4 or more" is "3 or fewer", so P(X≥4)=1−P(X≤3)P(X \ge 4) = 1 - P(X \le 3). Subtracting P(X≤4)P(X \le 4) throws away the value 4 itself.

Guessing on a quiz

A quiz has 12 multiple-choice questions, each with 4 options and one right answer. A student who has not studied guesses at random on every question, independently. Let X = the number of questions the student gets right.

1. What is the probability that the student gets exactly 3 questions right?

  1. 0.25
  2. 0.649
  3. 0.258
  4. 0.001
Show the answer

X is binomial with n=12n = 12 and p=0.25p = 0.25. P(X=3)P(X = 3)=(123)(0.25)3(0.75)9= \binom{12}{3}(0.25)^3(0.75)^9=220(0.015625)= 220(0.015625)(0.07508)(0.07508)≈0.258\approx 0.258.

  • 0.25: That is the chance of guessing one question right, not of exactly 3 right out of 12.
  • 0.649: That is P(X≤3)P(X \le 3), the probability of 3 or fewer.
  • Correct: 0.258: Right: the binomial formula with n = 12, p = 0.25, k = 3.
  • 0.001: That is one arrangement of 3 right and 9 wrong. Multiply by (123)=220\binom{12}{3} = 220 arrangements.

2. What is the probability that the student gets at least 6 questions right?

  1. 0.1576
  2. 0.0401
  3. 0.9456
  4. 0.0544
Show the answer

P(X≥6)P(X \ge 6)=1−P(X≤5)= 1 - P(X \le 5)=1−0.9456= 1 - 0.9456=0.0544= 0.0544, with X binomial, n=12n = 12, p=0.25p = 0.25.

  • 0.1576: That is P(X≥5)P(X \ge 5): subtracting P(X≤4)P(X \le 4) leaves 5 in.
  • 0.0401: That is P(X=6)P(X = 6) alone. At least 6 includes 7 through 12.
  • 0.9456: That is P(X≤5)P(X \le 5), the complement.
  • Correct: 0.0544: Right: 1 minus the cumulative probability up to 5.

3. What are the mean and standard deviation of X?

  1. Mean 3, standard deviation 1.5
  2. Mean 3, standard deviation 2.25
  3. Mean 6, standard deviation 1.5
  4. Mean 3, standard deviation 0.75
Show the answer

μX=np=12(0.25)=3\mu_X = np = 12(0.25) = 3 questions and σX\sigma_X=np(1−p)= \sqrt{np(1 - p)}=12(0.25)(0.75)= \sqrt{12(0.25)(0.75)}=2.25= \sqrt{2.25}=1.5= 1.5 questions.

  • Correct: Mean 3, standard deviation 1.5: Right: np and the square root of np(1 − p).
  • Mean 3, standard deviation 2.25: 2.25 is the variance; take its square root.
  • Mean 6, standard deviation 1.5: The mean is np = 3, not half of the 12 questions.
  • Mean 3, standard deviation 0.75: 0.75 is 1 − p, not the standard deviation.

4. Why is it reasonable to model Y with a binomial distribution?

  1. The 15 numbers are a random sample from the list, and 15 calls is a large enough number of trials
  2. Each call is answered or not, n = 15 is fixed, p = 0.20 each time, calls are independent
  3. About 20% of the 15 calls, which is 3 calls, will be answered
  4. The firm keeps dialing new numbers until someone answers a call
Show the answer

Binomial setting: each call is answered or not (two outcomes), the number of calls is fixed at 15, the chance of an answer is 0.20 on each call, and calls to numbers chosen at random from a huge list are independent.

  • The 15 numbers are a random sample from the list, and 15 calls is a large enough number of trials: Random choice helps independence, but the fixed number, two outcomes and constant p are also needed.
  • Correct: Each call is answered or not, n = 15 is fixed, p = 0.20 each time, calls are independent: Right: all four conditions, in context.
  • About 20% of the 15 calls, which is 3 calls, will be answered: That is the mean, not a reason the distribution is binomial.
  • The firm keeps dialing new numbers until someone answers a call: The firm dials exactly 15 numbers; a fixed number of trials is one of the conditions.

5. What is the probability that exactly 2 of the 15 calls are answered?

Type a number. A table answer and a calculator answer are both accepted.

Show the answer

6. What is the probability that fewer than 2 of the calls are answered?

Type a number. A table answer and a calculator answer are both accepted.

Show the answer

"Fewer than 2" means 0 or 1: P(Y≤1)P(Y \le 1)=P(Y=0)= P(Y = 0)+P(Y=1)+ P(Y = 1)=(0.80)15= (0.80)^{15}+15(0.20)+ 15(0.20)(0.80)14(0.80)^{14}≈0.1671\approx 0.1671.

Part 9 · Summary

Summary

When a yes-or-no trial repeats n times independently with the same success probability p, the number of successes is binomial: P(X=k)P(X = k)=(nk)pk(1−p)n−k= \binom{n}{k}p^k(1 - p)^{n - k}, with mean np and standard deviation np(1−p)\sqrt{np(1 - p)}. Name the distribution and its parameters, translate the event into an inequality, and use the complement for "at least" questions. A tiny probability under a claim casts doubt on the claim.
