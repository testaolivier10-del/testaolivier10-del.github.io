Conditional Probability

Given B, the outcomes in B are the new whole, so P(A|B) = (P(A∩B))/(P(B)) .

Practice 3: Analyze Data

Part 1 · Hook

Why this matters

A screening test catches 90% of people who have a condition. You test positive. Is there a 90% chance you have it? Surprisingly, no: in a typical population it is closer to 38%. The difference is which group a probability is about, and a tree diagram makes it visible.

Part 5 · Step by step

How it works, step by step

  1. You are told that B happened→only the outcomes in B are possible now, so B becomes the new total
  2. So the share of B that is also A→is the conditional probability: P(A|B)=P(A∩B)P(B)P(A \mid B) = \frac{P(A \cap B)}{P(B)}
  3. Rearrange the rule→the multiplication rule: P(A∩B)=P(A)⋅P(B|A)P(A \cap B) = P(A) \cdot P(B \mid A), which is multiplying along a tree's path
  4. Add every path that ends in the result you want→you get its total probability, and dividing one path by it reverses the condition

Part 6 · Key ideas

Key ideas

  • A conditional probability P(A|B)P(A \mid B) is the probability of A given that B happened: P(A|B)=P(A∩B)P(B)P(A \mid B) = \frac{P(A \cap B)}{P(B)}. In a table, divide by the total of B's row or column.
  • The multiplication rule: P(A∩B)=P(A)⋅P(B|A)P(A \cap B) = P(A) \cdot P(B \mid A). Drawing without replacement changes the second probability.
  • A tree diagram shows stages: first-stage probabilities, then conditional ones. Multiply along a path; add paths.
  • P(A|B)P(A \mid B) and P(B|A)P(B \mid A) are usually different. Name the group after the bar.

Part 7 · Misconception

A common mistake

The wrong idea: If the test is positive for 90% of people with the condition, then 90% of people who test positive have it.

What actually happens: Those are different conditional probabilities: P(positive|condition)P(\text{positive} \mid \text{condition})=0.90= 0.90, but P(condition|positive)P(\text{condition} \mid \text{positive})=0.0360.0936= \frac{0.036}{0.0936}≈0.385\approx 0.385.

1. What is the probability that the adult gets news mainly online, given that the adult is under 40?

  1. 0.33
  2. 0.710
  3. 0.6
  4. 0.465
Show the answer

Given under 40, the 220 adults under 40 are the new total: P(online|under 40)P(\text{online} \mid \text{under } 40)=132220= \frac{132}{220}=0.6= 0.6.

  • 0.33: That is 132400\frac{132}{400}, the joint probability of under 40 and online.
  • 0.710: That is 132186\frac{132}{186}, the probability of under 40 given online: the condition is reversed.
  • Correct: 0.6: Right: 132 of the 220 adults under 40.
  • 0.465: That is 186400\frac{186}{400}, the probability of online for everyone.

2. Given that the adult gets news mainly from TV, what is the probability that the adult is under 40?

  1. 0.3
  2. 0.165
  3. 0.55
  4. 0.423
Show the answer

The condition is TV, so divide by the 156 TV viewers: P(under 40|TV)P(\text{under } 40 \mid \text{TV})=66156= \frac{66}{156}≈0.423\approx 0.423.

  • 0.3: That is 66220\frac{66}{220}, the probability of TV given under 40: the condition is reversed.
  • 0.165: That is 66400\frac{66}{400}, a joint probability.
  • 0.55: That is 220400\frac{220}{400}, the probability of under 40 for everyone.
  • Correct: 0.423: Right: 66 of the 156 TV viewers.

Part 9 · Summary

Summary

Given B, the outcomes in B are the new whole, so P(A|B)=P(A∩B)P(B)P(A \mid B) = \frac{P(A \cap B)}{P(B)}. Rearranged, the multiplication rule P(A∩B)=P(A)⋅P(B|A)P(A \cap B) = P(A) \cdot P(B \mid A) is what a tree diagram does along each path. Adding paths gives a total probability, and dividing reverses a condition, which usually changes the answer.
