Parameters of Random Variables

The mean of a discrete random variable weights each value by its probability: μ_X = ∑ x_ip_i .

Practice 3: Analyze DataPractice 4: Interpret Results

Part 1 · Hook

Why this matters

A food truck plans tomorrow's supplies. Some orders are 1 taco, some are 5. How many tacos is an average order? Not the middle of 1 to 5: small orders are far more common. Weight each order size by how often it happens and you get the mean of the random variable, the number every planner wants.

Part 5 · Step by step

How it works, step by step

  1. Some values of X are more likely than others→a plain average of the values is wrong; each value must count by its probability
  2. Multiply each value by its probability and add→the mean (expected value) μX=∑xipi\mu_X = \sum x_i p_i, the long-run average
  3. Weight the squared distances from the mean the same way→the variance; its square root is the standard deviation σX\sigma_X
  4. Interpret both in context→the mean is the average over many repetitions; the SD is how far values typically land from it

Part 6 · Key ideas

Key ideas

  • The mean or expected value of a discrete random variable is μX=E(X)=∑xipi\mu_X = E(X) = \sum x_i p_i: each value times its probability, added up. It is the long-run average and need not be a possible value.
  • The standard deviation is σX\sigma_X=∑(xi−μX)2pi= \sqrt{\sum (x_i - \mu_X)^2 p_i}: a typical distance of the values from the mean, in the units of X.
  • Show the expression, the substitution and the answer: 1(0.20)+2(0.35)+…1(0.20) + 2(0.35) + \dots=2.5= 2.5.
  • Interpret in context: "Over many orders, the average number of tacos per order is about 2.5."

Part 7 · Misconception

A common mistake

The wrong idea: The mean of X is the average of its possible values: 1+2+3+4+55\frac{1 + 2 + 3 + 4 + 5}{5}=3= 3 tacos.

What actually happens: Each value counts by its probability. Small orders are more likely, so μX=∑xipi=2.5\mu_X = \sum x_i p_i = 2.5 tacos.

1. What is the mean (expected value) of X?

Show the answer

μX\mu_X=∑xipi= \sum x_i p_i=1(0.20)= 1(0.20)+2(0.35)+ 2(0.35)+3(0.25)+ 3(0.25)+4(0.15)+ 4(0.15)+5(0.05)+ 5(0.05)=0.20= 0.20+0.70+ 0.70+0.75+ 0.75+0.60+ 0.60+0.25+ 0.25=2.5= 2.5 tacos.

  • 3 tacos: That averages 1 to 5 without the probabilities.
  • 2 tacos: That is the most likely value, not the mean.
  • Correct: 2.5 tacos: Right: each value weighted by its probability.
  • 0.2 tacos: That averages the probabilities, which always gives 1 divided by the number of values.

2. What is the standard deviation of X, to two decimal places?

  1. 1.25 tacos
  2. 1.58 tacos
  3. 0.25 tacos
  4. 1.12 tacos
Show the answer

σX\sigma_X=∑(xi−μX)2pi= \sqrt{\sum (x_i - \mu_X)^2 p_i}=(1−2.5)2(0.20)+(2−2.5)2(0.35)+(3−2.5)2(0.25)+(4−2.5)2(0.15)+(5−2.5)2(0.05)= \sqrt{(1 - 2.5)^2(0.20) + (2 - 2.5)^2(0.35) + (3 - 2.5)^2(0.25) + (4 - 2.5)^2(0.15) + (5 - 2.5)^2(0.05)}=1.25= \sqrt{1.25}≈1.12\approx 1.12 tacos.

  • 1.25 tacos: That is the variance. Take its square root.
  • 1.58 tacos: That treats 1 to 5 as a data set with no probabilities.
  • 0.25 tacos: That is the squared deviation of 2 or 3 from the mean, not the whole sum.
  • Correct: 1.12 tacos: Right: the square root of the weighted squared deviations, 1.25.

3. Which is the best interpretation of the mean of X in context?

  1. Over many orders, the average number of tacos per order is about 2.5
  2. Most orders contain 2.5 tacos, the most common order size
  3. Half of the orders have more than 2.5 tacos and half have fewer
  4. The next order will contain about 2 or 3 tacos
Show the answer

An expected value is a long-run average: over many randomly chosen orders, the average number of tacos per order is about 2.5.

  • Correct: Over many orders, the average number of tacos per order is about 2.5: Right: a long-run average over many orders.
  • Most orders contain 2.5 tacos, the most common order size: No order contains 2.5 tacos; the most common is 2.
  • Half of the orders have more than 2.5 tacos and half have fewer: That would describe a median; here 55% of orders have fewer than 2.5.
  • The next order will contain about 2 or 3 tacos: The mean does not predict one order; it describes the long run.

6. For a randomly chosen family in a town, the expected number of children is 1.8. A student says this is impossible, since no family has 1.8 children. How should you respond?

  1. The student is right: it should be rounded to 2 children
  2. The expected value is probably a mistake, since it is not a whole number of children
  3. It is an average over many families, so it need not be a possible value
  4. It means most families in the town have 1 or 2 children
Show the answer

An expected value is a long-run average over many families. It need not be one of the possible values.

  • The student is right: it should be rounded to 2 children: Rounding throws away information; the average is 1.8.
  • The expected value is probably a mistake, since it is not a whole number of children: Means of counts are often not whole numbers.
  • Correct: It is an average over many families, so it need not be a possible value: Right: the long-run mean can fall between whole numbers.
  • It means most families in the town have 1 or 2 children: The mean alone does not say what most families have.
