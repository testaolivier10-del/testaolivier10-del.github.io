Carrying Out a Chi-Square Test for Homogeneity or Independence

Carrying out a chi-square test: find each expected count, add the components ((O − E)²)/E to get χ² , use the chi-square distribution with (r − 1)(c − 1) degrees of freedom for the right-tail p-value, compare it with α , and conclude about the association or the distributions in context.

Practice 3: Analyze DataPractice 4: Interpret Results

Part 1 · Hook

Why this matters

Of 300 randomly chosen students, those who keep a phone in the bedroom seem to sleep less: 35% of them sleep under 7 hours, against 20% of the others. The expected counts are ready. Now one number, χ2\chi^2, has to sum up how far the whole table is from "no association", and a new distribution has to say whether that number is surprising.

Part 5 · Step by step

How it works, step by step

  1. For each cell, compare the observed count with the expected count→the term (O−E)2E\frac{(O - E)^2}{E} is that cell's contribution: large where the table departs from H0H_0
  2. Add the terms over every cell→χ2=∑(O−E)2E\chi^2 = \sum \frac{(O - E)^2}{E}, which is 0 when the table matches H0H_0 and grows as it departs
  3. If H0H_0 is true, χ2\chi^2 follows a chi-square distribution with df=(r−1)(c−1)\text{df} = (r - 1)(c - 1)→the p-value is the area to the right of the observed χ2\chi^2 under that curve
  4. Compare the p-value with α\alpha→conclude about an association (or a difference in distributions) in context, then describe it from the largest terms

Part 6 · Key ideas

Key ideas

  • χ2=∑(O−E)2E\chi^2 = \sum \frac{(O - E)^2}{E} over every cell; each term is a chi-square component.
  • Degrees of freedom: df\text{df}=(rows−1)(columns−1)= (\text{rows} - 1)(\text{columns} - 1).
  • The p-value is always the right-tail area: only large χ2\chi^2 values are evidence against H0H_0.
  • Conclude: "There is (is not) convincing evidence of an association between ... and ... for ..." (independence) or "that the distribution of ... differs among ..." (homogeneity).

Part 7 · Misconception

A common mistake

The wrong idea: The cell with the largest observed count contributes the most to χ2\chi^2.

What actually happens: What matters is the gap between observed and expected, relative to expected: (O−E)2E\frac{(O - E)^2}{E}. A big count close to its expected count adds almost nothing.

Phones in the bedroom and sleep

A random sample of 300 students at a large high school is asked whether they keep their phone in the bedroom at night and how many hours they usually sleep. A chi-square test for independence is planned at α=0.05\alpha = 0.05; the conditions are met. (Invented data.)

Phone in bedroomUnder 7 hours7 to 8 hoursOver 8 hoursTotal

No205030100
Total9014070300

1. What is the value of the chi-square statistic? Give two decimal places.

Type a number. A table answer and a calculator answer are both accepted.

Show the answer

Expected counts: 60, 93.3, 46.7 (yes) and 30, 46.7, 23.3 (no). χ2\chi^2=(70−60)260= \frac{(70 - 60)^2}{60}+(90−93.3)293.3+ \frac{(90 - 93.3)^2}{93.3}+…+ \dots+(30−23.3)223.3+ \frac{(30 - 23.3)^2}{23.3}=8.21= 8.21.

  • Answer: 8.21

2. How many degrees of freedom does the test have?

Type a number. A table answer and a calculator answer are both accepted.

Show the answer

df=(2−1)(3−1)=2\text{df} = (2 - 1)(3 - 1) = 2.

  • Answer: 2

3. What is the p-value? Give four decimal places.

Type a number. A table answer and a calculator answer are both accepted.

Show the answer

P(χ2≥8.21)P(\chi^2 \ge 8.21) with df=2\text{df} = 2 is 0.0165, the area in the right tail.

  • Answer: 0.0165

4. State the conclusion of the test in context.

Write the conclusion in context.

Show the answer

Reject H0H_0: convincing evidence of an association, in context, for the population sampled.

  • A full-credit answer: Because the p-value of 0.0165 is less than 0.05, we reject the null hypothesis. There is convincing evidence of an association between keeping a phone in the bedroom and usual hours of sleep for students at the school.

5. Which cell contributes most to the chi-square statistic, and what does it show?

  1. No phone, under 7 hours: 20 observed, 30 expected
  2. Phone, 7 to 8 hours: the largest observed count in the table, 90
  3. Phone, under 7 hours: 70 observed against 60 expected
  4. No phone, over 8 hours: 30 observed against 23.3 expected
Show the answer

Each cell's term (O−E)2E\frac{(O - E)^2}{E} shows where the table departs most from H0H_0.

  • Correct: No phone, under 7 hours: 20 observed, 30 expected: Right: its term is (20−30)230=3.33\frac{(20 - 30)^2}{30} = 3.33, the largest of the six.
  • Phone, 7 to 8 hours: the largest observed count in the table, 90: A large count is not the same as a large gap. Here 90 is close to the expected 93.3, a term of only 0.12.
  • Phone, under 7 hours: 70 observed against 60 expected: This term is (70−60)260=1.67\frac{(70 - 60)^2}{60} = 1.67. The same gap of 10 counts for more where only 30 were expected.
  • No phone, over 8 hours: 30 observed against 23.3 expected: This term is 1.90, second largest. The no phone, under 7 hours cell is larger.

Type the expression as on the exam, with the values substituted.

Show the answer

(30−22.4)222.4\frac{(30 - 22.4)^2}{22.4}=57.7622.4= \frac{57.76}{22.4}=2.58= 2.58.

  • Answer: (30−22.4)222.4\frac{(30 - 22.4)^{2}}{22.4}

7. A two-way table classifies students by class year (4 categories) and favorite lunch option (5 categories). How many degrees of freedom does a chi-square test for independence have?

  1. 19
  2. 7
  3. 20
  4. 12
Show the answer

df=(r−1)(c−1)\text{df} = (r - 1)(c - 1).

  • 19: That is the number of cells minus 1. Use (r−1)(c−1)(r - 1)(c - 1).
  • 7: That adds (4−1)+(5−1)(4 - 1) + (5 - 1). Multiply instead.
  • 20: That is the number of cells.
  • Correct: 12: Right: (4−1)(5−1)(4 - 1)(5 - 1)=3×4= 3 \times 4=12= 12.
