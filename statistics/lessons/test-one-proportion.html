Carrying Out a Test for a Population Proportion

A complete test runs state, plan, do, conclude.

Practice 3: Analyze DataPractice 4: Interpret Results

Part 1 · Hook

Why this matters

A school district starts a mentoring program, and this year's random sample shows 14% of students chronically absent, down from 18% last year. The superintendent wants to announce that absence has dropped. A complete significance test says whether the data back her up, and it also says what she still cannot claim.

Part 5 · Step by step

How it works, step by step

  1. The p-value is less than α\alpha→the result is too unusual under H0H_0: reject H0H_0, and there is convincing evidence for HaH_a
  2. The p-value is greater than α\alpha→fail to reject H0H_0: there is not convincing evidence for HaH_a (which does not make H0H_0 true)
  3. The data come from a random sample→the conclusion applies to the population that was sampled
  4. There was no random assignment to treatments→the test can show a change but not what caused it

Part 6 · Key ideas

Key ideas

  • Four steps: State (H0H_0, HaH_a, α\alpha, pp defined), Plan (procedure, conditions), Do (zz, p-value), Conclude.
  • Conclusion: "Because the p-value of __ is less than α\alpha = __, we reject H0H_0. There is convincing evidence that the true proportion of all __ is __ than __."
  • Fail to reject: "there is not convincing evidence that ...", still naming the direction of HaH_a. Never "accept H0H_0" or "prove".
  • A two-sided test at α=0.05\alpha = 0.05 almost always rejects when p0p_0 is outside the 95% interval, and fails to reject when it is inside.
  • Random sampling lets you generalize; only random assignment lets you claim cause.

Part 7 · Misconception

A common mistake

The wrong idea: The p-value of 0.38 is greater than 0.05, so we accept H0H_0: the proportion has not changed.

What actually happens: Fail to reject H0H_0: there is not convincing evidence that the proportion has changed. The data are consistent with H0H_0, but a test cannot show that H0H_0 is true.

A mentoring program and chronic absence

Last year, 18% of a large school district's students were chronically absent (missed 10% or more of school days). This year the district started a mentoring program and wants to know whether the proportion has gone down. A researcher selects a random sample of 450 of the district's 21,000 students; 63 of them are chronically absent so far. She tests H0:p=0.18H_0: p = 0.18 against Ha:p<0.18H_a: p < 0.18 at α=0.05\alpha = 0.05, where pp is the true proportion of all the district's students who are chronically absent this year.

1. Calculate the test statistic. Give two decimal places.

Type a number. A table answer and a calculator answer are both accepted.

Show the answer

zz=0.14−0.180.18(0.82)/450= \frac{0.14 - 0.18}{\sqrt{0.18(0.82)/450}}=−0.040.0181= \frac{-0.04}{0.0181}=−2.21= -2.21. Conditions: random sample; 450 is less than 10% of 21,000; 450(0.18)=81450(0.18) = 81 and 450(0.82)=369450(0.82) = 369 are at least 10.

  • Answer: -2.21

2. Find the p-value. Give four decimal places.

Type a number. A table answer and a calculator answer are both accepted.

Show the answer

Ha:p<0.18H_a: p < 0.18, so the p-value is the left tail: P(Z≤−2.21)=0.0136P(Z \le -2.21) = 0.0136. Technology (a 1-proportion z test) gives z=−2.21z = -2.21 and p-value 0.0136.

  • Answer: 0.0136

3. State the conclusion of the test at α=0.05\alpha = 0.05.

Write the conclusion in context.

Show the answer

Because 0.0136<0.050.0136 < 0.05, reject H0H_0. The conclusion is about the parameter, in context, with evidence language and the direction of HaH_a.

  • A full-credit answer: Because the p-value of 0.0136 is less than α = 0.05, we reject H0. There is convincing evidence that the true proportion of all the district's students who are chronically absent this year is less than 0.18.

4. Suppose the researcher had chosen α=0.01\alpha = 0.01 before collecting the data. What would she conclude?

  1. Reject H0H_0, because the p-value is small
  2. Fail to reject H0H_0: not convincing evidence that p<0.18p < 0.18
  3. Accept H0H_0: the true proportion is 0.18
  4. Change to α=0.05\alpha = 0.05 so the result is significant
Show the answer

Because 0.0136>0.010.0136 > 0.01, she would fail to reject H0H_0: there would not be convincing evidence at the 1% level that the true proportion is less than 0.18. That does not mean the proportion is 0.18.

  • Reject H0H_0, because the p-value is small: At α=0.01\alpha = 0.01, the p-value 0.0136 is not small enough: it is greater than 0.01.
  • Correct: Fail to reject H0H_0: not convincing evidence that p<0.18p < 0.18: Right: 0.0136>0.010.0136 > 0.01.
  • Accept H0H_0: the true proportion is 0.18: A test never accepts H0H_0; failing to reject means the evidence is not strong enough.
  • Change to α=0.05\alpha = 0.05 so the result is significant: The significance level is fixed before the data; changing it afterward to get a result is not allowed.

6. A test of H0:p=0.25H_0: p = 0.25 against Ha:p≠0.25H_a: p \ne 0.25 gives a p-value of 0.38. Which conclusion is worded correctly?

  1. We accept H0H_0: the true proportion is 0.25
  2. We fail to reject H0H_0: not convincing evidence that p≠0.25p \ne 0.25
  3. There is a 38% chance that the true proportion is 0.25
  4. The data prove that the proportion is still 0.25, just as it was before
Show the answer

Because 0.38>0.050.38 > 0.05, fail to reject H0H_0, and say the evidence is not convincing for HaH_a. Never "accept" H0H_0 or say it is true.

  • We accept H0H_0: the true proportion is 0.25: A test never accepts H0H_0; a large p-value means the data are consistent with it, not that it is true.
  • Correct: We fail to reject H0H_0: not convincing evidence that p≠0.25p \ne 0.25: Right: the decision, then evidence language about HaH_a.
  • There is a 38% chance that the true proportion is 0.25: A p-value is not the probability that H0H_0 is true.
  • The data prove that the proportion is still 0.25, just as it was before: Tests never prove; failing to reject only means the evidence was not strong enough.

7. Put the four steps of a significance test in order.

  1. State: hypotheses, α\alpha, and the parameter in context
  2. Plan: name the procedure and check the conditions
  3. Do: compute the test statistic and the p-value
  4. Conclude: compare the p-value with α\alpha and state the conclusion in context
Show the answer

State what you are testing, plan how (procedure and conditions), do the calculation, then conclude in context.

  • Correct order: 1. State: hypotheses, α\alpha, and the parameter in context 2. Plan: name the procedure and check the conditions 3. Do: compute the test statistic and the p-value 4. Conclude: compare the p-value with α\alpha and state the conclusion in context

Part 9 · Summary

Summary

A complete test runs state, plan, do, conclude. It states hypotheses about pp with α\alpha, names the one-sample z test for a proportion and checks random, 10% and large counts with p0p_0, computes zz and the p-value, then concludes: compare the p-value with α\alpha, reject or fail to reject H0H_0, and say whether there is convincing evidence for HaH_a about the true proportion of all ..., in context. Never accept H0H_0, and claim cause only from a randomized experiment.
