Carrying Out a Test for a Population Mean or Population Mean Difference

Carry out a one-sample (or paired) t test by computing t = (x-bar − μ_0)/(s/√n) with n − 1 df, finding the p-value in the direction of H_a , and comparing it with α .

Practice 3: Analyze DataPractice 4: Interpret Results

Part 1 · Hook

Why this matters

The delivery sample came back: 45 deliveries, mean 32.4 minutes, standard deviation 6.8. That is 2.4 minutes over the advertised 30. Is that a real slowdown or the ordinary wobble of a random sample? One number, the t statistic, measures the gap in standard errors, and the t distribution turns it into a p-value.

Part 5 · Step by step

How it works, step by step

  1. Assume H0H_0 is true: the true mean is μ0\mu_0→sample means vary around μ0\mu_0 with standard error sn\frac{s}{\sqrt{n}}
  2. Measure the gap in standard errors→t=x¯−μ0s/nt = \frac{\bar{x} - \mu_0}{s/\sqrt{n}}, with df=n−1\text{df} = n - 1
  3. Find the area beyond tt in the direction of HaH_a→that area is the p-value (double the tail for ≠\ne)
  4. Compare the p-value with α\alpha→reject H0H_0 if p<αp < \alpha, fail to reject otherwise, and state the evidence for HaH_a in context

Part 6 · Key ideas

Key ideas

  • t statistic: t=x¯−μ0s/nt = \frac{\bar{x} - \mu_0}{s/\sqrt{n}} (paired: tt=x¯d−0sd/n= \frac{\bar{x}_d - 0}{s_d/\sqrt{n}}), df=n−1\text{df} = n - 1.
  • p-value: right tail for >>, left tail for <<, twice the smaller tail for ≠\ne. Table B brackets it; technology gives it exactly.
  • Conclusion: "Because the p-value of 0.0112 is less than 0.05, we reject H0H_0. There is convincing evidence that the true mean ... is greater than ..."
  • A large p-value is "not convincing evidence", never "accept H0H_0" or "the mean is μ0\mu_0".

Part 7 · Misconception

A common mistake

The wrong idea: A p-value of 0.0112 means there is a 1.12% chance that the null hypothesis is true.

What actually happens: The p-value is computed assuming H0H_0 is true. It is the probability of a sample mean at least as extreme as the one observed, by chance alone, when the true mean is μ0\mu_0.

Testing the delivery time claim

A company advertises a mean delivery time of 30 minutes in one city. A manager suspects the true mean is longer and takes a random sample of 45 of last month's thousands of deliveries (invented data). She tests H0:μ=30H_0: \mu = 30 against Ha:μ>30H_a: \mu > 30, where μ\mu is the true mean delivery time of all the company's deliveries in the city, at α=0.05\alpha = 0.05.

3. State the conclusion of the test at α=0.05\alpha = 0.05.

Write the conclusion in context.

Show the answer

Compare the p-value with alpha, then state the evidence for HaH_a in context, naming the parameter.

  • A full-credit answer: Because the p-value of 0.0112 is less than 0.05, we reject the null hypothesis. There is convincing evidence that the true mean delivery time of all the company's deliveries in the city is greater than 30 minutes.

4. Interpret the p-value of 0.0112 in context.

Write the conclusion in context.

Show the answer

A p-value is a probability computed assuming the null hypothesis is true: the chance of a sample result at least as extreme as the one observed, in the direction of HaH_a.

  • A full-credit answer: Assuming the true mean delivery time of all the company's deliveries in the city is 30 minutes, there is a 0.0112 probability of getting a sample mean of 32.4 minutes or more by chance in a random sample of 45 deliveries.

5. For the sleep study (H0:μ=8H_0: \mu = 8, Ha:μ≠8H_a: \mu \ne 8, p=0.080p = 0.080), state the conclusion at α=0.05\alpha = 0.05.

Write the conclusion in context.

Show the answer

0.080 is greater than 0.05, so fail to reject H0H_0. The data are not convincing evidence of a difference, which is not the same as showing the mean is 8.

  • A full-credit answer: Because the p-value of 0.080 is greater than 0.05, we fail to reject the null hypothesis. There is not convincing evidence that the true mean nightly sleep of students at the university is different from 8 hours.

6. A t test gives p=0.27p = 0.27 at α=0.05\alpha = 0.05. Which statement is correct?

  1. The data do not give convincing evidence against the null hypothesis
  2. The data show that the null hypothesis is true
  3. There is a 27% chance that the null hypothesis is true
  4. The sample mean equals the hypothesized mean
Show the answer

0.27>0.050.27 > 0.05: fail to reject H0H_0. That is "not convincing evidence", never "accept H0H_0".

  • Correct: The data do not give convincing evidence against the null hypothesis: A large p-value means results like these are common when H0H_0 is true, so there is not convincing evidence for HaH_a.
  • The data show that the null hypothesis is true: Failing to reject is not proof of H0H_0; the true mean could differ by an amount this sample could not detect.
  • There is a 27% chance that the null hypothesis is true: A p-value is computed assuming H0H_0 is true; it is not the probability of H0H_0.
  • The sample mean equals the hypothesized mean: The sample mean almost surely differs from μ0\mu_0; the difference is just not convincing.
