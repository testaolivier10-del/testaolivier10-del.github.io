Unit 4 · Topic 4.7 Beta

Constructing a Confidence Interval for the Difference Between Two Population Means

To estimate μ_1 − μ_2 from two independent samples or randomized groups, use (x-bar_1 − x-bar_2) ±t*√((s_1²)/(n_1) + (s_2²)/(n_2)) with technology df or the conservative df.

Practice 2: Collect DataPractice 3: Analyze DataPractice 4: Interpret Results

Part 1 · Hook

Why this matters

Twenty-eight volunteers learned new words, half in short sessions spread over a week and half in one long cram. A week later the spaced group recalled 5.7 more words on average. How big is the real advantage? A two-sample t interval turns the two groups' means and spreads into a range of plausible values for the true difference.

Part 5 · Step by step

How it works, step by step

  1. The parameter is μ1−μ2\mu_1 - \mu_2, the difference in true means→its point estimate is x¯1−x¯2\bar{x}_1 - \bar{x}_2
  2. Replace σ1\sigma_1 and σ2\sigma_2 with s1s_1 and s2s_2→the standard error is s12n1+s22n2\sqrt{\frac{s_1^2}{n_1} + \frac{s_2^2}{n_2}}
  3. Estimating two standard deviations adds uncertainty→use t∗t^*, with technology df (Welch) or the conservative df, the smaller of n1−1n_1 - 1 and n2−1n_2 - 1
  4. Check random, 10% and normal/large sample for each group→build (x¯1−x¯2)(\bar{x}_1 - \bar{x}_2)±t∗s12n1+s22n2\pm t^*\sqrt{\frac{s_1^2}{n_1} + \frac{s_2^2}{n_2}} and interpret it in context, order of subtraction stated

Part 6 · Key ideas

Key ideas

  • Two-sample t interval: (x¯1−x¯2)(\bar{x}_1 - \bar{x}_2)±t∗s12n1+s22n2\pm t^*\sqrt{\frac{s_1^2}{n_1} + \frac{s_2^2}{n_2}}.
  • Degrees of freedom: technology (Welch) gives a non-integer df; the conservative df is the smaller of n1−1n_1 - 1 and n2−1n_2 - 1, which gives a slightly wider interval. Both are accepted.
  • Conditions for each group: random (two independent random samples or random assignment), 10% (when sampling), normal/large sample.
  • Two independent groups use this interval; two linked measurements on each unit are paired and use a one-sample t interval on the differences.

Part 7 · Misconception

A common mistake

The wrong idea: Build a t interval for each group; if they overlap, there is no difference.

What actually happens: Two separate intervals answer a different question and can overlap even when the difference is clear. Estimate the difference directly with one two-sample t interval for μ1−μ2\mu_1 - \mu_2.

1. Which procedure estimates how much better spaced practice is, on average?

  1. A paired t interval for the mean difference
  2. A two-sample z interval for pS−pMp_S - p_M
  3. Two one-sample t intervals, one per group
  4. A two-sample t interval for μS−μM\mu_S - \mu_M
Show the answer

Parameter: μS−μM\mu_S - \mu_M, the true mean recall with spaced practice minus with massed practice, for volunteers like these. Procedure: a two-sample t interval.

  • A paired t interval for the mean difference: The volunteers were not paired: each was in one group only, so there are no natural pairs to subtract.
  • A two-sample z interval for pS−pMp_S - p_M: Words recalled is quantitative, so the parameter is a difference of means.
  • Two one-sample t intervals, one per group: Two separate intervals do not estimate the difference, and overlapping intervals are hard to judge.
  • Correct: A two-sample t interval for μS−μM\mu_S - \mu_M: Two separate groups from random assignment, a quantitative response and unknown population standard deviations: a two-sample t interval for the difference in true mean recall.

2. Each group has only 14 students. How is the normal/large sample condition met?

  1. The two groups together have 28 students
  2. Neither boxplot shows strong skew or outliers
  3. The volunteers were randomly assigned to the two groups
  4. 14 is less than 10% of the students at their school
Show the answer

Conditions: random assignment (random); no 10% check for an experiment; normal/large sample from the two boxplots, each without strong skew or outliers.

  • The two groups together have 28 students: Each group is checked on its own, and 14 is less than 30.
  • Correct: Neither boxplot shows strong skew or outliers: With n<30n < 30 in both groups, each group's graph must show no strong skew and no outliers. Both boxplots are roughly symmetric with no outliers.
  • The volunteers were randomly assigned to the two groups: That is the random condition. Shape is a separate check.
  • 14 is less than 10% of the students at their school: The 10% condition does not apply to random assignment, and it is about independence, not shape.

6. A two-sample t interval uses samples of n1=18n_1 = 18 and n2=25n_2 = 25. What are the conservative degrees of freedom?

Type a number. A table answer and a calculator answer are both accepted.

Show the answer

Conservative degrees of freedom: the smaller of n1−1=17n_1 - 1 = 17 and n2−1=24n_2 - 1 = 24, so 17. Technology would give a larger, non-integer df.

  • Answer: 17

7. Which study calls for a two-sample t interval rather than a paired t interval?

  1. Battery life of 30 phones of brand A and 30 phones of brand B, chosen at random
  2. Blood pressure of 30 patients measured before and after taking a new medicine for a month
  3. Tread wear on the left and right front tires of 30 cars
  4. Scores of 30 sets of twins, one twin per teaching method
Show the answer

Two-sample procedures need two independent groups. Paired procedures are for two linked measurements on each unit.

  • Correct: Battery life of 30 phones of brand A and 30 phones of brand B, chosen at random: Two separate random samples with no link between a phone of brand A and a phone of brand B.
  • Blood pressure of 30 patients measured before and after taking a new medicine for a month: Each patient gives two linked values: paired.
  • Tread wear on the left and right front tires of 30 cars: Left and right tires on the same car are a natural pair.
  • Scores of 30 sets of twins, one twin per teaching method: Twins are matched pairs.

Part 9 · Summary

Summary

To estimate μ1−μ2\mu_1 - \mu_2 from two independent samples or randomized groups, use (x¯1−x¯2)(\bar{x}_1 - \bar{x}_2)±t∗s12n1+s22n2\pm t^*\sqrt{\frac{s_1^2}{n_1} + \frac{s_2^2}{n_2}} with technology df or the conservative df. Check random, 10% and normal/large sample for each group, and interpret the interval as capturing the true difference in means, in the stated order.
