Sampling Distributions for Sample Proportions

For random samples, p-hat is centered at p with a spread that shrinks as n grows.

Practice 3: Analyze DataPractice 4: Interpret Results

Part 1 · Hook

Why this matters

A seed company prints "85% sprout" on every packet. A garden store plants 200 seeds and only 160 come up: 80%. Is the company exaggerating, or is that just the luck of the draw? To answer, you need to know how much a sample proportion of 200 seeds bounces around when the truth really is 85%.

Part 5 · Step by step

How it works, step by step

  1. Each sampled seed sprouts (1) or not (0), with a 0-or-1 spread of p(1−p)\sqrt{p(1 - p)}→averaging nn of them gives σp^\sigma_{\hat{p}}=p(1−p)n= \sqrt{\frac{p(1 - p)}{n}}, centered at μp^=p\mu_{\hat{p}} = p
  2. The sample is drawn without replacement→the formula is accurate only when nn is at most 10% of the population: the 10% condition
  3. At least 10 successes and 10 failures are expected (np≥10np \ge 10, n(1−p)≥10n(1 - p) \ge 10)→the sampling distribution is approximately normal: the large counts condition
  4. Both conditions hold→standardize with zz=p^−pσp^= \frac{\hat{p} - p}{\sigma_{\hat{p}}} and find probabilities about p^\hat{p} from the normal distribution

Part 6 · Key ideas

Key ideas

  • μp^=p\mu_{\hat{p}} = p and σp^\sigma_{\hat{p}}=p(1−p)n= \sqrt{\frac{p(1 - p)}{n}}, using the true pp.
  • 10% condition: nn at most 10% of the population, so the standard deviation formula holds when sampling without replacement.
  • Large counts condition: np≥10np \ge 10 and n(1−p)≥10n(1 - p) \ge 10, so the shape is approximately normal. Never "n≥30n \ge 30" for proportions.
  • When large counts fails, the distribution is skewed: right when pp is near 0, left when pp is near 1.
  • Four times the sample size halves σp^\sigma_{\hat{p}}.

Part 7 · Misconception

A common mistake

The wrong idea: The 10% condition is what makes the distribution of p^\hat{p} normal, so a sample under 10% of the population can always use a normal model.

What actually happens: The 10% condition protects the standard deviation formula (independence). The normal shape comes from the large counts condition, np≥10np \ge 10 and n(1−p)≥10n(1 - p) \ge 10. Check both, for different reasons.

Testing a seed company's sprouting rate

A seed company says that 85% of its tomato seeds sprout. A garden store plants a random sample of 200 seeds from a shipment of 50,000 seeds and records the proportion that sprout. Suppose the company's figure is correct, so p=0.85p = 0.85.

The setting for the sampling distribution of the sample proportion
QuantityValue
Population50,000 seeds in the shipment
True proportion that sprout, pp0.85
Sample size, nn200 seeds, chosen at random

1. What are the mean and standard deviation of the sampling distribution of p^\hat{p}, the proportion of the 200 seeds that sprout?

  1. Mean 0.85, standard deviation 0.0252
  2. Mean 0.85, standard deviation 0.000638
  3. Mean 0.85, standard deviation 0.357
  4. Mean 170, standard deviation 5.05
Show the answer

μp^=p=0.85\mu_{\hat{p}} = p = 0.85 and σp^\sigma_{\hat{p}}=p(1−p)n= \sqrt{\frac{p(1 - p)}{n}}=0.85(0.15)200= \sqrt{\frac{0.85(0.15)}{200}}=0.0252= 0.0252.

  • Correct: Mean 0.85, standard deviation 0.0252: Right: the mean is pp and the standard deviation is p(1−p)/n\sqrt{p(1 - p)/n}.
  • Mean 0.85, standard deviation 0.000638: That is p(1−p)n\frac{p(1 - p)}{n} without the square root: the variance, not the standard deviation.
  • Mean 0.85, standard deviation 0.357: That is p(1−p)\sqrt{p(1 - p)}, the spread for a single seed. Divide by nn inside the square root.
  • Mean 170, standard deviation 5.05: Those describe the count of seeds that sprout, npnp and np(1−p)\sqrt{np(1 - p)}, not the proportion.

2. Calculate the standard deviation of the sampling distribution of p^\hat{p}. Give four decimal places.

Type a number. A table answer and a calculator answer are both accepted.

Show the answer

σp^\sigma_{\hat{p}}=p(1−p)n= \sqrt{\frac{p(1 - p)}{n}}=0.85(0.15)200= \sqrt{\frac{0.85(0.15)}{200}}=0.0006375= \sqrt{0.0006375}=0.0252= 0.0252. The 10% condition holds (200 is less than 10% of 50,000), so this formula applies.

  • Answer: 0.0252

3. Why is it reasonable to use a normal model for the sampling distribution of p^\hat{p} here?

  1. Because n=200n = 200 is at least 30
  2. Because 200 is less than 10% of the 50,000 seeds in the shipment
  3. Both 200(0.85)=170200(0.85) = 170 and 200(0.15)=30200(0.15) = 30 are at least 10
  4. Because the seeds were chosen at random
Show the answer

The large counts condition: np=200(0.85)=170np = 200(0.85) = 170 and n(1−p)n(1 - p)=200(0.15)= 200(0.15)=30= 30 are both at least 10, so the distribution of p^\hat{p} is approximately normal.

  • Because n=200n = 200 is at least 30: The rule of 30 is for sample means. For proportions, check the expected numbers of successes and failures.
  • Because 200 is less than 10% of the 50,000 seeds in the shipment: That is the 10% condition. It justifies the standard deviation formula, not the normal shape.
  • Correct: Both 200(0.85)=170200(0.85) = 170 and 200(0.15)=30200(0.15) = 30 are at least 10: Right: the large counts condition, with both counts at least 10.
  • Because the seeds were chosen at random: Random selection is needed, but it does not make the shape normal. The shape depends on the expected numbers of successes and failures.

4. Assuming the company's figure is correct, find the probability that 80% or fewer of the 200 seeds sprout.

Type a number. A table answer and a calculator answer are both accepted.

Show the answer

p^\hat{p} is approximately normal with mean 0.85 and standard deviation 0.0252. zz=0.80−0.850.0252= \frac{0.80 - 0.85}{0.0252}=−1.98= -1.98, so P(p^≤0.80)P(\hat{p} \le 0.80)=P(Z≤−1.98)= P(Z \le -1.98)=0.024= 0.024. Technology (normal, mean 0.85, SD 0.0252, below 0.80) gives 0.0239.

  • Answer: 0.0238

5. A pollster keeps everything else the same but takes a random sample 4 times as large. By what factor does the standard deviation of p^\hat{p} change?

  1. It is divided by 4
  2. It is divided by 2
  3. It is multiplied by 2
  4. It does not change, because pp is the same
Show the answer

σp^\sigma_{\hat{p}}=p(1−p)n= \sqrt{\frac{p(1 - p)}{n}} has nn under a square root. Replacing nn with 4n4n divides it by 4=2\sqrt{4} = 2.

  • It is divided by 4: The sample size is inside a square root, so the effect is 4\sqrt{4}, not 4.
  • Correct: It is divided by 2: Right: 4n\sqrt{4n} is twice n\sqrt{n}.
  • It is multiplied by 2: A larger sample gives less spread, not more.
  • It does not change, because pp is the same: The formula depends on nn as well as pp.

6. About 3% of a factory's light bulbs are defective. An inspector takes a random sample of 250 bulbs from a large batch. What can you say about the shape of the sampling distribution of the proportion defective?

  1. Approximately normal, because 250 is a large sample
  2. Skewed left, because most bulbs are not defective
  3. Approximately normal, because n(1−p)=242.5n(1 - p) = 242.5
  4. Skewed right, because 250(0.03)=7.5250(0.03) = 7.5 is less than 10
Show the answer

np=250(0.03)=7.5np = 250(0.03) = 7.5, which is less than 10. Large counts fails, so the distribution is skewed right (it piles up near 0 and has a longer tail to the right).

  • Approximately normal, because 250 is a large sample: Size alone is not enough: the expected number of defective bulbs is only 7.5.
  • Skewed left, because most bulbs are not defective: With pp near 0, the proportion piles up near 0 and stretches to the right: skewed right.
  • Approximately normal, because n(1−p)=242.5n(1 - p) = 242.5: Both counts must be at least 10, and np=7.5np = 7.5 is not.
  • Correct: Skewed right, because 250(0.03)=7.5250(0.03) = 7.5 is less than 10: Right: large counts fails for the defective bulbs.

7. The garden store plants a random sample of 200 seeds and only 78% sprout. Assuming 85% of all the company's seeds sprout, P(p^≤0.78)=0.0028P(\hat{p} \le 0.78) = 0.0028. Build a sentence that uses this probability to judge the company's claim.

Write the conclusion in context.

Show the answer

A probability is useful only when you tie it back to the claim. If the claim were true, 78% or fewer sprouting would happen in about 3 of every 1,000 samples, so it would be very unusual. That casts doubt on the claim.

  • A full-credit answer: Assuming 85% of all the company's seeds sprout, a sample proportion of 0.78 or less in a random sample of 200 seeds would be very unusual (probability 0.0028), so this result gives reason to doubt the company's claim.

Part 9 · Summary

Summary

For random samples, p^\hat{p} is centered at pp with a spread that shrinks as nn grows. It has mean pp and standard deviation p(1−p)/n\sqrt{p(1 - p)/n} (accurate when the sample is at most 10% of the population) and is approximately normal when np≥10np \ge 10 and n(1−p)≥10n(1 - p) \ge 10. Then zz=p^−pσp^= \frac{\hat{p} - p}{\sigma_{\hat{p}}} turns a question about a sample proportion into a normal probability, which you can tie back to a claim about pp.
