Sampling Distributions for the Difference Between Sample Proportions

For two independent random samples, p-hat_1 − p-hat_2 is centered at p_1 − p_2 and has standard deviation √((p_1(1 − p_1))/(n_1) + (p_2(1 − p_2))/(n_2)) : the variances add.

Practice 3: Analyze DataPractice 4: Interpret Results

Part 1 · Hook

Why this matters

A district says 70% of its high schoolers ride the bus but only 55% of its middle schoolers do. A reporter checks with two random samples and gets a gap of 0.02 instead of 0.15. Is the district wrong, or is that just what samples do? To answer, you need to know how a difference of two sample proportions behaves from sample to sample.

Part 5 · Step by step

How it works, step by step

  1. Take a random sample from each of two populations, independently→each sample proportion has its own chance error, and so does their difference p^1−p^2\hat{p}_1 - \hat{p}_2
  2. Each sample proportion is centered at its true proportion→the difference is centered at p1−p2p_1 - p_2, so it is an unbiased estimator of the true difference
  3. The two chance errors come from separate samples and do not cancel→the variances add: σ2\sigma^2=p1(1−p1)n1= \frac{p_1(1-p_1)}{n_1}+p2(1−p2)n2+ \frac{p_2(1-p_2)}{n_2}, and the standard deviation is its square root
  4. Both samples have at least 10 expected successes and 10 expected failures→the sampling distribution is approximately normal, so z-scores and normal areas give probabilities

Part 6 · Key ideas

Key ideas

  • Mean: μp^1−p^2\mu_{\hat{p}_1 - \hat{p}_2}=p1−p2= p_1 - p_2.
  • Standard deviation: σp^1−p^2\sigma_{\hat{p}_1 - \hat{p}_2}=p1(1−p1)n1+p2(1−p2)n2= \sqrt{\frac{p_1(1-p_1)}{n_1} + \frac{p_2(1-p_2)}{n_2}}. Add variances, never standard deviations.
  • Shape: approximately normal when n1p1n_1 p_1, n1(1−p1)n_1(1-p_1), n2p2n_2 p_2 and n2(1−p2)n_2(1-p_2) are each at least 10.
  • Conditions: two independent random samples (or two randomly assigned groups), and each sample less than 10% of its population when sampling without replacement.

Part 7 · Misconception

A common mistake

The wrong idea: Subtracting the sample proportions means you subtract their standard deviations too, so the difference varies less than either one.

What actually happens: Each sample brings its own chance error, and the errors add up rather than cancel. The variances add, so a difference varies more than either sample proportion alone.

Bus riders in two school levels

A school district knows from its bus card records that 70% of its high school students and 55% of its middle school students ride a district bus at least once a week. A reporter takes independent random samples of 100 high school students and 80 middle school students and finds the sample proportion of bus riders in each, p^H\hat{p}_H and p^M\hat{p}_M. (Invented data.)
Population proportions and sample sizes
GroupProportion who rideSample size
High school0.70100
Middle school0.5580

1. What is the mean of the sampling distribution of p^H−p^M\hat{p}_H - \hat{p}_M?

  1. 0.6250.625
  2. 1.271.27
  3. 00
  4. 0.150.15
Show the answer

The difference p^H−p^M\hat{p}_H - \hat{p}_M is an unbiased estimator of pH−pM=0.15p_H - p_M = 0.15.

  • 0.6250.625: That averages the two proportions. The distribution of a difference is centered at the difference, 0.70−0.550.70 - 0.55.
  • 1.271.27: That divides 0.70 by 0.55. A difference is centered at pH−pMp_H - p_M, a subtraction, not a ratio.
  • 00: Zero would be the mean only if the two population proportions were equal. Here they differ by 0.15.
  • Correct: 0.150.15: Right: the mean of a difference in sample proportions is the difference in the population proportions, 0.70−0.55=0.150.70 - 0.55 = 0.15.

2. What is the standard deviation of the sampling distribution of p^H−p^M\hat{p}_H - \hat{p}_M? Give three decimal places.

Type a number. A table answer and a calculator answer are both accepted.

Show the answer

σ\sigma=0.70(0.30)100+0.55(0.45)80= \sqrt{\frac{0.70(0.30)}{100} + \frac{0.55(0.45)}{80}}=0.0021+0.00309= \sqrt{0.0021 + 0.00309}=0.072= 0.072.

  • Answer: 0.072

3. Which check shows that the sampling distribution of p^H−p^M\hat{p}_H - \hat{p}_M is approximately normal?

  1. Both population proportions, 0.70 and 0.55, are above 0.5
  2. Both samples were selected at random from the district
  3. 100(0.70)=70100(0.70) = 70, 100(0.30)=30100(0.30) = 30, 80(0.55)=4480(0.55) = 44 and 80(0.45)=3680(0.45) = 36 are each at least 10
  4. The combined sample size, 100+80=180100 + 80 = 180, is at least 30
Show the answer

Approximate normality needs at least 10 expected successes and 10 expected failures in each sample.

  • Both population proportions, 0.70 and 0.55, are above 0.5: Proportions above 0.5 do not make a shape normal. The counts npn p and n(1−p)n(1 - p) in each sample must reach 10.
  • Both samples were selected at random from the district: Random selection lets you generalize, but it says nothing about the shape. The shape comes from the large counts condition.
  • Correct: 100(0.70)=70100(0.70) = 70, 100(0.30)=30100(0.30) = 30, 80(0.55)=4480(0.55) = 44 and 80(0.45)=3680(0.45) = 36 are each at least 10: Right: the large counts condition is checked for both samples, successes and failures, four counts in all.
  • The combined sample size, 100+80=180100 + 80 = 180, is at least 30: Comparing nn with 30 is a rule for sample means. For proportions, check the four counts n1p1n_1 p_1, n1(1−p1)n_1(1 - p_1), n2p2n_2 p_2, n2(1−p2)n_2(1 - p_2) against 10.

Type a number. A table answer and a calculator answer are both accepted.

Show the answer

zz=0.05−0.150.072= \frac{0.05 - 0.15}{0.072}=−1.39= -1.39, so P(p^H−p^M≤0.05)P(\hat{p}_H - \hat{p}_M \le 0.05)=0.0826= 0.0826 (the area to the left of zz under the standard normal curve).

5. In a large city, 35% of adults under 30 and 25% of adults 30 or older use a bike share at least once a month. Independent random samples of 250 younger adults and 200 older adults are taken. Write the expression for the standard deviation of p^young−p^old\hat{p}_{\text{young}} - \hat{p}_{\text{old}}, then give its value to three decimal places.

Type the expression as on the exam, with the values substituted.

Show the answer

σ\sigma=0.35(0.65)250+0.25(0.75)200= \sqrt{\frac{0.35(0.65)}{250} + \frac{0.25(0.75)}{200}}=0.00091+0.00094= \sqrt{0.00091 + 0.00094}=0.043= 0.043.

  • Answer: 0.35⋅0.65250+0.25⋅0.75200\sqrt{\frac{0.35 \cdot 0.65}{250} + \frac{0.25 \cdot 0.75}{200}}

6. Which change makes the standard deviation of p^1−p^2\hat{p}_1 - \hat{p}_2 smaller, with the population proportions kept the same?

  1. Using a larger difference between the populations
  2. Combining the two samples into one
  3. Subtracting in the other order, p^2−p^1\hat{p}_2 - \hat{p}_1
  4. Taking larger samples from both populations
Show the answer

Both terms p(1−p)n\frac{p(1-p)}{n} shrink as their nn grows.

  • Using a larger difference between the populations: The question keeps the proportions fixed. The spread is lowered by larger samples.
  • Combining the two samples into one: One combined sample gives one proportion. It no longer measures a difference between two groups.
  • Subtracting in the other order, p^2−p^1\hat{p}_2 - \hat{p}_1: Switching the order changes the sign of the mean, not the spread. The variances add either way.
  • Correct: Taking larger samples from both populations: Right: each variance term has its sample size in the denominator, so larger samples shrink both terms.

Part 9 · Summary

Summary

For two independent random samples, p^1−p^2\hat{p}_1 - \hat{p}_2 is centered at p1−p2p_1 - p_2 and has standard deviation p1(1−p1)n1+p2(1−p2)n2\sqrt{\frac{p_1(1-p_1)}{n_1} + \frac{p_2(1-p_2)}{n_2}}: the variances add. When all four large counts are at least 10, its distribution is approximately normal, so you can find probabilities with z-scores.
