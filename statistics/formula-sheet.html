The reference sheet, explained

The exam gives you a reference sheet: the formulas below and three tables. Knowing what each formula means, and when to use it, is what earns the points. Written in our own words and layout.

Three habits that save points

  • Show the expression, then the numbers, then the answer. Write the formula's structure, substitute the values, then give the result. A calculator result alone is not work.
  • Name the distribution or the procedure. "X is binomial with $n = 10$ and $p = 0.3$", "a one-sample z test for a proportion", with the boundary and the direction.
  • Table or technology, both count. A p-value from Table B written as a range and the same p-value from a calculator are both right; keep full precision until the end.

Reading the tables

  • Table A: standard normal probabilities. The area to the left of zz, for zz from −3.4-3.4 to 3.43.4 in steps of 0.01. Round zz to two decimal places, find the row for the first decimal and the column for the second. For an area to the right, subtract from 1.
  • Table B: t critical values. One row per degrees of freedom (1 to 30, then 40, 50, 60, 80, 100, 1000 and infinity); columns are upper-tail probabilities, with confidence levels along the bottom. When your df is not a row, use the next lower row. A t statistic between two columns gives a range for the p-value.
  • Table C: chi-square critical values. One row per degrees of freedom (1 to 30, then 40 to 100); columns are upper-tail probabilities. A statistic between two columns gives a range for the p-value, such as "between 0.025 and 0.05".

Not on the sheet: know these

  • z=x−μσz = \frac{x - \mu}{\sigma}. A z-score: how many standard deviations a value is from the mean. Comparisons of the Distributions for One Quantitative Variable
  • 1.5×IQR1.5 \times \text{IQR}. The outlier rule: a value more than 1.5×IQR1.5 \times \text{IQR} below Q1Q_1 or above Q3Q_3 is an outlier. Summary Statistics for One Quantitative Variable
  • The empirical rule. In a normal distribution, about 68%, 95% and 99.7% of values fall within 1, 2 and 3 standard deviations of the mean. The Normal Distribution
  • Conditions. Random sample or random assignment; the 10% condition when sampling without replacement; large counts (at least 10 successes and 10 failures) for proportions; a normal population or a large sample for means; expected counts at least 5 for chi-square. Sampling Distributions for Sample Proportions
  • Degrees of freedom. n−1n - 1 for one-sample and paired t; technology df (or the smaller n−1n - 1) for two-sample t; (rows−1)(columns−1)(\text{rows} - 1)(\text{columns} - 1) for chi-square. Carrying Out a Chi-Square Test for Homogeneity or Independence
  • Slope and correlation. The 2026 sheet gives only y^=a+bx\hat{y} = a + bx: read aa, bb, rr, r2r^2 and ss from computer output, and interpret each in context. Least-Squares Regression
