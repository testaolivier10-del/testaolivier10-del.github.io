Regression lab

Fit a model to the data and let the residuals tell you if it fits.

About this tool

Fit a model to real-looking data the way a graphing calculator does: linear, quadratic, cubic, quartic, exponential, logarithmic or sinusoidal. The model is drawn through the points, each residual is a short line from the point to the model, and the residual plot sits underneath. Residuals that scatter with no pattern mean the model fits the shape of the data; a curve or a wave in them means another model will do better. Drag the prediction line to read the model's value to three decimal places.

How the fits are found

  • Least squares. Each model's constants make the sum of the squared residuals as small as possible, the way the calculator's regressions do.
  • Residual = actual yy − predicted yy. Above the model is positive, below is negative.
  • Exponential (y=a⋅bxy = a \cdot b^x) is fit as a line through (x,ln⁡y)(x, \ln y), and logarithmic (y=a+bln⁡xy = a + b\ln x) as a line through (ln⁡x,y)(\ln x, y), as ExpReg and LnReg do; so the data need y>0y > 0 or x>0x > 0.
  • Sinusoidal (y=asin⁡(bx+c)+dy = a\sin(bx + c) + d): the frequency bb is found by search, then aa, cc and dd by least squares. Use radians.
  • Three decimals. Constants are stored, not rounded: a prediction made from rounded constants can miss the third decimal. Round only the final answer.
  • Pattern test. The residual plot is read by a runs test on the signs of the residuals in order of xx: long runs of one sign mean a pattern.
