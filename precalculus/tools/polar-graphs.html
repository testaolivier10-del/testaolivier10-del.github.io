Polar graphs

Sweep the angle and watch the polar curve draw itself.

About this tool

A polar curve r=f(θ)r = f(\theta) drawn as the angle sweeps, beside the graph of rr against θ\theta. The current point and its distance from the origin are the same orange in both views, so you can see a value of rr become a distance along a ray. The shaded bands show where the point moves away from the origin and where it moves toward it. In Interval, a secant line on the graph of rr against θ\theta gives the average rate of change of rr.

How the pictures are drawn

  • Polar to rectangular: x=rcos⁡θx = r\cos\theta and y=rsin⁡θy = r\sin\theta; back again, r=x2+y2r = \sqrt{x^2 + y^2} and tan⁡θ=yx\tan\theta = \frac{y}{x} with θ\theta chosen in the point's quadrant.
  • Negative r: a point with r<0r < 0 is |r||r| units from the origin on the ray opposite the angle. Both views draw those parts dashed.
  • Distance from the origin is |r||r|. It increases where r>0r > 0 and rr is increasing, or where r<0r < 0 and rr is decreasing; it decreases in the other two cases.
  • Average rate of change of rr on [θ1,θ2][\theta_1, \theta_2] is r(θ2)−r(θ1)θ2−θ1\frac{r(\theta_2) - r(\theta_1)}{\theta_2 - \theta_1}, the slope of the secant line on the graph of rr against θ\theta, in units of rr per radian.
  • Shapes: r=acos⁡(nθ)r = a\cos(n\theta) is a rose with nn petals when nn is odd and 2n2n when nn is even; r=a+bcos⁡θr = a + b\cos\theta has an inner loop when |a|<|b||a| < |b| and is a cardioid when |a|=|b||a| = |b|.
  • Keyboard: on the picture, the arrow keys turn the angle by π24\frac{\pi}{24} (Shift: π6\frac{\pi}{6}), Home and End go to 00 and 2π2\pi.
