Rate of change

See average rates as secant slopes, then read concavity from them.

About this tool

An average rate of change is the slope of a secant line. Here the secants are drawn over equal intervals, side by side, each in its own color, with its slope beside it and as a bar below the graph. When the bars grow, the rate of change is increasing and the graph is concave up; when they shrink, it is concave down. In Estimate, use one secant line to estimate a value and see whether the estimate lands over or under the curve, and why.

How the rates are found

  • Average rate of change of ff on [a,b][a, b]: f(b)−f(a)b−a\frac{f(b) - f(a)}{b - a}, the slope of the secant line through (a,f(a))(a, f(a)) and (b,f(b))(b, f(b)), in output units per input unit.
  • Equal intervals. Compare rates only over intervals of the same length. If they increase from each interval to the next, the rate of change of ff is increasing and ff is concave up there; if they decrease, concave down. Constant rates mean a line.
  • Estimate with a secant line: use a point on that line, f(a)+m(x−a)f(a) + m(x - a), with mm the slope.
  • Over or under: on an interval where ff is concave up, the graph lies below the secant line, so the estimate is too big (an overestimate); concave down, it lies above and the estimate is an underestimate. Whether ff goes up or down does not decide it.
  • Keyboard: the points on the graph take the arrow keys (Shift for bigger steps).
