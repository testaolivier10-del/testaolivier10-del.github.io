Functions, Graphs and Notation

A function gives each input one output, written f(input) = output . The domain is every allowed input (no zero denominators, no square roots of negatives); the range is every output. Zeros are inputs with output 0 ; the y -intercept is (0, f(0)) . Piecewise-defined functions use the piece whose condition the input meets.

  • Function: each input gives exactly one output. f(3)=7f(3) = 7 means the input 33 gives the output 77.
  • Domain: the inputs a function accepts. Range: the outputs it produces.
  • Zero: an input with output 00, an xx-intercept of the graph. yy-intercept: the point (0,f(0))(0, f(0)).
  • To evaluate, replace every xx with the input in parentheses: f(−2)f(-2), f(x+1)f(x + 1).

f(3) = 7 names one point (3, 7) on the graph of f → the domain leaves out inputs that make a denominator 0 or a square root's inside negative → a is a zero of f and the graph meets the x -axis at (a, 0) → first find the piece whose condition holds for the input, then use only that piece

function
A rule that gives each input exactly one output. f(3)=7f(3) = 7 means the input 33 gives the output 77.
domain
The set of inputs a function accepts. For a formula, every real number that gives a real output: no zero denominators and no square roots of negative numbers.
range of a function
The set of outputs a function produces as the input runs through its domain.
graph of a function
All the points (x,f(x))(x, f(x)) for xx in the domain, drawn in the plane: inputs across, outputs up.
zero of a function
An input aa with f(a)=0f(a) = 0. On the graph, a zero is an xx-intercept, the point (a,0)(a, 0).
y-intercept
The point where a graph crosses the yy-axis, (0,f(0))(0, f(0)). A function has at most one.
piecewise-defined function
A function that uses different formulas on different parts of its domain. To evaluate it, find the piece whose condition the input meets.

Interval Notation and Inequalities

Interval notation writes a stretch of the number line with its two ends: brackets for included ends, parentheses for excluded ends and for ±∞ , and ∪ to join pieces. Solve linear inequalities like equations, flipping the sign whenever you multiply or divide by a negative, and check one value.

  • [a,b][a, b] includes both ends; (a,b)(a, b) includes neither; [a,b)[a, b) includes aa but not bb.
  • ∞\infty is not a number you reach, so it always gets a parenthesis: [2,∞)[2, \infty).
  • Solve a linear inequality like an equation, but flip the sign when you multiply or divide by a negative.
  • For a compound inequality a≤2x+3<ba \le 2x + 3 < b, do each step to all three parts.

it gets a bracket, [ or ] , and a closed dot on a graph → it gets a parenthesis; ∞ and −∞ always take parentheses → the inequality sign flips, because negation reverses order ( 2 < 3 but −2 > −3 ) → it is two intervals joined by a union: (−∞, a)∪(a, ∞)

interval notation
A way to write a stretch of the number line by its ends: a bracket for an included end, a parenthesis for an excluded end or for ±∞\pm\infty. [−1,4)[-1, 4) means −1≤x<4-1 \le x < 4. A closed interval includes both ends; an open interval includes neither.
linear inequality
A statement that one quantity is less than (or greater than) another, such as 3−2x>73 - 2x > 7. A linear inequality, with the variable to the first power, is solved like an equation, but the sign flips when you multiply or divide both sides by a negative number.

Lines, Slope and Linear Equations

Slope is the change in y divided by the change in x , the same between any two points of a line. Point-slope form y − y_1 = m(x − x_1) builds a line from one point and the slope, and y = y_1 + m(x − x_1) gives its value at any x . In context, slope is output units per input unit.

  • Slope mm=change in ychange in x= \frac{\text{change in } y}{\text{change in } x}: positive rises, negative falls, 00 is horizontal, a vertical line has none.
  • Point-slope form y−y1=m(x−x1)y - y_1 = m(x - x_1); slope-intercept form y=mx+by = mx + b with bb the yy-intercept.
  • In context the slope has units of output per input, like dollars per minute.

its slope is (y_2 − y_1)/(x_2 − x_1) , rise over run, subtracted in the same order → point-slope form y − y_1 = m(x − x_1) gives the line, and y = y_1 + m(x − x_1) gives any value on it → y changes by m⋅Δx , the same for every equal step: a line has a constant rate of change

slope
The change in yy divided by the change in xx between two points of a line, y2−y1x2−x1\frac{y_2 - y_1}{x_2 - x_1}. A line has the same slope between any two of its points.
point-slope form
Point-slope form y−y1=m(x−x1)y - y_1 = m(x - x_1) is the line through (x1,y1)(x_1, y_1) with slope mm; slope-intercept form y=mx+by = mx + b has slope mm and yy-intercept (0,b)(0, b).

Quadratics: Zeros and the Quadratic Formula

A quadratic f(x) = ax² + bx + c graphs as a parabola with its vertex at x = −b/2a . Solve f(x) = 0 by factors, by square roots or by the quadratic formula; the discriminant b² − 4ac counts the real zeros. Completing the square gives vertex form a(x − h)² + k , which shows the vertex (h, k) .

  • A quadratic function is f(x)=ax2+bx+cf(x) = ax^2 + bx + c with a≠0a \ne 0; its graph is a parabola.
  • Solve by factors, by square roots ((x−1)2=4(x - 1)^2 = 4 gives x−1=±2x - 1 = \pm 2) or by the quadratic formula.
  • The vertex is at x=−b2ax = -\frac{b}{2a}; completing the square gives vertex form a(x−h)2+ka(x - h)^2 + k.

each factor equal to 0 gives a solution → the quadratic formula x = (−b ± √(b² − 4ac))/2a gives the solutions → there are two, one or no real zeros → the vertex is (h, k) , the axis is x = h , and a > 0 opens up, a < 0 opens down

quadratic function
A function f(x)=ax2+bx+cf(x) = ax^2 + bx + c with a≠0a \ne 0. Its graph is a parabola.
parabola
The U-shaped graph of a quadratic function; it opens up when a>0a > 0 and down when a<0a < 0, and is symmetric about the vertical line through its vertex.
vertex of a parabola
The turning point of a parabola, at x=−b2ax = -\frac{b}{2a}. In vertex form y=a(x−h)2+ky = a(x - h)^2 + k the vertex is (h,k)(h, k).
quadratic formula
The solutions of ax2+bx+c=0ax^2 + bx + c = 0 are xx=−b±b2−4ac2a= \frac{-b \pm \sqrt{b^2 - 4ac}}{2a}. The discriminant b2−4acb^2 - 4ac tells how many are real: two, one or none. Completing the square rewrites a quadratic in vertex form.

Polynomial Arithmetic and Factoring

Multiply polynomials by distributing every term and combining like terms. Factor by taking out a common factor first, then using a difference of squares a² − b² = (a − b)(a + b) , a sum or difference of cubes, or grouping. A product equals 0 when a factor does, and p(a) = 0 means x − a is a factor.

  • A polynomial is a sum of terms axnax^n with whole-number powers, like x3+x2−7x+2x^3 + x^2 - 7x + 2.
  • Multiply by distributing every term of one factor over every term of the other, then combine like terms.
  • Factor in order: common factor, then patterns (difference of squares, sum or difference of cubes), then grouping.

take it out first: x³ − 9x = x(x² − 9) → use the pattern: x² − 9 = (x − 3)(x + 3) → factor by grouping: x²(x + 2) − 4(x + 2) = (x² − 4)(x + 2) → each factor equal to 0 gives a solution, and p(a) = 0 means x − a is a factor

polynomial
A sum of terms axnax^n with whole-number powers nn, such as x3+x2−7x+2x^3 + x^2 - 7x + 2.
factoring
Writing an expression as a product: take out a common factor, use patterns such as the difference of squares a2−b2=(a−b)(a+b)a^2 - b^2 = (a - b)(a + b) and the sum or difference of cubes, or factor by grouping.

Complex Numbers

The imaginary unit i has i² = −1 , so √(−b²) = bi . Complex numbers a + bi add and multiply like binomials, with i² replaced by −1 . A quadratic with real coefficients and a negative discriminant has two complex solutions that are complex conjugates, a ± bi .

  • The imaginary unit ii has i2=−1i^2 = -1. A complex number is a+bia + bi with real aa (real part) and bb (imaginary part).
  • The complex conjugate of a+bia + bi is a−bia - bi, and (a+bi)(a−bi)(a + bi)(a - bi)=a2+b2= a^2 + b^2, a real number.
  • Powers of ii repeat every four: i,−1,−i,1i, -1, -i, 1.

square roots of negatives exist: √(−16) = 4i → work like binomials, then replace i² with −1 → its two solutions are complex conjugates a + bi and a − bi

complex number
A number a+bia + bi, where aa and bb are real and ii is the imaginary unit, with i2=−1i^2 = -1. aa is the real part and bb the imaginary part.
complex conjugate
The complex conjugate of a+bia + bi is a−bia - bi. Their product, a2+b2a^2 + b^2, is real.

Rational Expressions

A rational expression is a quotient of polynomials, undefined where its denominator is 0 . Factor, then cancel common factors only, keeping the original exclusions. Add with a common denominator, multiply across, divide by the reciprocal, and split a sum in the numerator term by term.

  • A rational expression is a quotient of two polynomials, like x−3x−2\frac{x - 3}{x - 2}. It is undefined where its denominator is 00.
  • Factor first, then cancel common factors, never terms. Keep the excluded inputs of the original.
  • Add with a common denominator; multiply straight across; divide by multiplying by the reciprocal.

a is excluded from the domain, before and after any simplifying → it cancels, but the excluded input stays excluded → rewrite both over a common denominator, then combine the numerators → each term can be divided by the denominator separately: (2x² + 3)/x = 2x + 3/x

rational expression
A quotient of two polynomials, p(x)q(x)\frac{p(x)}{q(x)}, undefined where q(x)=0q(x) = 0. Simplify by cancelling common factors; the inputs excluded from the original stay excluded.

Systems of Equations

Solve a system by substitution or elimination, removing one variable at a time. Fitting a model through data points means writing one equation per point and solving for the constants, stored at full precision.

  • A solution of a system makes every equation true at once; for two lines it is where they cross.
  • To fit y=ax2+bx+cy = ax^2 + bx + c to three points, substitute each point to get three equations in aa, bb and cc.
  • With a calculator, store each constant; rounding early spoils every value that comes after.

a system with as many equations as unknowns → one equation in one variable, which you solve → all the unknowns, at full precision

system of equations
Two or more equations with the same unknowns, to be solved together. A solution makes every equation true at once. Two lines that cross have one solution; parallel lines have none; the same line has infinitely many.

Exponent and Radical Rules

Same base: add exponents to multiply, subtract to divide, multiply exponents for a power of a power. b^(−n) is a reciprocal and b^(m/n) is a root then a power. Writing both sides with one base turns 4^x = 32 into 2x = 5 .

  • bm⋅bn=bm+nb^m \cdot b^n = b^{m + n}, bmbn=bm−n\frac{b^m}{b^n} = b^{m - n}, (bm)n=bmn(b^m)^n = b^{mn}, (ab)n=anbn(ab)^n = a^n b^n, b0=1b^0 = 1.
  • b−n=1bnb^{-n} = \frac{1}{b^n}: a negative exponent is a reciprocal, never a negative number.
  • bm/n=(bn)mb^{m/n} = (\sqrt[n]{b})^m: the denominator is the root, the numerator the power. Take the root first to keep numbers small.

add the exponents: b^mb^n = b^(m + n) → multiply the exponents: (b^m)^n = b^mn → take a reciprocal ( b^(−n) = 1/(b^n) ) or a root ( b^(1/n) = n√b )

exponent rules
The laws of exponents for a positive base bb: bm⋅bn=bm+nb^m \cdot b^n = b^{m + n}, bmbn=bm−n\frac{b^m}{b^n} = b^{m - n}, (bm)n=bmn(b^m)^n = b^{mn}, b0=1b^0 = 1, a negative exponent b−n=1bnb^{-n} = \frac{1}{b^n}, and a rational exponent bm/n=(bn)mb^{m/n} = (\sqrt[n]{b})^m.
radical
A root written with the radical sign, such as the square root b\sqrt{b} or the cube root b3\sqrt[3]{b}. The nnth root of bb is the number whose nnth power is bb, and it equals the rational exponent b1/nb^{1/n}.

Right-Triangle Trigonometry

In a right triangle, name the legs from the angle θ : opposite and adjacent. Then sin θ = opposite/hypotenuse , cos θ = adjacent/hypotenuse and tan θ = opposite/adjacent . The ratios depend only on the angle, so one angle and one side are enough to find the other sides.

  • The hypotenuse is the longest side, so sin⁡θ\sin\theta and cos⁡θ\cos\theta are between 00 and 11 for an acute angle.
  • tan⁡θ\tan\theta=oppositeadjacent= \frac{\text{opposite}}{\text{adjacent}} is rise over run: the slope of the hypotenuse when the adjacent side lies flat.
  • To find a side: write the ratio, put in the known values, then multiply (unknown on top) or divide (unknown on the bottom).

the leg across from θ is the opposite side, the leg touching θ is the adjacent side, and the side across from the right angle is the hypotenuse → the ratios of their sides are the same, whatever the triangle's size, so each ratio depends only on θ → sin θ = opposite/hypotenuse , cos θ = adjacent/hypotenuse , tan θ = opposite/adjacent (SOH CAH TOA) → pick the ratio that links the known side to the unknown one, write the equation, and solve it

trigonometric ratio
A ratio of two sides of a right triangle, named from an acute angle θ\theta: sin⁡θ\sin\theta=oppositehypotenuse= \frac{\text{opposite}}{\text{hypotenuse}}, cos⁡θ\cos\theta=adjacenthypotenuse= \frac{\text{adjacent}}{\text{hypotenuse}}, tan⁡θ\tan\theta=oppositeadjacent= \frac{\text{opposite}}{\text{adjacent}} (SOH CAH TOA). It depends only on the angle, not on the size of the triangle.
hypotenuse
The side of a right triangle across from the right angle, and its longest side. The other two sides, the legs, are named from an acute angle θ\theta: the opposite side is across from θ\theta, the adjacent side touches θ\theta.

Circles, Arcs and Special Triangles

The 45-45-90 triangle has sides 1:1:√2 and the 30-60-90 triangle 1:√3:2 ; they give exact sines and cosines at 30^(∘) , 45^(∘) and 60^(∘) . An arc cut off by a central angle of θ degrees is the fraction θ/360 of the circumference 2πr . The circle with center (h, k) and radius r is (x − h)² + (y − k)² = r² .

  • 45-45-90: sides 1:1:21 : 1 : \sqrt{2}. 30-60-90: sides 1:3:21 : \sqrt{3} : 2, with the short leg across from 30∘30^\circ.
  • So sin⁡30∘=12\sin 30^\circ = \frac{1}{2}, cos⁡30∘=32\cos 30^\circ = \frac{\sqrt{3}}{2}, sin⁡45∘\sin 45^\circ=cos⁡45∘= \cos 45^\circ=22= \frac{\sqrt{2}}{2}, sin⁡60∘=32\sin 60^\circ = \frac{\sqrt{3}}{2}, cos⁡60∘=12\cos 60^\circ = \frac{1}{2}.
  • Arc length is a fraction of the circumference: s=θ360⋅2πrs = \frac{\theta}{360} \cdot 2\pi r for an angle of θ\theta degrees.
  • The circle with center (h,k)(h, k) and radius rr is (x−h)2+(y−k)2=r2(x - h)^2 + (y - k)^2 = r^2. The right side is r2r^2, not rr.

with legs 1 , the hypotenuse is √(1² + 1²) = √2 → you get a 30-60-90 triangle with short leg 1 , hypotenuse 2 and long leg √(2² − 1²) = √3 → its arc has length s = θ/360 ⋅2πr → the Pythagorean theorem gives its equation, (x − h)² + (y − k)² = r²

special right triangle
One of the two right triangles whose sides are known exactly from one side: the 45-45-90 triangle, with sides in the ratio 1:1:21 : 1 : \sqrt{2}, and the 30-60-90 triangle, with sides 1:3:21 : \sqrt{3} : 2 (the short leg across from 30∘30^\circ).
arc length
The length of a piece of a circle. A central angle of θ\theta degrees cuts off the fraction θ360\frac{\theta}{360} of the circumference, so the arc has length s=θ360⋅2πrs = \frac{\theta}{360} \cdot 2\pi r.
equation of a circle
The equation (x−h)2+(y−k)2=r2(x - h)^2 + (y - k)^2 = r^2 of the circle with center (h,k)(h, k) and radius rr: it says the point (x,y)(x, y) is at distance rr from the center. The circle of radius 1 centered at the origin is x2+y2=1x^2 + y^2 = 1.
