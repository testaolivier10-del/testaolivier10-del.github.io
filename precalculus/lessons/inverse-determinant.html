Unit 4 · Topic 4.11

The Inverse and Determinant of a Matrix

For a 2×2 matrix the determinant ad − bc is the signed area of the parallelogram made by its columns.

Practice 1: Procedural and Symbolic Fluency

Beyond the exam. The AP® Precalculus exam does not test this unit. Many classes teach it, and it prepares you for calculus.

Part 1 · Hook

Why this matters

A secret message is scrambled by multiplying blocks of numbers by a matrix. To read it, you need the matrix that undoes the scrambling. One number, the determinant, tells you whether such an undo matrix exists, and it also measures how much the matrix stretches areas.

Part 5 · Step by step

How it works, step by step

  1. For A=[abcd]A = \left[ \begin{matrix} a & b \\ c & d \end{matrix} \right], compute ad−bcad - bc→the determinant det⁡A\det A
  2. Draw the two columns of AA as vectors from the origin→they make a parallelogram whose area is |det⁡A|\left| \det A \right|
  3. det⁡A≠0\det A \ne 0→AA has an inverse: swap aa and dd, negate bb and cc, divide by det⁡A\det A
  4. det⁡A=0\det A = 0→the columns lie on one line, the parallelogram is flat, and AA has no inverse

Part 6 · Key ideas

Key ideas

  • The determinant of [abcd]\left[ \begin{matrix} a & b \\ c & d \end{matrix} \right] is ad−bcad - bc. Its absolute value is the area of the parallelogram made by the two columns.
  • The inverse matrix A−1A^{-1} satisfies AA−1=A−1A=IAA^{-1} = A^{-1}A = I. For det⁡A≠0\det A \ne 0: A−1A^{-1}=1ad−bc[d−b−ca]= \frac{1}{ad - bc}\left[ \begin{matrix} d & -b \\ -c & a \end{matrix} \right].
  • det⁡A=0\det A = 0 means no inverse: two different inputs give the same product, so nothing can undo AA.
  • If AX=BAX = B and AA has an inverse, then X=A−1BX = A^{-1}B.

Part 7 · Misconception

A common mistake

The wrong idea: The inverse of [abcd]\left[ \begin{matrix} a & b \\ c & d \end{matrix} \right] is [1a1b1c1d]\left[ \begin{matrix} \frac{1}{a} & \frac{1}{b} \\ \frac{1}{c} & \frac{1}{d} \end{matrix} \right].

What actually happens: Reciprocals of the entries do not undo matrix multiplication. Swap aa and dd, change the signs of bb and cc, and divide by ad−bcad - bc; then check that AA−1=IAA^{-1} = I.

A=[4322],B=[6342]A = \left[ \begin{matrix} 4 & 3 \\ 2 & 2 \end{matrix} \right], \qquad B = \left[ \begin{matrix} 6 & 3 \\ 4 & 2 \end{matrix} \right]

1. What is det⁡A\det A?

No calculator. Type the exact value (a fraction, π, e or a root is fine).

Show the answer

det⁡A\det A=4(2)−3(2)= 4(2) - 3(2)=8−6= 8 - 6=2= 2.

  • Answer: 2

2. Which matrix has no inverse?

  1. BB, since det⁡B=0\det B = 0
  2. AA, since det⁡A=2\det A = 2
  3. Both, since neither is the identity
  4. Neither, since no entry is zero
Show the answer

det⁡B\det B=6(2)−3(4)= 6(2) - 3(4)=12−12= 12 - 12=0= 0, so BB has no inverse. det⁡A=2≠0\det A = 2 \ne 0, so AA does.

  • Correct: BB, since det⁡B=0\det B = 0: Determinant 00: no inverse.
  • AA, since det⁡A=2\det A = 2: A nonzero determinant means AA is invertible.
  • Both, since neither is the identity: A matrix need not be II to have an inverse; it needs a nonzero determinant.
  • Neither, since no entry is zero: Nonzero entries do not guarantee an inverse; det⁡B=0\det B = 0.

Part 9 · Summary

Summary

For a 2×22 \times 2 matrix the determinant ad−bcad - bc is the signed area of the parallelogram made by its columns. If it is not 00, the inverse is 1ad−bc[d−b−ca]\frac{1}{ad - bc}\left[ \begin{matrix} d & -b \\ -c & a \end{matrix} \right], which undoes the matrix and solves AX=BAX = B as X=A−1BX = A^{-1}B. If it is 00, there is no inverse.
