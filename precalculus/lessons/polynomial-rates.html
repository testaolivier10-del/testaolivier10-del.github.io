Polynomial Functions and Rates of Change

A polynomial function has a degree and a leading term.

Practice 2: Multiple RepresentationsPractice 3: Communication and Reasoning

Part 1 · Hook

Why this matters

A greenhouse warms all morning, peaks in the late afternoon and cools at night. A polynomial function can follow that day: its graph turns at the warmest moment, and it bends where the warming starts to slow. This topic names those turning and bending points and finds them on graphs, tables and a calculator.

Part 5 · Step by step

How it works, step by step

  1. A polynomial function switches from increasing to decreasing at x=ax = a→it has a local maximum at aa (decreasing to increasing gives a local minimum)
  2. The domain is limited to a closed interval→an included endpoint can also be a local extremum, so check the endpoints
  3. You compare every local extremum value and every endpoint value→the greatest is the global maximum and the least is the global minimum
  4. The rate of change switches from increasing to decreasing, or back→the graph changes concavity: a point of inflection

Part 6 · Key ideas

Key ideas

  • A polynomial function p(x)p(x)=anxn+⋯+a1x+a0= a_n x^n + \cdots + a_1 x + a_0 has degree nn and leading term anxna_n x^n.
  • Local maximum: an output at least as large as at nearby inputs; local minimum: at most as large. Global: over the whole domain.
  • Between two distinct real zeros there is at least one local extremum. An even-degree polynomial has a global maximum or minimum.
  • A point of inflection is where the graph changes concavity: the rate of change switches from increasing to decreasing, or back.

Part 7 · Misconception

A common mistake

The wrong idea: The global maximum is the largest local maximum.

What actually happens: Check the endpoints too. On [−3,2.5][-3, 2.5], p(x)=x44−2x2+3p(x) = \frac{x^4}{4} - 2x^2 + 3 has a local maximum 33 at x=0x = 0, but p(−3)=5.25p(-3) = 5.25 is the global maximum.

1. The function p(x)=x44−2x2+3p(x) = \frac{x^4}{4} - 2x^2 + 3 is defined only for −3≤x≤2.5-3 \le x \le 2.5. At which inputs does pp have a local minimum?

  1. x=−2x = -2 and x=2x = 2
  2. x=0x = 0
  3. x=−3x = -3 and x=0x = 0
  4. x=−1x = -1 and x=1x = 1
Show the answer

A local minimum is where the output is less than or equal to the outputs at nearby inputs: where pp changes from decreasing to increasing, at x=−2x = -2 and x=2x = 2 (both with p=−1p = -1).

  • Correct: x=−2x = -2 and x=2x = 2: Right: pp switches from decreasing to increasing at both.
  • x=0x = 0: At x=0x = 0, pp switches from increasing to decreasing: a local maximum.
  • x=−3x = -3 and x=0x = 0: These are local maxima: the output there is greater than at every nearby input.
  • x=−1x = -1 and x=1x = 1: At x=−1x = -1 the graph is still rising and at x=1x = 1 it is still falling; it does not turn around at either, so neither is a local minimum.

2. The function p(x)=x44−2x2+3p(x) = \frac{x^4}{4} - 2x^2 + 3 is defined only for −3≤x≤2.5-3 \le x \le 2.5. What is the global maximum value of pp on its domain?

No calculator. Type the exact value (a fraction, π, e or a root is fine).

Show the answer

Compare the local maximum values and the endpoint values: p(0)=3p(0) = 3, p(−3)p(-3)=814−18+3= \frac{81}{4} - 18 + 3=5.25= 5.25 and p(2.5)≈0.27p(2.5) \approx 0.27. The greatest is 5.255.25, at the included endpoint x=−3x = -3.

  • Answer: 5.25

3. The function p(x)=x44−2x2+3p(x) = \frac{x^4}{4} - 2x^2 + 3 is defined only for −3≤x≤2.5-3 \le x \le 2.5. How many points of inflection does the graph of pp have?

  1. One
  2. Two
  3. Three
  4. Four
Show the answer

A point of inflection is where the graph changes concavity. pp is concave up near x=−2x = -2, concave down near x=0x = 0 and concave up near x=2x = 2: two switches, at about x=−1.15x = -1.15 and x=1.15x = 1.15.

  • One: Look at both sides: the graph changes from concave up to concave down left of x=0x = 0 and back again right of it.
  • Correct: Two: Right: concave up, then concave down around x=0x = 0, then concave up again; it switches twice.
  • Three: The turning points −2-2, 00 and 22 are extrema, not inflection points.
  • Four: Count where the bending changes, not where the direction changes.

6. A polynomial function ff has zeros at x=1x = 1 and x=4x = 4 and no zeros between them. Which must be true?

  1. ff is increasing on [1,4][1, 4].
  2. ff has a local maximum or a local minimum between 11 and 44.
  3. f(2.5)=0f(2.5) = 0
  4. ff has a point of inflection at x=2.5x = 2.5.
Show the answer

Between two distinct real zeros, a polynomial function's graph must turn around to come back to the xx-axis, so it has at least one local extremum there.

  • ff is increasing on [1,4][1, 4].: ff starts and ends at 00 on [1,4][1, 4], so it cannot rise the whole way.
  • Correct: ff has a local maximum or a local minimum between 11 and 44.: Right: ff leaves 00 and comes back to 00, so it turns around in between.
  • f(2.5)=0f(2.5) = 0: There are no zeros between 11 and 44.
  • ff has a point of inflection at x=2.5x = 2.5.: Nothing forces a change in concavity at the midpoint.

Part 9 · Summary

Summary

A polynomial function has a degree and a leading term. Where it switches between increasing and decreasing it has a local maximum or minimum; on a closed interval the endpoints count too, and the greatest and least of all these values are the global maximum and minimum. Where its rate of change switches between increasing and decreasing, the graph has a point of inflection.
