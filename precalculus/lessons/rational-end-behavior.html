Rational Functions and End Behavior

A rational function's end behavior matches the quotient of its leading terms.

Practice 2: Multiple RepresentationsPractice 3: Communication and Reasoning

Part 1 · Hook

Why this matters

Order more posters and the average cost of each one drops, but never below the cost of the paper and ink. That floor is a horizontal asymptote. Functions that divide one polynomial by another level off, grow without bound, or hug a slanted line, and the leading terms tell you which.

Part 5 · Step by step

How it works, step by step

  1. |x||x| grows large in p(x)q(x)\frac{p(x)}{q(x)}→each polynomial behaves like its leading term, so the function behaves like their quotient
  2. The quotient of leading terms is a constant cc (equal degrees)→limx→±∞r(x)=c\lim_{x \to \pm\infty} r(x) = c and y=cy = c is a horizontal asymptote
  3. The denominator has the higher degree→the quotient goes to 00: the horizontal asymptote is y=0y = 0
  4. The quotient of leading terms is linear (top degree one more)→the graph approaches a slant asymptote y=mx+by = mx + b

Part 6 · Key ideas

Key ideas

  • A rational function is r(x)=p(x)q(x)r(x) = \frac{p(x)}{q(x)} with polynomials pp and qq.
  • End behavior comes from the quotient of the leading terms, written in limit notation.
  • Horizontal asymptote y=cy = c when limx→∞r(x)=c\lim_{x \to \infty} r(x) = c or limx→−∞r(x)=c\lim_{x \to -\infty} r(x) = c: equal degrees give the ratio of leading coefficients; a bigger bottom degree gives y=0y = 0.
  • Slant asymptote when the top's degree is one more: rewrite as a line plus a fraction that goes to 00.

Part 7 · Misconception

A common mistake

The wrong idea: A graph can never cross its horizontal asymptote.

What actually happens: A horizontal asymptote describes the ends only. 5x+2x2+1\frac{5x + 2}{x^2 + 1} has asymptote y=0y = 0 yet crosses it at x=−0.4x = -0.4.

  2. limx→∞s(x)=0\lim_{x \to \infty} s(x) = 0

Show the answer

For large xx, s(x)s(x) behaves like 3x2x2=3\frac{3x^2}{x^2} = 3: limx→∞s(x)=3\lim_{x \to \infty} s(x) = 3.

  • limx→∞s(x)=∞\lim_{x \to \infty} s(x) = \infty: Top and bottom both have degree 22, so their quotient levels off.
  • limx→∞s(x)=0\lim_{x \to \infty} s(x) = 0: 00 is the limit when the denominator has the higher degree; here the degrees are equal.
  • Correct: limx→∞s(x)=3\lim_{x \to \infty} s(x) = 3: Right: the quotient of the leading terms is 3x2x2=3\frac{3x^2}{x^2} = 3.
  • limx→∞s(x)\lim_{x \to \infty} s(x)=−1.5= -1.5: −1.5-1.5 is s(0)s(0), the yy-intercept.

2. What is the horizontal asymptote of the graph of ss?

  1. y=3y = 3
  2. y=0y = 0
  3. x=3x = 3
  4. y=−32y = -\frac{3}{2}
Show the answer

limx→±∞s(x)=3\lim_{x \to \pm\infty} s(x) = 3, so the line y=3y = 3 is a horizontal asymptote.

  • Correct: y=3y = 3: Right: the outputs approach 33 as x→±∞x \to \pm\infty.
  • y=0y = 0: y=0y = 0 would need the denominator to have the higher degree.
  • x=3x = 3: A horizontal asymptote is a horizontal line, y=cy = c.
  • y=−32y = -\frac{3}{2}: That is the ratio of the constant terms, s(0)s(0); end behavior uses the leading terms.

3. For large values of |x||x|, are the outputs of ss above or below 33?

  1. Above 33, because the graph rises toward the asymptote
  2. Below 33, because s(x)=3−9x2+2s(x) = 3 - \frac{9}{x^2 + 2}
  3. Exactly 33 once xx is large enough

3x2−3=3(x2+2)−93x^2 - 3 = 3(x^2 + 2) - 9, so s(x)=3−9x2+2s(x) = 3 - \frac{9}{x^2 + 2}. The fraction is positive and shrinks toward 00: the outputs climb toward 33 from below.

  • Correct: Below 33, because s(x)=3−9x2+2s(x) = 3 - \frac{9}{x^2 + 2}: Right: the subtracted fraction is positive, so s(x)<3s(x) < 3 for every xx.
  • Exactly 33 once xx is large enough: 9x2+2\frac{9}{x^2 + 2} gets small but never reaches 00.
  • Above on the right and below on the left: ss is even: s(−x)=s(x)s(-x) = s(x), so both sides behave alike.

When the denominator's degree is greater, the quotient of leading terms (5x\frac{5}{x} here) goes to 00 at both ends.

Part 9 · Summary

Summary

A rational function's end behavior matches the quotient of its leading terms. Equal degrees give a horizontal asymptote at the ratio of the leading coefficients; a larger denominator degree gives y=0y = 0; a numerator one degree larger gives a slant asymptote. State the end behavior in limit notation, such as limx→∞r(x)=3\lim_{x \to \infty} r(x) = 3.
