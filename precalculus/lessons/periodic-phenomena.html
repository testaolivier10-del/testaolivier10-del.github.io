Unit 3 · Topic 3.1

Periodic Phenomena

A periodic function repeats its outputs every P units of input, f(x + P) = f(x) , and the smallest such P is the period.

Practice 2: Multiple RepresentationsPractice 3: Communication and Reasoning

Part 1 · Hook

Why this matters

Your heart pushes out the same pressure wave with every beat. A lighthouse beam sweeps past the same window every few seconds, and the sea rises and falls about twice a day. Each of these repeats a pattern, so once you know one full cycle you can predict the output at any time at all, next minute or next year.

Part 5 · Step by step

How it works, step by step

  1. The outputs of ff repeat the same pattern every PP units of input→ff is periodic, and the smallest such positive PP is the period: f(x+P)=f(x)f(x + P) = f(x) for every xx
  2. You know ff on one full cycle, an interval of length PP→copy that piece of graph left and right, PP units at a time, to get the whole graph
  3. You need the output at an input far from the known cycle→add or subtract whole periods until the input lands in the cycle: with P=6P = 6, f(47)f(47)=f(47−7⋅6)= f(47 - 7 \cdot 6)=f(5)= f(5)
  4. Every feature of one cycle repeats→intervals where ff increases or decreases, concavity and average rates of change all repeat every PP units

Part 6 · Key ideas

Key ideas

  • A periodic function repeats its outputs: there is a positive number PP with f(x+P)=f(x)f(x + P) = f(x) for every input xx. The smallest such PP is the period.
  • One full cycle, any interval of length PP, holds all the information. Shift inputs by whole periods to reach any other input.
  • Over any interval one full period long, the average rate of change is 00, because the output ends where it started.
  • Two inputs can share an output without being a period apart: a cycle usually passes through each in-between value twice.

Part 7 · Misconception

A common mistake

The wrong idea: The period is the distance between two inputs that have the same output.

What actually happens: Inside one cycle the graph usually reaches the same output twice, once going up and once going down. The period is the length of the smallest interval after which the whole pattern repeats, so check that the graph matches from that point on, not only one value.

Check yourself

Graph

A periodic function ff

The graph shows a periodic function ff on [0,12][0, 12]. On each piece, ff is linear.

1. What is the period of ff?

  1. 44
  2. 22
  3. 66
  4. 1212
Show the answer

The highest points are at x=1x = 1, 55 and 99, four units apart, and the whole pattern on [1,5][1, 5] repeats on [5,9][5, 9]. The period is 44.

  • Correct: 44: From one highest point to the next is 5−1=45 - 1 = 4, and the graph matches from there on.
  • 22: That is the distance from a highest point (x=1x = 1) to the next lowest point (x=3x = 3), only part of a cycle.
  • 66: Shifting by 6 does not keep the outputs: f(1)=5f(1) = 5 but f(7)=−1f(7) = -1.
  • 1212: f(x+12)=f(x)f(x + 12) = f(x) does hold, but 12 is three periods. The period is the smallest positive value that works.

2. The pattern in the graph continues for all xx. What is f(39)f(39)?

No calculator. Type the exact value (a fraction, π, e or a root is fine).

Show the answer

The period is 44 and 39=9⋅4+339 = 9 \cdot 4 + 3, so f(39)=f(3)=−1f(39) = f(3) = -1.

  • Answer: -1

3. What is the average rate of change of ff on [5,9][5, 9]?

  1. 00
  2. −32-\frac{3}{2}
  3. 54\frac{5}{4}
  4. −3-3
Show the answer

[5,9][5, 9] is exactly one period long, so f(9)=f(5)=5f(9) = f(5) = 5 and the average rate of change is 5−59−5=0\frac{5 - 5}{9 - 5} = 0: the secant line through (5,5)(5, 5) and (9,5)(9, 5) is horizontal.

  • Correct: 00: The output ends where it started, so the change in output is 00.
  • −32-\frac{3}{2}: This uses f(7)=−1f(7) = -1 as an endpoint, −1−59−5\frac{-1 - 5}{9 - 5}. The interval ends at x=9x = 9, where f(9)=5f(9) = 5.
  • 54\frac{5}{4}: This is f(9)f(9) divided by the length of the interval. A rate needs the change in output, f(9)−f(5)f(9) - f(5).
  • −3-3: That is the slope of the falling piece on (5,7)(5, 7), not the slope of the secant line from x=5x = 5 to x=9x = 9.

4. The pattern continues for all xx. On which interval is ff decreasing?

  1. (41,43)(41, 43)
  2. (43,45)(43, 45)
  3. (40,42)(40, 42)
  4. (42,44)(42, 44)
Show the answer

In the first cycle ff decreases on (1,3)(1, 3). Adding ten periods (4040) gives (41,43)(41, 43).

  • Correct: (41,43)(41, 43): (1,3)(1, 3) plus 10⋅4=4010 \cdot 4 = 40 is (41,43)(41, 43).
  • (43,45)(43, 45): This is (3,5)(3, 5) plus 4040, where ff rises from −1-1 to 55.
  • (40,42)(40, 42): This is (0,2)(0, 2) plus 4040: ff rises on (40,41)(40, 41) before it starts to fall.
  • (42,44)(42, 44): This is (2,4)(2, 4) plus 4040: ff falls only until x=43x = 43 and then rises.

5. A function gg satisfies g(x+10)=g(x)g(x + 10) = g(x) and g(x+5)=g(x)g(x + 5) = g(x) for every xx, and no smaller positive number works. What is the period of gg?

  1. 55
  2. 1010
  3. 1515
  4. 5050
Show the answer

The period is the smallest positive PP with g(x+P)=g(x)g(x + P) = g(x) for all xx. Both 5 and 10 work, and 5 is smaller.

  • Correct: 55: The smallest shift that leaves every output unchanged is 5.
  • 1010: 10 works because it is two periods; the period is the smallest shift that works.
  • 1515: 15 also works (three periods), but it is not the smallest.
  • 5050: This multiplies the two shifts; the period is the smallest shift that works, 5.

6. A periodic function hh has period 8. What is the average rate of change of hh on [3,11][3, 11]?

  1. 00
  2. h(11)8\frac{h(11)}{8}
  3. 18\frac{1}{8}
  4. It depends on the formula for hh
Show the answer

11=3+811 = 3 + 8, one period later, so h(11)=h(3)h(11) = h(3) and the average rate of change is h(11)−h(3)8=0\frac{h(11) - h(3)}{8} = 0.

  • Correct: 00: One period apart, the outputs are equal, so the change in output is 00.
  • h(11)8\frac{h(11)}{8}: This divides one output by the length of the interval. A rate needs the change in output.
  • 18\frac{1}{8}: This is one over the period. It is not a rate of change of hh.
  • It depends on the formula for hh: Whatever the formula, h(11)=h(3)h(11) = h(3) because the inputs are one period apart.

Part 9 · Summary

Summary

A periodic function repeats its outputs every PP units of input, f(x+P)=f(x)f(x + P) = f(x), and the smallest such PP is the period. Learn one cycle and you know the function everywhere: shift any input by whole periods into the cycle. Increasing and decreasing intervals, concavity and rates of change repeat every period, and the average rate of change over a full period is 00.
