Unit 1 · Topic 1.3

Rates of Change in Linear and Quadratic Functions

A linear function has one rate of change.

Practice 1: Procedural and Symbolic FluencyPractice 2: Multiple Representations

Part 1 · Hook

Why this matters

Drop a ball from a tall building. It falls 55 meters in the first second, 1515 in the next, 2525 in the third: the distance per second grows by the same 1010 meters each time. That steady change in the rate is the signature of a quadratic, just as a steady rate is the signature of a line.

Part 5 · Step by step

How it works, step by step

  1. A function is linear→its average rate of change is the same on every interval: the slope
  2. A function is quadratic and you take equal input steps→the first differences change by a constant amount: the second differences are constant
  3. The average rates over consecutive equal intervals increase (or decrease)→the graph is concave up (or concave down) there
  4. You estimate f(c)f(c) with a point on a secant line on an interval where ff is concave down→the graph lies above the secant line, so the estimate is an underestimate

Part 6 · Key ideas

Key ideas

  • The secant line through (a,f(a))(a, f(a)) and (b,f(b))(b, f(b)) has slope f(b)−f(a)b−a\frac{f(b) - f(a)}{b - a}, the average rate of change of ff on [a,b][a, b].
  • Linear: constant rate of change. Quadratic: rates over consecutive equal intervals change linearly, so second differences are constant.
  • Concave up: graph below the secant line, estimate too big. Concave down: graph above it, estimate too small.

Part 7 · Misconception

A common mistake

The wrong idea: A secant estimate is an overestimate whenever the function is increasing.

What actually happens: Over or under depends on concavity on the interval, not on direction. The ball's height rises on [0,1][0, 1], but it is concave down on [0,3][0, 3], so the secant estimate 66 is below the true h(1)=16h(1) = 16.

3. Which statement about the graph of ff on [0,5][0, 5] is supported by the table?

  1. It is concave down, because the second differences are positive numbers that stay the same.
  2. It is concave up, because f(5)>f(0)f(5) > f(0).

Show the answer

Over equal steps, increasing average rates of change mean the rate of change is increasing, so ff is concave up.

  • It is concave down, because the second differences are positive numbers that stay the same.: Positive second differences mean the rates are going up, which is concave up.
  • It is concave up, because f(5)>f(0)f(5) > f(0).: Comparing two outputs gives direction, not concavity. Concavity comes from how the rates change.
  • Correct: It is concave up, because the average rates over consecutive equal intervals increase.: Right: the rates −1,3,7,11,15-1, 3, 7, 11, 15 increase.
  • It is a straight line, because the second differences are constant.: A line has constant first differences; constant second differences mean a parabola.

5. A ball's height is h(t)=−5t2+20t+1h(t) = -5t^2 + 20t + 1 meters after tt seconds. Use the secant line on [0,3][0, 3] to estimate h(1)h(1). Is the estimate an overestimate or an underestimate?

  1. 66; an overestimate, since hh is increasing on [0,1][0, 1]
  2. 66; an underestimate, since hh is concave down on [0,3][0, 3]
  3. 1616; exact, since the point (1,16)(1, 16) is on the graph of hh
  4. 55; an underestimate, because the slope is positive
Show the answer

The estimate is a point on the secant line: y=1+5(1)=6y = 1 + 5(1) = 6. hh is concave down on [0,3][0, 3] (its rates fall), so its graph lies above the secant line between t=0t = 0 and t=3t = 3, and 66 is an underestimate. Indeed h(1)=16h(1) = 16.

  • 66; an overestimate, since hh is increasing on [0,1][0, 1]: Whether hh rises does not decide over or under; the bending does. The graph is above the secant line.
  • Correct: 66; an underestimate, since hh is concave down on [0,3][0, 3]: Right: the secant line gives 1+5(1)=61 + 5(1) = 6, and the true height h(1)=16h(1) = 16 is above it.
  • 1616; exact, since the point (1,16)(1, 16) is on the graph of hh: 1616 is h(1)h(1) itself; the secant line's value at t=1t = 1 is 66.
  • 55; an underestimate, because the slope is positive: 55 is the slope. The estimate is the line's value at t=1t = 1: 1+5⋅1=61 + 5 \cdot 1 = 6.

6. A ball's height is h(t)=−5t2+20t+1h(t) = -5t^2 + 20t + 1 meters after tt seconds. What happens to the average rates of change of hh on [0,1][0, 1], [1,2][1, 2], [2,3][2, 3] and [3,4][3, 4]?

  1. They are 15,5,−5,−1515, 5, -5, -15: they decrease by 1010 each second.
  2. They are 16,21,16,116, 21, 16, 1: they rise, then fall.
  3. They each equal 55, the slope of the secant line on [0,3][0, 3].
  4. They are 15,5,−5,−1515, 5, -5, -15, which drop by 55 from one to the next.
Show the answer

h(0),h(0),h(1),h(1),…,\ldots,h(4)h(4)=1,= 1,16,16,21,21,16,16,11, so the rates over unit intervals are 15,5,−5,−1515, 5, -5, -15. They change linearly (by −10-10 each step), so hh is concave down.

  • Correct: They are 15,5,−5,−1515, 5, -5, -15: they decrease by 1010 each second.: Right: a quadratic's rates over consecutive equal intervals change by a constant amount.
  • They are 16,21,16,116, 21, 16, 1: they rise, then fall.: These are heights, not rates of change.
  • They each equal 55, the slope of the secant line on [0,3][0, 3].: Only a line has the same rate on every interval.
  • They are 15,5,−5,−1515, 5, -5, -15, which drop by 55 from one to the next.: 15−5=1015 - 5 = 10: the rates drop by 1010 each step.

Part 9 · Summary

Summary

A linear function has one rate of change. A quadratic's average rates over consecutive equal intervals change by a constant amount, so its second differences are constant; increasing rates mean concave up, decreasing rates concave down. The average rate of change on [a,b][a, b] is the slope of the secant line, and a secant estimate is too big where the graph is concave up and too small where it is concave down.
