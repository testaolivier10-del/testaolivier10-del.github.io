Complex Numbers

The imaginary unit i has i² = −1 , so √(−b²) = bi .

Practice 1: Procedural and Symbolic Fluency

Part 1 · Hook

Why this matters

For centuries mathematicians rejected −1\sqrt{-1} as impossible, until they noticed it made their formulas work. The parabola y=x2+4x+13y = x^2 + 4x + 13 never touches the xx-axis, yet the quadratic formula still gives it two solutions, −2±3i-2 \pm 3i. Topic 1.5 counts every zero of a polynomial function, including these, so you need the arithmetic of ii.

Part 5 · Step by step

How it works, step by step

  1. ii is defined by i2=−1i^2 = -1→square roots of negatives exist: −16=4i\sqrt{-16} = 4i
  2. You add, subtract or multiply complex numbers→work like binomials, then replace i2i^2 with −1-1
  3. The discriminant of a quadratic with real coefficients is negative→its two solutions are complex conjugates a+bia + bi and a−bia - bi

Part 6 · Key ideas

Key ideas

  • The imaginary unit ii has i2=−1i^2 = -1. A complex number is a+bia + bi with real aa (real part) and bb (imaginary part).
  • The complex conjugate of a+bia + bi is a−bia - bi, and (a+bi)(a−bi)(a + bi)(a - bi)=a2+b2= a^2 + b^2, a real number.
  • Powers of ii repeat every four: i,−1,−i,1i, -1, -i, 1.

Part 7 · Misconception

A common mistake

The wrong idea: −8i2=−8-8i^2 = -8.

What actually happens: i2=−1i^2 = -1, so −8i2=−8(−1)=8-8i^2 = -8(-1) = 8. The sign flips whenever i2i^2 is replaced.

6. How many real solutions does x2+4x+13=0x^2 + 4x + 13 = 0 have, and what kind of solutions does it have?

  1. None real; two complex solutions that are complex conjugates
  2. Two real solutions, −2-2 and 33
  3. One real solution, −2-2, where the parabola turns
  4. Zero solutions of any kind, since the parabola stays above the axis
Show the answer

b2−4acb^2 - 4ac=16−52= 16 - 52=−36<0= -36 < 0: no real solutions, and the quadratic formula gives the pair −2±3i-2 \pm 3i. For real coefficients, such solutions come as a number and its complex conjugate.

  • Correct: None real; two complex solutions that are complex conjugates: Right: the discriminant is −36-36, and the solutions −2+3i-2 + 3i and −2−3i-2 - 3i are conjugates.
  • Two real solutions, −2-2 and 33: −2-2 and 33 are the parts of −2+3i-2 + 3i, not separate solutions.
  • One real solution, −2-2, where the parabola turns: x=−2x = -2 is where the vertex is; the parabola never reaches the xx-axis.
  • Zero solutions of any kind, since the parabola stays above the axis: It has no real solutions, but it does have two complex ones.

Part 9 · Summary

Summary

The imaginary unit ii has i2=−1i^2 = -1, so −b2=bi\sqrt{-b^2} = bi. Complex numbers a+bia + bi add and multiply like binomials, with i2i^2 replaced by −1-1. A quadratic with real coefficients and a negative discriminant has two complex solutions that are complex conjugates, a±bia \pm bi.
