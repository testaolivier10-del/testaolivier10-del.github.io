Unit 1 · Topic 1.2

Rates of Change

The average rate of change of f on [a, b] is (f(b) − f(a))/(b − a) , the slope of the line through (a, f(a)) and (b, f(b)) , in output units per input unit.

Practice 1: Procedural and Symbolic FluencyPractice 3: Communication and Reasoning

Part 1 · Hook

Why this matters

A hiking app says you averaged 1.9 miles per hour. You did not walk at that speed every minute: you were faster on the flat start and slower on the climb. That single number is an average rate of change, and comparing such averages over short stretches is how precalculus says how fast a function changes at a moment.

Part 5 · Step by step

How it works, step by step

  1. Pick an interval [a,b][a, b] and find f(a)f(a) and f(b)f(b)→the average rate of change is f(b)−f(a)b−a\frac{f(b) - f(a)}{b - a}, in output units per input unit
  2. Draw the line through (a,f(a))(a, f(a)) and (b,f(b))(b, f(b))→its slope is that average rate of change
  3. Shrink the interval around a point x=cx = c→the average rate of change estimates how fast ff changes at cc
  4. Compare such estimates at two points over equally short intervals→the larger one tells where ff is changing faster

Part 6 · Key ideas

Key ideas

  • The average rate of change of ff on [a,b][a, b] is f(b)−f(a)b−a\frac{f(b) - f(a)}{b - a}: the slope of the line through (a,f(a))(a, f(a)) and (b,f(b))(b, f(b)).
  • Units: output units per input unit, like miles per hour or dollars per year.
  • Positive: the output ends higher. Negative: it ends lower. Zero: the outputs at the ends are equal.
  • To compare rates at two points, use short intervals of the same length around each.

Part 7 · Misconception

A common mistake

The wrong idea: The interval with the biggest change in output has the biggest average rate of change.

What actually happens: Divide by the length of the interval first. The hiker gained 4.54.5 miles on [1,3][1, 3] but only 2.52.5 on [0,1][0, 1], yet the rate on [0,1][0, 1] is higher: 2.52.5 against 2.252.25 miles per hour.

6. The concentration of a medicine in the blood is C(t)=12tt+2C(t) = \frac{12t}{t + 2} milligrams per liter, tt hours after a dose. Which interval's average rate of change gives the best estimate of how fast CC changes at t=4t = 4?

  1. [0,8][0, 8]
  2. [4,10][4, 10]
  3. [3.9,4.1][3.9, 4.1]
  4. [2,6][2, 6]
Show the answer

The shorter the interval around a point, the closer the average rate of change is to how fast the function changes at that point.

  • [0,8][0, 8]: A long interval mixes the fast early change with the slow later change.
  • [4,10][4, 10]: This interval is long and starts at 44, so it reflects mostly later, slower change.
  • Correct: [3.9,4.1][3.9, 4.1]: Right: a short interval around t=4t = 4 uses outputs very close to the one at t=4t = 4.
  • [2,6][2, 6]: Better than a long interval, but [3.9,4.1][3.9, 4.1] is much shorter around t=4t = 4.
