Quadratic functions are the first functions in this course whose rate of change is not constant. Before studying that in Topic 1.3, you need four algebra skills: solving with factors, solving with the quadratic formula, finding the vertex, and completing the square.

Quadratics and parabolas

A quadratic function has the form $f(x) = ax^2 + bx + c$ with $a \ne 0$. Its graph is a parabola: a U shape that opens up when $a > 0$ and down when $a < 0$. The turning point is the vertex, and the parabola is symmetric about the vertical line through it, the axis of symmetry.

Solving with factors

If a product is $0$, one of its factors is $0$. So when $ax^2 + bx + c$ can be written as a product, each factor gives a solution. For $x^2 - 5x + 6$, look for two numbers with product $6$ and sum $-5$: $-2$ and $-3$. Then $(x - 2)(x - 3) = 0$, so $x = 2$ or $x = 3$.

The equation must equal $0$ first. To solve $x^2 = 5x - 6$, move everything to one side before you look for factors.

The quadratic formula and the discriminant

Every equation $ax^2 + bx + c = 0$ can be solved with the quadratic formula:

$$x = \frac{-b \pm \sqrt{b^2 - 4ac}}{2a}$$

Worked example 1. Solve $2x^2 + 3x - 4 = 0$ exactly.

$a = 2$, $b = 3$, $c = -4$. Then $b^2 - 4ac = 9 - 4(2)(-4) = 9 + 32 = 41$.

$x = \frac{-3 \pm \sqrt{41}}{4}$. These are exact; $\sqrt{41}$ does not simplify.

The number under the square root, $b^2 - 4ac$, is the discriminant. It tells you how many real solutions there are without solving:

The vertex and completing the square

The vertex of $y = ax^2 + bx + c$ is at $x = -\frac{b}{2a}$. For $y = 2x^2 - 8x + 5$: $x = -\frac{-8}{4} = 2$, and $y = 8 - 16 + 5 = -3$, so the vertex is $(2, -3)$.

Completing the square rewrites a quadratic in vertex form $a(x - h)^2 + k$, where the vertex $(h, k)$ can be read off.

Worked example 2. Write $x^2 + 6x + 5$ in vertex form.

Half of $6$ is $3$, and $(x + 3)^2 = x^2 + 6x + 9$. That has $9$ where we need $5$, so subtract $4$:

$x^2 + 6x + 5 = (x + 3)^2 - 4$. The vertex is $(-3, -4)$.

Vertex form also makes solving easy: $(x + 3)^2 - 4 = 0$ gives $(x + 3)^2 = 4$, so $x + 3 = \pm 2$ and $x = -1$ or $x = -5$. Remember the $\pm$; a square root has two signs.

To build a parabola from its vertex and one more point, start with $y = a(x - h)^2 + k$ and use the point to find $a$. With vertex $(-1, 2)$ and point $(0, 5)$: $5 = a(1)^2 + 2$, so $a = 3$ and $y = 3(x + 1)^2 + 2$.
