Many quantities need two pieces of information: not just "how much" but "which way". A wind of 20 km/h from the west is different from a wind of 20 km/h from the north. A vector is a quantity with a magnitude (size) and a direction. You draw it as an arrow; you compute with it through its components.

Components

An arrow from $(x_1, y_1)$ to $(x_2, y_2)$ moves $x_2 - x_1$ across and $y_2 - y_1$ up. Those two numbers are its components:

$$\mathbf{v} = \left\langle x_2 - x_1, y_2 - y_1 \right\rangle.$$

Only the move matters, not where the arrow starts. The arrow from $(2, 1)$ to $(1, 4)$ and the arrow from $(0, 0)$ to $(-1, 3)$ are the same vector, $\left\langle -1, 3 \right\rangle$. Another way to write $\left\langle a, b \right\rangle$ is $a\mathbf{i} + b\mathbf{j}$, where $\mathbf{i} = \left\langle 1, 0 \right\rangle$ and $\mathbf{j} = \left\langle 0, 1 \right\rangle$.

Magnitude and direction

The magnitude is the arrow's length, from the Pythagorean theorem: $\left\| \left\langle a, b \right\rangle \right\| = \sqrt{a^2 + b^2}$. Going the other way, a vector with magnitude $m$ pointing at angle $\theta$ from the positive $x$-axis has components $\left\langle m\cos\theta, m\sin\theta \right\rangle$, the same idea as a point on a circle of radius $m$.

Worked example 1. A boat heads $30^\circ$ north of east at $12$ km/h, and a current pushes it south at $4$ km/h. Find its actual velocity vector and speed.

Heading: $\left\langle 12\cos 30^\circ, 12\sin 30^\circ \right\rangle = \left\langle 6\sqrt{3}, 6 \right\rangle$, using $\cos 30^\circ = \frac{\sqrt{3}}{2}$ and $\sin 30^\circ = \frac{1}{2}$.

Current: $\left\langle 0, -4 \right\rangle$ (south is the negative $y$ direction).

Sum: $\left\langle 6\sqrt{3}, 2 \right\rangle$. Speed: $\sqrt{(6\sqrt{3})^2 + 2^2} = \sqrt{108 + 4} = \sqrt{112} = 4\sqrt{7}$ km/h, a little over $10.5$ km/h.

Adding, subtracting and scaling

To add $\mathbf{u} + \mathbf{v}$ on a picture, put the tail of $\mathbf{v}$ at the tip of $\mathbf{u}$; the sum runs from the tail of $\mathbf{u}$ to the tip of $\mathbf{v}$. In components you just add matching entries.

The dot product and angles

The dot product of $\mathbf{u} = \left\langle a, b \right\rangle$ and $\mathbf{v} = \left\langle c, d \right\rangle$ is the number $\mathbf{u} \cdot \mathbf{v} = ac + bd$. It is also $\left\| \mathbf{u} \right\| \left\| \mathbf{v} \right\| \cos\theta$, where $\theta$ is the angle between the two arrows when they start at the same point. So

$$\cos\theta = \frac{\mathbf{u} \cdot \mathbf{v}}{\left\| \mathbf{u} \right\| \left\| \mathbf{v} \right\|}.$$

Worked example 2. Find the angle between $\left\langle 1, 0 \right\rangle$ and $\left\langle 1, \sqrt{3} \right\rangle$.

Dot product: $1(1) + 0(\sqrt{3}) = 1$. Lengths: $1$ and $\sqrt{1 + 3} = 2$.

$\cos\theta = \frac{1}{1 \cdot 2} = \frac{1}{2}$, so $\theta = \frac{\pi}{3}$.

A dot product of $0$ means $\cos\theta = 0$: the vectors are perpendicular. $\left\langle 2, 3 \right\rangle \cdot \left\langle -3, 2 \right\rangle = -6 + 6 = 0$. A positive dot product means the angle is less than $\frac{\pi}{2}$; a negative one, more.
