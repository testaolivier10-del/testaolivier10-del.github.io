In Topic 3.13 a single pair $(r, \theta)$ named a single point. Now let $r$ depend on $\theta$. As the direction $\theta$ sweeps around the pole, the distance $r = f(\theta)$ changes, and the points trace a curve. This page shows how to read and sketch such curves from the ordinary graph of $r$ against $\theta$, and names the families you will see.

A function of the angle

A polar function $r = f(\theta)$ has the angle as its input and a signed distance as its output. Its graph is the set of points with polar coordinates $(f(\theta), \theta)$. You can also graph $r$ against $\theta$ on ordinary axes, with $\theta$ across and $r$ up. The two pictures carry the same information:

Sketching from the graph of $r$

Worked example 1. Sketch $r = 3\sin(2\theta)$ (Figure 1).

Key values. $r = 0$ at $\theta = 0, \frac{\pi}{2}, \pi, \frac{3\pi}{2}$; $r = 3$ at $\frac{\pi}{4}$ and $\frac{5\pi}{4}$; $r = -3$ at $\frac{3\pi}{4}$ and $\frac{7\pi}{4}$.

First quarter turn, $0 \le \theta \le \frac{\pi}{2}$: $r$ rises from $0$ to $3$ and falls back to $0$, all positive. The points leave the pole, reach distance $3$ along the ray $\frac{\pi}{4}$, and return: one petal in Quadrant I.

Second quarter turn, $\frac{\pi}{2} \le \theta \le \pi$: $r$ is negative. The angle points into Quadrant II, so the points land in Quadrant IV: a petal there, with its tip at $\left(-3, \frac{3\pi}{4}\right)$, which is the same point as $\left(3, \frac{7\pi}{4}\right)$.

The next two quarter turns draw petals in Quadrants III and II. Four humps, four petals.

The inner loop

Worked example 2. Where does $r = 1 + 2\cos\theta$ form its inner loop?

$r = 0$ where $\cos\theta = -\frac{1}{2}$: $\theta = \frac{2\pi}{3}$ and $\frac{4\pi}{3}$. Between them, $r < 0$; at $\theta = \pi$, $r = -1$.

On $\left(\frac{2\pi}{3}, \frac{4\pi}{3}\right)$ the angle points left, but the negative $r$ puts the points to the right of the pole. The small loop runs from the pole out to $(1, 0)$ in rectangular coordinates and back. Outside that interval $r > 0$ and the big loop is drawn, reaching $(3, 0)$ at $\theta = 0$.

Common slips

  • Plotting a negative $r$ in the quadrant the angle points to. Reverse through the pole.
  • Counting petals as $n$ when $n$ is even. Even $n$ gives $2n$.
  • Confusing $r = 4\cos\theta$ (a circle through the pole) with $r = 4$ (a circle around the pole).
