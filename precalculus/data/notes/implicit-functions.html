So far most functions have come as formulas: $y = f(x)$, or $x(t)$ and $y(t)$. Many curves are more natural as a single equation tying $x$ and $y$ together. A circle is the clearest case: $x^2 + y^2 = 25$ says "the distance from the origin is 5" without ever saying $y = \ldots$.

Explicit and implicit

Implicit$x^2 + xy + y^2 = 7$put in $x$, solve for every $y$

The graph of an implicitly defined function is the set of all points $(x, y)$ that make its equation true. To check whether a point is on the curve, substitute both coordinates: $(1, 2)$ is on $x^2 + xy + y^2 = 7$ because $1 + 2 + 4 = 7$, and $(2, 2)$ is not because $4 + 4 + 4 = 12$.

Finding points

Worked example 1. Find every point of $x^2 + xy + y^2 = 7$ with $x = 1$.

Substitute: $1 + y + y^2 = 7$, so $y^2 + y - 6 = 0$.

Factor: $(y - 2)(y + 3) = 0$, so $y = 2$ or $y = -3$.

The points are $(1, 2)$ and $(1, -3)$. One $x$, two $y$-values: this curve is not the graph of a function of $x$.

Splitting a curve into functions

A curve that fails the vertical line test can often be cut into pieces that pass it. For the circle $x^2 + y^2 = 25$, solving for $y$ gives $y = \pm\sqrt{25 - x^2}$: the upper half $y = \sqrt{25 - x^2}$ and the lower half $y = -\sqrt{25 - x^2}$ are each functions of $x$.

Worked example 2. Solve $x^2 + xy + y^2 = 7$ for $y$.

Treat it as a quadratic in $y$: $y^2 + (x)y + (x^2 - 7) = 0$, with $a = 1$, $b = x$, $c = x^2 - 7$.

The quadratic formula: $y = \frac{-x \pm \sqrt{x^2 - 4(x^2 - 7)}}{2} = \frac{-x \pm \sqrt{28 - 3x^2}}{2}$.

The "+" sign gives the upper piece and the "−" sign the lower piece. Check with $x = 1$: $\frac{-1 \pm \sqrt{25}}{2}$ gives $2$ and $-3$, as before.

The square root also tells you how wide the curve is. Real values of $y$ exist only when $28 - 3x^2 \ge 0$, that is $-\sqrt{\frac{28}{3}} \le x \le \sqrt{\frac{28}{3}}$. At the two ends the square root is $0$ and the two pieces meet.

Symmetry from the equation

You can read symmetry without graphing. Swapping $x$ and $y$ in $x^2 + xy + y^2 = 7$ gives the same equation, so if $(a, b)$ is on the curve, so is $(b, a)$: the curve is symmetric about the line $y = x$. Replacing $x$ with $-x$ and $y$ with $-y$ also gives the same equation, so $(-a, -b)$ is on it too.

Implicit equations that are functions

Some implicit equations do give one $y$ for each $x$. In $y^3 + y = x$, the left side increases whenever $y$ increases, so two different $y$-values can never give the same $x$. This defines $y$ as a function of $x$, even though solving for $y$ by hand is hard. A table is easy, though: choose $y$, compute $x$. From the points $(2, 1)$ and $(10, 2)$, the average rate of change of $y$ with respect to $x$ is $\frac{2 - 1}{10 - 2} = \frac{1}{8}$, the slope of the segment joining them.
