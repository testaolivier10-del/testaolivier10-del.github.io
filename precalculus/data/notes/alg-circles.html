The next topic puts angles on a circle and reads sine and cosine as coordinates. Three tools make that easy: two right triangles whose sides you know exactly, the length of an arc, and the equation of a circle.

The two special right triangles

Most right triangles have messy side ratios. Two have ratios you can write exactly, and they appear again and again.

45-45-90. Two equal angles mean two equal legs. With legs $1$, the Pythagorean theorem gives a hypotenuse of $\sqrt{1 + 1} = \sqrt{2}$. Any 45-45-90 triangle is a scaled copy: legs $a$, hypotenuse $a\sqrt{2}$.

30-60-90. Cut an equilateral triangle with sides $2$ down the middle. Each half has a hypotenuse $2$, a short leg $1$ (half the base) and a long leg $\sqrt{2^2 - 1^2} = \sqrt{3}$. The short leg is across from $30^\circ$, the long leg across from $60^\circ$.

Reading SOH CAH TOA off these triangles gives exact values:

Angle$\sin$$\cos$$\tan$
$30^\circ$$\frac{1}{2}$$\frac{\sqrt{3}}{2}$$\frac{1}{\sqrt{3}} = \frac{\sqrt{3}}{3}$
$45^\circ$$\frac{\sqrt{2}}{2}$$\frac{\sqrt{2}}{2}$$1$
$60^\circ$$\frac{\sqrt{3}}{2}$$\frac{1}{2}$$\sqrt{3}$

($\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}$ and $\frac{\sqrt{2}}{2}$ are the same number: multiply top and bottom by $\sqrt{2}$.)

Worked example 1. A 30-60-90 triangle has hypotenuse $10$. Find both legs.

Scale $1 : \sqrt{3} : 2$ so the hypotenuse is $10$: multiply by $5$. The short leg is $5$ and the long leg is $5\sqrt{3}$.

Arc length

A central angle has its vertex at the center of a circle. An angle of $\theta$ degrees is the fraction $\frac{\theta}{360}$ of a full turn, so it cuts off that fraction of the circumference $2\pi r$:

$s = \frac{\theta}{360} \cdot 2\pi r$.

Worked example 2. A circle has radius 9 cm. How long is the arc cut off by a central angle of $80^\circ$?

$s = \frac{80}{360} \cdot 2\pi(9) = \frac{2}{9} \cdot 18\pi = 4\pi$ cm, about 12.6 cm.

Notice what happens when the arc is exactly as long as the radius: $\frac{\theta}{360} \cdot 2\pi r = r$ gives $\theta = \frac{360}{2\pi} = \frac{180}{\pi} \approx 57.3^\circ$, whatever the size of the circle. That angle is about to get its own name.

The equation of a circle

A circle is every point at distance $r$ from its center $(h, k)$. The horizontal and vertical distances from the center, $x - h$ and $y - k$, are the legs of a right triangle with hypotenuse $r$. The Pythagorean theorem gives

$(x - h)^2 + (y - k)^2 = r^2$.

Watch the signs: a center at $(2, -1)$ gives $(x - 2)^2 + (y + 1)^2 = r^2$. And the right side is the radius squared: radius $3$ means $= 9$.

The simplest case is the circle of radius $1$ centered at the origin, $x^2 + y^2 = 1$. The point on it reached by turning $30^\circ$ up from the positive $x$-axis is the top corner of a 30-60-90 triangle with hypotenuse $1$: it is $(\cos 30^\circ, \sin 30^\circ) = \left(\frac{\sqrt{3}}{2}, \frac{1}{2}\right)$. Check: $\frac{3}{4} + \frac{1}{4} = 1$. That picture is exactly where the next topic starts.
