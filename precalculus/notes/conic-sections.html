SAlgebra you need

Beyond the exam. The AP® Precalculus exam does not test this unit. Many classes teach it, and it prepares you for calculus.

A double cone, two ice-cream cones point to point, can be sliced by a plane in different ways. A cut straight across gives a circle; a tilted cut gives an ellipse; a cut parallel to the side gives a parabola; a steep cut through both cones gives the two branches of a hyperbola. These curves are the conic sections. Each is an implicitly defined curve, with an equation in xx and yy.

Parabolas

You know y=a(x−h)2+ky = a(x - h)^2 + k, a parabola with vertex (h,k)(h, k) that opens up if a>0a > 0 and down if a<0a < 0. Swap the roles of xx and yy and you get a parabola that opens sideways:

x−h=a(y−k)2.x - h = a(y - k)^2.

It has vertex (h,k)(h, k) and opens right if a>0a > 0, left if a<0a < 0. A sideways parabola is not the graph of a function of xx, since most xx-values have two yy-values.

Ellipses

An ellipse with center (h,k)(h, k) has the standard form

(x−h)2a2+(y−k)2b2=1,a>0,b>0.\frac{(x - h)^2}{a^2} + \frac{(y - k)^2}{b^2} = 1, \qquad a > 0, \; b > 0.

It reaches aa units left and right of the center and bb units up and down. The farthest points along the longer direction are the vertices; the longer axis has length 2a2a or 2b2b, whichever is larger. When a=ba = b the ellipse is a circle of radius aa.

Worked example 1. Describe (x−1)216+(y+2)24\frac{(x - 1)^2}{16} + \frac{(y + 2)^2}{4}=1= 1.

Center: x−1x - 1 and y+2=y−(−2)y + 2 = y - (-2) give (1,−2)(1, -2).

Distances: a2=16a^2 = 16 so a=4a = 4 across; b2=4b^2 = 4 so b=2b = 2 up and down. The ellipse is wider than tall.

Vertices: (1±4,−2)(1 \pm 4, -2), that is (5,−2)(5, -2) and (−3,−2)(-3, -2); the long axis has length 88. The top and bottom points are (1,0)(1, 0) and (1,−4)(1, -4).

Hyperbolas

Change the plus sign to a minus and the curve breaks into two branches:

(x−h)2a2−(y−k)2b2=1.\frac{(x - h)^2}{a^2} - \frac{(y - k)^2}{b^2} = 1.

This hyperbola opens left and right. Its vertices are (h±a,k)(h \pm a, k), and far from the center each branch gets closer and closer to the asymptotes y−k=±ba(x−h)y - k = \pm\frac{b}{a}(x - h). If the yy term comes first, (y−k)2b2−(x−h)2a2\frac{(y - k)^2}{b^2} - \frac{(x - h)^2}{a^2}=1= 1, it opens up and down with vertices (h,k±b)(h, k \pm b) and the same asymptotes.

Worked example 2. Describe x24−y29=1\frac{x^2}{4} - \frac{y^2}{9} = 1.

Center (0,0)(0, 0), a=2a = 2, b=3b = 3; the xx term is positive, so it opens left and right. Vertices (2,0)(2, 0) and (−2,0)(-2, 0). Asymptotes y=±32xy = \pm\frac{3}{2}x.

It never meets the yy-axis: x=0x = 0 gives −y29=1-\frac{y^2}{9} = 1, so y2=−9y^2 = -9, which has no real solution.
