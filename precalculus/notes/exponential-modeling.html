When a quantity changes by the same factor over equal stretches of time, an exponential model f(t)=abtf(t) = ab^t describes it: a phone losing value, a medicine leaving the blood, a gym gaining members. This page builds such models three ways, rewrites them for other time units, and uses them to predict and to find when a value is reached. It is the content of Question 2 whenever the context is exponential.

Reading aa and bb in context

a=f(0)a = f(0) is the initial value, the amount when t=0t = 0. bb is the growth factor per unit of tt: each unit, the amount is multiplied by bb. A factor b=1.035b = 1.035 means a 3.5%3.5\% increase per unit; b=0.8b = 0.8 means the amount keeps 80%80\%, a 20%20\% decrease. Always say what t=0t = 0 is and what one unit of tt is.

Worked example 1 (two points). A phone costs $800$800 new and is worth $512$512 after 22 years. Find V(t)=abtV(t) = ab^t.

a=V(0)=800a = V(0) = 800.

Over the 22-year gap the value is multiplied by 512800=0.64\frac{512}{800} = 0.64. That is two yearly factors: b2=0.64b^2 = 0.64.

Take the square root (rational exponent 12\frac{1}{2}): b=0.641/2=0.8b = 0.64^{1/2} = 0.8. So V(t)=800(0.8)tV(t) = 800(0.8)^t: the phone keeps 80%80\% of its value each year.

If neither point is at t=0t = 0, find bb the same way, then work back to aa. Through (1,30)(1, 30) and (3,270)(3, 270): b2=9b^2 = 9, so b=3b = 3, and a⋅31=30a \cdot 3^1 = 30 gives a=10a = 10.

With real data, use exponential regression: enter the lists, run the regression, and store aa and bb at full precision. For the medicine data on the practice page, the calculator gives b=0.79686...b = 0.79686..., a decrease of about 20.314%20.314\% per hour.

Other time units

Rational exponents convert the factor to another unit. If the yearly factor is 1.21.2, the monthly factor mm satisfies m12=1.2m^{12} = 1.2, so m=1.21/12m = 1.2^{1/12}. The model can be written P(t)P(t)=1200(1.21/12)12t= 1200(1.2^{1/12})^{12t}, which is the same function as 1200(1.2)t1200(1.2)^t.

Do not split the percent: 20%20\% a year is not 2012%\frac{20}{12}\% a month, because each month builds on the last. Likewise, 30%30\% a week is a daily factor of 1.31/7≈1.0381.3^{1/7} \approx 1.038, not 1+0.371 + \frac{0.3}{7}.

A half-life hh means the amount halves every hh units: A(t)=A0(12)t/hA(t) = A_0\left(\frac{1}{2}\right)^{t/h}. After 1515 years with a 55-year half-life, 8080 grams becomes 80(12)3=1080\left(\frac{1}{2}\right)^3 = 10 grams.

Where the model can be trusted

A model built from data is supported near that data. Between data points, predictions are usually good. Far past the last data point, nothing in the data shows the pattern continues: the phone may stop losing value at a scrap price, and the body may clear a medicine differently once little is left. Say this with the numbers: "the data stop at t=5t = 5, so a prediction at t=24t = 24 is not supported by the data." In context, the domain may also have a hard edge, such as a value that cannot fall below a floor.
