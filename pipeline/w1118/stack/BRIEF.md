## Narration (vo.txt, exactly this; about 520 characters)
She's trying a book stacking trick: make the top book hang completely past the edge of the table. No glue. Can she do it? A book can stick out as long as the weight above it stays over the book below. So the top book slides out HALF a length. The next one, a quarter. Then a sixth. Then an eighth. Add them up... and the top book is clear of the table. Those steps are half of the harmonic series, one plus a half plus a third and on and on, and that sum never stops growing. Stack enough books and it reaches as far as you like. Just slowly. Two full book lengths takes thirty one books.

## Facts (checked; every drawing must match)
- Identical books of length L, one per level (the classic block stacking problem). Built top down: the k-th book from the top sticks out L/(2k) past the book under it (the last one past the table edge). Top: L/2, then L/4, L/6, L/8.
- Total overhang with n books = (L/2)(1 + 1/2 + ... + 1/n). n = 4: (L/2)(2.0833) = 1.042 L, so the top book's back end is 0.042 L past the edge: the whole top book is beyond the table. Draw it to scale: the gap must be visible but small.
- Why it balances: the centre of mass of all books above any level sits exactly over the edge of the book (or table) below. A thin vertical guide line from each sub stack's centre of mass down to the supporting edge may be drawn at the moment it is described.
- Harmonic series 1 + 1/2 + 1/3 + ... diverges. Overhang of 2 L needs H_n >= 4: n = 31 (H_30 = 3.995, H_31 = 4.027). 3 L needs 227 books.
- How she builds it (physically right, ACTIONS CONNECT): start with the 4 books stacked flush at the table edge. She slides the top book out L/2 (fingertips on its spine/side, pushing it outward along the book below). Then she pushes the top two together out L/4 relative to the third (hand on the second book's side, both move as one). Then the top three by L/6, then all four by L/8 past the table edge. Books move only while her hand pushes them; they never move by themselves. At the edge moments, a tiny wobble that settles is allowed (no tipping).
