## Narration (vo.txt, exactly this; about 560 characters with the CTA)
This farmer's milk keeps going sour before his cart reaches the city, and the von Thunen model says he's farming the wrong thing. Two hundred years ago, a German farmer named von Thunen worked out why. Land near the city costs more, but the trip to market costs less. So what grows where depends on how far it has to travel, and how fast it spoils. Milk and vegetables, right next to the city. Then firewood, because it's heavy. Then grain, because it keeps. And way out at the edge... cattle, because they walk to market themselves. Our farmer is out in the grain ring. So he sells the cows, and plants wheat. Free practice for this on levlprep dot com.

## Facts (checked)
- Johann Heinrich von Thünen, The Isolated State (Der isolierte Staat), 1826; he ran his own estate, Tellow, in Mecklenburg, Germany, and used its accounts.
- Assumptions: one central market city, flat uniform land, one mode of transport, farmers maximise profit. Land rent (bid rent) is highest near the city and falls with distance as transport cost rises.
- Rings from the city out: 1 dairy and market gardening (perishable, before refrigeration), 2 forest for firewood and timber (heavy, costly to haul), 3 grain and field crops (keep well, lighter per value), 4 livestock ranching (animals walked to market), then wilderness. Draw them in that order and label in plain words: MILK + VEGETABLES, FIREWOOD, GRAIN, CATTLE.
- Before refrigeration raw milk sours within about a day in summer heat: the story is set on a hot summer morning. Do not give an exact hour count on screen.
- Keep it a model, not a law: do not claim every real farm followed it.
