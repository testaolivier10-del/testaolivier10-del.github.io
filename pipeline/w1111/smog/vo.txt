In the Great Smog of London, this bus conductor has to guide his own bus on foot, with a flare. The driver can't see the road. So where is it all coming from? The city's own coal fires, on a cold, still week. Normally smoke is warmer than the air above it, so it rises and drifts away. But this week a layer of warm air has settled over London... like a LID. The smoke climbs, meets air warmer than itself, and stops. It's called a thermal inversion. For five days, London breathes its own smoke. At least four thousand people die. And he's still out in front, guiding the bus home.
