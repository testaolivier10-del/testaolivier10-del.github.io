One idea (AP Macroeconomics 4.4, banking and the expansion of the money supply): a bank keeps only a fraction of its deposits as cash reserves and lends out the rest, so it cannot pay everyone at once; a run can break even a healthy bank.

## Narration (vo.txt, exactly this)
There's a bank run on his bank, and the line already goes around the block. Should he stay and get his cash out, or go home? Here's the problem. His money isn't sitting in the vault. A bank keeps only a small part of everyone's deposits as cash. The rest is out on loan, as somebody's house, somebody's shop. On a normal day that works, because only a few people want cash. But if EVERYONE comes at once... the vault runs dry, even at a healthy bank. The fear makes itself true. That's why deposits are insured. So he can go home. ## Facts (checked; every drawing must match)
- Fractional reserve banking: banks hold only part of deposits as reserves (cash in the vault and balances at the central bank) and lend the rest (mortgages, business loans). For the drawing use a simple illustration labelled as an example: of every 100 deposited, 10 kept as cash and 90 out on loan. Label it "example". Do not state any legal reserve percentage as fact (the US requirement has been zero since 2020).
- On a normal day only a small share of depositors withdraw, so the reserves are enough.
- A bank run: if many depositors withdraw at once the reserves run out, and the bank cannot call the loans back quickly; fear of failure causes the failure (self fulfilling), even at a bank that is otherwise sound.
- Deposit insurance (in the US the FDIC, up to $250,000 per depositor per bank) exists so depositors do not need to run. On screen you may show "INSURED up to $250,000 (US)". No real bank's name or logo: invent a plain one (for example "CITY SAVINGS BANK").
- The vault drawing: the cash that is there must visibly be about a tenth of what the depositors' balances add up to; the rest is shown leaving the vault earlier as loans to a house and a shop (drawn as a small house and a small shop connected to the bank by the money flowing to them), never as money that vanished.

## Premises
Morning on a town street. A man stands in a long line outside his bank. His phone shows a news alert (invented: "Is City Savings in trouble?"). He looks at the phone, looks down the line toward the doors, worried: stay or go? We go through the bank's wall into the vault: it is mostly empty shelves with one small stack of cash; his deposit is not there. Lines of light show where the rest went: a house, a shop. A normal day: two or three people come, the small stack is plenty. Then everyone at once: the stack is gone in a moment, the doors close on the line. Then the shield: INSURED. His face eases; he puts the phone away, steps out of the line and leaves it (he may simply turn his head and the camera lets the line slide past: the model has no walk; do not fake one).
