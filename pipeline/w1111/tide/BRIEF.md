## Narration (vo.txt, exactly this)
The tide is coming in, and his car is parked on the sand. The water's only crept up a few steps in the last hour, so he figures he has plenty of time. Does he? A tide doesn't rise at a steady pace. Its height follows a curve close to a sine wave. Near low tide the curve is almost flat, so the water barely moves. Halfway up, it's at its steepest. In the first hour, the sea comes up about one twelfth of the way. In the third and fourth hours... it comes up HALF. Same tide. It just saves its speed for the middle. And his car is still parked on the sand.

## Facts (checked; every drawing must match)
- A common tide takes about 6 hours from low to high (high tides are about 12 h 25 min apart). Height against time is close to a sine curve.
- Rule of twelfths: of the whole rise, hour 1 brings about 1/12, hour 2 2/12, hour 3 3/12, hour 4 3/12, hour 5 2/12, hour 6 1/12. Hours 3 and 4 together bring half. With an exact sine over 6 hours: hour 1 is 6.7%, hours 3 and 4 together exactly 50%.
- On a gently sloping beach the waterline moves up the sand in step with the height, so it advances fastest mid tide.
- The curve drawn on screen must be a true half cosine from low to high: h(t) = (1 - cos(pi t / 6)) / 2. Any marker (water level on a tide pole, the waterline on the sand, a dot on the curve) must move by exactly this function of the story clock. Never draw the water rising evenly.
