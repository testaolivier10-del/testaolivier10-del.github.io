## Facts on screen
One story clock TAU(t) drives everything through h = (1 - cos(pi tau / 6)) / 2: the waterline on the sand (screen y = 1002.2 + 564 h, so its position is exactly proportional to h), the water level on the 12 band tide pole, and the dot on the curve (which sits level with the water on the pole). The curve drawn is that same function over 6 hours. During the time lapse (27.95 to 31.10 s) the clock runs at one constant rate from hour 2.0 to hour 4.0, so the speeding up on screen is the sine curve alone. Hour 1 column is 6.7% of the height and is labelled ABOUT 1/12; hours 3 and 4 fill exactly from 25% to 75% and are labelled 1/2. Before the lapse the clock creeps (hour 1.0 to 1.07 over the first 10.8 s), then runs slowly to hour 2.0 by 27.95 s; after it, it creeps from 4.0, and the last shot starts at 4.75. A thin wave slides in and out around the true line (zero mean, 7 px).
Space is compressed (the whole run from the hour one line to the car is drawn inside one frame), so "a few steps" is a small gap on screen: the gap to the stick is one tenth of the distance left to the car, which is the true ratio.
