**Per section**
- **S1 (0–4):** The kitchen scene, title and floating molecules are clean. "Same molecule?" is a mild hook, and the line is a flat statement. The molecules are small and fade in over a busy window. Little happens beyond the title. The spearmint leaf and bread are in his hand and sit right.
- **S2 (4–8.5):** The close-up on the leaf in his fist is nice. The bread at the bottom right is a cut-off blob that looks like a stray object. His other hand is not shown holding it.
- **S3 (8.5–13.8):** The title "SPEARMINT = CARVONE" crowds a large molecule at about 12 s, and its "SPEARMINT" label is clipped at the right edge. The rye and caraway scene is attractive, and the seeds appearing is a good beat. Small ground labels such as "CARAWAY SEEDS" are weak on a phone.
- **S4 (13.8–17.2):** "Same atoms, same bonds" is shown with two ball-and-stick molecules. They read as true mirror images, with the C=O at bottom-left versus bottom-right and the substituents flipped. The background is blurred and dull. The labels sit on top of the "levlprep.com" watermark (about 17 s).
- **S5 (17.2–23.4):** The hands are the weak point. Two floating forearms come up from the bottom edge with no body, and the sleeves are cut off. The "Left" and "Right" labels sit on the arms. The red ghost-hand overlay is clever, but it looks like a drawing glitch. The "THUMBS DON'T MATCH" label covers the wrist. The "lay one on the other" action looks like a swap, not a hand moving. The idea is still clear.
- **S6 (23.4–25.5):** "Chiral" is shown as a molecule with muddy colour and low contrast. The title colour is dim olive on a busy background and hard to read.
- **S7 (25.5–28.2):** A close-up of the man asking "how does his nose tell". It is a fine bridge, but it is nearly static.
- **S8 (28.2–33.2):** The receptor scene is a good colour change, with purple and blue pockets. The pocket shapes and the carvone shapes do not clearly match each other. The "WRONG POCKET: NO FIT" label is faint and tiny, and "each fits its own" is tiny. The science claim is acceptable: chiral receptors, each version fits differently. It is simplified but not wrong. The nose, a small circle, is too small to read.
- **S9 (33.2–37.1):** The iris-style transition back to the kitchen is nice. "Mint" and "Mint or rye" carry the payoff. The molecule floats above his head with no source, so it moves by itself. The label text "One smells like mint…" is fine.
- **S10 (37.1–end):** The ending is a call to action. One hand holds the leaf at arm's length, while the bread floats at the end of a nearly absent arm with a blob hand, so the bread has no real support. The "levlprep.com" button overlaps the bread at the lower right. The video ends on the ad, not on a payoff image.

**Whole video**
- **Hook:** It is clean, but the opener is only a statement and a question. Nothing makes me want to know the answer.
- **Story and idea:** The idea lands: mirror-image molecules smell different, and our receptors are chiral too. The pacing is good, and the flow from kitchen to hands to nose is logical.
- **Fun:** It reads as a lesson. The man's face never changes, so there is no small reaction when he smells mint or rye.
- **Polish and consistency:** The style is consistent, and the film finish and blur are fine. The hand scene breaks the "one connected body" rule. Several labels are too small for a phone.
- **Motion:** The camera moves slowly throughout, with no long static frame.
- **Chemistry:** Correct as far as I can see. The molecules look like true mirror images, and the receptor claim is not wrong.
- **Contact:** The leaf is held well. The bread hand is weak in S2, S6 and S10, and the S9 molecule is unattached. Floating forearms in S5 are not a connected body.
