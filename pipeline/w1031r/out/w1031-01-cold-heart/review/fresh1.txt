## Section grades

**S1 Hook and haul (0–4.8 s): 5/10.**
- **Why:** The title is strong, but the picture doesn't show a rough lift.
  - Both faces sit forehead to forehead, so it reads as a hug or a tender carry.
  - The man's head rises about 35 px in the first second, then sits still until the yank ring pulses at 3.5–4.2 s, which barely moves him.
  - The hook says "too roughly" and the frame never shows roughness.
- **Fixes:**
  - Make the pull violent: lean back, the man's torso jerks up and out of the hole, and the sled slides under the rescuer's knees.
  - Add a big splash and a camera shake.
  - Separate the two heads so they aren't nose to nose.
  - Add a red "WRONG" tag or a red flash on the yank.

**S2 Through the ice (4.8–9.3): 4/10.**
- **Why:** This is the same wide frame as S1 for 4.5 s. Only the title changes.
  - "He went through the ice… too long" has nothing to show. There's no fall, no cracking ice, no clock or cold cue.
  - It breaks the "no static frame over about 2 s" rule.
- **Fixes:**
  - Cut to a brief flashback: a dark figure breaking through the ice, with ripples and a shiver or frost build-up on his parka.
  - Or run a slow push-in on the man while a "minutes in water" clock ticks.
  - Add a camera drift either way.

**S3 Heart lens and VF (9.3–18.8): 7/10.** This is the best section.
- **Why:** The lens opens cleanly, the slow beat and twitch read, and the "IF JOLTED" tag makes the VF cause legible.
- **Problems:**
  - From 12.0 s the lens is a 340 px disc stuck over the rescuers' torsos, and the scene below it is dead for about 6 s.
  - The VF trace at 15.4–18 s is an almost regular zigzag. It looks like flutter or VT, not chaotic VF. It needs irregular amplitude and rate.
  - The heart "quiver" is mostly a few cracks drawn on a normal heart. Add real wobble or squirm.
  - The trace strip and chip text are small. The chip is about 12 px tall on the 360 px contact sheet, which is about 36 px at full size.
  - The 9.28 s "opening" lens is a tiny dot floating above the rescuer's helmet, not clearly from his chest.
- **Fixes:** Anchor the lens beam visibly to the man's sternum, make the VF trace irregular, enlarge the trace and chip, and let the haul keep moving behind the lens.

**S4 Rough pull and her hand on his shoulder (18.8–25.1): 5/10.**
- **Why:** Cause and effect fail here.
  - The yank at about 21–22 s produces no visible rise.
  - The man's posture is nearly identical from 19 to 25 s, so "he eases him back down" isn't shown.
  - The second rescuer walks in at 22.0–22.8 s, then pops at 23.03 s into a bent, legless orange blob behind the first rescuer. That is a pose swap with no transition.
  - Her face reads male, and the narration says "her".
  - The hand lands on the first rescuer's shoulder, hidden behind his body, so the moment of contact is small.
  - The tow rope vanishes when she arrives.
  - The zoom at 25.05 s is the clearest frame in the section, and it is one frame long.
- **Fixes:**
  - Show a real yank: the man lifts, the water slaps, and a red flash marks the jolt.
  - Show her walk, kneel and touch in one continuous move.
  - Use a clear side view of her gloved hand pressing his shoulder.
  - Show him easing back down, with the man sinking back a few px.
  - Hold the zoom on the touch for 1 s or more.
  - Give her a female face and hair.

**S5 The sled slide (25.1–33.1): 6.5/10.**
- **Why:** The scene is clean and readable. The man is flat, wrapped and strapped, with two ropes. The parallax, the stride and the ambulance drift all work.
- **Problems:**
  - It's a hard cut to a new place with no hole, and the moon appears for the first time. The man's move from hole to sled is never shown.
  - The man is only a tiny gray head at the sled's left end. At phone size he is barely a person.
  - From about 31 s the camera push-in crops the red rescuer at the left edge and the orange rescuer at the right edge. Their faces are cut off at 32–34 s.
  - Nothing shows the sled sliding: no runner shadow, no snow spray, no trail line.
  - The red rescuer's face points back toward the sled while he walks away from it. Say so with a glance back, or turn him forward.
  - The drifting blue rectangle chip in the foreground (about x 130, y 548 on the contact sheet) looks like a glitch.
- **Fixes:**
  - Add a one-beat bridge from the hole to the sled.
  - Make the man's face bigger and warmer, with a blanket line.
  - Pull the camera back so both rescuers stay in frame.
  - Add runner shadow and snow spray.
  - Remove the stray rectangle.

**S6 Payoff lens and end (33.1–41.3): 6/10.**
- **Why:** The slow steady beat is correct and calm, which closes the idea.
- **Problems:**
  - The title "SLOW BUT STEADY / ALL THE WAY TO SHORE" appears at 33.07 s, but the words come at 36.8 s.
  - The last 3.3 s is nearly a hold on one frame, with only the heart beating.
  - The shore is never shown, so "all the way to shore" is a promise with no picture.
  - The lens right edge touches the orange helmet at about 34 s.
  - The ECG trace is faint.
  - The ending is a plain card, with no resolution.
- **Fixes:**
  - Pull back and show the ambulance and shore at the end.
  - Show the rescuers reaching the shore line and stopping, then fade the lens out.
  - Retime the title to the voice.
  - Add a closing beat at 38–41 s such as a sunrise glow, a wave or a paramedic hand-off.
