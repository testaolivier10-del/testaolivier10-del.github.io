**Flagged lines**

1. **"The people pulling him out are only trying to help." / "So they keep him flat…" (d, and a logic gap)**
   - "Scary part" sets up the rescuers as a danger, but the next line shows them being careful. That reads as a contradiction.
   - "So" has nothing to attach to. A viewer doesn't know why flat or slow matters, or that rough movement is what sets off the fibrillation.
   - **Rewrite:** "And here's the scary part: even a kind, quick pull could do it. So the rescuers lie him flat and slide him slowly across the ice, like he's made of glass. Every bump is a risk."

2. **"his heart keeps its slow, steady beat" (d)**
   - "Slow" is unexplained. A viewer will wonder why a good outcome is a slow heartbeat.
   - **Rewrite:** "And because they're that careful... his cold heart keeps beating, slow but steady."

3. **"Lift him too roughly, and this hypothermia rescue could stop his heart." (a, mild)**
   - "This hypothermia rescue" sounds like a label from a textbook or a title card.
   - **Rewrite:** "Lift him too roughly, and you could stop his heart."

4. **"ONE hard jolt can tip it into ventricular fibrillation, where the heart just quivers instead of pumping." (d, mild)**
   - The term is explained, but it's heavy for a first listen. The definition does carry it.
   - **Optional rewrite:** "ONE hard jolt can throw it into a quiver, called ventricular fibrillation, where it stops pumping."

**Passes:** "He went through the ice…" and "A heart this cold gets twitchy." Both are conversational and have varied rhythm. Nothing in the script has three flat, same-shaped sentences in a row, so there are no (c) flags and no (b) list-style problems.

**Main issue:** The cause-and-effect chain (cold, then twitchy, then a jolt is dangerous, then careful handling, then a steady beat) breaks at the "helping" line. The viewer has to guess why careful handling matters.
