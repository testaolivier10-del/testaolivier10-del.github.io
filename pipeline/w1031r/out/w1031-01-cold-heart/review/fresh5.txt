# Review of "Cold Heart" (EMT Traps #2), 41 s

I looked at the stills sheets, the hand crops, the 2 fps motion sheets, the old sheet and both thumbnails. It is clearly better than the old version. The look is cleaner, the heart lens works, and the sled walk is much more believable. It is not at 8/10 yet. The main problems are small figures in wide shots, a weak and cropped ending, a few clipped or overlapping elements, and long stretches where the picture barely changes.

## Section grades

**S1 Hook and haul (0–4.7 s): 6.5**
- The hook line is strong, but the picture doesn't show danger. Both figures are small in a wide shot, and the bottom 40% of the frame is empty ice.
- The yank at 0.25–0.5 s reads well (bubbles, his body rises when the rescuer pulls). From 1.0 to 2.5 s the frame is nearly static, with only a small slump.
- The man shows no hands. His arm ends in a cuff, and at 2.75–3.0 s it dangles.
- The two faces sit forehead to forehead like a hug. It reads as odd and intimate, not as a haul.
- Fixes:
  - Open tight, like thumb A (rescuer's fist on his chest, his head snapping back), then pull out.
  - Add 2–3 distinct yanks with a camera nudge on each.
  - Keep the faces from touching.
  - Give the man hands or gloved fists.

**S2 Under the ice (4.7–8.8 s): 7.5**
- The cut is clear and the black-water mood is good. The hanging legs and bubbles read instantly.
- The title ("THROUGH THE ICE / WAY TOO LONG") sits on the bright ice band at 4.8 s and 8.0 s. "WAY TOO LONG" runs into the ice edge.
- The legs hang almost still, and there is little sense of cold or depth.
- Fixes:
  - Move the title down into dark water, or darken the band behind it.
  - Add a slow drift or sink of the legs, slow bubbles, and light shafts from the hole.
  - Add a slow push-in.

**S3 Heart lens and VF (8.8–18.8 s): 7**
- The lens opening from the chest is a good device. Slow, cold beat → twitch → chaos is a clear arc.
- For 10 s the scene underneath is almost frozen. Only the heart and trace change, which is the static-frame problem.
- The lens (10–18 s) sits on the rescuer's helmet and forehead.
- The chip "SLOW, COLD BEAT" overlaps the lens top edge. "VENTRICULAR FIBRILLATION" is tiny red text on a dark-red pill, hard to read on a phone.
- The VF trace is a thin line low in the lens.
- At 16–18 s the heart outline barely changes, so the "quivers" claim isn't shown on the heart itself.
- The caption and the "levlprep.com" watermark sit over the hole and the rescuer's boot at 12–18 s.
- Fixes:
  - Make the heart visibly quiver, with a non-uniform shiver and a colour shift.
  - Enlarge the trace and the VF chip.
  - Move the lens off the faces, for example above the head with the beam coming up from his chest.
  - Keep the figures alive underneath with breathing, steam, a headlamp sweep and small camera drift.

**S4 Rough pull and her hand on him (18.8–25.1 s): 6.5**
- The yank at 21.0 s works, with his head thrown back and bubbles. The woman's walk-in is a good beat.
- A stray ring or target marker sits on the man's chin or collar at 18.78 s. Remove it.
- The "haul eases him back down" beat is too subtle. His body barely lowers between 22 and 24 s.
- At 22–24 s she is cropped at the right edge. Her arm looks long from the edge to his shoulder.
- Her hand does land on his shoulder (the tech check is 2.6 px), but at phone size it is a dark blob on a dark parka.
- At 25.05 s there is a tight three-face close-up, then a hard cut to the wide. The close-up is crowded, with three heads touching.
- Fixes:
  - Frame her fully in the shot.
  - Show his torso clearly sinking and settling as her hand lands.
  - Add a short glow or colour change on contact.
  - Hold the three-head close-up, or cut it.

**S5 Sled slide and payoff lens (25.1–38.3 s): 7**
- Layout and causality are good. The sled moves only while the ropes are taut and the rescuers walk. Feet look planted, and the walk cycle is fine.
- The characters are tiny from 25 to 31 s, with a big empty lower third and a lot of flat ice.
- From 31 to 38 s the framing crops the red rescuer at the left edge, so only his legs show. The woman is cut at the right edge at 30.6 s. The man's head sits near the left edge.
- The payoff lens is small and plain, and its trace is thin. "SLOW, STEADY BEAT" is a pale mint chip with low contrast.
- The camera change from 31 to 38 s is a push, but the content is the same.
- Fixes:
  - Keep both rescuers in frame, or deliberately frame the sled and man.
  - Raise the characters by moving the ground line up.
  - Add ice-crack detail and parallax.
  - Use a bigger, warmer lens with a bolder trace.

**S6 The shore and end (38.3–41.3 s): 5.5**
- This is the weakest section. The ambulance is small and sits at the far left. The "warm light over him" is barely visible.
- The man is cropped at the right edge and hidden behind the sled. The woman is cropped at the right edge too.
- The red rescuer is huge in the foreground and blocks the payoff.
- The last 1.3 s is a title card, "levlprep.com", with empty sky above. No end beat shows the man arriving.
- Fixes:
  - Frame the ambulance with open doors and a warm light cone across the sled and his face.
  - Let the sled come to a stop in the light, and show the rescuers lifting or stopping.
  - Hold a clean final image on him warm and safe.
  - Fill the empty sky with a moon, stars and snow.

## Concrete flaws

- 0–4.7 s, centre: tiny figures, empty lower 40%, forehead-to-forehead faces.
- 4.8 s and 8.0 s, top: the title overlaps the bright ice band.
- 9.3–18.8 s, top-centre: the lens sits over the rescuer's helmet. The "SLOW, COLD BEAT" chip clips the lens top. The VF chip is tiny.
- 12–18 s, bottom: the caption and watermark cover the hole, ripples and boot.
- 18.78 s, man's chin: a stray ring or marker.
- 22–25 s, right edge: the woman is cropped and her arm is long.
- 25.05 s: a crowded three-head close-up, then a hard cut.
- 31–38 s, left edge: the red rescuer is cropped to legs. The man's head is at the edge.
- 38.3–41.3 s: the ambulance is tiny, the man is hidden or cropped, and the warm light is faint. The end card sits under an empty sky.
- Throughout: the man has no visible hands, only a cuff dot.
