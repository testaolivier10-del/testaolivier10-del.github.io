## Section grades

**S1 Hook and haul (0–4.8 s): 6/10.** The title is a clear hook and the night scene is atmospheric. But the "rough haul" doesn't read as rough.
- The man pops up out of the hole in the first 0.5 s, then both figures hold nearly the same pose from about 1.0 to 4.8 s. That is a static stretch of over 3 s.
- The rescuer is forehead to forehead with the man, with the glove resting on his shoulder. It reads as a hug or a tender lift, not a wrong move.
- The red pulse ring at 2.5–4.5 s is the only cue that something is wrong.
- The orange rescuer is cropped at the left edge, holding a slack rope, and does nothing.
- **Fixes:**
  - Show a visible jerk: the man's body stretches, his head snaps back, water sheets off him.
  - Pull the faces apart so the rescuer leans back, looks away or strains.
  - Put the glove on the chest or harness strap, and keep the man in the hole while he's hauled.
  - Add a camera push or shake on the yank.

**S2 Through the ice (4.8–9.3): 5/10.** Almost nothing happens for 4.5 s, and the visuals contradict the line.
- "He went through the ice… in that freezing lake way too long" plays over the same two figures from S1. The man is already half out and calm.
- Only the title and a slow zoom change.
- There is no ice-break moment, no clock or frost cue, and no sign of how long he has been in the water. Frost on his hat is the only hint.
- At 9.0 s the lens dot appears on his chest with nothing to explain it.
- **Fixes:**
  - Cut to a short ice-crack or splash beat, or a wide shot of the hole and the broken edge.
  - Show his hands clawing at the ice.
  - Add a rising frost or time cue on the line "way too long".
  - Use the 2 s hold for a camera move and a different framing.

**S3 Heart lens and VF (9.3–18.8): 6.5/10.** The lens idea works and the heart art is clean, but the lens is hard to read.
- The lens grows from his chest (9.28) and then parks in the upper left. It covers the rescuer's face and headlamp from 10 to 18 s.
- The ECG strip is a thin line at the bottom of a small circle, so it's unreadable on a phone. The VF squiggle (about 15–18 s) is a red sliver.
- The "IF JOLTED: VENTRICULAR FIBRILLATION" chip is small and crowded under the title.
- "SLOW, COLD BEAT" sits tight against the title and the lens rim.
- No jolt is shown. The man and rescuer freeze behind the lens for 9.5 s, so the cause (rough pull → VF) is only spoken.
- The "extra twitchy beat" is not visible at this size.
- **Fixes:**
  - Make the ECG trace a large, wide band, about 3 times taller, with a visible twitch spike.
  - Make the heart shake on screen, with a flash or shock cue at "ONE hard jolt".
  - Move the lens so it doesn't sit over the faces.
  - Make the chips about 1.4 times larger.
  - Give the man a small shiver or breath animation behind the lens.

**S4 Rough pull and her hand on his shoulder (18.8–25.1): 6/10.** The yank has real energy, but the key contact is lost.
- The yank at 21.0 is good: his head snaps back, there's a splash, and the hands are on his shoulder.
- Before that, 19–20.8 s is nearly static.
- The orange rescuer arrives at 21.8–22.8 and crouches directly behind the red rescuer. Their bodies stack like a piggyback, with a limb tangle at 22.5–23.5.
- Her hand on the man's shoulder is hidden by the red rescuer's body, or it's just a dark glove blob near the red rescuer's face and chin. I couldn't confirm it touches him.
- The zoom at 24.5–25.05 crops the man at the right edge exactly when the calm touch should land. The hand-on-shoulder beat, which is the story's key beat, isn't legible.
- "Her" is not distinguishable: she has the same face and build as the other rescuers.
- The moon sits behind "A FAST" and "COULD" in the title from 21 to 24 s, which hurts legibility.
- The rope trailing off the left edge disappears at 21.5.
- **Fixes:**
  - Stage her at the man's other side, clearly in profile, with a visible hand on his shoulder and a gentle press.
  - Give her a different look (hair, build).
  - Hold a 1 s close-up of the hand landing.
  - Show the man lowering as his hands come down slowly.
  - Move the moon out of the title zone.

**S5 The sled slide (25.1–33.1): 7/10.** This is the cleanest section visually. The man is flat, wrapped and strapped, the sled rides on the ice, and both ropes end in hands.
- The wide shot at 25.1 is a hard cut from the zoom. It works as a "reset" but is abrupt.
- The tow and brake roles look odd. Red at the tail and orange at the front face each other with the sled between them. That reads as two people holding a stretcher, not one towing and one steadying.
- The orange rescuer stands idle 25–26.5 s with a slack rope. Red enters at 26 s mid-stride.
- Feet look planted, but there's very little sense of weight or speed. The ice moves only slightly.
- The camera pushes in at 31 to 32 s and loses both rescuers. Only a red leg remains at the left and the sled is cropped on the right.
- The bottom 40% of the frame is empty ice.
- **Fixes:**
  - Turn the front rescuer to face forward.
  - Add ice-spray or track marks behind the sled.
  - Keep the camera wide, or track with the sled.
  - Fill the lower frame with ice cracks and a long sled shadow, or shift the whole composition lower.

**S6 Payoff lens and end (33.1–41.3): 6.5/10.** The beat is correct and the message lands, but the screen is static for too long and the end is weak.
- The heart lens at 33.5–38.2 shows a calm green beat, but the trace is tiny.
- The lens covers the ambulance behind it, and the chip overlaps the trees.
- The scene holds on the same flat sled crop for about 5 s (33–38 s).
- The lens shifts off at 38.25 and the rescuers cut in, then a pull-back at 39.5–41.3.
- The payoff is spoken ("all the way to shore") but no shore is shown. The ambulance is the only destination, and it's small and far away.
- The end card is the wordmark only, with no CTA or lingering visual.
- **Fixes:**
  - End on a reveal: the sled arriving at the ambulance or the shore.
  - Make the trace large and steady, with a satisfying contrast to the earlier VF.
  - Move the lens above the man's head and clear of the ambulance.
  - Add a soft glow or breath at the last beat.
