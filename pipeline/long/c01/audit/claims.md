# Factual claims to check (yt-apchem-01: units, exponents, scientific notation; AP Chemistry)
1. Water is made of molecules far too small to see.
2. AP Chemistry exam graders (Chief Reader reports) repeatedly name unit slips (missing units, g vs kg or J vs kJ, °C vs K) and number slips as common ways students lose points.
3. Chemistry uses the metric system (SI units).
4. kilo = 1000; milli = 1/1000; micro = 1/1,000,000; nano = 1/1,000,000,000. 1 kg = 1000 g; 1000 mL = 1 L.
5. The AP Chemistry equations and constants sheet given on the exam lists the metric prefixes.
6. 1 dollar = 100 cents, so (100 cents / 1 dollar) = 1. (1000 m / 1 km) = 1.
7. 2.45 km × (1000 m / 1 km) = 2450 m. The method is called dimensional analysis (also factor label method).
8. 4250 mg = 0.00425 kg via mg → g → kg.
9. Density is mass per volume; water's density is about 1 g/mL (1.00 g/mL).
10. K = °C + 273.15; 25 °C ≈ 298 K. Forgetting to convert to kelvin is a frequently cited lost point on the AP Chem free response.
11. Story: 100 drops fill 5 mL, so one drop = 0.05 mL (typical faucet/dropper drop about 0.05 mL, 20 drops per mL, is a plausible size).
12. 1 drop/s × 0.05 mL/drop × 60 s/min × 60 min/h × 24 h/day = 4320 mL/day ≈ 4.3 L/day ("over four liters a day").
13. Practice: 30 m/s in km/h (answer withheld; see questions file).
14. 10⁴ = 10,000; a positive power of ten is 1 followed by that many zeros.
15. 10² × 10³ = 10⁵ (multiply: add exponents); 10⁵ / 10² = 10³ (divide: subtract); (10²)³ = 10⁶ (power of a power: multiply). These rules need the same base.
16. 10⁰ = 1; 10²/10⁵ = 10⁻³ = 1/1000 = 0.001; a negative exponent makes a number small, not negative.
17. 10⁴ + 10⁴ = 20,000 = 2 × 10⁴ (not 10⁸).
18. A billion = 10⁹; a billion billion = 10¹⁸; a thousand of those = 10²¹ (a number with 21 zeros).
19. A single water molecule is roughly 3 × 10⁻¹⁰ m across (about 0.3 nm).
20. Ten million water molecules side by side would stretch about 3 mm, which is less than the width of a 0.05 mL drop (a 0.05 mL sphere is about 4.6 mm across).
21. A glass of water holds about 8 × 10²⁴ molecules (a typical glass, about 240 mL).
22. Scientific notation: coefficient at least 1 and less than 10, times a power of ten giving how many places the decimal moved; large numbers (point moved left) get positive exponents, small numbers (moved right) negative exponents.
23. Earth to Sun distance about 149,600,000 km = 1.496 × 10⁸ km.
24. A fine smoke particle can be about 0.000000650 m (650 nm) wide = 6.50 × 10⁻⁷ m.
25. (3.0 × 10⁴)(2.0 × 10⁻⁶) = 6.0 × 10⁻²; division: divide coefficients, subtract exponents.
26. 52 × 10⁵ = 5.2 × 10⁶.
27. 6.0 × 10³ + 4.0 × 10² = 6.4 × 10³ (exponents must match before adding).
28. On a calculator the EE / EXP key means "× 10 to the"; entering "× 10" and then EE gives an answer ten times too big; 3.2 EE (−) 5 = 3.2 × 10⁻⁵, shown as 3.2E-5.
29. One gram of water contains about 3.34 × 10²² molecules (from Avogadro's number 6.022 × 10²³ and molar mass 18.02 g/mol).
30. 0.05 mL × 1 g/mL × 3.34 × 10²² molecules/g = 1.67 × 10²¹ molecules; 0.05 = 5 × 10⁻²; 5 × 3.34 = 16.7; 16.7 × 10²⁰ = 1.67 × 10²¹; "almost two thousand billion billion".
