## Units and dimensional analysis
[u1] Say your lab partner tells you the sample is twelve point six. Twelve point six what? Grams? Milliliters? Degrees? Until it has a unit, a number in chemistry doesn't tell you anything. The unit is part of the measurement, so it stays on the number in every line you write.
[u2] Chemistry uses the metric system, and instead of inventing new units for every size, it puts a prefix in front. Kilo means a thousand, so a kilogram is a thousand grams. Milli means a thousandth, so a thousand milliliters make one liter. Micro is a millionth, and nano is a billionth. The exam's equation sheet lists these, so you don't have to memorize them, you just have to use them in the right direction.
[u3] So how do you switch units? Think about changing money. If one dollar gets you a hundred cents, then a hundred cents over one dollar is just a way of writing ONE. A conversion factor works the same way. A thousand meters over one kilometer equals one, because they're the same length. Multiply by it, and the number and unit change together, but the amount doesn't, because you only multiplied by one.
[u4] The whole trick is which way up you put the factor. Units cancel like numbers do: the same unit on the top and on the bottom divides out. So put the unit you HAVE on the bottom, and the unit you want on top. A trail is two point four five kilometers long. Times a thousand meters over one kilometer, the kilometers cancel, and you get two thousand four hundred fifty meters. This is called dimensional analysis.
[u5] Then check the size. A meter is much smaller than a kilometer, so you should need MORE of them, and you do. If your number had gotten smaller instead, the factor was upside down. That one quick check catches most unit mistakes.
[u6] When there's no single factor between two units, chain a few. Say you want four thousand two hundred fifty milligrams in kilograms. Milligrams to grams first, then grams to kilograms. Each factor cancels what the last one left behind, and you get point zero zero four two five kilograms.
[u7] And any time you see the word per, that's a conversion factor too. Density is mass per volume. Water's is about one gram per milliliter, so it converts a volume of water into a mass. Temperature is the odd one out, because it's an add, not a ratio. Kelvin is Celsius plus two seventy three point one five, so twenty five degrees Celsius is about two ninety eight kelvin. Forgetting that costs a LOT of points on the exam.
[u8] (story) Back in the kitchen, Maya starts where any chemist would, by measuring. She holds a medicine cup under the faucet and counts the drops. A hundred drops fill it to five milliliters, so one drop is five milliliters over a hundred, which is point zero five milliliters. And with the timer on her phone, the faucet drips about once every second.

## Powers of ten
[e1] Chemistry's numbers get ridiculously big and small, so it leans on exponents. An exponent is the small raised number that tells you how many times to multiply the base by itself. In ten to the fourth, the base is ten, and it's ten times ten times ten times ten, which is ten thousand. So a positive power of ten is just a one followed by that many zeros.
[e2] Now, what happens when you multiply two of them? Ten squared times ten cubed. Write it out: two tens, then three tens. That's five tens in a row, so it's ten to the fifth. When you multiply, you ADD the exponents. Dividing works the other way. Ten to the fifth over ten squared: two tens on the bottom cancel two on top, so you subtract, and get ten cubed.
[e3] And a power of a power multiplies. Ten squared, cubed, means ten squared three times over, which is six tens. Ten to the sixth. And all of these need the same base.
[e4] Keep dividing and something interesting happens. Ten cubed over ten cubed is ten to the zero, but anything divided by itself is one. So ten to the zero is one. Go one step further. Ten squared over ten to the fifth is ten to the negative three, and writing it out leaves three tens on the bottom: one over a thousand. So a negative exponent doesn't make a number negative. It makes it SMALL. Ten to the negative three is point zero zero one.
[e5] One trap. These rules are for multiplying and dividing, not adding. Ten to the fourth plus ten to the fourth is ten thousand plus ten thousand, which is twenty thousand. That's two times ten to the fourth, not ten to the eighth.
[e6] (story) Theo wants to know how big the answer is going to be, so Maya gives him a hint. It's a number with about twenty one zeros. He starts writing zeros and runs out of room. So she shows him the shortcut. A billion is ten to the ninth, a billion billions is ten to the ninth times ten to the ninth, so you add: ten to the eighteenth. And a thousand of those is ten to the twenty first.
[e7] (story) Then he asks how small a single water molecule is. It's roughly three times ten to the negative tenth of a meter across. That's three over ten billion of a meter. Line up ten million of them, and they'd stretch about three millimeters, still less than the width of that drop.
[p2q] Your turn, two quick ones. First, ten to the sixth times ten to the negative ninth, all divided by ten to the negative two. Second, ten cubed plus ten cubed.
[p2a] On top, six plus negative nine is negative three, so ten to the negative three. Then divide: negative three minus negative two. Subtracting a negative adds, so that's negative one. Ten to the negative one, which is point one. And ten cubed plus ten cubed is a thousand plus a thousand, two thousand. That's two times ten cubed, not ten to the sixth.

## Scientific notation
[s1] A glass of water has about eight times ten to the twenty fourth molecules. Written out, that's twenty four zeros, and you WILL miscount them. Scientific notation tucks the zeros into an exponent. It has two parts: a coefficient, which is at least one and less than ten, and a power of ten that says how far the decimal point moved.
[s2] To convert a big number, slide the decimal point left until one nonzero digit is in front of it. The Earth is about one hundred forty nine million six hundred thousand kilometers from the Sun. Slide the point left eight places, and it's one point four nine six times ten to the eighth. Big number, moved left, positive exponent.
[s3] A small number goes the other way. A fine smoke particle is about point zero zero zero zero zero zero six five zero meters wide. Slide the point RIGHT, past the six, which is seven places, and you get six point five zero times ten to the negative seventh. Small number, moved right, negative exponent.
[s4] To multiply, handle the two parts separately. Three point zero times ten to the fourth, times two point zero times ten to the negative sixth. Coefficients: three times two is six. Powers: add the exponents, four plus negative six, ten to the negative two. So six point zero times ten to the negative two. Dividing works the same way, but you divide and subtract.
[s5] Sometimes the coefficient comes out too big, like fifty two times ten to the fifth. Fifty two is five point two times ten, so make the coefficient ten times smaller and the power of ten ten times bigger: five point two times ten to the sixth. Same number, proper form.
[s6] Adding is different again. The exponents have to match first, the way you line up decimal points. Six point zero times ten cubed plus four point zero times ten squared: rewrite the second one as point four zero times ten cubed, then add the coefficients, and you get six point four times ten cubed. That's six thousand plus four hundred.
[s7] And on your calculator, use the E E or E X P key. It already means times ten to the. To enter three point two times ten to the negative fifth, type three point two, E E, negative five. Don't type times ten as well, or your answer comes out ten times too big. That one shows up on exams every year.
[s8] (story) Now Maya can finally count. She looks up one fact chemists measured long ago: one gram of water holds about three point three four times ten to the twenty second molecules. She builds the chain. Point zero five milliliters per drop, times one gram per milliliter, times three point three four times ten to the twenty second molecules per gram. Milliliters cancel, grams cancel, and she's left with molecules.
[s9] (story) Point zero five is five times ten to the negative two. Five times three point three four is sixteen point seven, and negative two plus twenty two is twenty. But sixteen point seven isn't a proper coefficient, so she fixes it: one point six seven times ten to the twenty first. That's almost two thousand billion billion molecules... in ONE drop.
[p3q] Your turn. Multiply four point zero times ten to the fifth by six point zero times ten to the negative eighth, and give the answer in proper scientific notation.
[p3a] Four times six is twenty four, and five plus negative eight is negative three. So it's twenty four times ten to the negative third. But twenty four is too big for a coefficient. Make it two point four, and bump the power up by one: two point four times ten to the negative second. Stopping at twenty four times ten to the negative third is the most common slip here. The value is right, but it isn't scientific notation.

## Recap
[rc] So here's the whole video in one picture. Every number carries its unit, and a conversion factor is just one, written with the unit you have on the bottom. When you multiply powers of ten you add the exponents, when you divide you subtract, and a negative exponent means small. And scientific notation is a coefficient from one up to ten, times a power of ten, with the decimal moving left for big numbers and right for small ones.
