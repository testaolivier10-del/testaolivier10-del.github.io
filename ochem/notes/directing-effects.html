The question: where does the arenium ion's positive charge land best?

Recall from the EAS lesson that the arenium ion's positive charge delocalizes by resonance onto three ring carbons — the carbons ortho and para to wherever the electrophile attacked. A substituent already on the ring dramatically changes how stable each of those three resonance forms is, which decides which position (ortho/para vs. meta relative to the existing substituent) gives the most stable arenium ion — and therefore, by the same logic as every other regioselectivity question in this course, where the reaction actually happens.

Activators: donate electron density, direct ortho/para, speed up the reaction

A group with a lone pair adjacent to the ring (OH, OR, NH₂, NR₂) can donate that lone pair directly into the ring by resonance — and critically, that donation is only possible in the resonance structures where the positive charge sits on the ring carbon directly attached to the substituent, which only happens for ortho and para attack. This makes ortho/para attack pass through a much more stabilized (lower energy, faster-forming) arenium ion, so these groups are ortho/para directors and also activators — they speed up the overall reaction rate compared to benzene itself, since the electron-donation that directs also raises the ring's general nucleophilicity.

Alkyl groups (like the methyl in toluene) are also ortho/para directors and mild activators — not through resonance donation (no lone pair) but through the same weak hyperconjugation/induction effect that stabilizes carbocations generally (Module 7), which similarly stabilizes the arenium ion most when positive charge lands on the substituted carbon itself (ortho/para attack).

Deactivators: all slow the reaction down, but they do not all direct to the same place

Halogens (F, Cl, Br, I) are unusual: their strong electronegativity inductively withdraws electron density (deactivating — slowing the reaction relative to benzene), yet they still direct ortho/para, because their lone pairs can still donate by resonance into the ring (just less effectively than OH/NH₂, since halogen lone pairs sit in orbitals that overlap poorly with carbon's — the same overlap-mismatch idea from acid chloride resonance donation, Esters & Amides lesson). Inductive withdrawal wins on rate; resonance donation still wins on regiochemistry.

Everything else strongly electron-withdrawing (NO₂, C=O-containing groups like CHO/COOH/COR, CN, SO₃H) is both deactivating and meta-directing: these groups either withdraw electron density purely inductively (with no lone pair or pi system oriented to donate back into the ring), or — worse — carry their own adjacent positive/partial-positive character (like a carbonyl carbon) that would be directly destabilized by having ring positive charge concentrated next to it in the ortho/para resonance forms. Attack at the meta position avoids ever putting positive charge on the substituted carbon itself, making meta attack the "least bad" (least destabilized) option available.
