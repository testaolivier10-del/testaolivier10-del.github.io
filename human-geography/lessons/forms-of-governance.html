Unit 4 · Topic 4.7

Forms of Governance

States share power across space in two main ways.

Practice 1: Concepts and ProcessesPractice 2: Spatial Relationships

Part 1 · Hook

Why this matters

Move from Texas to California and the minimum wage, the sales tax and many school rules change, though you never leave the country. Move from one region of France to another and they mostly stay the same. The difference is how each state shares power across space: the United States is federal, France is unitary.

Part 5 · Step by step

How it works, step by step

  1. A state is small or culturally similar across its land→it can run most matters from the capital, so it is often unitary
  2. A state is very large or holds many nations and languages→regions want to run some matters their own way, so it is often federal
  3. A constitution protects regional powers→laws differ from one region to the next, while the national government keeps defense and money

Part 6 · Key ideas

Key ideas

  • A unitary state concentrates power in the national government; regions carry out its decisions and have only the powers it gives them (France, Japan, China).
  • A federal state divides power by constitution between a national government and regional governments, each with its own powers (the United States, Germany, India, Brazil, Nigeria).
  • Federal systems often suit very large or culturally diverse states. A unitary state can still have an autonomous region (Åland in Finland).
  • A federal state still has a national government. Always name it when you describe federalism.

Part 7 · Misconception

A common mistake

The wrong idea: In a federal state, the regions run everything and there is no real national government.

What actually happens: A federal state has a national government that runs matters such as defense, money and foreign policy, while regional governments run others, such as schools and police.

1. Which form of government does Country Q have?

  2. Unitary, run from the capital

Show the answer

In Country Q, schools, police and some taxes belong to the provinces, and the constitution protects those powers: a federal state.

  • Correct: Federal: Right: Its provinces have their own powers, set out in a constitution.
  • Unitary, run from the capital: In a unitary state, the national government decides most matters, as in Country P.
  • Autonomous: An autonomous region is a part of a state, not a whole system of government.
  • Stateless: Stateless describes a nation with no state.

2. Which row of the table best shows that Country P is a unitary state?

  1. Army and money
  2. Income tax and who collects it
  3. Police and courts in each province
  4. Who can change these powers
Show the answer

In a unitary state, the national government holds final power and can give or take powers from regions by ordinary law. That is what the last row shows for Country P.

  • Army and money: Both countries give these to the national government.
  • Income tax and who collects it: This row shows that Country Q shares taxes; it does not show who holds final power in P.
  • Police and courts in each province: This row matters, but the strongest sign is who can change the powers.
  • Correct: Who can change these powers: Right: The national parliament alone can change who decides what.

3. A family moves from one province of Country Q to another. Based on the table, what is most likely to change for them?

  1. The money they use to buy food and pay rent
  2. The army that defends them
  3. What their children are taught at school
  4. Whether they pay any national income tax
Show the answer

In a federal state, laws differ from one subnational unit to the next for the matters that belong to them, such as schools in Country Q.

  • The money they use to buy food and pay rent: Money is decided by the national government in both countries.
  • The army that defends them: The army is national in both countries.
  • Correct: What their children are taught at school: Right: Each province decides its own school lessons.
  • Whether they pay any national income tax: The national government taxes income in every province.

4. What does the table show about both countries?

  1. Neither has a national government
  2. Both have a national government that controls the army and money
  3. Both let provinces decide what schools teach
  4. Both need the provinces to agree before powers change
Show the answer

Readers who score the exam report that students describe a federal state as if it had no national government. It does: power is shared between the national and the subnational levels.

  • Neither has a national government: Both have one; the first row shows it.
  • Correct: Both have a national government that controls the army and money: Right: A federal state still has a strong national level.
  • Both let provinces decide what schools teach: In Country P, the national government decides.
  • Both need the provinces to agree before powers change: That is true of Country Q alone.

5. Which statement correctly compares unitary and federal states?

  1. Unitary states have no regions of any kind; federal states are split into regions
  2. Unitary states are large; federal states are small
  3. Both have a national government; federal ones also protect regional powers
  4. Federal states have no national government; unitary states have one
Show the answer

A compare speaks of both sides. Both forms have a national government; in a federal state the constitution also protects powers for regional governments.

  • Unitary states have no regions of any kind; federal states are split into regions: Unitary states have regions too, but those carry out national decisions.
  • Unitary states are large; federal states are small: Small Switzerland is federal and large China is unitary.
  • Correct: Both have a national government; federal ones also protect regional powers: Right: The difference is whether regional powers are protected by a constitution.
  • Federal states have no national government; unitary states have one: Federal states have a national government that runs defense and money.

Part 9 · Summary

Summary

States share power across space in two main ways. A unitary state concentrates power in the national government, which gives regions only the powers it chooses. A federal state divides power by constitution between a national government and regional governments, so laws differ from region to region. Large or culturally diverse states are often federal, and both forms always keep a national government.
