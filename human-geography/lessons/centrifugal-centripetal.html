Unit 4 · Topic 4.10

Consequences of Centrifugal and Centripetal Forces

Centrifugal forces pull a state apart; centripetal forces hold it together.

Practice 2: Spatial Relationships

Part 1 · Hook

Why this matters

In 1885, the last spike was driven on a railway that tied British Columbia to the rest of Canada; the province had joined Canada in 1871 on the promise of that line. In 1991, Somalia's government collapsed and did not return for many years. Some forces hold states together; others pull them apart. What happens to a state depends on which side is stronger.

Part 5 · Step by step

How it works, step by step

  1. Regions are unequal, or groups have rival identities→centrifugal forces grow: resentment, separatist and ethnic nationalist movements
  2. Centrifugal forces overwhelm the national government→the state may break apart, or fail and lose control of its land
  3. A state shares money fairly, links its regions and builds a shared identity→centripetal forces grow and cultural cohesion increases

Part 6 · Key ideas

Key ideas

  • Centrifugal forces can lead to failed states (Somalia after 1991), uneven development, stateless nations and ethnic nationalist movements.
  • Centripetal forces can lead to ethnonationalism that unites a nation-state, fairer spread of roads, rail and services, and cultural cohesion (Swahili in Tanzania).
  • One trait can do both at different scales: French unites Quebec but can divide Canada.

Part 7 · Misconception

A common mistake

The wrong idea: A trait such as language or religion is always either a centripetal or a centrifugal force.

What actually happens: It depends on the scale and the state. A language shared by a whole state unites it; a language spoken by one region's majority can unite that region and divide the state.

Text

A report on invented Country R

Country R became independent in 1962. Its government made Rava, a language spoken by few people as a first language, the language of every school, so children of all peoples learn it. In 1975 a railway was finished from the port to the far north. Since 1990, the richer south has paid into a fund that builds clinics in the poorer north.

Yet in 2024 the Ostin people of the western hills, who speak their own language, held protests asking for their own schools. Many Ostin say jobs and roads go to the south first.

Invented for practice.

1. Which policy in the passage is a centripetal force?

  1. Teaching Rava in every school
  2. The Ostin protests for schools in their own language
  3. Jobs and roads going to the south first
  4. The Ostin speaking their own language
Show the answer

A centripetal force unites a state. A shared school language links all of Country R's peoples.

  • Correct: Teaching Rava in every school: Right: A shared language learned by all peoples pulls the state together.
  • The Ostin protests for schools in their own language: Demands for separate schools pull away from the center.
  • Jobs and roads going to the south first: Unequal treatment is a centrifugal force.
  • The Ostin speaking their own language: A separate language can pull a group apart from the state.

2. According to the passage, what is the main centrifugal force at work among the Ostin?

  1. The railway from the port to the far north
  2. The fund that builds clinics in the north
  3. The year Country R became independent
  4. A feeling that jobs and roads favor the south
Show the answer

Uneven development, or the belief that one region gets more, is a centrifugal force. The passage says many Ostin feel jobs and roads go to the south first.

  • The railway from the port to the far north: The railway links regions; it is centripetal.
  • The fund that builds clinics in the north: Sharing money with poorer areas is centripetal.
  • The year Country R became independent: A date is not a force.
  • Correct: A feeling that jobs and roads favor the south: Right: Uneven development is a centrifugal force.

3. How does the clinic fund described in the passage help hold Country R together?

  1. It moves people from the north to the south
  2. It makes the Ostin language the national language of Country R
  3. It spreads services to poorer areas, easing resentment
  4. It gives the north its own army
Show the answer

Fairer sharing of money and services, as in Canada's payments to poorer provinces, is a centripetal force: it lowers the sense of unfairness that drives regions apart.

  • It moves people from the north to the south: The fund builds clinics; it does not move people.
  • It makes the Ostin language the national language of Country R: Rava is the school language.
  • Correct: It spreads services to poorer areas, easing resentment: Right: Sharing money more fairly is centripetal.
  • It gives the north its own army: The passage says nothing about an army.

4. The Ostin language binds the Ostin together but sets them apart from the rest of Country R. What does this show?

  1. Language is a centripetal force in each case
  2. One trait can unite at one scale and divide at another
  3. Language has no effect on how united or divided a state is
  4. The Ostin are a failed state
Show the answer

Scale matters: the same language that is centripetal for the Ostin (regional or local scale) is centrifugal for Country R (national scale), just as French unites Quebec but can divide Canada.

  • Language is a centripetal force in each case: Here it divides Country R at the national scale.
  • Correct: One trait can unite at one scale and divide at another: Right: Language unites the Ostin locally and divides the state nationally.
  • Language has no effect on how united or divided a state is: The protests show it does.
  • The Ostin are a failed state: The Ostin are a people, not a state.

5. After its national government collapsed in 1991, Somalia had no working government across most of its land for many years, while armed groups controlled different areas. What does this show?

  1. Centrifugal forces can cause a failed state
  2. Centripetal forces can lead to a failed state over time
  3. A strong ethnonationalism splits each state
  4. Supranationalism leads to failed states
Show the answer

When centrifugal forces, such as rivalry between armed groups, overwhelm the center, a state can fail: it can no longer keep order or provide services across its territory.

  • Correct: Centrifugal forces can cause a failed state: Right: Rival groups pulled the state apart until the center stopped working.
  • Centripetal forces can lead to a failed state over time: Centripetal forces hold a state together.
  • A strong ethnonationalism splits each state: Most Somalis share a language and religion; clan rivalry divided them.
  • Supranationalism leads to failed states: Somalia's collapse came from inside.

6. Match each example from Canada to the force it shows.

Forces in Canada

  1. Centripetal: transport linking regions
  2. Centripetal: fairer sharing of money
  3. Centrifugal: uneven development
  4. Centrifugal: a rival identity

Pick a piece, then the place it belongs. Drag works too. Pick a filled place to take its piece back.

Show the answer

Centripetal forces pull a state together (links, fair sharing); centrifugal forces pull it apart (uneven development, rival identities).

  • Centripetal: transport linking regions: A railway from coast to coast (1885)
  • Centripetal: fairer sharing of money: Payments to provinces with less money
  • Centrifugal: uneven development: Some provinces far richer than others
  • Centrifugal: a rival identity: A French-speaking majority in Quebec

Part 9 · Summary

Summary

Centrifugal forces pull a state apart; centripetal forces hold it together. When centrifugal forces win, the results can include uneven development, ethnic nationalist movements, stateless nations and even failed states such as Somalia after 1991. When centripetal forces win, the results include a shared identity, fairer sharing of roads, services and money, and cultural cohesion. The same trait can pull in both directions at different scales.
