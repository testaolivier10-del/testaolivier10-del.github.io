Unit 5 · Topic 5.9

The Global System of Agriculture

Global supply chains link farms, processors, shippers and stores across countries, and they are more than transport.

Practice 2: Spatial RelationshipsPractice 3: Data Analysis

Part 1 · Hook

Why this matters

If the price of coffee halves, a café owner in Seattle may barely notice. In a country where coffee brings in most of the money from exports, the same change can cut farm incomes, government budgets and school spending all at once.

Part 5 · Step by step

How it works, step by step

  1. A country earns most of its export income from one crop, such as coffee→its income depends on that crop's world price
  2. The world price of the crop falls sharply→export earnings and farm incomes drop
  3. Export earnings drop→the government has less money for services such as schools and roads

Part 6 · Key ideas

Key ideas

  • A global supply chain links every step and place a product passes through, from farm to consumer across countries. It is more than transport.
  • Commodity dependence means relying on one or a few raw exports. It makes a country vulnerable to price swings; adding value and diversifying reduce it.
  • Food distribution is shaped by politics and conflict, trade rules, roads and storage, and income, so hunger can exist where enough food is grown.

Part 7 · Misconception

A common mistake

The wrong idea: A global supply chain is just the ships and trucks that carry food.

What actually happens: Transport is one step. A supply chain includes growing, packing, storing, processing, ripening, marketing and selling, in different countries.

A global supply chain for bananas

1. According to the diagram, which step takes place in the importing country?

  1. The ripening center, where gas ripens the fruit
  2. The packing house, where fruit is washed and boxed
  3. The port of Guayaquil, where the ship is loaded
  4. The farm, where the bananas are grown and cut
Show the answer

Bananas are picked green in Ecuador, shipped cold and ripened near the stores that sell them, in the importing country.

  • Correct: The ripening center, where gas ripens the fruit: Right: the diagram places the ripening center among the steps in the importing country.
  • The packing house, where fruit is washed and boxed: The packing house is in Ecuador, the exporting country.
  • The port of Guayaquil, where the ship is loaded: Guayaquil is Ecuador's port, in the exporting country.
  • The farm, where the bananas are grown and cut: The farm is in Ecuador.

2. Why does the chain need a refrigerated ship?

  1. Bananas grow larger on the ship when kept cold
  2. Cold ships are cheaper than ordinary ships
  3. Bananas spoil fast, so they are kept cool at sea
  4. Importing countries pay a fee for each warm ship
Show the answer

Perishable goods need a cold chain from farm to store. Refrigerated shipping is what lets a tropical fruit reach stores thousands of kilometers away.

  • Bananas grow larger on the ship when kept cold: Bananas do not grow after they are cut; cold slows their ripening.
  • Cold ships are cheaper than ordinary ships: Refrigeration adds cost; it is used because the fruit would spoil without it.
  • Correct: Bananas spoil fast, so they are kept cool at sea: Right: cold storage slows ripening so the fruit survives weeks of shipping.
  • Importing countries pay a fee for each warm ship: The diagram gives the reason: spoilage, not fees.

3. A student says, "A global supply chain is just the ships and trucks that carry food." Based on the diagram, why is this incomplete?

  1. The diagram shows no ships or trucks on its route
  2. The chain ends at the moment the ship reaches the US port
  3. Transport happens before the farm grows the fruit
  4. It also includes growing, packing, ripening and selling
Show the answer

Exam readers have docked answers that reduce a supply chain to transport. A global supply chain is every step and place a product passes through, from farm to consumer.

  • The diagram shows no ships or trucks on its route: It does show a ship and two ports.
  • The chain ends at the moment the ship reaches the US port: The diagram continues to the ripening center and the store.
  • Transport happens before the farm grows the fruit: Transport comes after the fruit is grown and packed.
  • Correct: It also includes growing, packing, ripening and selling: Right: of seven steps, only some are transport; the others grow, prepare and sell the fruit.

4. A strike closes the US port for two weeks. Based on the diagram, what is the most likely effect?

  1. Farms in Ecuador grow bananas more slowly
  2. Fruit on waiting ships spoils and US stores run short
  3. Ripening centers in the US speed up their work to catch up
  4. Shoppers in Ecuador pay more for bananas
Show the answer

Global supply chains link distant places, so a problem in one place spreads along the chain to others.

  • Farms in Ecuador grow bananas more slowly: A US port strike does not change how bananas grow.
  • Correct: Fruit on waiting ships spoils and US stores run short: Right: a break at one step stops everything after it, and the fruit is perishable.
  • Ripening centers in the US speed up their work to catch up: With no fruit arriving, ripening centers have nothing to ripen.
  • Shoppers in Ecuador pay more for bananas: The fruit was bound for the US; Ecuador may even have extra.

5. Which concept does the table show most clearly?

  1. Subsistence agriculture
  2. Economies of scale
  3. Commodity dependence
  4. Domestication
Show the answer

Commodity dependence means relying on one or a few raw exports for most export earnings. Coffee alone is 60% of Country B's.

  • Subsistence agriculture: Export earnings come from selling abroad; subsistence farming is for the family's own use.
  • Economies of scale: The table shows what a country exports, not costs per unit.
  • Correct: Commodity dependence: Right: one raw farm product brings in most of the country's export earnings.
  • Domestication: Domestication is the change of wild species into crops; the table is about trade.

6. After Russia's full-scale invasion of Ukraine in February 2022, grain shipments from Ukraine's Black Sea ports were disrupted, and world wheat prices rose. What does this show about the global food system?

  1. Food prices depend on local weather alone
  2. Wheat is a subsistence crop that is not traded
  3. Politics and conflict can affect food supply in places far away
  4. Supply chains affect just the countries that grow the crop
Show the answer

Food distribution is shaped by politics, trade and transport routes, not just by how much food is grown. A UN and Türkiye brokered deal later let ships sail from July 2022 to July 2023.

  • Food prices depend on local weather alone: This case shows politics and conflict affecting prices, not weather alone.
  • Wheat is a subsistence crop that is not traded: Ukraine is a major wheat exporter; that is why prices moved.
  • Correct: Politics and conflict can affect food supply in places far away: Right: a war in one region raised prices for buyers in other regions that import grain.
  • Supply chains affect just the countries that grow the crop: Importing countries far from Ukraine felt the price rise.

Part 9 · Summary

Summary

Global supply chains link farms, processors, shippers and stores across countries, and they are more than transport. Countries that depend on one or a few raw farm exports face price swings, and food distribution depends on politics, trade, transport and income as well as on how much is grown.
