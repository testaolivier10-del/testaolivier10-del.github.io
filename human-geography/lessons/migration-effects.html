Unit 2 · Topic 2.12

Effects of Migration

Migration has economic, political, cultural and demographic effects on both the places people leave and the places they go.

Practice 2: Spatial Relationships

Part 1 · Hook

Why this matters

In 2024, migrants sent about 129 billion US dollars home to India, more than to any other country (World Bank, 2024 estimate). In Kerala, India, new houses paid for by workers in the Gulf line village roads. In Riverton, an invented farm town, a meat plant reopens with workers from abroad and church services change language. Migration reshapes both the places people leave and the places they go.

Part 5 · Step by step

How it works, step by step

  1. Young adults leave a place for jobs elsewhere→the origin grows older and the destination’s workforce grows
  2. Migrants earn wages at the destination→they send remittances home, which pay for food, schools and houses
  3. Doctors, nurses and engineers leave→the origin suffers a brain drain of skills it paid to train
  4. New residents bring their foods, languages and religions→the destination’s culture changes, and its people debate immigration policy

Part 6 · Key ideas

Key ideas

  • Remittances are money migrants send home. They raise incomes at the origin but can make families depend on work abroad.
  • Brain drain is the loss of skilled, educated people from a place, such as doctors and nurses leaving Zimbabwe and Nigeria.
  • Effects are economic (jobs, wages, remittances), political (debates and laws over immigration), cultural (food, language, religion) and demographic (age and sex structure).
  • Every effect has a place and a scale: local (a village, a town) and national (a country’s income or laws). Past migrations, such as Irish emigration after 1845, still shape places.

Part 7 · Misconception

A common mistake

The wrong idea: Migration affects only the country people move to.

What actually happens: Migration changes both ends. The origin gains remittances and may lose young and skilled workers; the destination gains workers and new culture and debates immigration policy.

3. Which question can this chart NOT answer?

Text

A news report from an invented town

Invented for practice. Riverton, a farm town in Country B, 2026:

Twenty years ago, Riverton was losing people. Then a meat-packing plant began hiring workers from abroad. The town’s population has grown by a third, the elementary school has added classrooms and two new grocery stores sell food from home countries. Church services are now held in three languages. At last month’s council meeting, some residents asked for more English classes and help for new families; others asked the national government to lower the number of work visas.

4. Which change in Riverton is an economic effect of migration?

  1. The meat-packing plant has the workers it needs
  2. Church services are held in three languages
  3. Residents asked the government to lower the number of work visas
  4. People debate what kind of town Riverton should be
Show the answer

Economic effects involve jobs, wages, businesses and taxes. In Riverton, migrants keep the plant running and support new stores.

  • Correct: The meat-packing plant has the workers it needs: Right: migrants fill jobs that kept the plant running.
  • Church services are held in three languages: that is a cultural effect.
  • Residents asked the government to lower the number of work visas: that is a political effect.
  • People debate what kind of town Riverton should be: that is a social and political effect.

5. Which change in Riverton is a cultural effect of migration?

  1. The population of the town has grown by a third in twenty years
  2. Grocery stores sell food from migrants’ home countries
  3. Workers fill jobs at the meat-packing plant
  4. Some residents asked for fewer work visas
Show the answer

Cultural effects include new foods, languages, religions and festivals, which change the look and life of the destination.

  • The population of the town has grown by a third in twenty years: that is a demographic effect.
  • Correct: Grocery stores sell food from migrants’ home countries: Right: new food and new languages change the town’s culture.
  • Workers fill jobs at the meat-packing plant: that is an economic effect.
  • Some residents asked for fewer work visas: that is a political effect.

6. What is a remittance?

  1. Money that migrants send to family in their home country
  2. A tax that migrants pay when they arrive in a new country
  3. Money a government pays to people who return home
  4. A permit that lets a guest worker stay for a set time
Show the answer

A remittance is money a migrant sends back to family or community in the place of origin. Remittances are one of the largest money flows to many lower-income countries.

  • Correct: Money that migrants send to family in their home country: Right: remittances flow from migrants back to their origin.
  • A tax that migrants pay when they arrive in a new country: remittances are personal transfers, not a tax.
  • Money a government pays to people who return home: that is a return payment, not a remittance.
  • A permit that lets a guest worker stay for a set time: that is a work visa.
