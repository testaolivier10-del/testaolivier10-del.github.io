Unit 3 · Topic 3.6

Contemporary Causes of Diffusion

Globalization links places through trade, travel, media and the internet, so traits now spread farther and faster than ever.

Practice 1: Concepts and ProcessesPractice 2: Spatial Relationships

Part 1 · Hook

Why this matters

By the 1970s, few young Māori in New Zealand could speak te reo Māori fluently; school, radio and television ran in English. Then families opened "language nests," and in 1987 te reo became an official language. The same fast links that spread English everywhere also helped a small language fight back. This topic is about both pulls at once.

Part 5 · Step by step

How it works, step by step

  1. The internet, phones, flights and trade link places more closely (globalization)→traits spread farther and faster than ever (time-space compression)
  2. The same brands, media and a few large languages reach many places→places grow more alike: cultural convergence
  3. Young people use dominant languages for school, work and media→small and Indigenous languages lose speakers and are threatened
  4. Groups use laws, schools and new media to protect their culture→places stay or become more distinct: cultural divergence

Part 6 · Key ideas

Key ideas

  • Globalization is the growing connection of places through trade, travel, media and the internet. It spreads culture through media, technology, politics, economics and social ties.
  • Cultural convergence: places grow more alike, with the same chain stores in Dubai and Toronto and English used for business and much of the internet.
  • Cultural divergence: places grow or stay different, as when France requires French songs on its radio (a 1994 law) or families revive te reo Māori.
  • Culture is made by people, so it changes. Growing cities mix people of many groups and spread popular culture, while video calls let migrants keep their culture from home.

Part 7 · Misconception

A common mistake

The wrong idea: Globalization only makes the world more alike.

What actually happens: It does both. The internet spread English and global brands (convergence), and the same tools gave te reo Māori a television channel in 2004 (divergence).

Young people in Country A: the internet and an Indigenous language

Invented data for practice.

1. Which statement best describes the trends in the chart from 2000 to 2020?

  1. Internet use rose; Language C at home fell
  2. Both daily internet use and Language C at home rose
  3. Both daily internet use and Language C at home fell
  4. Daily internet use fell while Language C at home rose
Show the answer

internet use climbed from 5% to 80%, while the share speaking Language C at home fell from 40% to 20%.

  • Correct: Internet use rose; Language C at home fell: Right: internet use climbed from 5% to 80%, while the share speaking Language C at home fell from 40% to 20%.
  • Both daily internet use and Language C at home rose: Language C fell from 40% to 20% over those years.
  • Both daily internet use and Language C at home fell: Internet use rose steeply, from 5% to 80%.
  • Daily internet use fell while Language C at home rose: This reverses both lines: internet use rose and Language C fell.

2. Which explanation of the 2000 to 2020 trends fits the idea of cultural convergence?

  1. Young people moved abroad, so Language C left the country
  2. Media in larger languages drew youth from Language C
  3. Language C speakers made new words for internet tools
  4. The internet stopped working in places where Language C is spoken
Show the answer

when young people spend more time with media in widely used languages, they use their small home language less, and places grow more alike: convergence.

  • Young people moved abroad, so Language C left the country: The chart shows people living in Country A, not migration. It tracks what they speak at home.
  • Correct: Media in larger languages drew youth from Language C: Right: when young people spend more time with media in widely used languages, they use their small home language less, and places grow more alike: convergence.
  • Language C speakers made new words for internet tools: That would keep the language alive and different, the opposite of the falling line.
  • The internet stopped working in places where Language C is spoken: Daily internet use rose steadily; nothing shows it stopped.

3. A student concludes, "The internet caused Language C to decline in every village of Country A." What is the main limitation of this conclusion?

  1. The chart covers too many years to show any trend
  2. The chart shows people of the wrong age for language
  3. National data do not prove a cause or show each village
  4. Percentages are not suited to comparing two trends
Show the answer

two lines moving together do not prove that one caused the other, and national data hide local differences, so some villages may have kept Language C strong.

  • The chart covers too many years to show any trend: Twenty-five years is enough to show a trend. The problem is the claim about cause and about every village.
  • The chart shows people of the wrong age for language: People aged 15 to 24 do speak languages at home; the age group is not the problem.
  • Correct: National data do not prove a cause or show each village: Right: two lines moving together do not prove that one caused the other, and national data hide local differences, so some villages may have kept Language C strong.
  • Percentages are not suited to comparing two trends: Percentages can be compared. The limit is about cause and scale.

4. From 2020 to 2025, the share speaking Language C at home rose from 20% to 23%, the same years a radio station and a phone app in Language C began. Which idea does this change best fit?

  1. Cultural convergence: the country growing more alike
  2. Relocation diffusion: Language C speakers arriving from abroad
  3. Colonialism: a foreign power ruling Country A
  4. Cultural divergence: a group reviving its own language
Show the answer

using new media to keep a small language alive makes the place more distinct again, which is cultural divergence.

  • Cultural convergence: the country growing more alike: The rise means more young people use their own language, which makes the place more different, not more alike.
  • Relocation diffusion: Language C speakers arriving from abroad: The chart gives no evidence of migrants; the change came with new local media.
  • Colonialism: a foreign power ruling Country A: Nothing in the data or the stem points to foreign rule.
  • Correct: Cultural divergence: a group reviving its own language: Right: using new media to keep a small language alive makes the place more distinct again, which is cultural divergence.

5. A traveler finds the same coffee chain, the same phone stores and the same hit songs in malls in Dubai, United Arab Emirates, and in Toronto, Canada. What does this show?

  1. Cultural convergence: distant places alike
  2. Cultural divergence: places growing more different
  3. Stimulus diffusion: each city inventing its own coffee
  4. Sequent occupance: older buildings reused in each city
Show the answer

shared brands and songs in cities far apart make those places look and sound more alike.

  • Correct: Cultural convergence: distant places alike: Right: shared brands and songs in cities far apart make those places look and sound more alike.
  • Cultural divergence: places growing more different: The traveler finds the same things in both cities, so they are growing more alike.
  • Stimulus diffusion: each city inventing its own coffee: The traveler finds the same chain, not new local versions.
  • Sequent occupance: older buildings reused in each city: Sequent occupance is about layers in one place, not about distant places matching.

6. Under a French law passed in 1994, radio stations in France must play a large share of songs in French. Which idea does this policy best show?

  1. A government spreading English through radio
  2. Relocation diffusion of French songs to Canada
  3. Hierarchical diffusion from small towns to Paris
  4. Politics used to resist cultural convergence
Show the answer

the law protects French-language music against songs in other languages, keeping French culture distinct.

  • A government spreading English through radio: The law favors French, not English.
  • Relocation diffusion of French songs to Canada: The law is about radio in France, not about people moving.
  • Hierarchical diffusion from small towns to Paris: The law covers stations across France; it is not about a ladder of places.
  • Correct: Politics used to resist cultural convergence: Right: the law protects French-language music against songs in other languages, keeping French culture distinct.

Part 9 · Summary

Summary

Globalization links places through trade, travel, media and the internet, so traits now spread farther and faster than ever. Media, technology, politics, economics and social ties all carry culture, and growing cities mix people of many groups. The result is cultural convergence, as places share brands, media and English, and cultural divergence, as groups protect or revive their own culture. Indigenous languages are threatened when young people switch to dominant languages, yet laws, schools and new media can help them recover, as te reo Māori has in New Zealand.
