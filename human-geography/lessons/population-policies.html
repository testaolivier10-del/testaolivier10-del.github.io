Unit 2 · Topic 2.7

Population Policies

Governments use pronatalist policies to raise births, antinatalist policies to lower them and immigration policies to decide who may move in.

Practice 1: Concepts and ProcessesPractice 2: Spatial Relationships

Part 1 · Hook

Why this matters

In 1980, China told most city couples they could have one child. In 2021, it told them they could have three, and in 2025 it began paying families for each child under 3. The same country, about forty years apart, wanted opposite things. Governments around the world try to steer births and migration. Do their policies work?

Part 5 · Step by step

How it works, step by step

  1. Fertility is high and the population is growing fast→a government may adopt an antinatalist policy
  2. The antinatalist policy and other changes lower births→fertility falls, sometimes below replacement level
  3. Fertility stays below replacement→the workforce shrinks and older people become a larger share, so the government may switch to a pronatalist policy
  4. Pronatalist policies work slowly or a little→many governments also use immigration policy to fill jobs now

Part 6 · Key ideas

Key ideas

  • A pronatalist policy tries to raise births (payments, paid leave, child care). An antinatalist policy tries to lower them (family planning, limits).
  • An immigration policy sets who may move into a country and how many, such as Canada’s points system (1967).
  • Policies can be voluntary or coercive, and they have intended and unintended effects: China’s one-child policy (1980 to 2015) lowered births but also raised the sex ratio at birth.
  • Always date a policy. China, Singapore and Iran each moved from antinatalist to pronatalist policies as fertility fell.

Part 7 · Misconception

A common mistake

The wrong idea: China still has a one-child policy.

What actually happens: China’s one-child policy ran from 1980 to 2015. It allowed two children from 2016 and three from 2021, and it has since added support for families. Always give the year.

Text

A speech by the family minister of an invented country

Invented for practice. Family minister of Country E, January 2026:

"Our total fertility rate has fallen to 1.3, and each year more people retire than start work. From July, every family will receive a payment for each child born, parents may share 18 months of paid leave, and child care will cost no more than 5 percent of a family’s income. We will also offer work visas to 60,000 nurses and builders from abroad each year to fill jobs now."

1. Which kind of policy do the payments, paid leave and cheaper child care in the speech make up?

  1. A pronatalist policy
  2. An antinatalist policy
  3. An immigration policy
  4. A policy to slow the rate of natural increase
Show the answer

A pronatalist policy encourages people to have more children, with money, leave or services. Country E has a TFR of 1.3, far below replacement.

  • Correct: A pronatalist policy: Right: they aim to raise the number of births.
  • An antinatalist policy: antinatalist policies try to lower births; these reward having children.
  • An immigration policy: the work visas are the immigration part; payments for births are not.
  • A policy to slow the rate of natural increase: raising births speeds up natural increase; it does not slow it.

2. Which demographic problem is the minister most trying to solve?

  1. A population growing too fast for its schools and housing
  2. Too few young workers to replace the people who retire
  3. A sex ratio with far more men than women in young adult cohorts
  4. Too many people moving abroad to find jobs in other countries
Show the answer

A TFR well below replacement shrinks each new cohort, so fewer people reach working age while large older cohorts retire. The policy tries to raise births and fill jobs now.

  • A population growing too fast for its schools and housing: a TFR of 1.3 means slow growth or decline, not fast growth.
  • Correct: Too few young workers to replace the people who retire: Right: she says more people retire than start work and the TFR is 1.3.
  • A sex ratio with far more men than women in young adult cohorts: the speech does not mention the sex ratio.
  • Too many people moving abroad to find jobs in other countries: the speech is about births and filling jobs, not people leaving.

3. Which statement describes the change shown in the table?

  1. China moved from limiting births toward allowing and supporting more
  2. China moved from encouraging births to limiting them
  3. China kept the same policy for the whole period
  4. China ended its birth limits in 1980
Show the answer

The table shows a strongly antinatalist policy (1980 to 2015) relaxed in 2016 and 2021, with support for child care added: a move toward pronatalist goals.

  • Correct: China moved from limiting births toward allowing and supporting more: Right: the limit rose from one child to two, then three.
  • China moved from encouraging births to limiting them: this reverses the order in the table.
  • China kept the same policy for the whole period: the rule changed in 2016 and again in 2021.
  • China ended its birth limits in 1980: 1980 is when the one-child policy began.

4. Which reason best explains why China relaxed its limits in 2016 and 2021?

  1. China’s population was growing too fast for its food supply
  2. Births fell, and fewer young workers would support more older people
  3. Most families had already been having three children
  4. Other countries required China to change its policy
Show the answer

By the 2010s China’s birth rate was low and its population was getting older, so its leaders tried to raise births. China’s population fell in 2022 for the first time in decades (National Bureau of Statistics of China, 2023).

  • China’s population was growing too fast for its food supply: that was the worry in 1980; by 2016 growth had slowed sharply.
  • Correct: Births fell, and fewer young workers would support more older people: Right: low fertility meant a shrinking workforce and a growing share of older people.
  • Most families had already been having three children: most urban families had one child under the old policy.
  • Other countries required China to change its policy: the change was China’s own decision about its population.

5. Singapore urged families to "Stop at Two" in the 1970s, then in 1987 began urging families who could afford it to have three or more. Which statement compares the two policies?

  1. The first was antinatalist, while the second was pronatalist
  2. Both policies were antinatalist, because both named a number of children
  3. The first was pronatalist, while the second was antinatalist
  4. Both policies were immigration policies
Show the answer

A compare speaks of both. Singapore’s 1970s policy aimed to lower births; after fertility fell far below replacement, the 1987 policy aimed to raise them.

  • Correct: The first was antinatalist, while the second was pronatalist: Right: Singapore first tried to lower births, then to raise them.
  • Both policies were antinatalist, because both named a number of children: naming a number is not the point; one aimed lower, one higher.
  • The first was pronatalist, while the second was antinatalist: this reverses the two.
  • Both policies were immigration policies: neither policy was about who may move in.

6. Canada uses a points system, begun in 1967, that ranks people who apply to move there by education, work skills and language. Which kind of policy is this, and what is one aim?

  1. A pronatalist policy to raise births among Canadians
  2. An antinatalist policy to slow Canada’s population growth
  3. A policy to move people from cities to rural areas of Canada
  4. An immigration policy that brings in skilled workers
Show the answer

An immigration policy sets who may move into a country and how many. Canada’s points system favors skills the economy needs.

  • A pronatalist policy to raise births among Canadians: it is about who moves in, not births.
  • An antinatalist policy to slow Canada’s population growth: it adds people; it does not lower births.
  • A policy to move people from cities to rural areas of Canada: the system is about entry to the country.
  • Correct: An immigration policy that brings in skilled workers: Right: it decides who may move to Canada, favoring skills.

Part 9 · Summary

Summary

Governments use pronatalist policies to raise births, antinatalist policies to lower them and immigration policies to decide who may move in. Policies can be voluntary or coercive and have intended and unintended effects. Many countries, including China (1980, 2016, 2021), Singapore and Iran, switched from lowering births to raising them as fertility fell.
