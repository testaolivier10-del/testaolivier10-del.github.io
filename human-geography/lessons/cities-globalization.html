Unit 6 · Topic 6.3

Cities and Globalization

World cities sit at the top of the urban hierarchy because they host the headquarters, banks, law firms, media and international bodies that steer the world economy.

Practice 2: Spatial RelationshipsPractice 5: Scale Analysis

Part 1 · Hook

Why this matters

A clothing company in Sweden designs a jacket. A bank in London lends the money to make it, a law firm in New York writes the contracts, a factory in Bangladesh sews it, and a shipping firm in Singapore moves it. Every one of those decisions was made in a city, and most were made in a handful of very connected cities. This lesson is about those cities and the ladder of cities below them.

Part 5 · Step by step

How it works, step by step

  1. Large firms want to be near banks, lawyers, investors and each other→their headquarters and top offices cluster in a few big cities
  2. Those cities hold the decisions, money and information that run the world economy→they become world cities at the top of the urban hierarchy
  3. World cities are tied to each other by flights, cables and money→ideas, investment and fashions spread from them down the hierarchy and out across the world
  4. Highly paid jobs and global money pour into world cities→housing costs rise, and the gap between high-wage and low-wage workers widens inside them

Part 6 · Key ideas

Key ideas

  • A world city (also called a global city) is a city whose firms, banks and organizations make decisions that reach across the world. Its power comes from its links, not its population.
  • The urban hierarchy ranks cities by the size of the area they serve and control: a few world cities at the top, then major regional cities, national cities, regional cities and small towns.
  • World cities form a network. In the 2024 GaWC study, London and New York were the two most connected cities, based on the offices of finance, law, consulting, accounting and advertising firms.
  • Global decisions made in world cities have local effects elsewhere: a choice in London can open or close a factory in Viet Nam.

Part 7 · Misconception

A common mistake

The wrong idea: The biggest cities in the world are the most important world cities.

What actually happens: World city status comes from global functions (headquarters, finance, media, links), not from population. Some megacities have huge populations but few global firms, while smaller cities can rank high because they host major banks, insurers or international bodies.

2. City Q has the largest population but few global firms and foreign banks. What does City Q show?

  1. A city's rank in the hierarchy is set by its population
  2. Large cities grow by attracting foreign banks
  3. Small cities like City S are usually world cities
  4. A very large city may have little global reach
Show the answer

City Q has 22 million people, so it is very large, yet it has few of the global functions that make a world city.

  • A city's rank in the hierarchy is set by its population: City Q is the largest but not the highest-ranked, so population does not set rank.
  • Large cities grow by attracting foreign banks: The table does not show how cities grow; City Q is large with few foreign banks.
  • Small cities like City S are usually world cities: City S has almost no global functions.
  • Correct: A very large city may have little global reach: Right: size and global function are different things.

4. Which of these is the best sign that a city is a world city?

  1. More than 20 million people live in it
  2. Many large firms run their worldwide business from it
  3. It is the oldest city in its country
  4. It covers more land than any other city in its region
Show the answer

A world city is defined by its global functions: headquarters, finance, media and links.

  • More than 20 million people live in it: That defines a metacity, which is about population.
  • Correct: Many large firms run their worldwide business from it: Right: global command functions make a world city.
  • It is the oldest city in its country: Age does not give a city global functions.
  • It covers more land than any other city in its region: Area does not give a city global functions.

5. In the 2024 GaWC ranking, London and New York were the two most connected cities. Which best explains their rank?

  1. They host the most offices of global finance, law and advertising firms
  2. They are the two largest cities in the world by population
  3. They are the capitals of the two largest countries by area
  4. They have the most factories of any cities
Show the answer

GaWC measured offices of firms that sell finance, law, accounting, consulting and advertising to other firms. London and New York have the most of these links.

  • Correct: They host the most offices of global finance, law and advertising firms: Right: global business services set the ranking.
  • They are the two largest cities in the world by population: Neither is among the very largest cities by population in 2025 (UN DESA).
  • They are the capitals of the two largest countries by area: New York is not a capital, and neither country is the largest by area.
  • They have the most factories of any cities: The ranking does not count factories.

Part 9 · Summary

Summary

World cities sit at the top of the urban hierarchy because they host the headquarters, banks, law firms, media and international bodies that steer the world economy. They form a network linked by flows of money, information and people, and decisions made in them spread down the hierarchy and out to places at every scale.
