Half of your AP® Human Geography exam score comes from three free-response questions (FRQs). You type the answers, and trained readers score them. The good news: the scoring is predictable. Each part asks for one thing, and if you do that thing you earn the point.

How the free-response section works

In May 2027 the free-response section lasts 75 minutes and has three questions, each worth 7 points:

  • Question 1 has no stimulus: you answer from what you know, with your own real examples.
  • Question 2 has one stimulus: a map, an image or a set of data, such as a table or chart.
  • Question 3 has two stimuli (maps, images or data), often to compare.

Every question has seven parts, A to G. Each part is worth exactly 1 point. Readers use a rubric, a scoring guide with one line for each point, and they check your answer against that line only. Writing more does not earn more; doing the right thing does. No calculator is allowed, and none is needed.

The task verbs

A task verb is the word that tells you what to do. Read it first and underline it.

VerbWhat earns the pointExample answer
IdentifyName it. A word or phrase is enough."Job opportunities."
DefineGive its meaning in a full sentence."A trend is a change in data over time."
DescribeGive its features: what, where, how much."Most farms are along the river in the south; few are in the northern hills."
ExplainSay how or why, with every link: cause, then because or so, then effect."The capital has more paid jobs, so people move there to earn more."
CompareSay how two things differ or match, speaking of both."The north is dry and lightly settled, whereas the south is wet and crowded."
Explain the degreeState the degree first (high, moderate, low), then explain."To a high degree: province data show where clinics are fewest, so..."

Where students lose points

Readers' reports from recent exams name the same slips every year:

  • Naming when the verb says explain or describe. "Jobs" identifies a reason; it does not explain one.
  • A chain with a missing link. Readers want each step: a new highway, so shorter drives to the edge of town, so more people shop there.
  • One side of a comparison. If two places are named, say something about both.
  • No degree. "Explain the degree" needs the degree stated, first.
  • Restating the question. Copying the prompt back earns nothing.
  • No place, no scale. "In some countries" is too vague. Name a real place and, when it helps, the scale of analysis.

A routine for every part

  1. Underline the task verb and circle what it is about.
  2. If there is a stimulus, find the evidence in it: a place, a value, a trend.
  3. Write one or two sentences that do exactly what the verb asks. For explain, use "because" or "so".
  4. Check: did I name a place? Did I answer at the scale asked?

For your exam: answer each part in its own space, labeled A to G, and do not save one answer for another part. Each part is scored on its own.
