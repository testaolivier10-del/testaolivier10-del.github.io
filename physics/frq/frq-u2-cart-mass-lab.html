Finding the mass of a cart

Students want to find the mass M of a lab cart without using a balance. The cart sits on a level, low-friction track. A light string tied to the cart runs over a light, frictionless pulley at the end of the track to a hanger, on which they can place slotted masses. The total hanging mass m is known.

Their results (use g = 9.8 m/s²):

Hanging mass m (kg)Acceleration a (m/s²)
0.0500.58
0.1001.08
0.1501.55
0.2001.97
0.2502.33

  1. (a) Describe a procedure the students could use to collect the acceleration data in the table. Include the equipment used to measure each quantity and a way to reduce experimental uncertainty. [3 points]

  2. (b) For the cart-string-hanger system, Newton's second law gives a = mg/(M + m). Indicate what quantities the students should plot to obtain a straight line, and explain how they can use the line to find M. [2 points]

  3. (c) Calculate the quantities you chose in part (b) and plot them on the grid. Label the axes with the quantities and units, choose a linear scale, and draw a best-fit line. [3 points]

  4. (d) Calculate the mass M of the cart using your best-fit line. [2 points]
