1. Which plot gives a straight line whose slope tells you the wheel’s rotational inertia?

  1. α against τ; the slope is 1/I
  2. α against τ; the slope is I
  3. α against 1/τ; the slope is I
  4. τ against α²; the slope is I
Show the answer

Newton’s second law in rotational form, τ = Iα, gives α = (1/I)τ: a line through the origin with slope 1/I (or plot τ against α for slope I).

  • Correct: α against τ; the slope is 1/I: Right: α = τ/I is a line through the origin with slope 1/I.
  • α against τ; the slope is I: Rearranged, α = (1/I)τ, so the slope is the reciprocal of I.
  • α against 1/τ; the slope is I: α grows with τ, so against 1/τ the points curve.
  • τ against α²; the slope is I: τ is proportional to α, not α².

2. A metal ring is clamped to the wheel, doubling its rotational inertia. With a torque of 1.00 N·m, its angular acceleration would be about

  1. 1.0 rad/s²
  2. 4.0 rad/s²
  3. 2.0 rad/s²
  4. 0.50 rad/s²
Show the answer

α = τ/I. At 1.00 N·m the wheel alone had α ≈ 2.0 rad/s²; doubling I halves it to about 1.0 rad/s².

  • Correct: 1.0 rad/s²: Right: α = τ/I, so doubling I halves α from 2.0 rad/s².
  • 4.0 rad/s²: A larger rotational inertia makes the wheel harder to speed up, not easier.
  • 2.0 rad/s²: The torque is the same but the inertia doubled, so α changes.
  • 0.50 rad/s²: α ∝ 1/I, not 1/I²: doubling I halves α.

3. A second student repeats the experiment with a rusty axle. Her line of α against τ is parallel to the first but crosses the τ-axis at 0.30 N·m. What best explains this?

  1. Friction at the axle exerts a torque of about 0.30 N·m that opposes the applied torque
  2. Rust on the axle gives the wheel a larger rotational inertia than before
  3. Her torque sensor reads each torque 0.30 N·m lower than the real value
  4. Below 0.30 N·m of applied torque, the wheel turns in the opposite direction
Show the answer

The net torque is τ_applied − τ_friction = Iα. The same slope means the same I; the intercept at 0.30 N·m means α stays zero until the applied torque exceeds a friction torque of about 0.30 N·m.

  • Correct: Friction at the axle exerts a torque of about 0.30 N·m that opposes the applied torque: Right: the wheel speeds up only once the applied torque beats the friction torque.
  • Rust on the axle gives the wheel a larger rotational inertia than before: A larger I would change the slope, but the lines are parallel.
  • Her torque sensor reads each torque 0.30 N·m lower than the real value: A sensor reading too low would shift the line the other way.
  • Below 0.30 N·m of applied torque, the wheel turns in the opposite direction: Below 0.30 N·m the wheel does not turn at all.

4. About how many revolutions does the wheel make in the 3.0 s?

  1. 1.4 rev
  2. 9.0 rev
  3. 2.9 rev
  4. 0.72 rev
Show the answer

One revolution is 2π rad, so 9.0 rad ÷ 2π = 1.43, about 1.4 rev.

  • Correct: 1.4 rev: Right: 9.0 rad ÷ 2π rad per revolution = 1.4 rev.
  • 9.0 rev: This treats each radian as a whole turn. One revolution is 2π rad, about 6.28 rad.
  • 2.9 rev: This divides by π instead of 2π. A full turn is 2π rad.
  • 0.72 rev: This halves the correct value. A revolution is 2π rad, not 4π rad.

5. A second chalk mark is drawn halfway between the first mark and the axle. During the same 3.0 s, its angular displacement is

  1. the same as the first mark’s
  2. half the first mark’s
  3. twice the first mark’s
  4. one quarter of the first mark’s
Show the answer

The wheel is a rigid system: every point on it turns through the same angle in the same time, so both marks have the same angular displacement (9.0 rad), the same ω and the same α.

  • Correct: the same as the first mark’s: Right: a rigid wheel turns as one piece, so every mark turns through the same angle.
  • half the first mark’s: Halving the distance from the axle changes how far the mark travels, not the angle it turns through.
  • twice the first mark’s: Being closer to the axle does not make a mark turn through more angle; the wheel turns as one piece.
  • one quarter of the first mark’s: No square law applies here: every mark on a rigid wheel turns through the same angle.

6. The wheel keeps the same angular acceleration but runs for 6.0 s instead of 3.0 s, starting from rest. Its angular displacement is

  1. 4 times as large
  2. 2 times as large
  3. the same
  4. 8 times as large
Show the answer

From rest, θ = ½αt². With α fixed, θ ∝ t², so twice the time gives 2² = 4 times the angle (36 rad).

  • Correct: 4 times as large: Right: from rest θ = ½αt², so doubling t multiplies θ by 2² = 4.
  • 2 times as large: That would be true at constant ω. With constant α from rest, θ grows with t².
  • the same: A longer time at the same α gives more turning, not the same.
  • 8 times as large: θ depends on t², not t³.

7. A Ferris wheel of radius 15 m makes one revolution every 60 s. What is the speed of a rider?

Type a number in m/s.

Show the answer

ω = 2π rad / 60 s = 0.105 rad/s, so v = rω = (15 m)(0.105 rad/s) = 1.57 m/s, about 1.6 m/s.

  • Answer: 1.6 m/s

8. A mechanic pulls on the end of a 0.30 m wrench with an 80 N force at 60° to the handle. What is the size of the torque about the bolt?

Type a number in N·m.

Show the answer

τ = rF sin θ = (0.30 m)(80 N)(sin 60°) = 20.8 N·m, about 21 N·m.

  • Answer: 21 N·m

9. A solid disk has mass 2.0 kg and radius 0.30 m. Its rotational inertia about its center is I = ½MR² (given). What is I?

Type a number in kg·m^2.

Show the answer

I = ½MR² = ½(2.0 kg)(0.30 m)² = 0.090 kg·m².

  • Answer: 0.090 kg·m^2

10. A 25 kg child sits 1.8 m from the pivot of a seesaw. How far from the pivot, on the other side, must a 30 kg child sit to balance it? Ignore the plank’s mass.

Type a number in m.

Show the answer

Balanced torques about the pivot: (25 kg)g(1.8 m) = (30 kg)g r, so r = 45/30 = 1.5 m. g cancels.

  • Answer: 1.5 m

11. A record turntable spins at 45 revolutions per minute. What is its angular velocity in rad/s?

Type a number in rad/s.

Show the answer

ω = (45 rev/min)(2π rad/rev)(1 min/60 s) = 4.71 rad/s, about 4.7 rad/s.

  • Answer: 4.7 rad/s

12. A point on the rim of a grinding wheel of radius 0.40 m has a tangential acceleration of 2.0 m/s². What is the wheel’s angular acceleration?

Type a number in rad/s^2.

Show the answer

α = a_T/r = (2.0 m/s²)/(0.40 m) = 5.0 rad/s².

  • Answer: 5.0 rad/s^2
