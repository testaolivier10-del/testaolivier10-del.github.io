A bowling ball that skids, then rolls

A bowling ball of radius 0.11 m is released on a level lane sliding at 8.0 m/s without spinning. High-speed video gives the speed of its center, vcm, and its angular velocity ω.

1. From the data, when has the ball begun rolling without slipping?

  1. By 1.2 s, when v_cm equals Rω
  2. At 0.0 s, because the ball is moving
  3. At 0.8 s, when ω reaches 35 rad/s
  4. Not yet, because v_cm keeps falling
Show the answer

Rolling without slipping needs v_cm = Rω. Rω = 0, 1.9, 3.9, 5.7, 5.7 m/s; v_cm = 8.0, 7.2, 6.5, 5.7, 5.7 m/s. They match by 1.2 s and stay matched.

  • Correct: By 1.2 s, when v_cm equals Rω: Right: 0.11 m × 52 rad/s = 5.7 m/s = v_cm.
  • At 0.0 s, because the ball is moving: At first ω = 0 while v_cm = 8.0 m/s: it slides without spinning.
  • At 0.8 s, when ω reaches 35 rad/s: Rω = 3.9 m/s, still less than v_cm = 6.5 m/s.
  • Not yet, because v_cm keeps falling: v_cm stops falling at 5.7 m/s, once it equals Rω.

2. While the ball skids, which way does the lane’s friction force on the ball point, and what does it do?

  1. Backward: it slows v_cm and speeds up the spin
  2. Forward: it speeds up v_cm and the spin
  3. Backward: it slows both v_cm and the spin
  4. No friction acts, because the lane is level
Show the answer

The bottom of the ball slides forward on the lane, so kinetic friction on the ball points backward. That reduces v_cm, and because it acts at the bottom, a distance R below the center, its torque increases ω until v_cm = Rω.

  • Correct: Backward: it slows v_cm and speeds up the spin: Right: one force, two effects.
  • Forward: it speeds up v_cm and the spin: v_cm falls in the data, so the friction force points backward.
  • Backward: it slows both v_cm and the spin: ω rises in the data; the backward force at the bottom gives a torque in the direction of rolling.
  • No friction acts, because the lane is level: A skidding ball rubs on the lane, so kinetic friction acts.

3. What happens to the ball’s total kinetic energy during the skid (0 to 1.2 s)?

  1. It decreases: kinetic friction dissipates energy
  2. It stays the same: the spin gains what v_cm loses
  3. It increases, because the ball starts to spin
  4. It decreases to zero, because the ball stops skidding
Show the answer

While the contact point slides, kinetic friction dissipates mechanical energy as thermal energy. Total kinetic energy (translational + rotational) falls during the skid, then stays constant once the ball rolls without slipping.

  • Correct: It decreases: kinetic friction dissipates energy: Right: sliding friction turns some kinetic energy into thermal energy.
  • It stays the same: the spin gains what v_cm loses: The spin gains some energy, but kinetic friction during sliding removes energy overall.
  • It increases, because the ball starts to spin: Nothing outside does positive work on the ball overall.
  • It decreases to zero, because the ball stops skidding: The ball keeps rolling at 5.7 m/s.

4. After 1.2 s the ball rolls without slipping on the level lane. Ignoring air and the small losses in the lane, why do v_cm and ω stay constant?

  1. Its contact point does not slide, so no friction acts
  2. Static friction keeps pushing the ball forward
  3. Kinetic friction balances the ball’s weight
  4. The ball’s spin pushes it along the lane
Show the answer

Rolling without slipping, the bottom point has zero velocity relative to the lane, so nothing slides. On a level lane with no other horizontal force, no friction acts, the net force and net torque are zero, and v_cm and ω stay constant.

  • Correct: Its contact point does not slide, so no friction acts: Right: no sliding, no kinetic friction; on a level lane no static friction is needed either.
  • Static friction keeps pushing the ball forward: A forward force would speed the ball up; the data show constant v_cm.
  • Kinetic friction balances the ball’s weight: Friction is horizontal and the weight vertical; they do not balance.
  • The ball’s spin pushes it along the lane: Spinning does not push the ball; with no net force v_cm stays constant.

5. Which graph of these data gives a straight line through the origin?

  1. K against ω²
  2. K against ω
  3. K against 1/ω
  4. √K against ω²
Show the answer

K = ½Iω², so K against ω² is a straight line through the origin with slope ½I.

  • Correct: K against ω²: Right: K = (½I)ω² is proportional to ω².
  • K against ω: K rises faster than ω; the points curve upward.
  • K against 1/ω: K increases with ω, so it falls along a curve against 1/ω.
  • √K against ω²: √K is proportional to ω, not ω².

6. Predict the flywheel’s kinetic energy at 50 rad/s.

  1. 1000 J
  2. 800 J
  3. 920 J
  4. 2500 J
Show the answer

K = ½Iω² = ½(0.80 kg·m²)(50 rad/s)² = 1000 J. The steps between rows grow (120, 200, 280 J), as a square law predicts.

  • Correct: 1000 J: Right: K = ½(0.80 kg·m²)(50 rad/s)² = 1000 J.
  • 800 J: This scales 640 J by 50/40, as if K were proportional to ω.
  • 920 J: This adds the last step (280 J) again, but each step is larger than the one before.
  • 2500 J: This forgets the ½I and uses ω² alone.

7. An engineer wants a flywheel of the same mass and radius to store more energy at the same ω. She should

  1. move more of its mass to the rim
  2. move more of its mass toward the axle
  3. make it a solid disk instead of a ring
  4. leave the shape, since K depends on mass
Show the answer

At a fixed ω, K = ½Iω² is proportional to I. Putting the same mass farther from the axle raises I, so a rim-heavy flywheel stores more energy.

  • Correct: move more of its mass to the rim: Right: that raises I, and K = ½Iω² rises with it.
  • move more of its mass toward the axle: That lowers I and so lowers the stored energy.
  • make it a solid disk instead of a ring: A solid disk has less of its mass at the rim than a ring of the same mass and radius.
  • leave the shape, since K depends on mass: K depends on I, which depends on where the mass is, not mass alone.

8. A screwdriver applies a constant torque of 1.5 N·m while turning a screw through 4 full revolutions. How much work is done?

Type a number in J.

Show the answer

Δθ = 4 × 2π rad = 25.1 rad, so W = τΔθ = (1.5 N·m)(25.1 rad) = 37.7 J, about 38 J.

  • Answer: 38 J

9. A bicycle wheel with I = 0.25 kg·m² spins at 12 rad/s. What is its angular momentum?

Type a number in kg·m^2/s.

Show the answer

L = Iω = (0.25 kg·m²)(12 rad/s) = 3.0 kg·m²/s.

  • Answer: 3.0 kg·m^2/s

10. A diver leaves the board turning slowly, then tucks into a ball. Ignoring air, why does she turn faster in the tuck?

  1. Her I falls while her angular momentum stays the same
  2. Tucking pushes against the air and creates a torque
  3. Gravity exerts a larger torque when she is tucked
  4. Her kinetic energy is conserved, so she speeds up
Show the answer

In the air, the only outside force is gravity, which acts at her center of mass and exerts no torque about it. Her L stays constant, so tucking (reducing I) increases ω.

  • Correct: Her I falls while her angular momentum stays the same: Right: L = Iω is constant in the air, so smaller I means larger ω.
  • Tucking pushes against the air and creates a torque: Air is ignored, and pushing on air would not be needed.
  • Gravity exerts a larger torque when she is tucked: Gravity acts at her center of mass, so it has no torque about it.
  • Her kinetic energy is conserved, so she speeds up: Her kinetic energy actually increases; L is what is conserved.

11. A satellite in a circular orbit fires a short rocket burst that raises it to a larger circular orbit (after a second burst). Its total mechanical energy

  1. increases, toward zero
  2. decreases, because it moves more slowly
  3. stays the same, because gravity is conservative
  4. becomes positive
Show the answer

E = −GMm/(2r): a larger r makes E less negative, that is, larger. The rocket bursts supply that energy. The satellite ends up slower, with less K but much more U.

  • Correct: increases, toward zero: Right: E = −GMm/(2r) is less negative at larger r.
  • decreases, because it moves more slowly: It does move more slowly, but E = −K becomes less negative.
  • stays the same, because gravity is conservative: The rocket does work on it from outside the satellite-planet system.
  • becomes positive: It is still in a bound orbit, so E is still negative.

12. A small flywheel with rotational inertia 0.050 kg·m² spins at 300 rad/s. How much kinetic energy does it store?

Type a number in J.

Show the answer

K = ½Iω² = ½(0.050 kg·m²)(300 rad/s)² = 2250 J, about 2.3 kJ.

  • Answer: 2.3 × 103 J
