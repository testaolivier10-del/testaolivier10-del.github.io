Unit 5 · Topic 5.5

Rotational Equilibrium

An object is in rotational equilibrium when the net torque on it is zero, so its angular velocity stays constant; it is in translational equilibrium when the net force is zero.

Practice 1: Creating RepresentationsPractice 2: Mathematical RoutinesPractice 3: Scientific Questioning and Argumentation

Part 1 · Hook

Why this matters

A shop sign sticks straight out from a wall on a beam held by a slanted cable. Nothing moves, yet the cable pulls hard and the bolt at the wall pushes in a direction you might not guess. Two rules, zero net force and zero net torque, tell you every force on it.

Part 5 · Step by step

How it works, step by step

  1. The net torque on an object is zero→its angular velocity does not change (rotational first law)
  2. The net force on it is also zero→its center of mass does not accelerate
  3. Both hold and the object starts at rest→it stays at rest: static equilibrium
  4. You take torques about the point where an unknown force acts→that force drops out of the torque equation
  5. One torque in the balance changes→the net torque is no longer zero and the object starts to turn

Part 6 · Key ideas

Key ideas

  • Rotational equilibrium: Στ = 0, so ω stays constant. Translational equilibrium: ΣF = 0. They are independent; static objects need both.
  • In equilibrium you may take torques about any point. Choose the point where an unknown force acts (a hinge, a support) to remove it.
  • A uniform object’s weight acts at its center; it has a torque unless that point is on the axis.
  • A hinge force usually has both a horizontal and a vertical part. Do not assume its direction.
  • Draw a force diagram first, each force at its point of application.

Part 7 · Misconception

A common mistake

The wrong idea: A hinge or pivot always pushes straight up, and its force must be included in the torque sum.

What actually happens: A hinge can push in any direction that keeps the net force zero, often slanted. Its torque about the hinge itself is zero, which is why the hinge is the best axis to choose.

Part 8 · Check yourself

Check yourself

Exam-style questions. Anything you miss goes into your review queue.

Diagram

A balanced meterstick

1. What is the mass m of the block?

Type a number in kg.

Show the answer

Take torques about the pivot (40 cm). The block, 0.30 m left, turns the stick counterclockwise; the stick’s weight acts at its center (50 cm), 0.10 m right, turning it clockwise. m g(0.30 m) = (0.150 kg) g(0.10 m), so m = 0.050 kg. g cancels.

  • Answer: 0.050 kg

2. Use g = 9.8 m/s². What is the size of the force the pivot exerts on the stick?

Type a number in N.

Show the answer

The stick is in translational equilibrium: F_pivot = (0.150 kg + 0.050 kg)(9.8 m/s²) = 1.96 N, about 2.0 N, upward.

  • Answer: 2.0 N

3. Which force diagram of the meterstick is correct?

  1. Pivot force up at 40 cm; the stick’s weight down at 50 cm; the string’s pull down at 10 cm
  2. Pivot force up at 40 cm; the stick’s weight down at 40 cm; the string’s pull down at 10 cm
  3. The stick’s weight down at 50 cm; the string’s pull down at 10 cm; nothing at the pivot
  4. Pivot force up at 40 cm; the stick’s weight down at 0 cm; the string’s pull down at 10 cm
Show the answer

Three forces act on the stick: the pivot’s normal force (up, at 40 cm), Earth’s gravitational force (down, at the center of mass, 50 cm) and the string’s tension (down, at 10 cm).

  • Correct: Pivot force up at 40 cm; the stick’s weight down at 50 cm; the string’s pull down at 10 cm: Right: each force at its point of application.
  • Pivot force up at 40 cm; the stick’s weight down at 40 cm; the string’s pull down at 10 cm: A uniform stick’s weight acts at its center of mass, the 50 cm mark.
  • The stick’s weight down at 50 cm; the string’s pull down at 10 cm; nothing at the pivot: Without an upward force the stick would fall; the pivot pushes up.
  • Pivot force up at 40 cm; the stick’s weight down at 0 cm; the string’s pull down at 10 cm: The weight acts at the center of mass, not at an end.

4. The block is moved from the 10 cm mark to the 20 cm mark and the stick is released. What happens?

  1. The stick tips so its right end goes down
  2. The stick tips so its left end goes down
  3. It stays balanced, because no mass changed
  4. It stays balanced, because the pivot force adjusts
Show the answer

The block’s torque about the pivot falls from mg(0.30 m) to mg(0.20 m); the stick’s own torque, (0.150 kg)g(0.10 m), is unchanged. The net torque is clockwise, so the stick starts to turn with its right end going down.

  • Correct: The stick tips so its right end goes down: Right: the block’s counterclockwise torque shrinks while the stick’s clockwise torque stays the same.
  • The stick tips so its left end goes down: Moving the block toward the pivot reduces its torque, so the left side loses.
  • It stays balanced, because no mass changed: The torque depends on the lever arm, which changed from 0.30 m to 0.20 m.
  • It stays balanced, because the pivot force adjusts: The pivot force acts at the axis and gives no torque, so it cannot restore the balance.

5. A student ignores the stick’s mass and finds m = 0. What is wrong with that reasoning?

  1. The stick’s center of mass is not at the pivot, so its weight exerts a torque
  2. The pivot force exerts a torque that the student left out
  3. The stick’s weight acts at the pivot and adds to the block’s
  4. Torques about a pivot ignore any masses that do not hang from strings
Show the answer

A uniform stick’s weight acts at its center of mass, 50 cm. That point is 0.10 m from the pivot, so the weight exerts a clockwise torque that the block must balance.

  • Correct: The stick’s center of mass is not at the pivot, so its weight exerts a torque: Right: the stick’s weight acts 0.10 m from the pivot.
  • The pivot force exerts a torque that the student left out: The pivot force acts at the axis, so its torque is zero.
  • The stick’s weight acts at the pivot and adds to the block’s: The weight acts at the 50 cm mark, not the pivot.
  • Torques about a pivot ignore any masses that do not hang from strings: Every force with a lever arm gives a torque, strings or not.

6. An object stays at rest without turning. Which conditions hold?

  1. The net force on it is zero and the net torque on it is zero
  2. The net force is zero; that alone keeps it from turning
  3. The net torque is zero; that alone keeps it from moving
  4. The forces on each side of the pivot are equal
Show the answer

Static equilibrium needs both ΣF = 0 (no change in the center of mass’s motion) and Στ = 0 (no change in rotation). Neither condition implies the other.

  • Correct: The net force on it is zero and the net torque on it is zero: Right: translational and rotational equilibrium are separate conditions.
  • The net force is zero; that alone keeps it from turning: Two equal, opposite forces along different lines give zero net force but a net torque.
  • The net torque is zero; that alone keeps it from moving: Zero net torque says nothing about the net force.
  • The forces on each side of the pivot are equal: Torques must balance, and equal forces at different lever arms do not.

7. A driver pushes up on one side of a steering wheel and pulls down equally hard on the other side. The wheel

  1. starts turning while its center stays put
  2. stays still, because the forces cancel
  3. moves up and down without turning
  4. turns and also moves sideways
Show the answer

The two forces are equal and opposite, so the net force is zero and the center stays at rest. Their lines of action are on opposite sides of the axis, so both torques are in the same direction and the net torque is not zero: the wheel starts to turn.

  • Correct: starts turning while its center stays put: Right: the net force is zero, but the two torques add.
  • stays still, because the forces cancel: The forces cancel, but their torques both point the same way.
  • moves up and down without turning: Equal and opposite forces leave the center of mass at rest.
  • turns and also moves sideways: With zero net force the center does not accelerate.

8. A uniform plank rests on two supports, one under each end. A painter walks from the middle of the plank toward the right support. Predict how each quantity changes.

VariableChange
Force from the right support—
Force from the left support—
Sum of the two support forces—
Show the answer

Translational equilibrium fixes the total support force at the total weight; rotational equilibrium decides how it splits, shifting load to the support the painter is nearer.

  • Force from the right support: increases. Taking torques about the left support, the painter’s lever arm grows, so the right support must push harder.
  • Force from the left support: decreases. Taking torques about the right support, the painter’s lever arm shrinks, so the left support pushes less.
  • Sum of the two support forces: no change. Net force zero: the two supports together hold the plank’s and painter’s weight, which do not change.

Part 9 · Summary

Summary

An object is in rotational equilibrium when the net torque on it is zero, so its angular velocity stays constant; it is in translational equilibrium when the net force is zero. A static object needs both. Take torques about the point where an unknown force (such as a hinge force) acts, and remember that a hinge force can point in any direction.
