Motion of Orbiting Satellites

For a satellite of small mass orbiting a massive body, total mechanical energy is conserved (gravity is the only force, and it is conservative) and angular momentum about the central body is conserved (gravity exerts no torque about its center).

Practice 2: Mathematical RoutinesPractice 3: Scientific Questioning and Argumentation

Part 1 · Hook

Why this matters

The International Space Station circles Earth about sixteen times a day, while the Moon takes a month. Comets swing close to the Sun at tremendous speed, then crawl through the far reaches of their orbits for decades. Two conservation laws you already have, energy and angular momentum, explain all of it.

Part 5 · Step by step

How it works, step by step

  1. A small satellite orbits a much more massive body, and only gravity acts→the total mechanical energy E = K + U of the system is constant
  2. Gravity points at the central body’s center→it exerts no torque about that point, so the satellite’s angular momentum L about it is constant
  3. On an elliptical orbit the satellite moves closer in→U becomes more negative and K grows: it speeds up (r v sin θ stays fixed)
  4. In a circular orbit, GMm/r² = mv²/r→K = GMm/(2r), U = −2K and E = −K: a bound orbit has E < 0
  5. The total energy is raised to zero→the object can just escape: v_esc = √(2GM/r)

Part 6 · Key ideas

Key ideas

  • Model: a satellite of much smaller mass than the central body; UG = −GMm/r, zero at infinite separation.
  • Circular orbit: E, U, K and L are all constant. v = √(GM/r), K = GMm/(2r), E = −K.
  • Elliptical orbit: E and L constant; K and U trade. Fastest at the closest point: rAvA = rBvB where v ⊥ r.
  • A bound orbit has E < 0. Escape speed sets E = 0: v = √(2GM/r).
  • Name the reason: no torque about the center (L), only a conservative force inside the system (E).

Part 7 · Misconception

A common mistake

The wrong idea: A satellite in orbit is weightless because there is no gravity in space, and a satellite moving faster must be in a higher orbit.

What actually happens: Gravity is what holds it in orbit; it is in free fall around the planet. And higher circular orbits are slower: v = √(GM/r) falls as r grows, even though the total energy is higher.

A satellite on an elliptical orbit

1. What is the satellite’s speed at B?

Type a number in m/s.

Show the answer

Earth’s pull points toward its center, so it exerts no torque about it and L is constant. At the closest and farthest points the velocity is perpendicular to r, so m r_A v_A = m r_B v_B: v_B = (7.0 × 10⁶)(8.7 × 10³)/(1.4 × 10⁷) = 4.4 × 10³ m/s.

  • Answer: 4.4 × 103 m/s

2. Why is the satellite’s angular momentum about Earth’s center the same at A and at B?

  1. Earth’s pull on it points at Earth’s center: no torque
  2. The satellite moves at a constant speed around it
  3. Gravity does no work on a satellite
  4. The satellite’s distance from Earth is constant
Show the answer

The only force (Earth’s gravitational force) points along the line to Earth’s center, so its lever arm about that point is zero. With no torque, L about Earth’s center is constant, even though r and v both change.

  • Correct: Earth’s pull on it points at Earth’s center: no torque: Right: a force along r has zero lever arm about the center.
  • The satellite moves at a constant speed around it: Its speed changes: fast at A, slow at B.
  • Gravity does no work on a satellite: On an elliptical orbit gravity does work, changing the speed.
  • The satellite’s distance from Earth is constant: The distance changes from 7.0 × 10⁶ m to 1.4 × 10⁷ m.

3. Which quantities for the satellite-Earth system are the same at A and at B?

  1. Total mechanical energy and angular momentum
  2. Kinetic energy and gravitational potential energy
  3. Speed and distance from Earth
  4. Kinetic energy and angular momentum
Show the answer

Gravity is the only force, and it is conservative, so E = K + U is constant. It exerts no torque about Earth’s center, so L is constant. K and U change, trading with each other.

  • Correct: Total mechanical energy and angular momentum: Right: E and L are constant; K and U trade.
  • Kinetic energy and gravitational potential energy: K is larger at A and U is more negative there; they trade.
  • Speed and distance from Earth: Both change around an elliptical orbit.
  • Kinetic energy and angular momentum: L is constant, but K is larger at A.

4. Taking U = 0 at infinite separation, how does the gravitational potential energy at A compare with that at B?

  1. It is twice as negative at A
  2. It is half as negative at A
  3. It is four times as negative at A
  4. It is the same at A and B
Show the answer

U_G = −GMm/r. At A, r is half as large, so U is twice as large in size and more negative: the satellite is deeper in Earth’s gravitational well.

  • Correct: It is twice as negative at A: Right: U = −GMm/r, and r_A is half of r_B.
  • It is half as negative at A: U is more negative closer in, not less.
  • It is four times as negative at A: U goes as 1/r, not 1/r².
  • It is the same at A and B: U depends on the distance from Earth’s center, which changes.

5. With U_G = 0 at infinite separation, a satellite in a bound orbit has total mechanical energy that is

  1. negative
  2. positive
  3. zero
  4. equal to its kinetic energy
Show the answer

U_G = −GMm/r is negative and larger in size than K for a bound orbit, so E = K + U < 0. To escape, E must be raised to zero or above.

  • Correct: negative: Right: it does not have enough energy to reach infinity.
  • positive: Positive total energy means the object escapes.
  • zero: Zero total energy is exactly the escape condition.
  • equal to its kinetic energy: U is negative and adds to K.

6. A satellite moves from a circular orbit of radius r to one of radius 2r around the same planet. Its kinetic energy becomes

  1. half as large
  2. twice as large
  3. one quarter as large
  4. the same
Show the answer

For a circular orbit K = ½mv² = GMm/(2r), so doubling r halves K (and v falls by √2).

  • Correct: half as large: Right: K = GMm/(2r) ∝ 1/r.
  • twice as large: A larger orbit is slower: v = √(GM/r).
  • one quarter as large: K goes as 1/r, not 1/r².
  • the same: The orbital speed depends on r.

Show the answer

E stays constant because only gravity, a conservative force inside the system, acts. Falling inward, U = −GMm/r becomes more negative, so K and the speed increase.

  • The comet’s speed: increases. U becomes more negative as it falls inward, so K grows with E fixed.

Part 9 · Summary

Summary

For a satellite of small mass orbiting a massive body, total mechanical energy is conserved (gravity is the only force, and it is conservative) and angular momentum about the central body is conserved (gravity exerts no torque about its center). In a circular orbit K = GMm/(2r) and E = −K; on an ellipse, K and U trade and the satellite is fastest at its closest point. Escape needs E = 0, v = √(2GM/r).
