Unit 6 · Topic 6.2

Torque and Work

A torque that turns an object through an angle does work W = τΔθ (angle in radians), or the area under a torque-angle graph when the torque varies.

Practice 1: Creating RepresentationsPractice 2: Mathematical Routines

Part 1 · Hook

Why this matters

Wind up a bucket from a well and your arm makes big slow circles while the bucket creeps up. Turning a crank is doing work, just as lifting the bucket directly would be. In rotation, work is torque times angle, and it lands exactly where energy bookkeeping says it should.

Part 5 · Step by step

How it works, step by step

  1. A force at the rim pushes the wheel through a distance s along the rim→it does work Fs on the wheel
  2. s = rθ and the torque is rF→the work is Fs = (rF)θ = τΔθ
  3. The torque changes with angle→the work is the area under the torque-angle graph
  4. The net torque does positive work on a wheel→its rotational kinetic energy increases by that amount
  5. A torque opposes the turning→it does negative work and takes kinetic energy out

Part 6 · Key ideas

Key ideas

  • W = τΔθ for a constant torque, with Δθ in radians: the partner of W = Fd.
  • For a changing torque, W is the area under the τ-θ graph.
  • Positive when the torque turns the object the way it is going; negative when it opposes.
  • The net work done on a turning object equals its change in rotational kinetic energy.
  • Convert revolutions to radians first: 1 rev = 2π rad.

Part 7 · Misconception

A common mistake

The wrong idea: Work done by a torque is τ times the number of revolutions (or degrees).

What actually happens: W = τΔθ only works with the angle in radians, because it comes from W = Fs with s = rθ. Four revolutions is 8π rad, about 25 rad.

Diagram

Winding up a bucket

1. What torque does the rope exert on the drum while the bucket rises at constant speed?

Type a number in N·m.

Show the answer

At constant speed the rope’s tension equals the bucket’s weight, (6.00 kg)(9.8 m/s²) = 58.8 N. The rope pulls tangent to the drum, so τ = rF = (0.0800 m)(58.8 N) = 4.70 N·m.

  • Answer: 4.70 N·m

2. How much work does the crank do on the drum while it turns through 25.0 rad? Give the value and its unit.

Type a number and its unit.

Show the answer

W = τΔθ = (4.704 N·m)(25.0 rad) = 117.6 J, about 118 J. (No friction and constant speed, so the crank’s torque equals the rope’s.)

  • Answer: 118 J

3. How far does the bucket rise?

Type a number in m.

Show the answer

The rope winds onto the drum without slipping: s = rθ = (0.0800 m)(25.0 rad) = 2.00 m.

  • Answer: 2.00 m

4. How does the work done by the torque on the drum compare with the increase in the bucket’s gravitational potential energy?

  1. They are equal: nothing speeds up and there is no friction
  2. The work is larger, because some goes into spinning the drum
  3. The work is smaller, because the drum’s radius is small
  4. The work is a rotational quantity, so it is not an energy
Show the answer

For the drum-rope-bucket system, the crank’s work all goes into gravitational potential energy: τΔθ = (r · mg)(θ) = mg(rθ) = mgΔy = 117.6 J. Nothing speeds up and there is no friction.

  • Correct: They are equal: nothing speeds up and there is no friction: Right: τΔθ = (rmg)θ = mg(rθ) = mgΔy.
  • The work is larger, because some goes into spinning the drum: The drum turns at a steady rate, so its kinetic energy does not change.
  • The work is smaller, because the drum’s radius is small: A small radius means a small torque but a large angle; the product is the same.
  • The work is a rotational quantity, so it is not an energy: Both are energies in joules; τΔθ = mgΔy here.

5. How much work does the motor do on the wheel from 0 to 8.0 rad?

Type a number in J.

Show the answer

Constant torque: W = τΔθ = (6.0 N·m)(8.0 rad) = 48 J, the rectangle under the graph.

  • Answer: 48 J

6. How much work does the motor do on the wheel from 0 to 14.0 rad?

Type a number in J.

Show the answer

Area under the graph: (6.0 N·m)(8.0 rad) + ½(6.0 N·m)(6.0 rad) = 48 + 18 = 66 J.

  • Answer: 66 J

7. The wheel (I = 0.50 kg·m²) starts from rest on a frictionless axle. How fast is it turning at 14.0 rad?

Type a number in rad/s.

Show the answer

The net work equals the change in rotational kinetic energy: 66 J = ½(0.50 kg·m²)ω², so ω = √(2 × 66/0.50) = √264 = 16.2 rad/s, about 16 rad/s.

  • Answer: 16 rad/s

Part 9 · Summary

Summary

A torque that turns an object through an angle does work W = τΔθ (angle in radians), or the area under a torque-angle graph when the torque varies. The net work done on a turning object changes its rotational kinetic energy; a torque that opposes the turning does negative work.
