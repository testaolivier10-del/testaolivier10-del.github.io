Unit 6 · Topic 6.4

Conservation of Angular Momentum

A system’s total angular momentum stays constant when the net external torque on it is zero.

Practice 2: Mathematical RoutinesPractice 3: Scientific Questioning and Argumentation

Part 1 · Hook

Why this matters

A spinning skater pulls in her arms and whirls into a blur. A diver tucks and flips. A collapsing star becomes a pulsar spinning hundreds of times a second. None of them gets a push from outside. They just rearrange their own mass, and angular momentum, which cannot change without an outside torque, does the rest.

Part 5 · Step by step

How it works, step by step

  1. The net external torque on a system is zero→no angular impulse comes from outside, so the total L stays the same
  2. Parts of the system push on each other→they trade angular momentum, but the total is unchanged
  3. The system pulls mass in toward the axis→I falls, so ω = L/I rises
  4. I falls with L fixed→K = L²/(2I) rises; internal work (muscles) supplies it
  5. An object sticks to a pivoted body→L about the pivot is conserved (the pivot force has no torque about it) even though linear momentum is not

Part 6 · Key ideas

Key ideas

  • L is constant when the net external torque is zero. State the system and this condition every time.
  • Choose the system so that the forces that change each part are internal.
  • Shape change at constant L: I₁ω₁ = I₂ω₂. Pulling mass in speeds up the spin.
  • Kinetic energy is not conserved in these changes: pulling in raises K; sticking collisions lower it.
  • A pivot pushes during a collision, so linear momentum changes, but its torque about the pivot is zero, so L about the pivot is conserved.

Part 7 · Misconception

A common mistake

The wrong idea: When clay hits and sticks to a rod hanging from a pivot, linear momentum is conserved, so (m + M)v_after = mv.

What actually happens: The pivot pushes on the rod during the collision, an outside force, so linear momentum is not conserved. That force acts at the pivot and has no torque about it, so angular momentum about the pivot is conserved: mvd = I_total ω.

Part 8 · Check yourself

Check yourself

Exam-style questions. Anything you miss goes into your review queue.

Diagram

Pulling weights in on a rotating stool

1. What is the student’s angular velocity after pulling the weights in?

Type a number in rad/s.

Show the answer

The net torque about the axle from outside the student-stool system is negligible, so L is constant: (3.0 kg·m²)(2.0 rad/s) = (1.2 kg·m²)ω, ω = 5.0 rad/s.

  • Answer: 5.0 rad/s

2. Why is the angular momentum of the student, weights and stool the same before and after?

  1. No net torque about the axle comes from outside the system
  2. Angular momentum is conserved whatever acts on a system
  3. The student pulls the weights in slowly
  4. The student’s arms exert no force on the weights
Show the answer

For the student-weights-stool system, the forces from the arms are internal. The external forces (gravity, the floor through the axle) have no lever arm about the vertical axle, and friction is negligible, so the net torque from outside is about zero and L stays constant.

  • Correct: No net torque about the axle comes from outside the system: Right: the axle is nearly frictionless, and gravity and the floor act along the axis.
  • Angular momentum is conserved whatever acts on a system: It is constant just when the net torque from outside the system is zero.
  • The student pulls the weights in slowly: Speed of pulling does not matter; the condition is zero net torque from outside.
  • The student’s arms exert no force on the weights: The arms do exert forces, but they are internal to the system.

3. By how much does the rotational kinetic energy of the system change?

Type a number in J.

Show the answer

Before: ½(3.0)(2.0)² = 6.0 J. After: ½(1.2)(5.0)² = 15 J. The change is +9 J.

  • Answer: 9.0 J

4. Where does the extra rotational kinetic energy come from?

  1. Work done by her muscles pulling the weights in
  2. The floor, through the stool’s axle
  3. Nowhere: kinetic energy is conserved in a closed system
  4. The angular momentum, which turns into energy
Show the answer

L stays the same, but K = L²/(2I) rises as I falls. The student’s arms pull the weights inward through a distance, doing positive work from stored chemical energy.

  • Correct: Work done by her muscles pulling the weights in: Right: the weights move inward against their tendency to keep moving in a straight line; the arms do positive work.
  • The floor, through the stool’s axle: The axle exerts no torque about the axis and does no work on the turning.
  • Nowhere: kinetic energy is conserved in a closed system: Mechanical energy is not conserved here; the student converts internal (chemical) energy.
  • The angular momentum, which turns into energy: Angular momentum stays the same; it is not converted into anything.

Diagram

Clay hits a hanging rod

5. What is the clay’s angular momentum about the pivot just before it hits?

Type a number in kg·m^2/s.

Show the answer

The clay moves horizontally on a line 0.80 m below the pivot: L = mvd = (0.050 kg)(8.0 m/s)(0.80 m) = 0.32 kg·m²/s.

  • Answer: 0.32 kg·m^2/s

6. What is the angular velocity of the rod and clay just after the collision?

Type a number in rad/s.

Show the answer

About the pivot, L is conserved: 0.32 kg·m²/s = (⅓ML² + mL²)ω = (0.256 + 0.032)ω, so ω = 0.32/0.288 = 1.11 rad/s, about 1.1 rad/s.

  • Answer: 1.1 rad/s

7. For the clay-rod system during the collision, which quantity stays the same?

  1. Angular momentum about the pivot
  2. Linear momentum of the clay and rod
  3. Kinetic energy of the clay and rod
  4. Each of momentum, angular momentum and energy
Show the answer

The only outside forces during the brief collision are gravity (no lever arm while the rod hangs straight down, and negligible over so short a time) and the pivot force, which acts at the axis. So the net torque about the pivot is zero and L about the pivot is constant. The pivot force is not zero, so linear momentum is not constant.

  • Correct: Angular momentum about the pivot: Right: the pivot’s force acts at the axis, so it exerts no torque about it.
  • Linear momentum of the clay and rod: The pivot pushes on the rod during the collision, an outside force, so linear momentum changes.
  • Kinetic energy of the clay and rod: The clay sticks; the collision is perfectly inelastic and kinetic energy is lost.
  • Each of momentum, angular momentum and energy: Angular momentum about the pivot is the one quantity whose condition is met.

8. What fraction of the clay’s kinetic energy is left just after the collision?

  1. About 11%
  2. Nearly 100%
  3. About 4%
  4. About 50%
Show the answer

Before: ½(0.050)(8.0)² = 1.6 J. After: ½(0.288)(1.111)² = 0.18 J, about 11%. The rest became thermal energy and sound when the clay deformed.

  • Correct: About 11%: Right: 0.18 J of 1.6 J.
  • Nearly 100%: A sticky collision is not elastic; kinetic energy is lost.
  • About 4%: This is the ratio of the clay’s mass to the total mass, which is not the energy ratio here.
  • About 50%: Half is not a rule for sticky collisions; compute both energies.

Part 9 · Summary

Summary

A system’s total angular momentum stays constant when the net external torque on it is zero. When the system changes shape, its rotational inertia changes and its angular velocity changes the opposite way (I₁ω₁ = I₂ω₂), while kinetic energy does not stay constant. Around a pivot, angular momentum about the pivot is conserved in a collision even though linear momentum is not.
