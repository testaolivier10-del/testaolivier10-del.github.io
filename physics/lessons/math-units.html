Units, Prefixes and Significant Figures

Work in SI units (m, kg, s).

Practice 2: Mathematical Routines

Part 1 · Hook

Why this matters

A speed sign says 100, but 100 what? In kilometers per hour that is highway speed; in meters per second it is faster than a race car. Every number in physics is a number and a unit, and most lost points on unit questions come from a conversion done in the wrong direction.

Part 3 · Step by step

How it works, step by step

  1. A number is reported without a unit→nobody can tell how big the quantity is, so a value is always a number times a unit
  2. Every quantity is converted to SI base units (m, kg, s) before you calculate→the units of the answer come out in SI too, with no hidden factors of 100 or 1000
  3. You multiply by a fraction equal to 1, such as (1 m / 100 cm)→the value does not change, but the unwanted unit cancels like a number
  4. A measured value is known only to a few digits→a calculated result keeps the precision of the least precise data, rounded once at the end

Part 4 · Key ideas

Key ideas

  • Physics 1 works in SI units: the meter (m), kilogram (kg) and second (s), and units built from them such as m/s.
  • A prefix is a power of ten: kilo = 10³, centi = 10⁻², milli = 10⁻³, micro = 10⁻⁶. So 1 km = 1000 m, 1 cm = 0.01 m, 1 g = 0.001 kg.
  • To convert, multiply by a fraction equal to 1 with the unit you want on top and the unit you are removing on the bottom. Check that the units cancel.
  • Areas need the factor twice: 1 m² = 100 cm × 100 cm = 10 000 cm².
  • Significant figures: multiplying or dividing, keep the fewest significant figures of the data; adding or subtracting, the fewest decimal places. Round once, at the end.

Part 5 · Misconception

A common mistake

The wrong idea: To change 72 km/h into m/s, multiply by 3.6, because there are 3600 seconds in an hour and 1000 meters in a kilometer.

What actually happens: Write the factors and let the units cancel: 72 km/h × (1000 m / 1 km) × (1 h / 3600 s) = 20 m/s. The number gets smaller, because a meter per second is a faster speed than a kilometer per hour.

Part 6 · Check yourself

Check yourself

Exam-style questions. Anything you miss goes into your review queue.

1. A toy car rolls 45 cm across a table. How far is that in meters?

Type a number in m.

Show the answer

1 m = 100 cm, so 45 cm × (1 m / 100 cm) = 0.45 m.

  • Answer: 0.45 m

2. A cyclist rides at 27 km/h. What is her speed in m/s?

Type a number in m/s.

Show the answer

27 km/h × (1000 m / 1 km) × (1 h / 3600 s) = 27 000 m / 3600 s = 7.5 m/s.

  • Answer: 7.5 m/s

3. A student records a length as 0.00620 m. How many significant figures does it have?

  1. 6
  2. 5
  3. 3
  4. 2
Show the answer

Leading zeros never count; the trailing zero after the decimal point does. 0.00620 = 6.20 × 10⁻³, three significant figures.

  • 6: This counts every digit, but the zeros in front of the 6 only show where the decimal point is.
  • 5: This counts the zeros after the decimal point, which only place the 6.
  • Correct: 3: Right: the leading zeros only place the decimal point; 6, 2 and the final 0 count.
  • 2: The final zero comes after a nonzero digit and after the decimal point, so it was measured and counts.

4. A sprinter runs 100.0 m in 12.47 s. A calculator shows 100.0 ÷ 12.47 = 8.019246191. How should the speed be reported?

  1. 8.019 m/s
  2. 8.0 m/s
  3. 8.019246191 m/s
  4. 8 m/s
Show the answer

Multiplying or dividing: keep as many significant figures as the least precise value. 100.0 m and 12.47 s each have four, so 8.019 m/s.

  • Correct: 8.019 m/s: Right: both measurements have four significant figures, so the result keeps four.
  • 8.0 m/s: Two figures throws away precision the measurements had; both data values have four.
  • 8.019246191 m/s: A calculator does not know how precise the data are; extra digits claim precision nobody measured.
  • 8 m/s: One figure throws away most of the precision of four-figure data.

5. A tabletop measures 1.2 m by 0.80 m. What is its area in square centimeters (cm²)?

Type a number in cm^2.

Show the answer

Area = 1.2 m × 0.80 m = 0.96 m². Each meter is 100 cm, and a square meter is 100 cm × 100 cm = 10 000 cm², so 0.96 m² = 9600 cm² (two significant figures).

  • Answer: 9.6 × 103 cm^2

6. A quantity is found by dividing a value in m/s by a value in s. What unit does the result have?

  1. m
  2. m/s²
  3. m·s
  4. s/m
Show the answer

Units cancel and combine like numbers: (m/s) ÷ s = m/s².

  • m: That would come from multiplying m/s by s, not dividing.
  • Correct: m/s²: Right: (m/s) ÷ s = m/(s·s) = m/s², read "meters per second squared".
  • m·s: Dividing by seconds puts another s in the denominator, not the numerator.
  • s/m: This flips the whole unit; only one more second goes below the line.

Part 7 · Summary

Summary

Work in SI units (m, kg, s). Convert by multiplying by fractions equal to 1 so the unwanted unit cancels, use each factor twice for an area, and keep as many significant figures as the least precise data, rounding once at the end.
