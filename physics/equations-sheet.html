The equations and constants sheet, explained

What each equation and constant on the exam sheet is for, and when to use it.

Three habits that save points

  • Use the g the question gives. The sheet prints g = 9.8 m/s²; 10 m/s² is accepted. Say which one you use, and keep it the same through the whole answer.
  • SI units before you substitute. Centimeters to meters, grams to kilograms, minutes to seconds. A newton is a kg·m/s² and a joule is a N·m.
  • Degree mode for angles in degrees. sin 30° = 0.5 only in degree mode; rotation formulas such as s = rθ need the angle in radians.

Energy, work and power

EquationWhat it isWhen you use itLearn it in
K = ½mv²Translational kinetic energy.Speed from energy, and energy from speed. It depends on v², so twice the speed gives four times the kinetic energy; ΔK is not ½m(Δv)².Unit 3
Translational Kinetic Energy
W = F_∥ d = F d cos θThe work done by a constant force: the part of the force along the displacement, times the distance.How much energy a force transfers. Positive when the force has a part along the motion, negative against it, zero when it is perpendicular (a normal force on a level floor, the string on a ball in a circle). For a changing force, use the area under the force-position graph.Unit 3
Work
ΔK = ΣW_i = ΣF_∥,i d_iThe work-energy theorem: the net work on an object, the sum of the work each force does, equals its change in kinetic energy.Speeds after a push, stopping distances with friction, and anything with a force-position graph.Unit 3
Work
U_s = ½k(Δx)²The elastic potential energy stored in an ideal spring.Spring launchers and bumpers, and oscillators. It depends on (Δx)², so twice the compression stores four times the energy.Unit 3
Potential Energy
U_G = −G m₁m₂ / rThe gravitational potential energy of two masses, zero when they are infinitely far apart.Satellites and orbits far from a surface, and escape. It is negative and rises toward zero as the masses separate.Unit 3
Potential Energy
ΔU_g = m g ΔyThe change in gravitational potential energy near Earth's surface.Anything that rises or falls a height Δy near the ground: ramps, hills, pendulums. Only changes matter, so choose the zero height that makes the problem simplest.Unit 3
Potential Energy
P_avg = W / Δt = ΔE / ΔtAverage power: how fast energy is transferred.Motors, people climbing stairs, lifting loads. The same work done in half the time takes twice the power.Unit 3
Power
P_inst = F_∥ v = F v cos θInstantaneous power: the part of a force along the velocity, times the speed.The power a force delivers at one moment, such as a car's engine at a given speed. At constant power, the driving force falls as the speed rises.Unit 3

What is not on the sheet

The exam gives you these values in the question, or expects you to reason without them. None of them is printed on the sheet.

  • Rotational inertia of a disk, rod, hoop or sphere. The question gives it when it is needed. Know that I = Σmr² for point masses, and that mass farther from the axis means a larger I. Rotational Inertia
  • Escape speed. Derive it: set the total energy ½mv² − GMm/r to zero and solve for v. Motion of Orbiting Satellites
  • Torricelli's result. Derive it from Bernoulli's equation: water leaving a hole a depth h below the surface moves at √(2gh). Fluids and Conservation Laws
  • Kepler's third law. Derive it: set the gravitational force equal to m·v²/r for a circular orbit, with v = 2πr/T. Circular Motion
  • The minimum speed at the top of a loop. Derive it: at the slowest speed, gravity alone supplies the centripetal force, so mg = mv²/r and v = √(gr). Circular Motion
  • The greatest speed of an oscillator. Derive it from energy: all of ½kA² becomes ½mv² at equilibrium, so v_max = A√(k/m) = 2πfA. Energy of Simple Harmonic Oscillators

On exam day you get this sheet of equations and constants, on paper and in the testing app. The sheet itself is published by the College Board; this page is our own guide to using it.

The sheet also lists the conventions every question assumes unless it says otherwise (inertial frames, no air resistance, ideal springs, strings and fluids), unit symbols, the metric prefixes from tera to pico, and sine, cosine and tangent at 0°, 30°, 37°, 45°, 53°, 60° and 90°.
