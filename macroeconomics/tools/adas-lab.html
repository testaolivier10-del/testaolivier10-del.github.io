AD-AS lab

Shock the economy, then watch wages and SRAS bring it back.

About this tool

Shock the economy and watch the AD-AS graph answer. Pick a cause (more consumer confidence, an oil price jump, a tax cut) and the curve it moves shifts, the price level and real GDP move to a new short-run equilibrium, and the gap against full employment (LRAS) appears. Then run the long run: with no policy, nominal wages adjust, SRAS shifts, and the economy returns to full employment. Once Unit 5 is open, the short-run and long-run Phillips curves sit beside the graph and move with it.

What the model is

A teaching model, drawn the way the exam scores graphs: straight AD and SRAS curves, LRAS vertical at full-employment output (YF), every shift by the same step, so only directions matter, never sizes.

  • Short run. A change in spending (consumption, investment, government purchases, net exports) shifts AD; a change in production costs (input prices, nominal wages, supply shocks, expected inflation) shifts SRAS. The new equilibrium is where AD meets SRAS.
  • Long run, no policy. In a recessionary gap unemployment is above the natural rate, so nominal wages and input prices fall and SRAS shifts right; in an inflationary gap they rise and SRAS shifts left. AD does not move. The adjustment ends where AD, SRAS and LRAS meet.
  • Phillips curves. Each AD-AS point maps to one Phillips point: real GDP above YF means unemployment below the natural rate, a higher price level means higher inflation. An AD shift moves along the short-run Phillips curve (SRPC); an SRAS shift shifts SRPC the opposite way; LRPC is vertical at the natural rate.

Test yourself gives short-run shocks, gaps with numbers, the long-run adjustment and the Phillips link, each step checked on its own.
