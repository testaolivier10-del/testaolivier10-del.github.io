Forex lab

Read the axis right, shift the right curve, follow the money.

About this tool

Two currency markets side by side: the market for one currency and its mirror, the market for the other. Pick what changes (tastes, incomes, price levels, real interest rates or a policy) and the right curve shifts in each market, one currency appreciates while the other depreciates, and the effects on net exports and financial capital flows follow.

What the model is

In the market for a currency, the vertical axis is its price in units of the other currency per unit of it: dollars per euro in the market for euros. Writing the ratio upside down is the most common way to lose the graph point.

  • Demand for euros comes from people outside the euro area buying its goods, services and assets: they need euros to pay.
  • Supply of euros comes from euro holders buying foreign goods, services and assets: they sell euros for the other currency.
  • The mirror market. Buying euros with dollars is selling dollars, so more demand for euros is more supply of dollars. When one currency appreciates, the other depreciates; the price of one is 1 divided by the price of the other.
  • Net exports. An appreciation makes a country’s goods pricier abroad and imports cheaper, so its net exports fall and AD shifts left; a depreciation does the reverse.
  • Capital flows. A higher real interest rate draws foreign financial capital in, raising the demand for the currency.

The rates shown on the axis are illustrative. Test yourself drills the axis ratio, curve shifts with the mirror market, net exports and capital flows.
