Graph studio

Shift the curve, mark the new equilibrium, see what the reader sees.

About this tool

The graph point is the one lost most often on the AP® Macroeconomics exam. Graph Studio has the eight graph kinds the course draws (nine graphs, since the short- and long-run Phillips curves share one): pick a graph, pick what happens, and shift the curve yourself. Test yourself grades every graph point by point, the way readers score it: the labels, the right curve shifted the right way, the new equilibrium, and what it means.

How it works

Explore. Choose a graph, then choose what happens. Drag the curve you think moves (or use its button and the arrow keys); the new equilibrium E2 follows where the curves cross. Show me draws the shift for you, and Why walks through the cause, the curve, the direction and the result. Details lists what shifts each curve.

Test yourself. Each problem is one graph, built one rubric point at a time: label the axes and curves, shift the right curve, move E2, then read the result. A slip is named the way a reader would see it, such as shifting aggregate demand when the change shifts short-run aggregate supply. Your first try at each step is what counts toward mastery.

Magnitude is never graded: only which curve moves, which way, and where E2 sits against E1.

The PPC and supply-and-demand drills are free. The other graphs' drills are part of Premium; Explore is open for every graph.
