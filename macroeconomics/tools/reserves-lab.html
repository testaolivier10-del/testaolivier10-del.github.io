Reserves lab

Move the administered rate and see what the policy rate does.

About this tool

The Federal Reserve sets interest rates in one of two ways, depending on how many reserves banks hold. With ample reserves (how the Fed works now) it sets administered rates: the interest rate on reserve balances (IORB) and the overnight reverse repurchase (ON RRP) rate. With limited reserves it changes the quantity of reserves: open market operations, the discount rate and the reserve requirement. Try every tool in each framework and watch the reserve market, the money market and AD-AS move together.

How this lab works

Pick a framework, a tool and an action. The reserve market shows the policy rate where reserve supply meets reserve demand. With ample reserves, supply sits where demand is flat at the administered rate, so only a change in that rate moves the policy rate. With limited reserves, supply meets demand on its steep part, so a change in reserves moves the rate.

The money market beside it shows the nominal interest rate that follows the policy rate, and AD-AS shows what that does to real output and the price level. The old curves stay drawn, faded and dashed. The graphs show directions, not sizes.
