Start at question 1

Graph

A shift of Country W's production possibilities curve

1. The graph shows Country W's production possibilities curve shifting out over a decade. Which event could cause this shift?

  1. A rise in the inflation rate
  2. More workers and better technology
  3. A recession that leaves factories idle
  4. A boom in which firms pay overtime
Show the answer

The PPC shows what the economy can produce with full use of its resources, so it shifts out when resources or technology grow: potential output rises.

  • A rise in the inflation rate: Faster price increases do not add workers, capital or technology.
  • Correct: More workers and better technology: Right: more resources or better technology let the economy produce more of both goods, so the PPC and potential output grow.
  • A recession that leaves factories idle: A recession moves production to a point inside the PPC; the curve itself does not move.
  • A boom in which firms pay overtime: A boom pushes output above potential for a while; it does not add resources.

2. What does the shift in the graph show about Country W's potential output?

  1. It stayed the same, because just the curve moved
  2. It can't be told from a PPC
  3. It increased
  4. It decreased
Show the answer

Potential output corresponds to the PPC: producing on the curve is full employment, and an outward shift is growth in potential output.

  • It stayed the same, because just the curve moved: The curve is what the economy can produce at full employment; when it moves out, potential output rises.
  • It can't be told from a PPC: Points on the PPC are full-employment output, so its shift shows the change in potential.
  • Correct: It increased: Right: an outward PPC shift means the economy can produce more at full employment.
  • It decreased: A decrease would be an inward shift of the curve.

3. Before the shift, Country W was in a recession with high cyclical unemployment. Where was it producing compared with the original PPC?

  1. At a point on the original curve
  2. At a point outside the original curve
  3. On the new curve, because of the shift
  4. At a point inside the original curve
Show the answer

A recession is a point inside the PPC; the economy at full employment is on it; growth in potential output shifts the curve out.

  • At a point on the original curve: On the curve means full employment; a recession has cyclical unemployment.
  • At a point outside the original curve: Points outside the curve can't be reached with the resources the economy had.
  • On the new curve, because of the shift: The new curve shows later, larger potential; in the recession it was below even the old one.
  • Correct: At a point inside the original curve: Right: idle workers and machines mean output below what the economy could produce.

4. Which country in the table has the highest GDP per capita?

  1. Berovia, at about 30,000 dollars per person
  2. Cestia, because its total GDP is the largest
  3. Halvet, because it is in the middle on both counts
  4. They are equal once population is taken into account
Show the answer

GDP per capita divides output by the number of people, so a small country can produce more per person than a large one.

  • Correct: Berovia, at about 30,000 dollars per person: Right: 300 billion/10 million300 \text{ billion} / 10 \text{ million}=30,000= 30{,}000, more than Halvet (20,000) or Cestia (15,000).
  • Cestia, because its total GDP is the largest: Cestia has the most output in total but twice as many people as Halvet, so only 15,000 per person.
  • Halvet, because it is in the middle on both counts: Halvet's 20,000 per person is below Berovia's 30,000.
  • They are equal once population is taken into account: Dividing each GDP by its population gives 20,000, 30,000 and 15,000: not equal.

5. Cestia has the largest GDP in the table. What do the data suggest about living standards there?

  1. Its people work the most hours, because output is the largest
  2. Output per person is the lowest of the three countries
  3. Living standards are the highest, because total output is the highest
  4. Living standards are impossible to compare using GDP data
Show the answer

Total GDP measures the size of an economy. To compare material living standards, divide by population.

  • Its people work the most hours, because output is the largest: The table shows nothing about hours worked; a big total mostly reflects a big population.
  • Correct: Output per person is the lowest of the three countries: Right: 1,200 billion shared by 80 million people is 15,000 each, the least in the table.
  • Living standards are the highest, because total output is the highest: Total GDP ignores how many people share it; per person, Cestia is lowest.
  • Living standards are impossible to compare using GDP data: GDP per capita is a reasonable first comparison of material living standards, with limits.

6. A student concludes from the table that a typical person in Berovia is better off than a typical person in Halvet. Which fact, if true, would most weaken that conclusion?

  1. Both countries count GDP in dollars
  2. Halvet's total GDP is larger than Berovia's
  3. Most of Berovia's income goes to a small share of its people
  4. Berovia has a smaller population than Halvet
Show the answer

GDP per capita says nothing about how output is shared, so it can hide large differences between the average and the typical person.

  • Both countries count GDP in dollars: Using the same unit makes the comparison fairer, not weaker.
  • Halvet's total GDP is larger than Berovia's: Total GDP is not the measure the student used; per person, Berovia is higher.
  • Correct: Most of Berovia's income goes to a small share of its people: Right: GDP per capita is an average. If income is very unequal, the typical person can earn far less than the average.
  • Berovia has a smaller population than Halvet: The table already shows that, and per capita figures account for it.

11. In GDP accounting, which of these is investment?

  1. A student puts savings into a bank account
  2. A household buys a new washing machine
  3. A firm builds a new warehouse
  4. A family buys shares of a technology company
Show the answer

In everyday speech "investing" means buying stocks; in GDP it means buying new capital goods, new homes or adding to inventories.

  • A student puts savings into a bank account: A deposit is saving; investment in GDP is spending on new capital goods.
  • A household buys a new washing machine: A household's purchase of a durable good is consumption.
  • Correct: A firm builds a new warehouse: Right: new capital built this year is investment.
  • A family buys shares of a technology company: Buying stock moves ownership of a company from one person to another; it is saving, not investment spending in GDP.

12. Why is GDP per capita a better measure than total GDP for comparing material living standards across countries?

  1. It subtracts the cost of pollution from output
  2. It adjusts for the number of people who share the output
  3. It includes the value of leisure and home production
  4. It shows how evenly income is shared within a country
Show the answer

A country with a larger population has a larger GDP for that reason alone; per capita figures remove the effect of size.

  • It subtracts the cost of pollution from output: No version of GDP subtracts pollution.
  • Correct: It adjusts for the number of people who share the output: Right: dividing by population gives output per person.
  • It includes the value of leisure and home production: GDP per capita leaves those out, exactly as total GDP does.
  • It shows how evenly income is shared within a country: It is an average and shows nothing about how income is shared.
