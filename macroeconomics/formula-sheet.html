Graphs and formulas you must know

The exam gives you no formula sheet. These are the calculations and the graphs it expects you to know, in our own words: what each means, when to use it, and the trap that costs points.

spending multiplier\text{spending multiplier}=11−MPC= \frac{1}{1 - \text{MPC}}=1MPS= \frac{1}{\text{MPS}}Worked: 11−0.8=5\frac{1}{1 - 0.8} = 5The spending (expenditure) multiplier. How much real GDP changes per dollar of new spending.MPC + MPS = 1; with MPC 0.8 the multiplier is 5, not 1.25.Unit 3

The graphs

Habits that save points

  • Label everything. Every axis, every curve and every point the question names (P1, Y1, YF). A mislabeled graph loses the graph point and usually the shift point. Shifts Versus Movements Along a Curve
  • Show the direction. Draw the shift with an arrow and label the new curve (AD2) and the new equilibrium. Where Curves Cross
  • Explain with every link. "Explain" needs the causal link, not the conclusion: the rate falls, so investment rises, so AD rises. Fiscal and Monetary Policy Actions in the Short Run
  • Show your work. "Calculate" needs the formula or the steps, not just the number. Multipliers

On exam day

  • Four-function calculator. Bluebook has one built in (add, subtract, multiply, divide, square root, percent) for both sections; no scientific or graphing calculator.
  • No formula sheet. Nothing on this page is given on exam day.
  • Graphs by hand. Free responses are handwritten in a paper booklet, graphs drawn by hand.
