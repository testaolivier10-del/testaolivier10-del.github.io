Unit 2 · Topic 2.6

Real v. Nominal GDP

Nominal GDP values output at current prices; real GDP values it at base-year prices, so only real GDP changes with production.

Practice 1: Principles and ModelsPractice 2: InterpretationPractice 3: Manipulation

Part 1 · Hook

Why this matters

A headline says GDP hit a record, up 6% in a year. Are people producing 6% more? Not if prices rose 7%: then the economy actually produced less. Nominal GDP mixes how much is made with what it sells for. Taking out the price changes gives real GDP, the number that tells you whether output really grew.

Part 5 · Step by step

How it works, step by step

  1. Nominal GDP values each year's output at that year's prices→it can rise because prices rose, because output rose, or both
  2. Real GDP values each year's output at base-year prices→it changes only when quantities produced change
  3. Divide nominal GDP by real GDP and multiply by 100→the GDP deflator, a price index for everything produced
  4. Divide nominal GDP by the deflator and multiply by 100→real GDP, whose percent change is the growth rate of output

Part 6 · Key ideas

Key ideas

  • Nominal GDP uses current prices; real GDP uses base-year prices, so it measures output.
  • GDP deflator =nominal GDPreal GDP×100= \frac{\text{nominal GDP}}{\text{real GDP}} \times 100; real GDP =nominal GDPdeflator×100= \frac{\text{nominal GDP}}{\text{deflator}} \times 100. Divide by the deflator, never multiply.
  • The deflator is an index (base year 100), not a percent or dollars. Below 100 means prices were lower than in the base year.
  • Growth rate of real GDP =new−oldold×100= \frac{\text{new} - \text{old}}{\text{old}} \times 100, using real values. Real GDP per capita measures living standards.

Part 7 · Misconception

A common mistake

The wrong idea: To get real GDP, multiply nominal GDP by the deflator, and a rise in nominal GDP means output grew.

What actually happens: Divide nominal GDP by the deflator and multiply by 100. Nominal GDP can rise from higher prices alone, so only real GDP shows whether output grew.

Scenario

A headline about record GDP

A news site in Country Q reports: "GDP hits a record! Nominal GDP rose 6% this year, the biggest jump in a decade." Further down, the article notes that the GDP deflator rose 7% over the same year and that the population grew 1%.

5. Based on the full article, what happened to Country Q's real GDP this year?

  1. It rose by about 13%
  2. It rose by about 5%, after population growth
  3. It fell by about 1%
  4. It rose by about 6%, a record
Show the answer

Nominal GDP growth is about real growth plus inflation, so real growth is about nominal growth minus the change in the deflator.

  • It rose by about 13%: A rising deflator is subtracted from nominal growth, not added.
  • It rose by about 5%, after population growth: Population growth matters for output per person, but the first step is removing price changes.
  • Correct: It fell by about 1%: Right: real growth is about nominal growth minus the rise in the deflator: 6−7=−1%6 - 7 = -1\%.
  • It rose by about 6%, a record: Six percent is the nominal rise, which includes higher prices.

6. In a year before the base year, a country's nominal GDP is 400 billion dollars and its GDP deflator is 80. What is its real GDP, in billions of dollars?

Type a number. A four-function calculator is allowed, as on the exam.

Show the answer

40080×100=500\frac{400}{80} \times 100 = 500 billion. Prices were lower than in the base year, so real GDP (at base-year prices) is larger than nominal GDP.

7. Why do economists use real GDP rather than nominal GDP to measure changes in output over time?

  1. Real GDP is measured in current prices
  2. Real GDP is larger than nominal GDP in each year
  3. Real GDP holds prices fixed, so only quantities move it
  4. Real GDP includes used goods and transfers
Show the answer

Valuing every year's output at the same base-year prices means changes in real GDP reflect changes in production.

  • Real GDP is measured in current prices: That is nominal GDP; real GDP uses base-year prices.
  • Real GDP is larger than nominal GDP in each year: Real GDP is smaller than nominal after the base year when prices have risen.
  • Correct: Real GDP holds prices fixed, so only quantities move it: Right: nominal GDP can rise from higher prices alone; real GDP removes that.
  • Real GDP includes used goods and transfers: Real and nominal GDP count the same goods; only the prices used differ.

Part 9 · Summary

Summary

Nominal GDP values output at current prices; real GDP values it at base-year prices, so only real GDP changes with production. The GDP deflator is nominal GDP divided by real GDP times 100, and real GDP is nominal GDP divided by the deflator times 100. The growth rate of real GDP is its percent change, and real GDP per capita measures living standards.
