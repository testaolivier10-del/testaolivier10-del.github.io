Unit 5 · Topic 5.6

Economic Growth

Long-run economic growth is a sustained rise in potential output and real GDP per capita.

Practice 1: Principles and ModelsPractice 2: InterpretationPractice 3: ManipulationPractice 4: Graphing and Visuals

Part 1 · Hook

Why this matters

An hour of work in a United States business today produces about three and a half times what an hour produced in 1960, and real GDP per person is about three and a half times as high. Business cycles came and went along the way, but the long climb came from something else: more and better machines, more skilled workers, and better ideas about how to produce.

Part 5 · Step by step

How it works, step by step

  1. Firms invest in equipment, workers gain skills, technology improves→productivity (output per worker) rises
  2. Productivity rises→potential output rises
  3. Potential output rises→LRAS shifts right and the PPC shifts out
  4. Output grows faster than population→real GDP per capita, the average standard of living, rises

Part 6 · Key ideas

Key ideas

  • Economic growth is a sustained rise in potential output and real GDP per capita, shown by the PPC shifting out and LRAS shifting right.
  • Its sources: more physical capital (machines, buildings), more human capital (skills, education, health), better technology, and more or better natural resources and labor.
  • Productivity, output per worker, is what lets living standards keep rising; per capita growth is roughly output growth minus population growth.
  • Choosing more capital goods today costs consumer goods now but shifts the PPC out further later. Moving from inside the PPC to the curve is recovery, not growth.

Part 7 · Misconception

A common mistake

The wrong idea: Moving from a point inside the PPC to a point on it is economic growth.

What actually happens: That is recovery: idle workers and factories go back to work. Economic growth is the PPC itself shifting out (and LRAS shifting right), because the economy can produce more than before.

Part 8 · Check yourself

Check yourself

Exam-style questions. Anything you miss goes into your review queue.

1. What do economists mean by long-run economic growth?

  1. A rise in nominal GDP from one year to the next
  2. A recovery from a recession, back to full-employment output and jobs
  3. A sustained rise in real GDP per capita as capacity grows
  4. A rise in the price level caused by strong demand
Show the answer

Economic growth: the PPC shifts out and LRAS shifts right, so real GDP per capita can rise year after year.

  • A rise in nominal GDP from one year to the next: Nominal GDP can rise just because prices rise; growth is measured in real terms.
  • A recovery from a recession, back to full-employment output and jobs: Moving back to full employment uses idle resources; it does not raise capacity.
  • Correct: A sustained rise in real GDP per capita as capacity grows: Right: growth is an increase in potential output (and output per person) over many years.
  • A rise in the price level caused by strong demand: A higher price level is inflation, not growth.

6. An economy's real GDP rises 4 percent as it recovers from a recession. Over the same years, its full-employment output does not change. Is this economic growth?

  1. Yes: any rise in real GDP, for whatever reason, counts as economic growth
  2. No: idle resources went back to work; capacity did not rise
  3. Yes: real GDP per capita rose, so the PPC shifted out
  4. No: growth is counted when nominal GDP rises, not real GDP
Show the answer

Recovery moves the economy toward its PPC; growth shifts the PPC out.

  • Yes: any rise in real GDP, for whatever reason, counts as economic growth: A rise in real GDP can be recovery; growth means capacity rises.
  • Correct: No: idle resources went back to work; capacity did not rise: Right: on the PPC the economy moved from inside toward the curve; the curve did not shift out.
  • Yes: real GDP per capita rose, so the PPC shifted out: Output per person can rise during a recovery while the PPC stays put.
  • No: growth is counted when nominal GDP rises, not real GDP: Growth is measured in real terms, and a rise in nominal GDP alone could be inflation.

Part 9 · Summary

Summary

Long-run economic growth is a sustained rise in potential output and real GDP per capita. It comes from more physical capital, more human capital, better technology and more resources, all of which raise productivity. On the graphs, the PPC shifts out and LRAS shifts right. Living standards rise when output grows faster than population, and a choice of more capital goods today raises growth later.
