Unit 2 · Topic 2.4

Price Indices and Inflation

A price index compares the cost of a fixed basket with its cost in the base year (index 100).

Practice 1: Principles and ModelsPractice 2: InterpretationPractice 3: Manipulation

Part 1 · Hook

Why this matters

A movie ticket that cost $5$5 when your parents were teenagers costs about $15$15 today. Did movies get three times better, or did money buy less? To answer, economists price the same basket of goods year after year. That one idea, a fixed basket, gives the consumer price index, the inflation rate, and a way to tell whether a raise actually makes you better off.

Part 5 · Step by step

How it works, step by step

  1. A fixed basket of goods is priced each year→its cost this year divided by its cost in the base year, times 100, is the consumer price index
  2. The index rises from one year to the next→the percent change in the index is the inflation rate
  3. Prices rise faster than a person's pay→their real wage (nominal wage divided by the index, times 100) falls
  4. Consumers switch away from goods whose prices rose→the fixed basket overstates the rise in the cost of living (substitution bias)

Part 6 · Key ideas

Key ideas

  • CPI =cost of basket this yearcost in base year×100= \frac{\text{cost of basket this year}}{\text{cost in base year}} \times 100. The base year is 100.
  • Inflation rate =new index−old indexold index×100= \frac{\text{new index} - \text{old index}}{\text{old index}} \times 100. Divide by the old index, not 100.
  • Real value =nominal valueprice index×100= \frac{\text{nominal value}}{\text{price index}} \times 100: what money buys in base-year dollars.
  • Deflation: prices fall. Disinflation: inflation slows but prices still rise. Substitution bias and quality gains make the CPI overstate inflation.

Part 7 · Misconception

A common mistake

The wrong idea: A CPI of 130 means prices rose 130%, and a falling inflation rate means prices are falling.

What actually happens: A CPI of 130 means prices are 30% above the base year. A falling inflation rate is disinflation: prices still rise, just more slowly. Only deflation means prices fall.

Scenario

Beef, chicken and a fixed basket

In Country Y the consumer price index basket includes 10 pounds of beef and 10 pounds of chicken each month. This year a disease among cattle doubles the price of beef while the price of chicken stays the same. Most families respond by buying much less beef and more chicken. The statistics office keeps the basket the same for the year.

4. How does the fixed basket affect the CPI's measure of how much living costs rose in Country Y?

  1. It shows no rise, because chicken prices did not change
  2. It overstates the rise, because families switched to cheaper chicken
  3. It understates the rise, because beef doubled in price
  4. It measures the rise exactly, because both meats stay in the basket
Show the answer

Substitution bias: when a price rises, people buy less of it and more of a substitute. A fixed basket misses the switch and overstates the rise in the cost of living.

  • It shows no rise, because chicken prices did not change: Beef is in the basket, and its price doubled, so the index rises.
  • Correct: It overstates the rise, because families switched to cheaper chicken: Right: the CPI prices the old amount of beef, but families now buy less of it, so their actual costs rose less.
  • It understates the rise, because beef doubled in price: The doubling is fully counted at the old quantity of beef; the error goes the other way.
  • It measures the rise exactly, because both meats stay in the basket: Keeping quantities fixed ignores how families changed what they buy.

5. In which year did Country Z have disinflation?

  1. Year 3
  2. Year 4
  3. Year 2
  4. Year 1
Show the answer

Disinflation is a fall in the inflation rate while prices keep rising. Deflation is a fall in prices themselves.

  • Correct: Year 3: Right: inflation fell from 4% to about 2.9%, but prices still rose, so that is disinflation.
  • Year 4: The CPI fell in Year 4, which is deflation, not disinflation.
  • Year 2: Year 2 is the first year with inflation (4%); there is no earlier rate for it to fall from.
  • Year 1: Year 1 is the base year, so there is no earlier index to compare it with.

7. A country's CPI is 130. What does that number mean?

  1. Prices rose 130% since the base year
  2. The inflation rate this year is 130%
  3. The basket costs 130 dollars
  4. The basket costs 30% more than it did in the base year
Show the answer

A price index compares a cost with the base year, where the index is 100. The rise since the base year is the index minus 100, in percent.

  • Prices rose 130% since the base year: An index of 130 is 30% above 100, not 130% above it.
  • The inflation rate this year is 130%: The index level is not the inflation rate; the rate compares this year's index with last year's.
  • The basket costs 130 dollars: The index is a ratio times 100, not a dollar amount.
  • Correct: The basket costs 30% more than it did in the base year: Right: the base year is 100, so 130 is 30% higher.

Part 9 · Summary

Summary

A price index compares the cost of a fixed basket with its cost in the base year (index 100). The consumer price index uses a basket households buy; the inflation rate is the percent change in the index. Dividing a nominal value by the index and multiplying by 100 gives a real value. Substitution bias, new goods and quality gains make the CPI overstate inflation.
