Where Curves Cross

Where two curves cross, they agree on one price and one quantity.

Practice 4: Graphing and Visuals

Part 1 · Hook

Why this matters

At a farmers market, the price of strawberries settles each morning at the point where what shoppers want matches what growers brought. When a heat wave cuts the harvest, that meeting point moves. Most exam graphs ask the same thing: where do the curves cross, and where do they cross after one of them moves?

Part 5 · Step by step

How it works, step by step

  1. Two curves cross at one point→that point gives the one price and quantity both curves agree on (E1)
  2. One curve shifts→the crossing moves along the other curve to a new point (E2)
  3. You draw dashed lines from E1 and E2 to both axes→you can compare the old and new price and quantity
  4. You state each change with its direction→"the price rises from P1 to P2" earns the point

Part 6 · Key ideas

Key ideas

  • The intersection point is where two curves cross: on a market graph, the price at which the quantity buyers want equals the quantity sellers offer.
  • When one curve shifts, the crossing slides along the curve that did not move.
  • A before-and-after comparison marks both crossings, E1 and E2, with dashed lines to both axes (P1, P2, Q1, Q2) and says which way each moved.

Part 7 · Misconception

A common mistake

The wrong idea: When a curve shifts right, the new crossing is always higher.

What actually happens: It depends on which curve moved. D shifting right slides up the upward curve S (higher price); S shifting right slides down the downward curve D (lower price). Follow the curve that did not move.

Part 8 · Check yourself

Check yourself

Exam-style questions. Anything you miss goes into your review queue.

1. Buyers want more umbrellas at every price after a rainy forecast. Shift the right curve and mark the new point where the curves cross.

Work on the graph (it needs JavaScript), then check.

Show the answer

D shifts right along the upward curve S, so the new crossing E2 is higher and to the right: a higher price and a larger quantity.

  • Key: demand shifts right

2. A new machine lets bakers make bread more cheaply, so at every price they offer more loaves. Shift the right curve and mark the new crossing.

Work on the graph (it needs JavaScript), then check.

Show the answer

S shifts right down along D, so E2 is lower and to the right: a lower price and a larger quantity.

  • Key: supply shifts right

3. On a market graph, what does the point where the two curves cross show?

  1. The highest price buyers will pay for the good
  2. The price at which the quantity buyers want equals the quantity offered
  3. The lowest price sellers will accept for the good
  4. The price at which buyers and sellers trade nothing
Show the answer

At the crossing both curves give the same quantity at the same price, so buyers and sellers agree.

  • The highest price buyers will pay for the good: Buyers would pay more for the first few units; the crossing is where the curves meet.
  • Correct: The price at which the quantity buyers want equals the quantity offered: Right: the two quantities match there.
  • The lowest price sellers will accept for the good: Sellers would accept less for the first few units; the crossing is where the curves meet.
  • The price at which buyers and sellers trade nothing: The crossing is where trade happens, not where it stops.

Part 9 · Summary

Summary

Where two curves cross, they agree on one price and one quantity. When one curve shifts, the crossing slides along the other curve to a new point. Mark both crossings with dashed lines to both axes, label the old and new values, and state the direction of each change: that before-and-after comparison is what readers score on every graph in the course.
