Unit 2 · Topic 2.7

Business Cycles

Real GDP rises in expansions and falls in contractions around potential output, the full-employment level that grows over time.

Practice 1: Principles and ModelsPractice 4: Graphing and Visuals

Part 1 · Hook

Why this matters

Every few years the news fills with layoffs, store closings and worried shoppers; a few years later it is help-wanted signs and rising prices. These swings are the business cycle. Behind them is a steadier line, the economy's potential output, and the distance between the two, the output gap, tells you whether to expect rising unemployment or rising inflation.

Part 5 · Step by step

How it works, step by step

  1. Total spending in the economy falls→firms sell less, cut production and real GDP falls: a contraction
  2. Real GDP falls below potential output→there is a negative (recessionary) output gap
  3. Firms need fewer workers→cyclical unemployment rises and the unemployment rate goes above the natural rate
  4. Later, real GDP rises above potential in a boom→unemployment falls below the natural rate and inflation tends to rise

Part 6 · Key ideas

Key ideas

  • The business cycle has two phases, expansion (real GDP rising) and contraction (real GDP falling). The peak and trough are the turning points.
  • Potential output is real GDP at full employment (the natural rate of unemployment). It grows over time.
  • Output gap = real GDP - potential output. Negative (recessionary): cyclical unemployment. Positive (inflationary): unemployment below the natural rate and rising inflation.
  • A recession is a severe, lasting contraction. On a PPC, a recession is a point inside the curve; potential output is a point on it.

Part 7 · Misconception

A common mistake

The wrong idea: Peak and trough are phases of the business cycle, and potential output is the most an economy can ever produce.

What actually happens: The phases are expansion and contraction; peak and trough are the turning points. Potential output is the sustainable full-employment level, and real GDP can rise above it for a while in a boom.

Scenario

A slowdown in Halvet

In Halvet, households and firms cut back on spending. For two quarters in a row real GDP falls, stores report weak sales and factories cut shifts. The unemployment rate rises from 4.5% to 7.0%. Economists estimate Halvet's natural rate of unemployment at 4.5%.

3. What is Halvet's cyclical unemployment rate now?

Type a number (%). A four-function calculator is allowed, as on the exam.

Show the answer

Cyclical = actual - natural =7.0−4.5=2.5%= 7.0 - 4.5 = 2.5\%.

  • Answer: 2.5 %

Scenario

A boom in Berovia

Berovia's economy is booming. Firms are running extra shifts and paying overtime, and some workers have put off retiring to take well-paid jobs. The unemployment rate is 3.0%, while the natural rate is estimated at 5.0%. Prices are rising faster each month.

4. What kind of output gap does Berovia have?

  1. No output gap, because the economy is growing
  2. A negative gap, because prices are rising
  3. A positive (inflationary) output gap
  4. A negative (recessionary) output gap
Show the answer

Unemployment below the natural rate means the economy is producing beyond its potential output for now.

  • No output gap, because the economy is growing: Growing output can be above, at or below potential; here it is above.
  • A negative gap, because prices are rising: Rising prices point the other way: they come with a positive gap.
  • Correct: A positive (inflationary) output gap: Right: unemployment is below the natural rate, so real GDP is above potential output.
  • A negative (recessionary) output gap: A negative gap comes with unemployment above the natural rate.

6. What is potential output?

  1. The real GDP an economy produces at full employment
  2. The largest real GDP an economy has reached in the past
  3. The real GDP an economy produces with zero unemployment
  4. The real GDP at the trough of a business cycle
Show the answer

Potential (full-employment) output is the sustainable level, at the natural rate of unemployment. It grows over time as the economy gains resources and better technology.

  • Correct: The real GDP an economy produces at full employment: Right: output when unemployment is at its natural rate.
  • The largest real GDP an economy has reached in the past: A past high may have been above potential during a boom.
  • The real GDP an economy produces with zero unemployment: Full employment leaves frictional and structural unemployment.
  • The real GDP at the trough of a business cycle: At a trough output is usually far below potential.

7. Build the explanation: households and firms in Country V cut their spending sharply. Explain what happens to output and unemployment.

Build the answer part by part, then write it in your own words.

Show the answer

Less spending means firms sell less, so they produce less and need fewer workers: real GDP falls below potential (a negative output gap) and cyclical unemployment rises.

  • An answer that earns the point: Total spending falls, so firms sell less and produce less: real GDP falls below potential output, which leads to a negative (recessionary) output gap. Cyclical unemployment rises because firms need fewer workers.

Part 9 · Summary

Summary

Real GDP rises in expansions and falls in contractions around potential output, the full-employment level that grows over time. The output gap is real GDP minus potential: a negative (recessionary) gap brings cyclical unemployment, and a positive (inflationary) gap brings unemployment below the natural rate and rising inflation. Peak and trough are the turning points between the phases.
