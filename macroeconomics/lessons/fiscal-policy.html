Unit 3 · Topic 3.8

Fiscal Policy

Fiscal policy uses government purchases, taxes and transfers to shift aggregate demand.

Practice 2: InterpretationPractice 3: ManipulationPractice 4: Graphing and Visuals

Part 1 · Hook

Why this matters

When a recession hits, lawmakers argue about road projects, tax rebates and checks to households. When prices are climbing too fast, they talk about cutting programs or raising taxes. Behind every one of those debates is the same model: government purchases and taxes change aggregate demand, and the multiplier decides how much.

Part 5 · Step by step

How it works, step by step

  1. Recessionary gap: the government raises purchases or cuts taxes→spending rises (directly, or through disposable income and consumption)
  2. Spending rises, with the multiplier→AD shifts right by a multiple of the change
  3. AD shifts right along SRAS→real GDP and the price level rise; unemployment falls
  4. Inflationary gap: the government cuts purchases or raises taxes→AD shifts left: the price level and real GDP fall toward full employment

Part 6 · Key ideas

Key ideas

  • Fiscal policy is the government's use of purchases (G), taxes and transfer payments to change aggregate demand. Discretionary means a deliberate decision.
  • Expansionary (recessionary gap): raise G, cut taxes or raise transfers, so AD shifts right. Contractionary (inflationary gap): the reverse.
  • A change in G has a bigger effect than the same change in taxes or transfers: spending multiplier 1 / MPS against MPC / MPS.
  • Size the policy with the gap: direction first, then divide the gap by the right multiplier.
  • Lags in recognizing, deciding and carrying out policy can make it arrive at the wrong time.

Part 7 · Misconception

A common mistake

The wrong idea: Transfer payments such as pensions are government purchases, so they use the spending multiplier.

What actually happens: Transfers buy no goods or services. They raise disposable income, and households save part, so they work like a tax cut, with a multiplier of MPC / MPS.

Scenario

Velora's overheated economy

Velora is an invented country. Its real GDP is 60 billion dollars above full-employment output, unemployment is below the natural rate, and prices are rising fast. Velora's MPC is 0.75. Its government wants to close the gap now rather than wait for the economy to adjust on its own.

1. Which fiscal policy fits Velora's situation?

  1. Expansionary: raise government spending or cut taxes on households
  2. Contractionary: cut government spending or raise taxes
  3. Raise government spending and cut taxes together
  4. Raise transfer payments to households
Show the answer

An inflationary gap calls for contractionary fiscal policy: less government spending or higher taxes, to shift AD left.

  • Expansionary: raise government spending or cut taxes on households: That would shift AD right and widen an inflationary gap.
  • Correct: Contractionary: cut government spending or raise taxes: Right: output is above full employment, so the government lowers spending to shift AD left.
  • Raise government spending and cut taxes together: Both raise AD, which is the wrong direction for an inflationary gap.
  • Raise transfer payments to households: More transfers raise disposable income and spending, shifting AD right.

4. If Velora's contractionary policy works, what happens in the short run to the price level and unemployment?

  1. The price level rises and unemployment falls further
  2. The price level falls and unemployment falls
  3. The price level falls and unemployment rises
  4. Neither changes until wages adjust
Show the answer

AD shifts left along SRAS: real GDP falls to YF, the price level falls, and unemployment rises back to the natural rate.

  • The price level rises and unemployment falls further: Those follow a rise in AD, not a fall.
  • The price level falls and unemployment falls: Less output needs fewer workers, so unemployment rises.
  • Correct: The price level falls and unemployment rises: Right: AD shifts left, so output, the price level and employment fall toward full-employment levels.
  • Neither changes until wages adjust: AD shifts at once along SRAS, so the price level and output change in the short run.

Part 9 · Summary

Summary

Fiscal policy uses government purchases, taxes and transfers to shift aggregate demand. In a recessionary gap, expansionary policy (more G, lower taxes, more transfers) shifts AD right, raising output and the price level; in an inflationary gap, contractionary policy shifts AD left. Size it with the gap: direction first, then divide the gap by the spending or tax multiplier.
