What the course covers

8 units and 81 topics in the order of the course framework (the Calculus AB topics only), plus 7 math topics placed where they are first needed. Each topic has an interactive lesson, a free notes page and exam-style practice, and each unit a printable unit sheet.

1,392 practice questions, many in stimulus sets of four or five that share one graph or table, as on the real exam. Every option has its own explanation, and typed answers are graded by their value, so any correct form counts. Practice checks what the exam checks: answers you type as expressions, graphs and tables, calculator and no-calculator parts, and justifications that name the theorem.

All 88 topics are built, each with its lesson, notes and questions.

Unit 1 · Limits and Continuity What a Limit Is and How to Write It

A function kk has k(3)=8k(3) = 8, and as xx approaches 3 from either side, the values k(x)k(x) close in on 6. Which is correct?

Show the answer

limx→3k(x)=6\lim_{x \to 3} k(x) = 6 and k(3)=8k(3) = 8 The limit is where the values head (6); the value at 3 is what kk actually gives there (8).
