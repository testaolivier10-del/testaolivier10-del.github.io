Unit 1 asked whether change can happen at an instant and built the tool to answer it: the limit. This page puts that tool to work. You will measure change over an interval, shrink the interval, and let a limit finish the job. Everything in Unit 2 grows out of the one formula you meet here.

Average rate of change: a change over a change

Suppose a delivery drone is 14 meters above the street at $t = 2$ seconds and 47 meters up at $t = 6$ seconds. It climbed $47 - 14 = 33$ meters in $6 - 2 = 4$ seconds, so on average it climbed $\frac{33}{4} = 8.25$ meters per second. That number is an average rate of change.

For any function $f$ on an interval $[a, b]$:

$$\text{average rate of change} = \frac{f(b) - f(a)}{b - a}.$$

Three habits keep this right every time:

  • Subtract in the same order on top and bottom. Later value minus earlier value, over later time minus earlier time.
  • Divide by the change in input. The difference $f(b) - f(a)$ is how much $f$ changed; it is not yet a rate.
  • Name the units. The units are output units per input unit: meters per second, gallons per minute, dollars per item.

An average describes a whole interval. The drone might have climbed fast at first and slowly later; 8.25 meters per second hides that. It also does not tell you how high the drone was. A sentence that interprets an average rate names the quantity, its units and the interval: "Between $t = 2$ and $t = 6$ seconds, the drone's altitude increased by an average of 8.25 meters per second."

Worked example 1. The volume of water in a tank is $V(t)$ gallons after $t$ minutes, with $V(3) = 120$ and $V(8) = 95$. Find the average rate of change of $V$ on $[3, 8]$ and interpret it.

Change in volume: $V(8) - V(3) = 95 - 120 = -25$ gallons. Change in time: $8 - 3 = 5$ minutes.

Average rate: $\frac{-25}{5} = -5$ gallons per minute.

Meaning: between $t = 3$ and $t = 8$ minutes, the volume of water decreased by an average of 5 gallons per minute. The negative sign means the tank was losing water.

The picture: a secant line

Plot the two points $(a, f(a))$ and $(b, f(b))$ on the graph of $f$ and draw the line through them. That line is a secant line. Its slope is rise over run, $\frac{f(b) - f(a)}{b - a}$, which is exactly the average rate of change. So every average rate you compute is the slope of a secant line, and every secant slope you read from a graph is an average rate.

Shrinking the interval: the difference quotient

To learn how fast $f$ changes at one input $x = a$, look at shorter and shorter intervals that start at $a$. Write the far end as $a + h$, where $h$ is the width of the interval. The average rate on that interval is the difference quotient:

$$\frac{f(a + h) - f(a)}{h}.$$

The number $h$ can be positive (an interval to the right of $a$) or negative (an interval to the left). Look at the figure: for $f(x) = \frac{x^2}{4} + 1$ and $a = 1$, the secant slopes for $h = 3, 2, 1, 0.4$ are $1.25, 1, 0.75, 0.6$. As $h$ shrinks, the second point slides toward $P$ and the secant lines pivot toward one line. Their slopes close in on a single number.

The instantaneous rate of change is a limit

You cannot simply put $h = 0$ into the difference quotient: that gives $\frac{f(a) - f(a)}{0} = \frac{0}{0}$, an indeterminate form. Instead, take the limit. The instantaneous rate of change of $f$ at $x = a$ is

$$\lim_{h \to 0} \frac{f(a + h) - f(a)}{h},$$

when that limit exists. The method is the limit algebra of Unit 1: expand, simplify until the factor $h$ cancels, then let $h \to 0$.

Worked example 2. Find the instantaneous rate of change of $f(x) = \frac{x^2}{4} + 1$ at $x = 1$.

Values: $f(1) = \frac{1}{4} + 1 = \frac{5}{4}$ and $f(1 + h) = \frac{(1 + h)^2}{4} + 1 = \frac{1 + 2h + h^2}{4} + 1$.

Difference: $f(1 + h) - f(1) = \frac{2h + h^2}{4}$. Keep the middle term $2h$ when you square $1 + h$; dropping it is the most common slip.

Quotient, for $h \ne 0$: $\frac{2h + h^2}{4h} = \frac{h(2 + h)}{4h} = \frac{2 + h}{4}$.

Limit: $\lim_{h \to 0} \frac{2 + h}{4} = \frac{2}{4} = \frac{1}{2}$. The rate of change of $f$ at $x = 1$ is $\frac{1}{2}$, the number the secant slopes in the figure were closing in on.

Worked example 3. A cart rolls so that it has traveled $s(t) = 5t^2$ meters after $t$ seconds. How fast is it moving at $t = 2$?

Difference quotient: $\frac{s(2 + h) - s(2)}{h} = \frac{5(4 + 4h + h^2) - 20}{h} = \frac{20h + 5h^2}{h} = 20 + 5h$.

For $h = 0.1$ the average is 20.5 meters per second; for $h = 0.01$ it is 20.05. The limit is $\lim_{h \to 0} (20 + 5h) = 20$, so at the instant $t = 2$ the cart is moving at 20 meters per second.

Both rates have the same units. The difference is what they describe: a stretch of time, or one moment.

Common mistakes

  • A value over a time is not a rate. The drone's altitude at $t = 5$ divided by 5 ignores where it started. A rate always compares two changes.
  • Substituting $h = 0$ too early. That gives $\frac{0}{0}$, which says only that more algebra is needed.
  • Losing the middle term. $(a + h)^2 = a^2 + 2ah + h^2$.
  • Treating an average as an instant. An average on $[2, 6]$ is not the rate at $t = 4$.
