You know the derivatives of $\sin x$ and $\cos x$. The other four trigonometric functions, $\tan x$, $\cot x$, $\sec x$ and $\csc x$, are all fractions built from those two. So the quotient rule, plus one identity, gives every one of their derivatives. This page works them out, shows a pattern that makes the signs easy to remember, and practices them on exact values.

Rewrite with sine and cosine

FunctionIn terms of sine and cosine
$\tan x$$\frac{\sin x}{\cos x}$
$\cot x$$\frac{\cos x}{\sin x}$
$\sec x$$\frac{1}{\cos x}$
$\csc x$$\frac{1}{\sin x}$

Every function in the table is a quotient, defined where its denominator is not 0. The one identity you need is the Pythagorean identity, $\sin^2 x + \cos^2 x = 1$.

The derivative of tangent

Worked example 1. Find $\frac{d}{dx}(\tan x)$.

High $= \sin x$, d-high $= \cos x$. Low $= \cos x$, d-low $= -\sin x$.

Quotient rule: $\frac{(\cos x)(\cos x) - (\sin x)(-\sin x)}{\cos^2 x} = \frac{\cos^2 x + \sin^2 x}{\cos^2 x}$.

Identity: the numerator is 1, so the derivative is $\frac{1}{\cos^2 x} = \sec^2 x$.

The picture agrees. The graph of $\tan x$ always rises on $\left( -\frac{\pi}{2}, \frac{\pi}{2} \right)$, gently through the origin with slope $\sec^2 0 = 1$, then more and more steeply near the asymptotes, where $\cos x \to 0$ and $\sec^2 x$ grows without bound. Since $\cos^2 x \le 1$, $\sec^2 x = \frac{1}{\cos^2 x} \ge 1$: no slope of $\tan x$ is ever less than 1.

The derivative of cotangent

Worked example 2. Find $\frac{d}{dx}(\cot x)$.

$\frac{(-\sin x)(\sin x) - (\cos x)(\cos x)}{\sin^2 x} = \frac{-\left( \sin^2 x + \cos^2 x \right)}{\sin^2 x} = \frac{-1}{\sin^2 x} = -\csc^2 x$.

Secant and cosecant: a constant on top

Worked example 3. Find $\frac{d}{dx}(\sec x)$.

High $= 1$, d-high $= 0$. Low $= \cos x$, d-low $= -\sin x$.

$\frac{(0)(\cos x) - (1)(-\sin x)}{\cos^2 x} = \frac{\sin x}{\cos^2 x} = \frac{1}{\cos x} \cdot \frac{\sin x}{\cos x} = \sec x\tan x$.

The same steps with $\csc x = \frac{1}{\sin x}$ give $\frac{0 - \cos x}{\sin^2 x} = -\csc x\cot x$.

All six, and the "co-" pattern

$f(x)$$f'(x)$PartnerPartner's derivative
$\sin x$$\cos x$$\cos x$$-\sin x$
$\tan x$$\sec^2 x$$\cot x$$-\csc^2 x$
$\sec x$$\sec x\tan x$$\csc x$$-\csc x\cot x$

Read across each row. The partner on the right is the "co-" function: cosine, cotangent, cosecant. Its derivative is the left-hand derivative with every function swapped for its co-function and a minus sign in front. So you only need to learn the left column; the right column follows.

Exact values

On no-calculator questions, evaluate these derivatives at the standard angles by first writing each piece in terms of sine and cosine.

Worked example 4. Find the slope of $y = \sec x$ at $x = \frac{\pi}{3}$.

$\cos \frac{\pi}{3} = \frac{1}{2}$ and $\sin \frac{\pi}{3} = \frac{\sqrt{3}}{2}$, so $\sec \frac{\pi}{3} = 2$ and $\tan \frac{\pi}{3} = \sqrt{3}$.

Slope: $\sec x\tan x = 2\sqrt{3}$.

Worked example 5. Write the line tangent to $y = \tan x$ at $x = \frac{\pi}{4}$.

Point: $\tan \frac{\pi}{4} = 1$. Slope: $\sec^2 \frac{\pi}{4} = \frac{1}{\left( \sqrt{2}/2 \right)^2} = 2$.

Line: $y = 1 + 2\left( x - \frac{\pi}{4} \right)$.

Combining with the other rules

These derivatives work with every rule so far. For $y = x\tan x$, the product rule gives $\tan x + x\sec^2 x$. For $y = \sec x - 2\cot x$, the difference and constant multiple rules give $\sec x\tan x + 2\csc^2 x$; note the double negative.

Common mistakes

  • Signs: only the "co-" derivatives are negative.
  • Squares: $(\tan x)' = \sec^2 x$, not $\sec x$; $(\sec x)' = \sec x\tan x$, not $\tan^2 x$.
  • Degrees: all of these formulas are for radians.
