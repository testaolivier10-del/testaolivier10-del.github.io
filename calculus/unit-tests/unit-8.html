The region RR between f(x)=3+2x−x2f(x) = 3 + 2x - x^2 and g(x)=x2−2x+3g(x) = x^2 - 2x + 3

1. Which integral gives the area of RR?

  1. ∫02[(3+2x−x2)−(x2−2x+3)]dx\int_0^2 \left[(3 + 2x - x^2) - (x^2 - 2x + 3)\right]\,dx
  2. ∫02[(x2−2x+3)−(3+2x−x2)]dx\int_0^2 \left[(x^2 - 2x + 3) - (3 + 2x - x^2)\right]\,dx
  3. ∫02(3+2x−x2)dx\int_0^2 (3 + 2x - x^2)\,dx
  4. ∫24[(3+2x−x2)−(x2−2x+3)]dx\int_2^4 \left[(3 + 2x - x^2) - (x^2 - 2x + 3)\right]\,dx
Show the answer

On [0,2][0, 2] the graph of ff is on top, so each vertical rectangle has height f(x)−g(x)f(x) - g(x), and the curves meet at x=0x = 0 and x=2x = 2.

  • Correct: ∫02[(3+2x−x2)−(x2−2x+3)]dx\int_0^2 \left[(3 + 2x - x^2) - (x^2 - 2x + 3)\right]\,dx: Top minus bottom, from the left crossing to the right one.
  • ∫02[(x2−2x+3)−(3+2x−x2)]dx\int_0^2 \left[(x^2 - 2x + 3) - (3 + 2x - x^2)\right]\,dx: Bottom minus top: every rectangle gets a negative height, and the integral comes out as −83-\frac{8}{3}.
  • ∫02(3+2x−x2)dx\int_0^2 (3 + 2x - x^2)\,dx: This is the area under ff all the way down to the xx-axis, which includes the space under gg.
  • ∫24[(3+2x−x2)−(x2−2x+3)]dx\int_2^4 \left[(3 + 2x - x^2) - (x^2 - 2x + 3)\right]\,dx: The limits are the yy-values 2 and 4. With dxdx the limits are xx-values: the crossings at x=0x = 0 and x=2x = 2.

2. How do you find the limits of integration for the area of RR without the graph?

  1. Solve f(x)=g(x)f(x) = g(x), which gives x=0x = 0 and x=2x = 2
  2. Solve f(x)=0f(x) = 0, which gives where ff meets the xx-axis
  3. Use the yy-values where the curves meet, 3 and 3
  4. Use the lowest and highest points of RR, y=2y = 2 and y=4y = 4
Show the answer

The region is closed where the curves meet. 3+2x−x23 + 2x - x^2=x2−2x+3= x^2 - 2x + 3 gives 2x2−4x=02x^2 - 4x = 0, so x=0x = 0 or x=2x = 2.

  • Correct: Solve f(x)=g(x)f(x) = g(x), which gives x=0x = 0 and x=2x = 2: The curves meet where f(x)=g(x)f(x) = g(x); those xx-values close the region.
  • Solve f(x)=0f(x) = 0, which gives where ff meets the xx-axis: Where ff meets the xx-axis matters for the area under ff, not for the region between two curves.
  • Use the yy-values where the curves meet, 3 and 3: An integral in dxdx runs between xx-values. The points meet at y=3y = 3, but the limits are their xx-coordinates.
  • Use the lowest and highest points of RR, y=2y = 2 and y=4y = 4: Heights of the region are not limits. The limits come from where the curves cross, at xx-values.

3. Where is RR tallest, and how tall is it there?

  1. At x=1x = 1, where f(x)−g(x)=2f(x) - g(x) = 2
  2. At x=1x = 1, where f(x)=4f(x) = 4
  3. At x=2x = 2, where the curves meet
  4. At x=0x = 0, where f(x)−g(x)=3f(x) - g(x) = 3
Show the answer

The height is h(x)=4x−2x2h(x) = 4x - 2x^2, with h′(x)=4−4x=0h'(x) = 4 - 4x = 0 at x=1x = 1, and h′h' changes from positive to negative there. h(1)=4−2=2h(1) = 4 - 2 = 2.

  • Correct: At x=1x = 1, where f(x)−g(x)=2f(x) - g(x) = 2: h(1)=2h(1) = 2 is the largest value of h(x)=4x−2x2h(x) = 4x - 2x^2 on [0,2][0, 2].
  • At x=1x = 1, where f(x)=4f(x) = 4: 4 is the height of the top curve above the xx-axis. The region's height is top minus bottom: 4−2=24 - 2 = 2.
  • At x=2x = 2, where the curves meet: Where the curves meet, the height of RR is 0, its smallest.
  • At x=0x = 0, where f(x)−g(x)=3f(x) - g(x) = 3: At x=0x = 0 both curves equal 3, so the height there is 0, not 3.

4. Which integral gives the area of RR?

  1. ∫−24[(y+4)−y22]dy\int_{-2}^{4} \left[(y + 4) - \frac{y^2}{2}\right]\,dy
  2. ∫−24[y22−(y+4)]dy\int_{-2}^{4} \left[\frac{y^2}{2} - (y + 4)\right]\,dy
  3. ∫08[2x−(x−4)]dx\int_{0}^{8} \left[\sqrt{2x} - (x - 4)\right]\,dx
  4. ∫28[(y+4)−y22]dy\int_{2}^{8} \left[(y + 4) - \frac{y^2}{2}\right]\,dy
Show the answer

Slice horizontally: each strip runs from the parabola on the left to the line on the right, so its length is right minus left, and yy runs from −2-2 to 44.

  • Correct: ∫−24[(y+4)−y22]dy\int_{-2}^{4} \left[(y + 4) - \frac{y^2}{2}\right]\,dy: Right minus left, in dydy, between the yy-values where the curves meet.
  • ∫−24[y22−(y+4)]dy\int_{-2}^{4} \left[\frac{y^2}{2} - (y + 4)\right]\,dy: Left minus right gives a negative number. The line x=y+4x = y + 4 is the right edge.
  • ∫08[2x−(x−4)]dx\int_{0}^{8} \left[\sqrt{2x} - (x - 4)\right]\,dx: With vertical slices the bottom edge changes at x=2x = 2: from x=0x = 0 to 22 the bottom is y=−2xy = -\sqrt{2x}, not y=x−4y = x - 4. One integral in xx is not enough.
  • ∫28[(y+4)−y22]dy\int_{2}^{8} \left[(y + 4) - \frac{y^2}{2}\right]\,dy: 2 and 8 are the xx-coordinates of the crossings. An integral in dydy needs the yy-coordinates, −2-2 and 44.

5. Integrating with respect to yy, what are the limits of integration for the area of RR?

  1. y=−2y = -2 and y=4y = 4, where y22=y+4\frac{y^2}{2} = y + 4
  2. x=2x = 2 and x=8x = 8, the xx-values where the curves meet
  3. y=0y = 0 and y=4y = 4, from the xx-axis up to the top
  4. x=0x = 0 and x=8x = 8, from the left edge to the right
Show the answer

y22=y+4\frac{y^2}{2} = y + 4 gives y2−2y−8=0y^2 - 2y - 8 = 0, so (y−4)(y+2)=0(y - 4)(y + 2) = 0: y=−2y = -2 and y=4y = 4.

  • Correct: y=−2y = -2 and y=4y = 4, where y22=y+4\frac{y^2}{2} = y + 4: In dydy, the limits are the lowest and highest yy of the region.
  • x=2x = 2 and x=8x = 8, the xx-values where the curves meet: These are xx-values. With dydy the limits must be yy-values.
  • y=0y = 0 and y=4y = 4, from the xx-axis up to the top: The region goes below the xx-axis, down to y=−2y = -2.
  • x=0x = 0 and x=8x = 8, from the left edge to the right: These are xx-values, and the integral is in dydy.

6. Why is it simpler to find the area of RR with horizontal strips than with vertical ones?

  1. Vertical strips change bottom curves at x=2x = 2
  2. Horizontal strips give a larger and more accurate area
  3. The region lies to the right of the yy-axis, where x>0x > 0
  4. Vertical strips would make the area of RR negative
Show the answer

Every horizontal strip runs from the same left curve to the same right curve. Vertical strips end on y=−2xy = -\sqrt{2x} for 0≤x≤20 \le x \le 2 and on y=x−4y = x - 4 for 2≤x≤82 \le x \le 8, so they need two integrals.

  • Correct: Vertical strips change bottom curves at x=2x = 2: One formula for each edge in yy; two pieces in xx.
  • Horizontal strips give a larger and more accurate area: Both ways give the same area, 18. The choice only changes how much work it takes.
  • The region lies to the right of the yy-axis, where x>0x > 0: Where the region sits does not decide the method. What matters is whether each edge is one formula.
  • Vertical strips would make the area of RR negative: Done correctly, vertical strips give +18+18 too, with two integrals.

10. Find the total area of the region between y=x3y = x^3 and y=xy = x.

No calculator. Type the exact value (a fraction, π, e or a root is fine).

Show the answer

x3=xx^3 = x at x=−1,0,1x = -1, 0, 1. On [−1,0][-1, 0] the cubic is on top; on [0,1][0, 1] the line is. Area =∫−10(x3−x)dx= \int_{-1}^{0} (x^3 - x)\,dx+∫01(x−x3)dx+ \int_0^1 (x - x^3)\,dx=14= \frac{1}{4}+14+ \frac{1}{4}=12= \frac{1}{2}.
