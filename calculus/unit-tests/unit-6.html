Show the answer

g′(x)=f(x)=x(x−4)g'(x) = f(x) = x(x - 4), which is negative for 0<x<40 < x < 4. So gg decreases on (0,4)(0, 4).

  • Correct: (0,4)(0, 4): Correct: g′=f<0g' = f < 0 on (0,4)(0, 4).
  • (0,2)(0, 2): On (0,2)(0, 2), ff is decreasing, which makes gg concave down. gg decreases wherever ff is negative: all of (0,4)(0, 4).
  • (4,6)(4, 6): There f>0f > 0, so gg increases.
  • (2,6)(2, 6): On (2,6)(2, 6), ff is increasing; that is where gg is concave up, not where it decreases.

2. At which xx does gg have a relative minimum, and why?

  1. x=2x = 2, because ff reaches its minimum value at x=2x = 2
  2. x=0x = 0, because g(0)=0g(0) = 0 at the start of the interval
  3. x=4x = 4, because f(4)=0f(4) = 0 and so g′(4)=0g'(4) = 0

Show the answer

g′=fg' = f is negative on (0,4)(0, 4) and positive on (4,6)(4, 6), so gg has a relative minimum at x=4x = 4. A zero of g′g' is not enough; the change of sign is the reason.

  • x=2x = 2, because ff reaches its minimum value at x=2x = 2: At x=2x = 2, g′=fg' = f is most negative, so gg is falling fastest; gg keeps falling until x=4x = 4.
  • x=0x = 0, because g(0)=0g(0) = 0 at the start of the interval: g(0)=0g(0) = 0 is just the starting value; gg is decreasing right after 0.
  • x=4x = 4, because f(4)=0f(4) = 0 and so g′(4)=0g'(4) = 0: The location is right, but f(4)=0f(4) = 0 alone is not a reason: g′g' must change sign there, from negative to positive.
  • Correct: x=4x = 4: g′=fg' = f changes from negative to positive: Correct: gg stops falling and starts rising at x=4x = 4.

3. Where is g″=f′g'' = f' positive, so that the graph of gg bends upward?

  1. (0,2)(0, 2)
  2. (0,4)(0, 4)
  3. (2,6)(2, 6)
  4. (4,6)(4, 6)
Show the answer

g″(x)=f′(x)=2x−4g''(x) = f'(x) = 2x - 4, positive for x>2x > 2. So gg is concave up on (2,6)(2, 6).

  • (0,2)(0, 2): On (0,2)(0, 2), ff is decreasing, so gg is concave down.
  • (0,4)(0, 4): On (0,4)(0, 4), f<0f < 0, which makes gg decrease; concavity is a different question.
  • Correct: (2,6)(2, 6): Correct: ff is increasing there, so g″=f′>0g'' = f' > 0.
  • (4,6)(4, 6): f>0f > 0 on (4,6)(4, 6) makes gg increase. Concavity of gg follows whether ff increases: from x=2x = 2 on.

  2. negative: on (0,4)(0, 4) the graph of ff is below the axis

  • Correct: negative: on (0,4)(0, 4) the graph of ff is below the axis: Correct: f<0f < 0 on (0,4)(0, 4), so the integral from 0 to 4 is negative.
  • positive, because an area is a positive number: The size of the region is positive, but it lies below the axis, so it counts as negative in ∫04f(t)dt\int_0^4 f(t)\,dt.
  • zero, because f(4)=0f(4) = 0 at the right end of [0,4][0, 4]: f(4)=0f(4) = 0 is the height at one point. g(4)g(4) adds up ff over all of [0,4][0, 4].

7. On a graph of the rate against time, the region for 5≤t≤65 \le t \le 6 lies below the t-axis and has area 120. What does it mean?

  1. The pool gains 120 gallons, since an area is positive
  2. The pool loses 120 gallons from t = 5 to t = 6 hours
  3. The pool holds 120 gallons at t = 6 hours
  4. The rate of draining grows by 120 gallons per hour
Show the answer

Area under a rate graph is an amount of change; a region below the axis is a decrease. From t=5t = 5 to t=6t = 6 the pool loses 120 gallons.

  • The pool gains 120 gallons, since an area is positive: A region below the axis comes from a negative rate, so the amount decreases.
  • Correct: The pool loses 120 gallons from t = 5 to t = 6 hours: Correct: area means gallons, and below the axis means the amount goes down.
  • The pool holds 120 gallons at t = 6 hours: The area is a change over the hour, not the amount in the pool.
  • The rate of draining grows by 120 gallons per hour: That reads the area as a change in the rate. The area is gallons, a rate times a time: here, water that leaves the pool.

9. A home uses electric power at a rate P(t)P(t) kilowatts, where tt is in hours. What does ∫024P(t)dt\int_0^{24} P(t)\,dt mean?

Show the answer

∫024P(t)dt\int_0^{24} P(t)\,dt is the limit of sums of (kilowatts) ×\times (hours): the energy, in kilowatt-hours, used from t=0t = 0 to t=24t = 24.

  • the power, in kilowatts, that the home is using at t = 24 hours: That is P(24)P(24), one value of the rate. The integral accumulates the rate over the day.
  • the average power of the home over the day, in kilowatts: An average would divide the accumulated energy by 24 hours. The integral is the accumulated energy itself.
  • how fast the power changes during the 24 hours, in kilowatts per hour: That would be a rate of change of PP, its derivative. The integral of PP adds up PP times time.
  • Correct: the kilowatt-hours of energy the home uses during the 24 hours: Correct: kilowatts times hours is kilowatt-hours, the energy used over the day.
