1. How does P′(3)P'(3) compare with the average rate of change of PP from t=1t = 1 to t=3t = 3?

  1. P′(3)P'(3) equals the average rate, since both use t=3t = 3
  2. P′(3)P'(3) is less than the average rate, which is 44
  3. P′(3)P'(3) is greater: 3 versus an average of 2
  4. They are not comparable, because their units differ
Show the answer

The slope of the tangent line is 3; the secant from (1,1.5)(1, 1.5) to (3,5.5)(3, 5.5) has slope 2. The population grows faster at t=3t = 3 than on average before it.

  • P′(3)P'(3) equals the average rate, since both use t=3t = 3: The average rate is the slope of a secant line through two points; it is 2, not 3.
  • P′(3)P'(3) is less than the average rate, which is 44: That divides 5.5−1.5=45.5 - 1.5 = 4 by 1 instead of by 2 hours.
  • Correct: P′(3)P'(3) is greater: 3 versus an average of 2: The average rate is 5.5−1.53−1=2\frac{5.5 - 1.5}{3 - 1} = 2.
  • They are not comparable, because their units differ: Both are in thousand bacteria per hour.

2. Which statement interprets P′(3)=3P'(3) = 3 correctly?

  1. After 3 hours, there are 3 thousand bacteria
  2. Each hour, the population triples
  3. During the first 3 hours, a total of 3 thousand bacteria were added to the culture
  4. At 3 hours, the population is growing at 3 thousand bacteria per hour
Show the answer

P′(3)=3P'(3) = 3 means that at t=3t = 3 hours the number of bacteria is increasing at 3 thousand bacteria per hour.

  • After 3 hours, there are 3 thousand bacteria: That would mean P(3)=3P(3) = 3; the graph shows P(3)=5.5P(3) = 5.5.
  • Each hour, the population triples: The derivative measures a rate of change in thousands per hour, not a growth factor.
  • During the first 3 hours, a total of 3 thousand bacteria were added to the culture: That is a total change over an interval; P′(3)P'(3) is a rate at one instant.
  • Correct: At 3 hours, the population is growing at 3 thousand bacteria per hour: Instant, quantity, direction and units are all named.

3. Which of these quantities has units of thousands of bacteria, not thousands of bacteria per hour?

  1. P(3)P(3)
  2. P′(3)P'(3)
  3. The slope of the tangent line at t=3t = 3
  4. P(5)−P(3)5−3\frac{P(5) - P(3)}{5 - 3}
Show the answer

Values of PP are counts (thousands); slopes, derivatives and difference quotients are rates (thousands per hour).

  • Correct: P(3)P(3): A value of PP is a number of bacteria, in thousands.
  • P′(3)P'(3): A derivative of PP is a rate, in thousands per hour.
  • The slope of the tangent line at t=3t = 3: A slope on this graph is thousands per hour.
  • P(5)−P(3)5−3\frac{P(5) - P(3)}{5 - 3}: A change in PP divided by a time is a rate.

4. What form does 1−cos⁡xx2\frac{1 - \cos x}{x^2} take as x→0x \to 0?

  1. 10\frac{1}{0}
  2. ∞∞\frac{\infty}{\infty}
  3. 20\frac{2}{0}
  4. 00\frac{0}{0}
Show the answer

As x→0x \to 0, 1−cos⁡x→01 - \cos x \to 0 and x2→0x^2 \to 0: the indeterminate form 00\frac{0}{0}.

  • 10\frac{1}{0}: cos⁡0=1\cos 0 = 1, so the top is 1−1=01 - 1 = 0.
  • ∞∞\frac{\infty}{\infty}: Neither part grows without bound near 0.
  • 20\frac{2}{0}: 1−cos⁡0=01 - \cos 0 = 0, not 2.
  • Correct: 00\frac{0}{0}: 1−cos⁡0=01 - \cos 0 = 0 and 02=00^2 = 0.

5. One application of L'Hospital's rule gives limx→0sin⁡x2x\lim_{x \to 0}\frac{\sin x}{2x}. What should come next?

  1. Still 00\frac{0}{0}, so apply the rule again to get 12\frac{1}{2}
  2. It equals 0, because sin⁡0=0\sin 0 = 0
  3. It does not exist, because the bottom is 0 at x=0x = 0
  4. Use the quotient rule on sin⁡x2x\frac{\sin x}{2x}, then substitute 0
Show the answer

sin⁡0=0\sin 0 = 0 and 2(0)=02(0) = 0: still 00\frac{0}{0}. Apply the rule again (both limits are 0): limx→0cos⁡x2\lim_{x \to 0}\frac{\cos x}{2}=12= \frac{1}{2}.

  • Correct: Still 00\frac{0}{0}, so apply the rule again to get 12\frac{1}{2}: Check the form at every round; a second application finishes it.
  • It equals 0, because sin⁡0=0\sin 0 = 0: The bottom is also 0, so the form is still indeterminate.
  • It does not exist, because the bottom is 0 at x=0x = 0: A zero bottom with a zero top is indeterminate, not a missing limit.
  • Use the quotient rule on sin⁡x2x\frac{\sin x}{2x}, then substitute 0: L'Hospital's rule differentiates top and bottom separately; the quotient rule is not part of it.

6. L'Hospital's rule replaces limf(x)g(x)\lim \frac{f(x)}{g(x)} by which limit?

  1. lim(f(x)g(x))′\lim \left(\frac{f(x)}{g(x)}\right)'
  2. limf′(x)g′(x)\lim \frac{f'(x)}{g'(x)}
  3. limf′(x)g(x)−f(x)g′(x)[g(x)]2\lim \frac{f'(x)g(x) - f(x)g'(x)}{[g(x)]^2}
  4. limg′(x)f′(x)\lim \frac{g'(x)}{f'(x)}
Show the answer

For a 00\frac{0}{0} or ∞∞\frac{\infty}{\infty} form, limf(x)g(x)\lim\frac{f(x)}{g(x)}=limf′(x)g′(x)= \lim\frac{f'(x)}{g'(x)}, when the second limit exists.

  • lim(f(x)g(x))′\lim \left(\frac{f(x)}{g(x)}\right)': That is the derivative of the quotient, a different function.
  • Correct: limf′(x)g′(x)\lim \frac{f'(x)}{g'(x)}: Differentiate the top and the bottom separately.
  • limf′(x)g(x)−f(x)g′(x)[g(x)]2\lim \frac{f'(x)g(x) - f(x)g'(x)}{[g(x)]^2}: That is the quotient rule, which is not part of L'Hospital's rule.
  • limg′(x)f′(x)\lim \frac{g'(x)}{f'(x)}: The derivatives keep their places: f′f' on top.

7. A particle has velocity v(4)=−5v(4) = -5 meters per second. Which is true at t=4t = 4?

  1. It is moving right at a speed of 5 meters per second
  2. It is moving left at a speed of −5-5 meters per second
  3. It is moving left at a speed of 5 meters per second
  4. It is slowing down, because the velocity is negative
Show the answer

The sign of the velocity gives the direction (left), and its absolute value gives the speed (5 m/s).

  • It is moving right at a speed of 5 meters per second: A negative velocity means motion in the negative direction.
  • It is moving left at a speed of −5-5 meters per second: Speed is never negative; the sign belongs to the velocity.
  • Correct: It is moving left at a speed of 5 meters per second: Negative velocity means leftward; speed is |−5|=5|-5| = 5.
  • It is slowing down, because the velocity is negative: Slowing down depends on the signs of both vv and aa.

9. A rectangle has sides xx and yy, both changing with time, and area A=xyA = xy. Which is dAdt\frac{dA}{dt}?

  1. xdydt+ydxdtx\frac{dy}{dt} + y\frac{dx}{dt}
  2. dxdt⋅dydt\frac{dx}{dt} \cdot \frac{dy}{dt}
  3. x+yx + y
  4. xdxdt+ydydtx\frac{dx}{dt} + y\frac{dy}{dt}
Show the answer

Product rule: dAdt=dxdty+xdydt\frac{dA}{dt} = \frac{dx}{dt}y + x\frac{dy}{dt}.

  • Correct: xdydt+ydxdtx\frac{dy}{dt} + y\frac{dx}{dt}: The product rule, with each side's rate of change.
  • dxdt⋅dydt\frac{dx}{dt} \cdot \frac{dy}{dt}: The derivative of a product is not the product of the derivatives.
  • x+yx + y: The rates of the sides must appear.
  • xdxdt+ydydtx\frac{dx}{dt} + y\frac{dy}{dt}: Each side is multiplied by the other side's rate, not its own.

10. A person 6 feet tall walks away from a 15-foot lamppost at 5 feet per second. How fast is the tip of the person's shadow moving along the ground? Include units.

No calculator. Type the exact value (a fraction, π, e or a root is fine), then its unit.

Show the answer

Let xx be the person's distance from the post and ss the shadow's length. Similar triangles: s6=x+s15\frac{s}{6} = \frac{x + s}{15}, so s=2x3s = \frac{2x}{3}. The tip is at x+s=5x3x + s = \frac{5x}{3}, which changes at 53⋅5=253\frac{5}{3} \cdot 5 = \frac{25}{3} feet per second.

  • Answer: 253\frac{25}{3} ≈ 8.333 feet per second

12. The volume of a balloon is V(r)V(r) cubic centimeters when its radius is rr centimeters. What are the units of V′(r)V'(r)?

  1. cubic centimeters
  2. cubic centimeters per centimeter
  3. centimeters per cubic centimeter
  4. cubic centimeters per second
Show the answer

V′(r)=dVdrV'(r) = \frac{dV}{dr} has units of VV over units of rr: cubic centimeters per centimeter.

  • cubic centimeters: Those are the units of V(r)V(r).
  • Correct: cubic centimeters per centimeter: Output units (cm³) per input unit (cm).
  • centimeters per cubic centimeter: That is the reciprocal: the output change goes on top.
  • cubic centimeters per second: The input here is the radius, not time.
