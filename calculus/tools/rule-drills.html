Rule drills

Build derivatives one rule at a time.

About this tool

Every derivative on the exam is built from a handful of rules. This drill takes one function at a time and builds its derivative piece by piece: you name the rule, differentiate each piece, then put them together. Each typed answer is checked by evaluating it, so any correct form counts.

The rule picker in Explore takes any function you type and shows its structure (which rule comes first, what is inside what) and its derivative.

How it works

The function is drawn as a tree: a product splits into its two factors uu and vv, a quotient into its top and bottom, a composition into its outer function and the inner function uu. Each piece lights up with its derivative as you get it right.

  • Power rule ddxxn=nxn−1\frac{d}{dx}x^n = nx^{n-1}; rewrite roots and fractions as powers first.
  • Product rule (uv)′=u′v+uv′(uv)′ = u′v + uv′.
  • Quotient rule (uv)′=u′v−uv′v2\left(\frac{u}{v}\right)′ = \frac{u′v - uv′}{v^2}.
  • Chain rule ddxf(g(x))\frac{d}{dx}f(g(x))=f′(g(x))g′(x)= f′(g(x))\,g′(x): the outer derivative at the inside, times the inside′s derivative.
  • Implicit: differentiate both sides; every yy term gets a factor dydx\frac{dy}{dx}.

Type answers like 3x^2 + 2cos(2x), e^(3x), 1/sqrt(x) or sec(x)^2; the line under the box shows how your answer was read. A wrong answer gets the name of the slip it matches: a forgotten inner derivative, the product rule taken as a product of derivatives, the quotient rule upside down, a missing dy/dx. New kinds appear as you reach the lessons that teach them.
