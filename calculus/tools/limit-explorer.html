Limit explorer

Close in on a point from both sides until the limit is clear.

About this tool

A limit asks one question: as xx gets close to aa, what number does f(x)f(x) get close to? Here the curve is a road. In the Close in on a point tab you drive two cars toward x=ax = a, one from each side; each car's tag reads f(x)f(x) where its nose is, and a magnifier follows them in. In the Out to infinity tab the cars drive far out, and the height levels off.

The curves cover every way a limit can behave: a hole, a point out of place that you can move back, a jump, a vertical asymptote, an oscillation that never settles, the squeeze theorem and limits at infinity. The “?” button holds the table of values, the limits and the continuity check.

How it works

Each car's nose is the point (x,f(x))(x, f(x)) on the road, whose center line is exactly y=f(x)y = f(x). Close in drives both cars to x=a−0.001x = a - 0.001 and x=a+0.001x = a + 0.001; the table behind “?” shows h=1,0.1,0.01,…h = 1, 0.1, 0.01, \ldots from each side.

  • Limit from the left limx→a−f(x)\lim_{x \to a^-} f(x): where f(x)f(x) heads as xx comes up to aa from below (the purple car).
  • Limit from the right limx→a+f(x)\lim_{x \to a^+} f(x): from above (the orange car).
  • The limit limx→af(x)\lim_{x \to a} f(x) exists only when both one-sided limits exist and are equal. It never depends on f(a)f(a).
  • Continuous at aa: f(a)f(a) is defined, the limit exists, and they are equal.
