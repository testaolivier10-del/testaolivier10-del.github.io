Limit explorer

Zoom in on a graph and a table until the limit is clear.

About this tool

A limit asks one question: as xx gets close to aa, what number does f(x)f(x) get close to? Here you zoom in on the graph around x=ax = a while a table of values closes in from both sides, and you watch the two one-sided limits arrive.

The gallery holds every way a limit can behave: a hole, a jump, a vertical asymptote, an oscillation that never settles, a point out of place that you can move back, limits at infinity and the squeeze theorem. The continuity checklist ticks as you zoom.

How it works

Each zoom step is ten times closer. The two tracers sit at x=a−hx = a - h (from the left) and x=a+hx = a + h (from the right), with h=1,0.1,0.01,…h = 1, 0.1, 0.01, \ldots; the table adds a row each time, and those rows are exactly where the tracers pass. Every curve, value and table cell comes from the same formula shown under Numbers & setup.

  • Limit from the left limx→a−f(x)\lim_{x \to a^-} f(x): where f(x)f(x) heads as xx comes up to aa from below.
  • Limit from the right limx→a+f(x)\lim_{x \to a^+} f(x): from above.
  • The limit limx→af(x)\lim_{x \to a} f(x) exists only when both one-sided limits exist and are equal. It never depends on f(a)f(a).
  • Continuous at aa: f(a)f(a) is defined, the limit exists, and they are equal.
