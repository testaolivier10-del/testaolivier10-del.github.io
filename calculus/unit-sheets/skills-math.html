Functions and Their Graphs

A function sends each input to one output. Read it from a formula (substitute with parentheses), a graph (heights, filled and open dots) or a table. Know the domain, write lines in point-slope form, compose functions inside first, move graphs with f(x − h) + k , and find vertical asymptotes where a denominator is 0.

  • A function gives one output for each input. Substitute with parentheses: if f(x)=x2−3xf(x) = x^2 - 3x, then f(a+1)=(a+1)2−3(a+1)f(a + 1) = (a + 1)^2 - 3(a + 1).
  • The domain is every input the rule accepts; the range is every output it produces. Rule out zero denominators and square roots of negatives.
  • On a graph, a filled dot is on the graph and an open circle is not. f(a)f(a) is the height of the graph at x=ax = a.
  • A line through (x1,y1)(x_1, y_1) with slope mm is y−y1=m(x−x1)y - y_1 = m(x - x_1) (point-slope form). Slope is rise over run.
  • f(g(x))f(g(x)) means gg first, then ff. Order matters: usually f(g(x))≠g(f(x))f(g(x)) \ne g(f(x)).
  • A rational function has a vertical asymptote where the denominator is 0 and the numerator is not.

Exponent Rules and Rewriting Powers

Exponent rules let you write every root and fraction as a power: 1/(x^n) = x^(−n) and n√(x^m) = x^(m/n) . Add exponents to multiply, subtract to divide, multiply for a power of a power, and remember an exponent applies only to the base it touches and never splits over a sum.

  • Multiply powers of the same base: add exponents. Divide: subtract. Power of a power: multiply.
  • x−n=1xnx^{-n} = \frac{1}{x^n}. A negative exponent means reciprocal, never a negative number.
  • xm/n=(xn)mx^{m/n} = \left(\sqrt[n]{x}\right)^m. Take the root first to keep numbers small: 82/3=22=48^{2/3} = 2^2 = 4.
  • An exponent applies only to what it touches: 3x−2=3x23x^{-2} = \frac{3}{x^2}, but (3x)−2=19x2(3x)^{-2} = \frac{1}{9x^2}.
  • Exponents do not split over sums: (x+3)−1≠x−1+3−1(x + 3)^{-1} \ne x^{-1} + 3^{-1} and x2+9≠x+3\sqrt{x^2 + 9} \ne x + 3.

so x^m⋅x^n = x^(m + n) , and (x^m)/(x^n) = x^(m − n) → a negative exponent is a reciprocal: x^(−n) = 1/(x^n) → a fractional exponent is a root: x^(1/2) = √x and x^(m/n) = (n√x)^m → expressions like 3/x² + 2√x become 3x⁻² + 2x^(1/2) , ready for calculus

Algebra for Limits: Factoring and Rationalizing

Limits that give zero over zero are settled with precalculus algebra: factor and cancel a common factor, multiply by a conjugate to clear a square root, or combine a complex fraction into one. Cancel factors, never terms, and expand powers in full. The simplified expression agrees with the original everywhere except the troublesome point.

  • Factoring toolkit: common factor; difference of squares a2−b2=(a−b)(a+b)a^2 - b^2 = (a - b)(a + b); trinomials (with the acac method when the leading coefficient is not 1); cubes a3±b3=(a±b)(a2∓ab+b2)a^3 \pm b^3 = (a \pm b)(a^2 \mp ab + b^2).
  • Cancel factors, never terms: x(x−4)(x−4)(x+4)\frac{x(x - 4)}{(x - 4)(x + 4)}=xx+4= \frac{x}{x + 4}, but x2+4x+2\frac{x^2 + 4}{x + 2} does not simplify.
  • Rationalizing: multiply top and bottom by the conjugate (A−B\sqrt{A} - B and A+B\sqrt{A} + B form a pair). Their product is A−B2A - B^2. Leave the other factor unmultiplied.
  • A complex fraction becomes one fraction with a common denominator; then divide.
  • Expand powers fully: (a+h)2=a2+2ah+h2(a + h)^2 = a^2 + 2ah + h^2. The middle term is the one that matters.

Trigonometry and the Unit Circle

Radians measure angles by arc length on the unit circle, where the point at angle θ is (cos θ, sin θ) . Exact values come from the first-quadrant special angles plus the quadrant signs. Tangent, secant, cosecant and cotangent are ratios of sine and cosine, and sin² x + cos² x = 1 links them. Keep calculators in radian mode.

  • Radians: π\pi radians =180∘= 180^{\circ}. To convert degrees to radians, multiply by π180\frac{\pi}{180}. Calculus always uses radians, including on the calculator.
  • On the unit circle, the point at angle θ\theta is (cos⁡θ,sin⁡θ)(\cos\theta, \sin\theta): cosine is the xx-coordinate, sine the yy-coordinate.
  • Know the first-quadrant values at π6,π4,π3\frac{\pi}{6}, \frac{\pi}{4}, \frac{\pi}{3}: 12,22,32\frac{1}{2}, \frac{\sqrt{2}}{2}, \frac{\sqrt{3}}{2}. Other quadrants use a reference angle and a sign.
  • sec⁡=1cos⁡\sec = \frac{1}{\cos}, csc⁡=1sin⁡\csc = \frac{1}{\sin}, cot⁡=cos⁡sin⁡\cot = \frac{\cos}{\sin}. sin2⁡x\sin^2 x means (sin⁡x)2(\sin x)^2.
  • Pythagorean identity: sin2⁡x+cos2⁡x=1\sin^2 x + \cos^2 x = 1; also 1+tan2⁡x=sec2⁡x1 + \tan^2 x = \sec^2 x and sin⁡2x=2sin⁡xcos⁡x\sin 2x = 2\sin x\cos x.

Exponentials and Logarithms

e^x is the exponential function calculus uses; it is always positive and passes through (0, 1) . ln x , defined for x > 0 , undoes it: e^(ln x) = x and ln (e^x) = x . Logs turn products into sums, quotients into differences and powers into multipliers, which is how you solve for a variable in an exponent.

  • ex>0e^x > 0 for every xx; e0=1e^0 = 1. The graph rises steeply to the right and approaches the xx-axis to the left.
  • ln⁡x\ln x is defined only for x>0x > 0; ln⁡1=0\ln 1 = 0, ln⁡e=1\ln e = 1. Its graph is the graph of exe^x reflected across y=xy = x.
  • Log rules: ln⁡(ab)=ln⁡a+ln⁡b\ln(ab) = \ln a + \ln b, ln⁡ab=ln⁡a−ln⁡b\ln\frac{a}{b} = \ln a - \ln b, ln⁡(ap)=pln⁡a\ln(a^p) = p\ln a. There is no rule for ln⁡(a+b)\ln(a + b).
  • ee and ln⁡\ln cancel: eln⁡x=xe^{\ln x} = x (x>0x > 0) and ln⁡(ex)=x\ln(e^x) = x. Use them to solve equations.

Inverse Functions

An inverse function runs a one-to-one function backward: f⁻¹(b) = a exactly when f(a) = b . Its graph is the reflection of f across y = x , so points swap coordinates and slopes become reciprocals. Find a formula by solving x = f(y) for y . The inverse trig functions use restricted ranges, and f⁻¹ never means 1/f .

  • f−1(b)=af^{-1}(b) = a means f(a)=bf(a) = b. So f(f−1(x))=xf(f^{-1}(x)) = x and f−1(f(x))=xf^{-1}(f(x)) = x on the right domains.
  • f−1(x)f^{-1}(x) is not 1f(x)\frac{1}{f(x)}. The −1-1 means "undo", not "reciprocal".
  • Only a one-to-one function (each output from one input; passes the horizontal line test) has an inverse. Restrict the domain if needed.
  • To find a formula: write x=f(y)x = f(y) and solve for yy.
  • arcsin⁡x\arcsin x and arctan⁡x\arctan x return angles in [−π2,π2]\left[-\frac{\pi}{2}, \frac{\pi}{2}\right]; arccos⁡x\arccos x returns angles in [0,π][0, \pi].

the inverse function f⁻¹ sends b back to a : f⁻¹(b) = a exactly when f(a) = b → swaps the coordinates of every point, reflecting the graph across y = x → slopes at matching points are reciprocals → their inverses use restricted ranges: arcsine and arctangent give angles from −π/2 to π/2, arccosine from 0 to π

Geometry for Calculus

Calculus word problems start with a geometric equation. Right angles call for the Pythagorean theorem; nested triangles call for similar triangles and a ratio; shapes call for their area or volume formulas. Label changing quantities with letters, fixed ones with numbers, and use a ratio to reduce a formula to one variable.

  • Pythagorean theorem: in a right triangle with legs aa, bb and hypotenuse cc, a2+b2=c2a^2 + b^2 = c^2. The hypotenuse is opposite the right angle.
  • Similar triangles have equal angles, so their sides are in proportion: ab=AB\frac{a}{b} = \frac{A}{B}. Look for a small triangle inside a big one.
  • Know the area and volume formulas: circle πr2\pi r^2, semicircle 12πr2\frac{1}{2}\pi r^2, trapezoid b1+b22h\frac{b_1 + b_2}{2}h, cylinder πr2h\pi r^2 h, cone 13πr2h\frac{1}{3}\pi r^2 h, sphere 43πr3\frac{4}{3}\pi r^3.
  • Draw the picture, label what changes with letters and what is fixed with numbers, then write one equation.

the Pythagorean theorem links the sides: x² + y² = z² → similar triangles: corresponding sides are in the same ratio, such as r/h = 4/10 → a formula like V = 1/3 πr²h becomes a function of one variable, V = 4π/75 h³
