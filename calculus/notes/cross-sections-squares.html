Volumes with Square and Rectangle Cross Sections

4 min read · freeNot practiced

Share to Google Classroom (opens in a new tab)

Volume by slicing

You know how to find the volume of a box: area of the face times thickness. A loaf of bread is not a box, but each thin slice of it almost is. Its face has some area AA and its thickness is small, so the slice's volume is about AA times the thickness. Add up the slices and you have the loaf.

Calculus makes that exact. Put the solid along the xx-axis and slice it with planes perpendicular to the axis. The slice at position xx is a flat shape, a cross section, with area A(x)A(x). A slab of thickness Δx\Delta x there has volume about A(x)ΔxA(x)\,\Delta x. Adding the slabs is a Riemann sum for AA, and letting them get thin gives

V=∫abA(x)dx.V = \int_a^b A(x)\,dx.

Everything in this topic, and in the next one, comes down to finding A(x)A(x).

Squares standing on a base

A common way to describe a solid: draw a region on the floor (the base), and stand a shape of a given kind on every line across it. In the figure, the base is the region under y=4−x2y = 4 - x^2, and on each segment perpendicular to the xx-axis stands a square. The segment is one side of the square.

The side of each square is the width of the base across that slice. For a base between a top curve y=f(x)y = f(x) and a bottom curve y=g(x)y = g(x), the side is top minus bottom, exactly the height you used for area between curves:

s(x)=f(x)−g(x),A(x)=s(x)2=(f(x)−g(x))2.s(x) = f(x) - g(x), \qquad A(x) = s(x)^2 = \left(f(x) - g(x)\right)^2.

Worked example. The base of a solid is the region under y=4−x2y = 4 - x^2 and above the xx-axis. Cross sections perpendicular to the xx-axis are squares. Find the volume.

Limits. 4−x2=04 - x^2 = 0 at x=±2x = \pm 2.

Side. s=(4−x2)−0=4−x2s = (4 - x^2) - 0 = 4 - x^2, so A(x)A(x) =(4−x2)2= (4 - x^2)^2 =16−8x2+x4= 16 - 8x^2 + x^4.

Volume.

V=∫−22(16−8x2+x4)dx=2[16x−8x33+x55]02=2(32−643+325)=51215.V = \int_{-2}^{2} \left(16 - 8x^2 + x^4\right)\,dx = 2\left[16x - \frac{8x^3}{3} + \frac{x^5}{5}\right]_0^2 = 2\left(32 - \frac{64}{3} + \frac{32}{5}\right) = \frac{512}{15}.

The integrand is even, so the integral from −2-2 to 22 is twice the integral from 00 to 22.

Square the difference, not each curve

The most common mistake is writing f(x)2−g(x)2f(x)^2 - g(x)^2. That is not the area of anything here. The square's side is the gap between the curves, and you square the gap.

Worked example. The base is the region between y=xy = \sqrt{x} and y=x2y = \frac{x}{2}. Cross sections perpendicular to the xx-axis are squares. Find the volume.

Limits. x=x2\sqrt{x} = \frac{x}{2} at x=0x = 0 and x=4x = 4; between them x\sqrt{x} is on top (at x=1x = 1: 1>121 > \frac{1}{2}).

Area. A(x)A(x) =(x−x2)2= \left(\sqrt{x} - \frac{x}{2}\right)^2 =x−x3/2+x24= x - x^{3/2} + \frac{x^2}{4}.

Volume.

V=∫04(x−x3/2+x24)dx=[x22−25x5/2+x312]04=8−645+163=815.V = \int_0^4 \left(x - x^{3/2} + \frac{x^2}{4}\right)\,dx = \left[\frac{x^2}{2} - \frac{2}{5}x^{5/2} + \frac{x^3}{12}\right]_0^4 = 8 - \frac{64}{5} + \frac{16}{3} = \frac{8}{15}.

Squaring each curve first would give ∫04(x−x24)dx\int_0^4 \left(x - \frac{x^2}{4}\right)\,dx =83= \frac{8}{3}, five times too big.

If the slices are perpendicular to the yy-axis, slice the base horizontally: the side is right minus left, written in yy, and the integral is in dydy between yy-values.

Units follow the formula: a side in feet gives an area in square feet, and integrating over feet gives cubic feet. There is no π\pi anywhere, because squares and rectangles have none in their area formulas, even when the base has curved edges.

With a calculator, find the base's crossings numerically and store them, write ∫ab(f(x)−g(x))2dx\int_a^b \left(f(x) - g(x)\right)^2\,dx as your setup, and let the calculator do the integral. Report three decimal places.
