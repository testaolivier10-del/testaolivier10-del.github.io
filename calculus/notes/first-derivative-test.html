The First Derivative Test

4 min read · freeNot practiced

Share to Google Classroom (opens in a new tab)

You now know where a function rises and where it falls. Put two intervals side by side and a peak or valley appears between them. The First Derivative Test is that observation, stated carefully enough to use as a justification. It is one of the most scored sentences on the whole exam.

The test

Let cc be a critical point of ff, and let ff be continuous at cc.

  • If f′f' changes from positive to negative at cc, then ff has a relative maximum at cc. The graph rises up to cc, then falls.
  • If f′f' changes from negative to positive at cc, then ff has a relative minimum at cc. The graph falls, then rises.
  • If f′f' does not change sign at cc, then ff has no relative extremum at cc. The graph keeps going the same way.

Notice what the test does not use: the value f′(c)f'(c). It works the same at a point where f′(c)=0f'(c) = 0 and at a corner or cusp where f′(c)f'(c) does not exist. What matters is the sign just before and just after.

Sign charts

A sign chart is a number line marked with the critical points and the sign of f′f' on each interval between them. It is a good way to organize the work:

Worked example 1. Find the relative extrema of f(x)=x3−3x2−9x+4f(x) = x^3 - 3x^2 - 9x + 4.

f′(x)f'(x) =3x2−6x−9= 3x^2 - 6x - 9 =3(x−3)(x+1)= 3(x - 3)(x + 1), zero at x=−1x = -1 and x=3x = 3.

Test values: f′(−2)=15>0f'(-2) = 15 > 0, f′(0)=−9<0f'(0) = -9 < 0, f′(4)=15>0f'(4) = 15 > 0. Sign chart: ++, then −- after −1-1, then ++ after 33.

At x=−1x = -1, f′f' changes from positive to negative: relative maximum, f(−1)=−1−3+9+4=9f(-1) = -1 - 3 + 9 + 4 = 9.

At x=3x = 3, f′f' changes from negative to positive: relative minimum, f(3)=27−27−27+4=−23f(3) = 27 - 27 - 27 + 4 = -23.

But the chart is scratch work. The scoring guidelines do not accept a sign chart by itself as a justification. Write the sentence: "f′f' changes from positive to negative at x=−1x = -1, so ff has a relative maximum there."

Points where f′f' does not exist

Worked example 2. f(x)=x5/3−5x2/3f(x) = x^{5/3} - 5x^{2/3}. Find its relative extrema.

f′(x)f'(x) =53x2/3−103x−1/3= \frac{5}{3}x^{2/3} - \frac{10}{3}x^{-1/3} =5x−103x1/3= \frac{5x - 10}{3x^{1/3}} =5(x−2)3x1/3= \frac{5(x - 2)}{3x^{1/3}}.

Critical points: x=2x = 2 (numerator zero) and x=0x = 0 (f′f' undefined; ff is defined and continuous there).

Signs: for x<0x < 0, both x−2x - 2 and x1/3x^{1/3} are negative, so f′>0f' > 0. For 0<x<20 < x < 2, f′<0f' < 0. For x>2x > 2, f′>0f' > 0.

So ff has a relative maximum at x=0x = 0 (f′f' changes from positive to negative at a cusp) and a relative minimum at x=2x = 2.

Zeros that are not extrema

A squared factor in f′f' is the usual reason a critical point is not an extremum. For f(x)=x4−4x3f(x) = x^4 - 4x^3:

f′(x)=4x3−12x2=4x2(x−3)f'(x) = 4x^3 - 12x^2 = 4x^2(x - 3)

The factor 4x24x^2 is never negative, so the sign of f′f' is the sign of x−3x - 3. At x=0x = 0, f′f' is negative on both sides: no extremum, even though f′(0)=0f'(0) = 0. At x=3x = 3, f′f' changes from negative to positive: a relative minimum.

Reading the test from a graph of f′f'

What the graph of f′f' does at x=cx = cWhat ff has at x=cx = c
Crosses the axis going down (++ to −-)relative maximum
Crosses the axis going up (−- to ++)relative minimum
Touches the axis and turns back (no sign change)no extremum
Has a peak or valley above or below the axisno extremum (that is about concavity)

The sentence to write

Name the function, name the point, give the direction of the change:

"f′f' changes from negative to positive at x=5x = 5, so ff has a relative minimum at x=5x = 5."

Answers that lose the point: "f′(5)=0f'(5) = 0" (no sign change), "it changes sign" (which function? which direction?), or "the graph crosses the axis" (which graph?). And remember this test is local: it finds relative extrema, not absolute ones. For the absolute maximum on an interval, you need the next topic.
