What a Limit Is and How to Write It

5 min read · freeNot practiced

Share to Google Classroom (opens in a new tab)

In the last topic, the average speeds of a falling stone over shorter and shorter intervals closed in on 32 feet per second: 48, 40, 33.6, 32.16, 32.016. We kept saying "closes in on." Calculus gives that phrase a name, the limit, and a notation that you will write thousands of times this year. This page is about what a limit means and how to write it so that it earns credit.

What a limit says

Take a function ff and a number aa. Let xx get closer and closer to aa, from both sides, without ever being equal to aa. Watch the outputs f(x)f(x). If they close in on one number LL, as close as you like, then LL is the limit of f(x)f(x) as xx approaches aa. We write limx→af(x)=L\lim_{x \to a} f(x) = L and read it as "the limit of ff of xx, as xx approaches aa, equals LL."

Three parts, each with a job:

  • "lim" says we are asking about a trend, not a single value.
  • "x→ax \to a" underneath says where the input is heading.
  • "=L= L" says where the outputs are heading.

Worked example 1. Write the falling-stone result from Topic 1.1 in limit notation.

The average speed over [1,1+h][1, 1 + h] was 32+16h32 + 16h feet per second, and we let hh shrink toward 0. So the input is hh, heading to 0:

limh→0(32+16h)=32\lim_{h \to 0} (32 + 16h) = 32

In words: as the interval length hh approaches 0, the average speed approaches 32 feet per second. That limit is the speed at the instant t=1t = 1.

The value at the point does not matter

This is the most important fact about limits, and the most surprising. The definition never lets xx equal aa. So the limit cannot depend on f(a)f(a).

In the figure, the curve heads to the open circle at (2,4)(2, 4) from both sides, so limx→2g(x)=4\lim_{x \to 2} g(x) = 4. The function's actual value at 2 is the filled dot: g(2)=1g(2) = 1. Both statements are true at once. Three situations come up again and again:

At x=ax = a the graph haslimx→af(x)\lim_{x \to a} f(x)f(a)f(a)
an unbroken curve through (a,L)(a, L)LLLL (they agree)
a hole at (a,L)(a, L) and a dot at (a,M)(a, M)LLMM
a hole at (a,L)(a, L) and no dotLLnot defined

Why build calculus on an idea that ignores the point itself? Because of Topic 1.1. At the instant t=1t = 1, the average-speed formula is zero divided by zero: it has no value at all. The limit is exactly the tool that looks at nearby inputs and never at the problem point.

Three ways to find a limit

A limit can be read from three kinds of information, and the next topics teach each one:

  • A graph (Topic 1.3): follow the heights as xx slides toward aa from the left and from the right.
  • A table (Topic 1.4): look at outputs for inputs like 1.9, 1.99, 1.999 and 2.1, 2.01, 2.001.
  • A formula (Topics 1.5 and 1.6): use rules and algebra to find the limit exactly.

Sometimes the outputs do not close in on a single number. The two sides might head to different heights, or the outputs might grow without stopping. Then there is no limit; Topic 1.3 shows how to recognize each case.

Reading limits in context

When the function describes something real, a limit statement becomes a sentence about a trend. If V(t)V(t) is the volume of water in a tank, in liters, tt hours after noon, then limt→3V(t)=12\lim_{t \to 3} V(t) = 12 means: as the time gets closer and closer to 3 p.m., the volume of water gets closer and closer to 12 liters. A careful interpretation names the quantity, its units and the input it approaches. It does not claim that V(3)=12V(3) = 12, because the limit statement alone does not say that.

Writing limits the way readers expect

On free-response questions, the notation is part of the answer. Habits that keep your work correct:

  • Keep "limx→a\lim_{x \to a}" in front of the expression on every line until the line where you actually get a number. Then drop it.
  • Put parentheses around a sum or difference after "lim": "limx→25+x\lim_{x \to 2} 5 + x" is ambiguous, while "limx→2(5+x)=7\lim_{x \to 2} (5 + x) = 7" is clear.
  • Use the equals sign only between things that are equal. Writing f(2)=4f(2) = 4 when you mean limx→2f(x)=4\lim_{x \to 2} f(x) = 4 is a different, and often false, statement.
  • Name the function: "limx→2g(x)=4\lim_{x \to 2} g(x) = 4", not "the limit is 4."

Worked example 2. A graph of ff heads to height 6 from both sides as xx approaches 3, and has a filled dot at (3,8)(3, 8). Write what you know.

Limit: limx→3f(x)=6\lim_{x \to 3} f(x) = 6. Value: f(3)=8f(3) = 8.

So limx→3f(x)≠f(3)\lim_{x \to 3} f(x) \ne f(3). Writing "f(3)=6f(3) = 6" would be wrong.

Going further. Mathematicians make "as close as we like" exact with two small numbers, ε\varepsilon and δ\delta: for every ε>0\varepsilon > 0 there is a δ>0\delta > 0 such that |f(x)−L|<ε|f(x) - L| < \varepsilon whenever 0<|x−a|<δ0 < |x - a| < \delta. The exam does not test this definition, but it is the idea behind every limit in the course.
