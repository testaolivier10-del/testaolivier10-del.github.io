Average and Instantaneous Rates of Change

6 min read · freeNot practiced

Share to Google Classroom (opens in a new tab)

Unit 1 asked whether change can happen at an instant and built the tool to answer it: the limit. This page puts that tool to work. You will measure change over an interval, shrink the interval, and let a limit finish the job. Everything in Unit 2 grows out of the one formula you meet here.

Average rate of change: a change over a change

Suppose a delivery drone is 14 meters above the street at t=2t = 2 seconds and 47 meters up at t=6t = 6 seconds. It climbed 47−14=3347 - 14 = 33 meters in 6−2=46 - 2 = 4 seconds, so on average it climbed 334=8.25\frac{33}{4} = 8.25 meters per second. That number is an average rate of change.

For any function ff on an interval [a,b][a, b]:

average rate of change=f(b)−f(a)b−a.\text{average rate of change} = \frac{f(b) - f(a)}{b - a}.

Three habits keep this right every time:

  • Subtract in the same order on top and bottom. Later value minus earlier value, over later time minus earlier time.
  • Divide by the change in input. The difference f(b)−f(a)f(b) - f(a) is how much ff changed; it is not yet a rate.
  • Name the units. The units are output units per input unit: meters per second, gallons per minute, dollars per item.

An average describes a whole interval. The drone might have climbed fast at first and slowly later; 8.25 meters per second hides that. It also does not tell you how high the drone was. A sentence that interprets an average rate names the quantity, its units and the interval: "Between t=2t = 2 and t=6t = 6 seconds, the drone's altitude increased by an average of 8.25 meters per second."

Worked example 1. The volume of water in a tank is V(t)V(t) gallons after tt minutes, with V(3)=120V(3) = 120 and V(8)=95V(8) = 95. Find the average rate of change of VV on [3,8][3, 8] and interpret it.

Change in volume: V(8)−V(3)=95−120=−25V(8) - V(3) = 95 - 120 = -25 gallons. Change in time: 8−3=58 - 3 = 5 minutes.

Average rate: −255=−5\frac{-25}{5} = -5 gallons per minute.

Meaning: between t=3t = 3 and t=8t = 8 minutes, the volume of water decreased by an average of 5 gallons per minute. The negative sign means the tank was losing water.

The picture: a secant line

Plot the two points (a,f(a))(a, f(a)) and (b,f(b))(b, f(b)) on the graph of ff and draw the line through them. That line is a secant line. Its slope is rise over run, f(b)−f(a)b−a\frac{f(b) - f(a)}{b - a}, which is exactly the average rate of change. So every average rate you compute is the slope of a secant line, and every secant slope you read from a graph is an average rate.

Shrinking the interval: the difference quotient

To learn how fast ff changes at one input x=ax = a, look at shorter and shorter intervals that start at aa. Write the far end as a+ha + h, where hh is the width of the interval. The average rate on that interval is the difference quotient:

f(a+h)−f(a)h.\frac{f(a + h) - f(a)}{h}.

The number hh can be positive (an interval to the right of aa) or negative (an interval to the left). Look at the figure: for f(x)=x24+1f(x) = \frac{x^2}{4} + 1 and a=1a = 1, the secant slopes for h=3,2,1,0.4h = 3, 2, 1, 0.4 are 1.25,1,0.75,0.61.25, 1, 0.75, 0.6. As hh shrinks, the second point slides toward PP and the secant lines pivot toward one line. Their slopes close in on a single number.

The instantaneous rate of change is a limit

You cannot simply put h=0h = 0 into the difference quotient: that gives f(a)−f(a)0=00\frac{f(a) - f(a)}{0} = \frac{0}{0}, an indeterminate form. Instead, take the limit. The instantaneous rate of change of ff at x=ax = a is

limh→0f(a+h)−f(a)h,\lim_{h \to 0} \frac{f(a + h) - f(a)}{h},

when that limit exists. The method is the limit algebra of Unit 1: expand, simplify until the factor hh cancels, then let h→0h \to 0.

Worked example 2. Find the instantaneous rate of change of f(x)=x24+1f(x) = \frac{x^2}{4} + 1 at x=1x = 1.

Values: f(1)=14+1=54f(1) = \frac{1}{4} + 1 = \frac{5}{4} and f(1+h)f(1 + h)=(1+h)24+1= \frac{(1 + h)^2}{4} + 1=1+2h+h24+1= \frac{1 + 2h + h^2}{4} + 1.

Difference: f(1+h)−f(1)=2h+h24f(1 + h) - f(1) = \frac{2h + h^2}{4}. Keep the middle term 2h2h when you square 1+h1 + h; dropping it is the most common slip.

Quotient, for h≠0h \ne 0: 2h+h24h\frac{2h + h^2}{4h}=h(2+h)4h= \frac{h(2 + h)}{4h}=2+h4= \frac{2 + h}{4}.

Limit: limh→02+h4\lim_{h \to 0} \frac{2 + h}{4}=24= \frac{2}{4}=12= \frac{1}{2}. The rate of change of ff at x=1x = 1 is 12\frac{1}{2}, the number the secant slopes in the figure were closing in on.

Worked example 3. A cart rolls so that it has traveled s(t)=5t2s(t) = 5t^2 meters after tt seconds. How fast is it moving at t=2t = 2?

Difference quotient: s(2+h)−s(2)h\frac{s(2 + h) - s(2)}{h}=5(4+4h+h2)−20h= \frac{5(4 + 4h + h^2) - 20}{h}=20h+5h2h= \frac{20h + 5h^2}{h}=20+5h= 20 + 5h.

For h=0.1h = 0.1 the average is 20.5 meters per second; for h=0.01h = 0.01 it is 20.05. The limit is limh→0(20+5h)=20\lim_{h \to 0} (20 + 5h) = 20, so at the instant t=2t = 2 the cart is moving at 20 meters per second.

Both rates have the same units. The difference is what they describe: a stretch of time, or one moment.

Common mistakes

  • A value over a time is not a rate. The drone's altitude at t=5t = 5 divided by 5 ignores where it started. A rate always compares two changes.
  • Substituting h=0h = 0 too early. That gives 00\frac{0}{0}, which says only that more algebra is needed.
  • Losing the middle term. (a+h)2=a2+2ah+h2(a + h)^2 = a^2 + 2ah + h^2.
  • Treating an average as an instant. An average on [2,6][2, 6] is not the rate at t=4t = 4.
