Area Between Curves (Functions of y)

4 min read · freeNot practiced

Share to Google Classroom (opens in a new tab)

Turning the strips sideways

In the last topic every rectangle stood up: it had width Δx\Delta x and reached from a bottom curve to a top curve. That works well when the top is one curve and the bottom is one curve all the way across. Some regions are not built like that.

Look at the region between the parabola x=y2x = y^2 and the line x=y+2x = y + 2 in the figure. Slice it vertically and the bottom of each strip is the lower half of the parabola for 0≤x≤10 \le x \le 1, but the line for 1≤x≤41 \le x \le 4. You would need two integrals and the formulas y=−xy = -\sqrt{x} and y=xy = \sqrt{x}. Slice it horizontally instead, and every strip runs from the parabola on the left to the line on the right. One formula, one integral.

Right minus left

A horizontal strip sits at height yy and has a small thickness Δy\Delta y. Its length is the distance from its left end to its right end, which is a difference of xx-values:

length=xright(y)−xleft(y).\text{length} = x_{\text{right}}(y) - x_{\text{left}}(y).

Each strip has area (xright−xleft)Δy\left(x_{\text{right}} - x_{\text{left}}\right)\Delta y. Stack the strips from the bottom of the region to the top and let them get thin:

A=∫cd(xright(y)−xleft(y))dy.A = \int_c^d \left(x_{\text{right}}(y) - x_{\text{left}}(y)\right)\,dy.

This is the same idea as top minus bottom, turned 90 degrees. The "larger" function is now the one farther to the right.

Two things change with the variable. The edges must be written as xx in terms of yy, and the limits are yy-values. If a curve is given as y=xy = \sqrt{x}, solve for xx (here x=y2x = y^2 with y≥0y \ge 0). That is just the inverse function.

Worked example. Find the area of the region between x=y2x = y^2 and x=y+2x = y + 2.

Limits. Set the edges equal: y2=y+2y^2 = y + 2, so y2−y−2=0y^2 - y - 2 = 0 and (y−2)(y+1)=0(y - 2)(y + 1) = 0. The curves meet at y=−1y = -1 and y=2y = 2. (The points are (1,−1)(1, -1) and (4,2)(4, 2), but the limits are their yy-coordinates.)

Right edge. Test y=0y = 0: the line gives x=2x = 2 and the parabola gives x=0x = 0. The line is on the right.

Integral.

A=∫−12[(y+2)−y2]dy=[y22+2y−y33]−12=103−(−76)=92.A = \int_{-1}^{2} \left[(y + 2) - y^2\right]\,dy = \left[\frac{y^2}{2} + 2y - \frac{y^3}{3}\right]_{-1}^{2} = \frac{10}{3} - \left(-\frac{7}{6}\right) = \frac{9}{2}.

With vertical strips you would need ∫012xdx+∫14(x−(x−2))dx\int_0^1 2\sqrt{x}\,dx + \int_1^4 \left(\sqrt{x} - (x - 2)\right)\,dx=43+196= \frac{4}{3} + \frac{19}{6}, which is also 92\frac{9}{2}. Same area, twice the work.

Choosing xx or yy

Look at the regionSliceIntegrand
One curve on top and one on the bottom all the way acrossVertical, in dxdxtop minus bottom
One curve on the left and one on the right all the way upHorizontal, in dydyright minus left
Curves given as x=g(y)x = g(y)Usually horizontalright minus left
A curve that is hard to solve for xxUsually verticaltop minus bottom

Either choice gives the same area if you set it up correctly. Pick the one with fewer pieces and easier antiderivatives.

Worked example. Find the area of the region bounded by y=ln⁡xy = \ln x, the yy-axis, y=0y = 0 and y=2y = 2.

Rewrite. y=ln⁡xy = \ln x means x=eyx = e^y. The left edge is the yy-axis, x=0x = 0.

Limits. The region runs from y=0y = 0 to y=2y = 2; they are given.

Integral. AA=∫02(ey−0)dy= \int_0^2 \left(e^y - 0\right)\,dy=e2−e0= e^2 - e^0=e2−1= e^2 - 1. In xx this region would need xx from 0 to e2e^2 and a top edge that changes at x=1x = 1: much harder.

Common slips

  • Limits in the wrong variable. In dydy the limits are yy-values. Writing the xx-coordinates of the crossings as limits gives a wrong number.
  • Left minus right. That gives a negative number. Test a yy between the limits in both edges; the larger xx is the right edge.
  • Edges not rewritten. x\sqrt{x} inside an integral in dydy makes no sense. Every edge must be xx in terms of yy.
  • Parentheses. (2−y)−(y2−4)(2 - y) - (y^2 - 4)=6−y−y2= 6 - y - y^2: subtract every term of the left edge.

With a calculator, solve for the crossing yy-values numerically (radian mode for trigonometry), store them, write the setup with dydy, and evaluate the integral numerically. Give the result correct to three decimal places.
