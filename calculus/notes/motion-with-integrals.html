SMath you need

In Unit 4 you started from a position function x(t)x(t) and differentiated: velocity v=x′v = x', acceleration a=v′a = v'. Now run the film backward. Given the velocity, integrals recover how far the particle moved and where it is. Two different questions hide here, and the exam asks both: how far did it end up from its start, and how far did it actually travel?

Displacement: the integral of velocity

Because vv is the derivative of xx, the Fundamental Theorem gives

∫abv(t)dt=x(b)−x(a).\int_a^b v(t)\,dt = x(b) - x(a).

This net change in position is the displacement on [a,b][a, b]. On a velocity graph it is the signed area: area above the tt-axis (moving right, or up, or forward) counts as positive, area below (moving left) counts as negative. So a trip right followed by an equal trip left has displacement 0.

Total distance: the integral of speed

A car's odometer does not subtract when you drive back home. Speed is |v(t)||v(t)|, and the total distance traveled is

∫ab|v(t)|dt.\int_a^b |v(t)|\,dt.

On the graph, that is the sum of the areas, every one counted as positive.

Worked example 1 (no calculator). A particle moves with v(t)=t2−4t+3v(t) = t^2 - 4t + 3 meters per second for 0≤t≤40 \le t \le 4. Find its displacement and its total distance.

Antiderivative: F(t)=t33−2t2+3tF(t) = \frac{t^3}{3} - 2t^2 + 3t.

Displacement: F(4)−F(0)F(4) - F(0)=643−32+12= \frac{64}{3} - 32 + 12=43= \frac{4}{3} meters.

Distance: v=(t−1)(t−3)v = (t - 1)(t - 3) changes sign at t=1t = 1 and t=3t = 3, so split there. F(1)−F(0)=43F(1) - F(0) = \frac{4}{3}; F(3)−F(1)F(3) - F(1)=0−43= 0 - \frac{4}{3}=−43= -\frac{4}{3}; F(4)−F(3)=43F(4) - F(3) = \frac{4}{3}.

Add the sizes: 43+43+43=4\frac{4}{3} + \frac{4}{3} + \frac{4}{3} = 4 meters. The particle traveled 4 m but ended only 43\frac{4}{3} m to the right of where it began.

Without a calculator you cannot integrate |v||v| directly. Find where vv changes sign, integrate vv on each piece, and add the absolute values. Check every zero, not just whole-number times.

Position from a starting point

Displacement tells you the change. To know the position itself, you need one known position, then add the change:

x(t)=x(t0)+∫t0tv(s)ds.x(t) = x(t_0) + \int_{t_0}^{t} v(s)\,ds.

Worked example 2 (graph). A particle's velocity is made of segments through (0,0)(0, 0), (2,4)(2, 4), (5,4)(5, 4), (8,−2)(8, -2) and (10,−2)(10, -2), in m/s, and x(0)=−3x(0) = -3. Find x(10)x(10), the total distance, and the time it is farthest right.

Areas: 4 on [0,2][0, 2], 12 on [2,5][2, 5], 4 on [5,7][5, 7] (where vv reaches 0), then 1 and 4 below the axis on [7,8][7, 8] and [8,10][8, 10].

x(10)x(10)=−3= -3+(4+12+4−1−4)+ (4 + 12 + 4 - 1 - 4)=−3= -3+15+ 15=12= 12 m. Total distance: 4+12+4+1+4=254 + 12 + 4 + 1 + 4 = 25 m.

The particle moves right while v>0v > 0 and left after vv changes sign at t=7t = 7, so it is farthest right at t=7t = 7: x(7)=−3+20=17x(7) = -3 + 20 = 17 m.

The same idea gives velocity from acceleration: v(t)v(t)=v(t0)= v(t_0)+∫t0ta(s)ds+ \int_{t_0}^{t} a(s)\,ds. With a(t)=6ta(t) = 6t and v(0)=−3v(0) = -3, v(2)=−3+12=9v(2) = -3 + 12 = 9 m/s.

With a calculator

When the velocity has no easy antiderivative, the calculator does the integral, and it can integrate |v||v| directly. Write the setup every time, then the value to three decimal places:

  • Displacement: ∫06(4−t+2sin⁡t)dt\int_0^6 (4 - t + 2\sin t)\,dt≈6.080\approx 6.080 feet.
  • Total distance: ∫06|4−t\int_0^6 |4 - t+2sin⁡t|dt+ 2\sin t|\,dt≈17.430\approx 17.430 feet.
  • Position, if x(0)=5x(0) = 5: x(6)x(6)=5= 5+∫06v(t)dt+ \int_0^6 v(t)\,dt≈11.080\approx 11.080 feet.

Radian mode, and keep full precision until the final answer.

Common slips

  • Giving the displacement when the question asks for total distance, or the reverse.
  • Forgetting the initial position, or starting the integral at 0 when the known position is at another time.
  • Putting the absolute value outside: |∫vdt|\left|\int v\,dt\right| is the size of the displacement, not the distance.
  • Units: the integral of feet per second over seconds is feet.
