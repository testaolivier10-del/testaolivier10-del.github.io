Same method, new slice

In the last topic you built solids by standing squares on a base and found VV=∫abA(x)dx= \int_a^b A(x)\,dx with A(x)=s2A(x) = s^2. Nothing about that method depended on the slices being squares. Stand triangles on the base, or half circles, and the volume is still the integral of the slice area. The only new work is the area formula for one slice.

The side ss is found exactly as before: it is the length of the base across the slice, s=f(x)−g(x)s = f(x) - g(x) for slices perpendicular to the xx-axis (or right minus left in yy for slices perpendicular to the yy-axis).

Equilateral triangles

An equilateral triangle with side ss has its peak above the middle of the base. Cut it down the middle and you get a right triangle with hypotenuse ss and short leg s2\frac{s}{2}. By the Pythagorean theorem, the height is

h=s2−(s2)2=32s,A=12⋅s⋅32s=34s2.h = \sqrt{s^2 - \left(\frac{s}{2}\right)^2} = \frac{\sqrt{3}}{2}s, \qquad A = \frac{1}{2} \cdot s \cdot \frac{\sqrt{3}}{2}s = \frac{\sqrt{3}}{4}s^2.

Worked example. The base of a solid is the region under y=xy = \sqrt{x} for 0≤x≤40 \le x \le 4. Cross sections perpendicular to the xx-axis are equilateral triangles. Find the volume.

Side. s=x−0=xs = \sqrt{x} - 0 = \sqrt{x}.

Area. A(x)A(x)=34(x)2= \frac{\sqrt{3}}{4}\left(\sqrt{x}\right)^2=34x= \frac{\sqrt{3}}{4}x.

Volume. VV=∫0434xdx= \int_0^4 \frac{\sqrt{3}}{4}x\,dx=34⋅x22|04= \frac{\sqrt{3}}{4} \cdot \frac{x^2}{2}\Big|_0^4=34⋅8= \frac{\sqrt{3}}{4} \cdot 8=23= 2\sqrt{3}.

Squares on the same base would give ∫04xdx\int_0^4 x\,dx=8= 8. The triangles keep 34≈0.433\frac{\sqrt{3}}{4} \approx 0.433 of that, which makes sense: a triangle fills less than half of the square on the same side.

Semicircles

When the slices are semicircles, the segment across the base is the diameter. The radius is half of it, s2\frac{s}{2}, and the area is half a circle:

A=12π(s2)2=π8s2.A = \frac{1}{2}\pi\left(\frac{s}{2}\right)^2 = \frac{\pi}{8}s^2.

Worked example. Same base, but the cross sections are semicircles with diameters in the base.

Area. A(x)=π8(x)2=π8xA(x) = \frac{\pi}{8}\left(\sqrt{x}\right)^2 = \frac{\pi}{8}x.

Volume. VV=π8∫04xdx= \frac{\pi}{8}\int_0^4 x\,dx=π8⋅8= \frac{\pi}{8} \cdot 8=π= \pi.

If you had used ss as the radius you would get π2⋅8=4π\frac{\pi}{2} \cdot 8 = 4\pi, four times too big. Halve the diameter before you square it.

Every area is a constant times s2s^2, so on one base every volume is that constant times ∫abs2dx\int_a^b s^2\,dx. Compute ∫s2\int s^2 once and you can answer for any shape. When the hypotenuse lies in the base, each leg is s2\frac{s}{\sqrt{2}}, so the area is 12(s2)2=s24\frac{1}{2}\left(\frac{s}{\sqrt{2}}\right)^2 = \frac{s^2}{4}.

Slips to watch for

  • 32s\frac{\sqrt{3}}{2}s is the triangle's height, not its area. The area has 34\frac{\sqrt{3}}{4}.
  • The side across the base is the semicircle's diameter. Radius =s2= \frac{s}{2}.
  • Square the whole side (f−g)(f - g), never each curve separately.
  • For slices perpendicular to the yy-axis, ss is right minus left in yy, and the integral is in dydy.
