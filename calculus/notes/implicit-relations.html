Behavior of Implicit Relations

4 min read · freeNot practiced

Share to Google Classroom (opens in a new tab)

A circle, an oval or a looping curve fails the vertical line test, so it is not the graph of a function y=f(x)y = f(x). You already know how to find its slope with implicit differentiation. This topic asks what that slope formula tells you about the shape: where the curve is flat, where it stands straight up, and where it has a local high or low point.

The slope formula as a fraction

Differentiate both sides of the curve's equation with respect to xx, write "=0= 0" (or the derivative of the right side), and solve. The result usually looks like a fraction in both xx and yy:

dydx=N(x,y)D(x,y)\frac{dy}{dx} = \frac{N(x, y)}{D(x, y)}

Worked example 1. Find dydx\frac{dy}{dx} for x2−xy+y2=12x^2 - xy + y^2 = 12.

ddx\frac{d}{dx} of both sides: 2x−(y+xdydx)+2ydydx2x - \left(y + x\frac{dy}{dx}\right) + 2y\frac{dy}{dx}=0= 0. (The product rule on xyxy gives two terms.)

Collect the dydx\frac{dy}{dx} terms: dydx(2y−x)=y−2x\frac{dy}{dx}(2y - x) = y - 2x, so dydx=y−2x2y−x\frac{dy}{dx} = \frac{y - 2x}{2y - x}.

Horizontal and vertical tangent lines

A horizontal tangent needs slope 0, so the numerator must be 0 while the denominator is not. A vertical tangent line needs the slope to be undefined because the denominator is 0 while the numerator is not. In both cases the point must actually be on the curve.

Worked example 2. Find the points of x2−xy+y2=12x^2 - xy + y^2 = 12 with horizontal and with vertical tangent lines.

Horizontal: y−2x=0y - 2x = 0, so y=2xy = 2x. Substitute into the curve: x2−2x2+4x2=12x^2 - 2x^2 + 4x^2 = 12, so 3x2=123x^2 = 12 and x=±2x = \pm 2. Points (2,4)(2, 4) and (−2,−4)(-2, -4); the denominator there is 66 and −6-6, not 0.

Vertical: 2y−x=02y - x = 0, so x=2yx = 2y. Substitute: 4y2−2y2+y2=124y^2 - 2y^2 + y^2 = 12, so y=±2y = \pm 2. Points (4,2)(4, 2) and (−4,−2)(-4, -2); the numerator there is −6-6 and 66, not 0.

The substitution step is the one students skip. The point (1,2)(1, 2) makes the numerator y−2xy - 2x zero, but 1−2+4=3≠121 - 2 + 4 = 3 \ne 12, so it is not on the curve, and its "slope" means nothing.

When both parts are zero

For the curve x3+y3=6xyx^3 + y^3 = 6xy, implicit differentiation gives dydx=2y−x2y2−2x\frac{dy}{dx} = \frac{2y - x^2}{y^2 - 2x}. At the origin, which is on the curve, both the numerator and the denominator are 0. The formula is undefined and decides nothing; this curve actually crosses itself there. Never write "00=0\frac{0}{0} = 0" or "00=1\frac{0}{0} = 1": that is a notation error.

High and low points of a curve

Near a point with a horizontal tangent, the curve is the graph of a function of xx, so the Second Derivative Test still works. Find d2ydx2\frac{d^2y}{dx^2} by differentiating the fraction for dydx\frac{dy}{dx} (quotient rule, treating yy as a function of xx), then substitute the point and the value dydx=0\frac{dy}{dx} = 0.

Worked example 3. For x2−xy+y2=12x^2 - xy + y^2 = 12, find d2ydx2\frac{d^2y}{dx^2} at (2,4)(2, 4).

d2ydx2\frac{d^2y}{dx^2}=(dydx−2)(2y−x)−(y−2x)(2dydx−1)(2y−x)2= \frac{\left(\frac{dy}{dx} - 2\right)(2y - x) - (y - 2x)\left(2\frac{dy}{dx} - 1\right)}{(2y - x)^2}.

At (2,4)(2, 4), dydx=0\frac{dy}{dx} = 0 and y−2x=0y - 2x = 0: (−2)(6)−036=−13\frac{(-2)(6) - 0}{36} = -\frac{1}{3}.

A horizontal tangent where the curve is concave down: (2,4)(2, 4) is a local high point of the curve, and in fact its highest point.

What earns the points

  • Apply ddx\frac{d}{dx} to both sides and keep "=0= 0" on the right.
  • Use the product rule on every xyxy-type term and the chain rule on every power of yy.
  • For a vertical tangent, say "the denominator is 0 and the numerator is not," never "dydx=0\frac{dy}{dx} = 0."
  • Substitute candidate points back into the curve's equation.
