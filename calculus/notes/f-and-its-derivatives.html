This topic pulls Unit 5 together. You are given one of ff, f′f' or f″f'', as a graph, a table or a formula, and asked about the others: where ff rises, where it peaks, how it bends, whether a tangent line over- or underestimates. Every year one free-response question is built this way around a graph of f′f'. The skill is graphical analysis: reading the right feature of the right function and saying so by name.

Always ask which function the given information is about. "Positive" means something different for ff, f′f' and f″f''.

Working from a graph of f′f'

Worked example 1. The graph of f′(x)=x3−3x2+2f'(x) = x^3 - 3x^2 + 2 on [−1,3.2][-1, 3.2] has a high point at x=0x = 0, a low point at x=2x = 2, and crosses the axis at x≈−0.73x \approx -0.73, x=1x = 1 and x≈2.73x \approx 2.73. Also f(0)=4f(0) = 4.

Relative maximum of ff: at x=1x = 1, because f′f' changes from positive to negative there.

Concave up: on (−1,0)(-1, 0) and (2,3.2)(2, 3.2), because f′f' is increasing there.

Inflection points: at x=0x = 0 and x=2x = 2, because f′f' changes from increasing to decreasing at 0 and from decreasing to increasing at 2.

At x=2x = 2: f′(2)=−2<0f'(2) = -2 < 0, so ff is decreasing there, and it has a point of inflection.

Tangent line at x=0x = 0: point (0,4)(0, 4), slope f′(0)=2f'(0) = 2, so y=4+2xy = 4 + 2x.

Notice the pattern in every justification: it names f′f' and says what f′f' does. "The graph changes direction at 1" names nothing and earns nothing.

Working from words

Contexts describe derivatives in words. Translate before you calculate:

  • "Increasing at a decreasing rate": f′>0f' > 0 and f″<0f'' < 0.
  • "Decreasing, but more and more slowly": f′<0f' < 0 and f″>0f'' > 0.
  • "The rate of change is greatest": f′f' has a maximum, which is where f″f'' changes from positive to negative, an inflection point of ff.
  • For motion, position ss: velocity s′s' and acceleration s″s''. With s′>0s' > 0 and s″<0s'' < 0, the object moves forward and slows down.

A global fact from f″f''

Sometimes the sign of f″f'' holds everywhere, and that gives a global answer. If f″(x)<0f''(x) < 0 for all xx and f′(2)=0f'(2) = 0, then f′f' is decreasing on the whole line: positive before 2 and negative after. So ff rises to x=2x = 2 and falls after, and f(2)f(2) is the absolute maximum, not just a relative one.

The two classic swaps

  • Zeros versus turns. On a graph of f′f', zeros (where it crosses the axis) are candidates for extrema of ff; turns (its peaks and valleys) are inflection points of ff.
  • Sign versus direction. The sign of f′f' gives the direction of ff; the direction of f′f' gives the concavity of ff. "f is increasing because f′f' is increasing" mixes them up.
