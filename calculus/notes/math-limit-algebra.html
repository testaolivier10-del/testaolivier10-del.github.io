Algebra for Limits: Factoring and Rationalizing

4 min read · freeNot practiced

Share to Google Classroom (opens in a new tab)

Here is the kind of limit you will meet again and again: limx→3x2−9x−3\lim_{x \to 3} \frac{x^2 - 9}{x - 3}. Substitute x=3x = 3 and both the top and the bottom are 0. The limit laws cannot help, because the denominator heads to 0. The way forward is to rewrite the fraction into a form where the trouble disappears. That takes three precalculus moves, and this page drills each one: factoring, rationalizing and simplifying complex fractions.

Factoring

To factor is to rewrite a sum as a product. A factored form shows what each piece is multiplied by, and that is what lets pieces cancel. The patterns you need:

PatternExample
Common factorx2−4x=x(x−4)x^2 - 4x = x(x - 4)
Difference of squares: a2−b2=(a−b)(a+b)a^2 - b^2 = (a - b)(a + b)x2−16=(x−4)(x+4)x^2 - 16 = (x - 4)(x + 4)
Trinomial x2+bx+cx^2 + bx + c: two numbers that multiply to cc and add to bbx2+2x−8=(x+4)(x−2)x^2 + 2x - 8 = (x + 4)(x - 2)
Difference of cubes: a3−b3a^3 - b^3=(a−b)(a2+ab+b2)= (a - b)(a^2 + ab + b^2)x3−8=(x−2)(x2+2x+4)x^3 - 8 = (x - 2)(x^2 + 2x + 4)
Sum of cubes: a3+b3a^3 + b^3=(a+b)(a2−ab+b2)= (a + b)(a^2 - ab + b^2)x3+1=(x+1)(x2−x+1)x^3 + 1 = (x + 1)(x^2 - x + 1)

A useful shortcut: if a polynomial is 0 at x=ax = a, then x−ax - a is one of its factors. So when substitution gives 0 on top at x=3x = 3, you already know to look for the factor x−3x - 3.

Worked example 1. Factor 2x2+5x−32x^2 + 5x - 3. The leading coefficient is not 1, which is where most errors happen.

Multiply a⋅c=2⋅(−3)=−6a \cdot c = 2 \cdot (-3) = -6. Find two numbers that multiply to −6-6 and add to b=5b = 5: they are 6 and −1-1.

Split the middle term and group: 2x2+6x−x−32x^2 + 6x - x - 3=2x(x+3)−1(x+3)= 2x(x + 3) - 1(x + 3)=(2x−1)(x+3)= (2x - 1)(x + 3).

Check by expanding: (2x−1)(x+3)(2x - 1)(x + 3)=2x2+6x−x−3= 2x^2 + 6x - x - 3=2x2+5x−3= 2x^2 + 5x - 3.

Cancel factors, never terms

Canceling divides the top and bottom by the same nonzero factor. A term that is added cannot be canceled. This is the single most common algebra error on limit questions.

When in doubt, test a number. At x=1x = 1, x2−4xx2−16\frac{x^2 - 4x}{x^2 - 16}=−3−15= \frac{-3}{-15}=15= \frac{1}{5}. The wrong answer x4\frac{x}{4} gives 14\frac{1}{4}; the right answer xx+4\frac{x}{x + 4} gives 15\frac{1}{5}.

Rationalizing with a conjugate

When a square root sits next to a number, as in x−2\sqrt{x} - 2, factoring does not apply directly. Instead multiply by its conjugate, the same expression with the opposite sign in the middle: x+2\sqrt{x} + 2. A conjugate pair multiplies to a difference of squares, and the square root disappears: (x−2)(x+2)=(x)2−22=x−4(\sqrt{x} - 2)(\sqrt{x} + 2) = (\sqrt{x})^2 - 2^2 = x - 4 To keep the fraction's value, multiply the top and the bottom by the conjugate. This is called rationalizing.

Worked example 2. Simplify x−2x−4\frac{\sqrt{x} - 2}{x - 4} for x≠4x \ne 4.

x−2x−4⋅x+2x+2=x−4(x−4)(x+2)=1x+2\frac{\sqrt{x} - 2}{x - 4} \cdot \frac{\sqrt{x} + 2}{\sqrt{x} + 2} = \frac{x - 4}{(x - 4)(\sqrt{x} + 2)} = \frac{1}{\sqrt{x} + 2}

Notice the bottom was not multiplied out. Leaving it as (x−4)(x+2)(x - 4)(\sqrt{x} + 2) is what makes the factor x−4x - 4 visible for canceling.

Near x=4x = 4, the simplified form heads to 14+2=14\frac{1}{\sqrt{4} + 2} = \frac{1}{4}.

Complex fractions

A complex fraction has fractions inside its numerator or denominator. Combine the small fractions over a common denominator first, then divide.

Worked example 3. Simplify 1x+2−12x\frac{\frac{1}{x + 2} - \frac{1}{2}}{x} for x≠0,−2x \ne 0, -2.

Top, over the common denominator 2(x+2)2(x + 2): 2−(x+2)2(x+2)\frac{2 - (x + 2)}{2(x + 2)}=−x2(x+2)= \frac{-x}{2(x + 2)}. The parentheses around x+2x + 2 matter: the minus sign applies to both terms.

Divide by xx, which is the same as multiplying by 1x\frac{1}{x}: −x2(x+2)⋅1x\frac{-x}{2(x + 2)} \cdot \frac{1}{x}=−12(x+2)= \frac{-1}{2(x + 2)}.

Near x=0x = 0, this heads to −14\frac{-1}{4}.

Expanding powers

Limits often contain expressions like (3+h)2−9(3 + h)^2 - 9. Expand every power completely before simplifying: (3+h)2−9=9+6h+h2−9=6h+h2=h(6+h)(3 + h)^2 - 9 = 9 + 6h + h^2 - 9 = 6h + h^2 = h(6 + h) Forgetting the middle term, 6h6h, is the classic slip; (a+h)2(a + h)^2 is never a2+h2a^2 + h^2. For a cube, (a+h)3(a + h)^3=a3+3a2h+3ah2+h3= a^3 + 3a^2h + 3ah^2 + h^3.

In every one of these moves, the goal is the same: produce a factor that matches the troublesome one in the denominator, so it can cancel. The next topic puts these moves to work on limits.
