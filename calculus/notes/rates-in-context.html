Motion is one story the derivative tells. It tells many others: how fast a medicine leaves the blood, how fast a town grows, how much one more kilogram of coffee adds to a roaster's cost. The calculus is identical in every case. What you must get right is the language: which quantity, increasing or decreasing, how fast, and in what units. This page also handles the second derivative in context, the rate at which a rate changes.

One pattern, many settings

QuantityIts derivative meansUnits of the derivative
temperature H(t)H(t), °C, tt in minuteshow fast it warms or cools°C per minute
population P(t)P(t), people, tt in yearshow fast the town growspeople per year
cost C(x)C(x), dollars, xx in kilogramscost of one more kilogram (approximately)dollars per kilogram

The sentence is always built the same way: "At [input value], [the quantity] is increasing (or decreasing) at [the size of the derivative] [units]."

Worked example 1. A town's population is P(t)=500e0.02tP(t) = 500e^{0.02t} people, tt years after 2020. Find and interpret P′(10)P'(10).

By the chain rule, P′(t)P'(t)=500⋅0.02e0.02t= 500 \cdot 0.02\,e^{0.02t}=10e0.02t= 10e^{0.02t}, so P′(10)=10e0.2≈12.2P'(10) = 10e^{0.2} \approx 12.2.

In 2030 the population is increasing at about 12.2 people per year.

Worked example 2. The concentration of a medicine is C(t)=8tt2+4C(t) = \frac{8t}{t^2 + 4} mg/L, tt hours after a dose. Find and interpret C′(1)C'(1) and C′(3)C'(3).

Quotient rule: C′(t)C'(t)=8(t2+4)−8t⋅2t(t2+4)2= \frac{8(t^2 + 4) - 8t \cdot 2t}{(t^2 + 4)^2}=32−8t2(t2+4)2= \frac{32 - 8t^2}{(t^2 + 4)^2}.

C′(1)=2425=0.96C'(1) = \frac{24}{25} = 0.96: one hour after the dose, the concentration is increasing at 0.96 mg/L per hour.

C′(3)C'(3)=32−72169= \frac{32 - 72}{169}=−40169= -\frac{40}{169}≈−0.24\approx -0.24: after three hours it is decreasing at about 0.24 mg/L per hour.

Rates of rates

A derivative is a function, so it has its own rate of change, the second derivative. In context, its units carry the input unit twice: if HH is in °C and tt in minutes, then H″(t)H''(t) is in °C per minute per minute.

Read the two signs separately. The sign of H′H' says whether the temperature rises or falls. The sign of H″H'' says whether that rate is growing or shrinking:

Q′Q'Q″Q''In words
++++increasing, faster and faster
++−-increasing, more and more slowly
−-−-decreasing, faster and faster
−-++decreasing, more and more slowly

Worked example 3. Soup cools by H(t)=20+70e−0.04tH(t) = 20 + 70e^{-0.04t} °C. Describe the cooling at t=10t = 10.

H′(t)=−2.8e−0.04tH'(t) = -2.8e^{-0.04t}, so H′(10)≈−1.877H'(10) \approx -1.877 °C per minute: the soup is cooling.

H″(t)=0.112e−0.04tH''(t) = 0.112e^{-0.04t}, so H″(10)≈0.075H''(10) \approx 0.075 °C per minute per minute: positive, so the rate of cooling is easing off.

Together: at 10 minutes the soup is cooling at about 1.88 °C per minute, and the cooling is slowing down.

Watch for a function that is already a rate. If water leaks out of a tank at L(t)L(t) liters per hour, then L′(3)=−2L'(3) = -2 means the leak rate is decreasing at 2 liters per hour per hour. It says nothing directly about the amount of water left.

Chained quantities

Sometimes one quantity depends on another that depends on time. A balloon's temperature reading depends on its altitude, and its altitude depends on time. The chain rule multiplies the rates, and the units cancel the same way:

ddtT(h(t))=T′(h(t))⋅h′(t),°Ffoot⋅feetminute=°Fminute\frac{d}{dt}T(h(t)) = T'(h(t)) \cdot h'(t), \qquad \frac{\text{°F}}{\text{foot}} \cdot \frac{\text{feet}}{\text{minute}} = \frac{\text{°F}}{\text{minute}}

Checking that the units cancel is a fast way to catch a setup error.

Slips that cost points

  • Reporting the amount (H(10)H(10)) when the question asks for its rate (H′(10)H'(10)).
  • Giving a second derivative the units of the first ("°C per minute" for H″H'').
  • Reading the sign of Q″Q'' as whether QQ itself increases.
  • Dropping the chain rule factor in models like e−0.04te^{-0.04t}, which also flips the sign.
  • On a calculator question, rounding before the end, or giving fewer than three decimal places.
