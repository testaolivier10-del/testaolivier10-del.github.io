Introduction to Optimization

Optimization finds the absolute maximum or minimum of a quantity in context.

Practice 1: Implementing Mathematical ProcessesPractice 3: Justification

Part 1 · Hook

Why this matters

A farmer with a fixed roll of fence wants the biggest pen; a theater wants the ticket price that brings in the most money. Both are asking for the absolute maximum of a function. The calculus is the tools you already have; the new skill is turning the words into that function.

Part 5 · Step by step

How it works, step by step

  1. A problem asks for the largest or smallest of something→name that quantity and write the objective function for it
  2. The objective function has two variables→use the given fact (a fixed perimeter, a fixed product) to write one variable in terms of the other
  3. The variable must make physical sense→find the domain: lengths and amounts are not negative
  4. Find the critical points and compare (or use one-critical-point reasoning)→the absolute extremum, justified, answered in context with units

Part 6 · Key ideas

Key ideas

  • Optimization: finding the absolute maximum or minimum of a quantity in context.
  • The objective function is the quantity to optimize, written in one variable with a domain.
  • Justify the absolute extremum: Candidates Test on a closed interval, or one critical point that is a relative extremum.
  • Answer the question asked: the dimensions or the price, with units, not just xx.

Part 7 · Misconception

A common mistake

The wrong idea: Find where the derivative is zero, and that is the answer.

What actually happens: A critical point is only a candidate. It could be a minimum when you want a maximum (the shipping cost above), or an endpoint could win. Justify that it is the absolute extremum, and answer in context.

A two-part pen against a river

A farmer has 600 feet of fence to build a rectangular pen against a straight river, split in two by a middle fence parallel to the ends. No fence is needed along the river. Each end and the divider has length xx feet; the side opposite the river has length yy feet.

1. Which function gives the area of the pen in terms of xx alone?

  1. A(x)=x(600−2x)A(x) = x(600 - 2x)
  2. A(x)=x(600−3x)A(x) = x(600 - 3x)
  3. A(x)=x(300−3x2)A(x) = x\left(300 - \frac{3x}{2}\right)
  4. A(x)=3x+yA(x) = 3x + y
Show the answer

Fence: two ends and a divider (3x3x) plus one long side (yy): 3x+y=6003x + y = 600, so y=600−3xy = 600 - 3x. Area A=xy=x(600−3x)A = xy = x(600 - 3x).

  • A(x)=x(600−2x)A(x) = x(600 - 2x): That forgets the middle fence: there are three pieces of length xx.
  • Correct: A(x)=x(600−3x)A(x) = x(600 - 3x): The fence is 3x+y=6003x + y = 600, so y=600−3xy = 600 - 3x and A=xyA = xy.
  • A(x)=x(300−3x2)A(x) = x\left(300 - \frac{3x}{2}\right): That fences the river side too (3x+2y=6003x + 2y = 600).
  • A(x)=3x+yA(x) = 3x + y: 3x+y3x + y is the length of fence, and it still has two variables.

  2. 0≤x≤6000 \le x \le 600
  3. 0≤x≤3000 \le x \le 300
  4. x≥0x \ge 0
Show the answer

Both x≥0x \ge 0 and y=600−3x≥0y = 600 - 3x \ge 0, so 0≤x≤2000 \le x \le 200. (The ends give zero area, so the maximum is inside.)

  • Correct: 0≤x≤2000 \le x \le 200: y=600−3x≥0y = 600 - 3x \ge 0 needs x≤200x \le 200; lengths cannot be negative.

  • x≥0x \ge 0: For x>200x > 200, yy would be negative.

5. Which argument shows that x=100x = 100 gives the absolute maximum area on [0,200][0, 200]?

  1. A′(100)=0A'(100) = 0, so A(100)A(100) is the largest area
  2. AA is continuous on [0,200][0, 200], and A(0)=0A(0) = 0, A(100)=30,000A(100) = 30{,}000, A(200)=0A(200) = 0: the largest candidate is A(100)A(100)
  3. A(100)>0A(100) > 0, so it is the maximum
  4. x=100x = 100 is the midpoint of [0,200][0, 200]
Show the answer

The candidates are x=0x = 0, x=200x = 200 (endpoints) and x=100x = 100 (the critical point). A(100)=30,000A(100) = 30{,}000 is the largest, so it is the absolute maximum.

  • A′(100)=0A'(100) = 0, so A(100)A(100) is the largest area: A critical point alone could be a minimum or neither.
  • Correct: AA is continuous on [0,200][0, 200], and A(0)=0A(0) = 0, A(100)=30,000A(100) = 30{,}000, A(200)=0A(200) = 0: the largest candidate is A(100)A(100): A Candidates Test: endpoints and the one critical point.
  • A(100)>0A(100) > 0, so it is the maximum: Many areas are positive; being positive does not make one largest.
  • x=100x = 100 is the midpoint of [0,200][0, 200]: The midpoint has no special meaning; it happens to work here.

6. A student finds that a cost function C(x)C(x) on (0,∞)(0, \infty) has one critical point, x=4x = 4, and that C″(x)>0C''(x) > 0 for all x>0x > 0. What can she conclude?

  1. C(4)C(4) is a relative minimum, but a lower cost may exist
  2. C(4)C(4) is the absolute minimum cost
  3. C(4)C(4) is the absolute maximum cost
  4. Nothing, because the interval has no endpoints
Show the answer

C″(4)>0C''(4) > 0 makes x=4x = 4 a relative minimum, and it is the only critical point on the interval, so C(4)C(4) is the absolute minimum.

  • C(4)C(4) is a relative minimum, but a lower cost may exist: With one critical point and concave up everywhere, nothing can be lower.
  • Correct: C(4)C(4) is the absolute minimum cost: One critical point that is a relative minimum on an interval gives the absolute minimum there.
  • C(4)C(4) is the absolute maximum cost: Concave up at a flat point is a minimum.
  • Nothing, because the interval has no endpoints: The one-critical-point argument works on open intervals.
