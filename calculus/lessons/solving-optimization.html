Solving Optimization Problems

Solve an optimization problem in a fixed order: draw and label, write the objective function, use the constraint to reduce it to one variable, find the domain, find the critical points, justify the absolute extremum with candidates or one-critical-point reasoning, and answer in context with units.

Part 1 · Hook

Why this matters

Packaging engineers squeeze the most volume out of a sheet of cardboard; utility planners route cable to spend the least money. Both run the same five-step routine: draw it, name the quantity, use the constraint, differentiate, and prove the answer is the best one.

Part 5 · Step by step

How it works, step by step

  1. Draw the situation and label the variables→you can see which quantities are fixed and which change
  2. Write the objective function and the constraint→the constraint lets you eliminate a variable: the objective becomes a function of one variable
  3. Find the domain from the physical limits→you know the endpoints and which critical points count
  4. Differentiate, solve, and compare candidates (or use one-critical-point reasoning)→the absolute extremum, justified, stated in context with units

Part 6 · Key ideas

Key ideas

  • Constraint: a fixed condition (a perimeter, a volume, a budget) that links the variables. Use it to write the objective in one variable.
  • Steps: draw, label, objective, constraint, one variable, domain, critical points, justify, answer in context.
  • Reject critical points outside the domain (the cut x=203x = \frac{20}{3} inches on a 10-inch side).
  • Calculator problems: solve C′(x)=0C'(x) = 0 with the solver or a graph, store the value, and compare candidates.

Part 7 · Misconception

A common mistake

The wrong idea: The objective function and the constraint are the same equation.

What actually happens: The constraint is the fixed fact (volume =32= 32); the objective is what you optimize (surface area). Using the constraint as if it were the objective, or forgetting to substitute it, is the most common setup error.

6. An open box with a square base of side xx and height hh must hold 32 cubic feet. You want to use the least material. Which is the constraint?

  1. S=x2+4xhS = x^2 + 4xh
  2. x2+4xh=32x^2 + 4xh = 32
  3. 4x+h=324x + h = 32
  4. x2h=32x^2 h = 32
Show the answer

The constraint is the fixed fact, the volume x2h=32x^2 h = 32; it lets you write h=32x2h = \frac{32}{x^2} and the objective S=x2+4xhS = x^2 + 4xh in one variable.

  • S=x2+4xhS = x^2 + 4xh: That is the objective function, the amount of material to minimize.
  • x2+4xh=32x^2 + 4xh = 32: 32 is a volume, not a surface area.
  • 4x+h=324x + h = 32: That mixes lengths; 32 is a volume.
  • Correct: x2h=32x^2 h = 32: The fixed volume links xx and hh.
