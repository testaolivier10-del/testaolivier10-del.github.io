Unit 8 · Topic 8.2 Beta

Motion with Integrals

Integrate velocity for displacement, x(b) − x(a) ; integrate speed |v| for total distance.

Practice 1: Implementing Mathematical ProcessesPractice 3: Justification

Part 1 · Hook

Why this matters

A runner jogs 3 km out along a trail and 3 km back. Her watch says she ran 6 km; her displacement is zero, because she ended where she started. Both numbers come from the same velocity graph. Integrate velocity and you get the change in position; integrate speed and you get the distance covered.

Part 5 · Step by step

How it works, step by step

  1. v(t)=x′(t)v(t) = x'(t)→∫abv(t)dt\int_a^b v(t)\,dt=x(b)−x(a)= x(b) - x(a), the displacement
  2. Area below the t-axis counts as negative in that integral→motion to the left cancels motion to the right
  3. Speed is |v(t)||v(t)|→∫ab|v(t)|dt\int_a^b |v(t)|\,dt counts every stretch as positive: the total distance
  4. The position at one time x(a)x(a) is known→x(t)x(t)=x(a)= x(a)+∫atv(s)ds+ \int_a^t v(s)\,ds gives the position at any time

Part 6 · Key ideas

Key ideas

  • Displacement on [a,b][a, b]: ∫abv(t)dt\int_a^b v(t)\,dt=x(b)−x(a)= x(b) - x(a). Its sign gives the direction of the net change.
  • Total distance traveled: ∫ab|v(t)|dt\int_a^b |v(t)|\,dt. Without a calculator, split at the zeros of vv and add the sizes of the pieces.
  • Position: x(t)x(t)=x(t0)= x(t_0)+∫t0tv(s)ds+ \int_{t_0}^{t} v(s)\,ds. The initial position is not optional.
  • Velocity from acceleration works the same way: v(t)v(t)=v(t0)= v(t_0)+∫t0ta(s)ds+ \int_{t_0}^{t} a(s)\,ds.
  • Units: integrating meters per second over seconds gives meters.

Part 7 · Misconception

A common mistake

The wrong idea: The total distance on [0,4][0, 4] for v(t)=t2−4t+3v(t) = t^2 - 4t + 3 is ∫04v(t)dt\int_0^4 v(t)\,dt=43= \frac{4}{3} m.

What actually happens: That is the displacement. v<0v < 0 on (1,3)(1, 3), so the particle moves left and that stretch subtracts. Total distance: ∫04|v(t)|dt\int_0^4 |v(t)|\,dt=43= \frac{4}{3}+43+ \frac{4}{3}+43+ \frac{4}{3}=4= 4 m.

4. Which expression gives the total distance a particle with velocity v(t)v(t) travels from t=1t = 1 to t=4t = 4?

  1. |∫14v(t)dt|\left|\int_1^4 v(t)\,dt\right|
  2. ∫14v(t)dt\int_1^4 v(t)\,dt
  3. ∫14v′(t)dt\int_1^4 v'(t)\,dt
  4. ∫14|v(t)|dt\int_1^4 |v(t)|\,dt
Show the answer

Total distance =∫14|v(t)|dt= \int_1^4 |v(t)|\,dt; the absolute value goes inside the integral.

  • |∫14v(t)dt|\left|\int_1^4 v(t)\,dt\right|: That is the size of the displacement; back-and-forth motion cancels inside.
  • ∫14v(t)dt\int_1^4 v(t)\,dt: That is the displacement.
  • ∫14v′(t)dt\int_1^4 v'(t)\,dt: That is v(4)−v(1)v(4) - v(1), a change in velocity.
  • Correct: ∫14|v(t)|dt\int_1^4 |v(t)|\,dt: The integral of speed counts every stretch as positive.

Part 9 · Summary

Summary

Integrate velocity for displacement, x(b)−x(a)x(b) - x(a); integrate speed |v||v| for total distance. To find a position, start from a known position and add the integral of velocity. Split at the zeros of vv when working by hand, and always state units.
