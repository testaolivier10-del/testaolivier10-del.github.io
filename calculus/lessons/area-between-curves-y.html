Unit 8 · Topic 8.5 Beta

Area Between Curves (Functions of y)

When a region is easier to cut sideways, write its edges as x in terms of y and integrate right minus left from the lowest y to the highest: A = ∫ from c to d of (x_right − x_left) dy .

Question set for this topic

Part 1 · Hook

Why this matters

Some regions are awkward to slice top to bottom. A pond whose banks are a parabola opening sideways and a straight wall has a bottom edge that switches curves halfway across. Turn your knife sideways: every horizontal strip runs from the same left bank to the same right bank, and one integral does the job.

Part 5 · Step by step

How it works, step by step

  1. Write each edge of the region as xx in terms of yy→a horizontal strip at height yy runs from xleft(y)x_{\text{left}}(y) to xright(y)x_{\text{right}}(y)
  2. The strip has length right minus left and thickness Δy\Delta y→its area is (xright−xleft)Δy\left(x_{\text{right}} - x_{\text{left}}\right)\Delta y
  3. Stack the strips from the bottom of the region to the top→the sum becomes ∫cd(xright−xleft)dy\int_c^d \left(x_{\text{right}} - x_{\text{left}}\right)\,dy
  4. The integral is in dydy→the limits cc and dd are the yy-values where the region starts and ends

Part 6 · Key ideas

Key ideas

  • Integrating with respect to yy: area =∫cd(xright(y)−xleft(y))dy= \int_c^d \left(x_{\text{right}}(y) - x_{\text{left}}(y)\right)\,dy, right minus left.
  • Rewrite each curve as x=x = (something in yy) first: y=xy = \sqrt{x} becomes x=y2x = y^2, y=ln⁡xy = \ln x becomes x=eyx = e^y.
  • Limits are yy-values. Find them by setting the two xx-expressions equal and solving for yy.
  • Choose yy when each side edge is one formula but the top or bottom edge changes partway across; slicing in xx would take two integrals.
  • Both ways give the same area when done correctly.

Part 7 · Misconception

A common mistake

The wrong idea: For an integral in dydy, you can keep the xx-values of the crossing points as the limits.

What actually happens: The limits belong to the variable of integration. With dydy, use the yy-coordinates of the crossings: for x=y2x = y^2 and x=y+2x = y + 2 that is y=−1y = -1 to y=2y = 2, not x=1x = 1 to x=4x = 4.

Check yourself

The region RR between x=y22x = \frac{y^2}{2} and x=y+4x = y + 4

1. Which integral gives the area of RR?

  1. ∫−24[(y+4)−y22]dy\int_{-2}^{4} \left[(y + 4) - \frac{y^2}{2}\right]\,dy
  2. ∫−24[y22−(y+4)]dy\int_{-2}^{4} \left[\frac{y^2}{2} - (y + 4)\right]\,dy
  3. ∫08[2x−(x−4)]dx\int_{0}^{8} \left[\sqrt{2x} - (x - 4)\right]\,dx
  4. ∫28[(y+4)−y22]dy\int_{2}^{8} \left[(y + 4) - \frac{y^2}{2}\right]\,dy
Show the answer

Slice horizontally: each strip runs from the parabola on the left to the line on the right, so its length is right minus left, and yy runs from −2-2 to 44.

  • Correct: ∫−24[(y+4)−y22]dy\int_{-2}^{4} \left[(y + 4) - \frac{y^2}{2}\right]\,dy: Right minus left, in dydy, between the yy-values where the curves meet.
  • ∫−24[y22−(y+4)]dy\int_{-2}^{4} \left[\frac{y^2}{2} - (y + 4)\right]\,dy: Left minus right gives a negative number. The line x=y+4x = y + 4 is the right edge.
  • ∫08[2x−(x−4)]dx\int_{0}^{8} \left[\sqrt{2x} - (x - 4)\right]\,dx: With vertical slices the bottom edge changes at x=2x = 2: from x=0x = 0 to 22 the bottom is y=−2xy = -\sqrt{2x}, not y=x−4y = x - 4. One integral in xx is not enough.
  • ∫28[(y+4)−y22]dy\int_{2}^{8} \left[(y + 4) - \frac{y^2}{2}\right]\,dy: 2 and 8 are the xx-coordinates of the crossings. An integral in dydy needs the yy-coordinates, −2-2 and 44.

2. Find the area of RR.

No calculator. Type the exact value (a fraction, π, e or a root is fine).

Show the answer

∫−24(y+4−y22)dy\int_{-2}^{4} \left(y + 4 - \frac{y^2}{2}\right)\,dy=[y22+4y−y36]−24= \left[\frac{y^2}{2} + 4y - \frac{y^3}{6}\right]_{-2}^{4}=403−(−143)= \frac{40}{3} - \left(-\frac{14}{3}\right)=18= 18.

  • Answer: 18

3. A thin horizontal strip of RR sits at height yy, for −2≤y≤4-2 \le y \le 4. Write its length L(y)L(y).

Type an expression for L(y)=L(y) =.

Show the answer

The right edge is x=y+4x = y + 4 and the left edge is x=y22x = \frac{y^2}{2}, so L(y)=(y+4)−y22L(y) = (y + 4) - \frac{y^2}{2}.

  • Answer: L(y)=L(y) = y+4−y22y + 4 - \frac{y^{2}}{2}

4. Integrating with respect to yy, what are the limits of integration for the area of RR?

  1. y=−2y = -2 and y=4y = 4, where y22=y+4\frac{y^2}{2} = y + 4
  2. x=2x = 2 and x=8x = 8, the xx-values where the curves meet
  3. y=0y = 0 and y=4y = 4, from the xx-axis up to the top
  4. x=0x = 0 and x=8x = 8, from the left edge to the right
Show the answer

y22=y+4\frac{y^2}{2} = y + 4 gives y2−2y−8=0y^2 - 2y - 8 = 0, so (y−4)(y+2)=0(y - 4)(y + 2) = 0: y=−2y = -2 and y=4y = 4.

  • Correct: y=−2y = -2 and y=4y = 4, where y22=y+4\frac{y^2}{2} = y + 4: In dydy, the limits are the lowest and highest yy of the region.
  • x=2x = 2 and x=8x = 8, the xx-values where the curves meet: These are xx-values. With dydy the limits must be yy-values.
  • y=0y = 0 and y=4y = 4, from the xx-axis up to the top: The region goes below the xx-axis, down to y=−2y = -2.
  • x=0x = 0 and x=8x = 8, from the left edge to the right: These are xx-values, and the integral is in dydy.

5. Find the area of the region bounded by y=ln⁡xy = \ln x, the yy-axis, y=0y = 0 and y=2y = 2.

No calculator. Type the exact value (a fraction, π, e or a root is fine).

Show the answer

Write the curve as x=eyx = e^y. Each horizontal strip runs from the yy-axis (x=0x = 0) to x=eyx = e^y, so the area is ∫02(ey−0)dy\int_0^2 \left(e^y - 0\right)\,dy=e2−1= e^2 - 1.

  • Answer: e2−1e^{2} - 1 ≈ 6.389

6. Find the area of the region bounded by x=y2−4x = y^2 - 4 and x=2−yx = 2 - y.

No calculator. Type the exact value (a fraction, π, e or a root is fine).

Show the answer

y2−4=2−yy^2 - 4 = 2 - y gives y2+y−6=0y^2 + y - 6 = 0, so y=−3y = -3 or y=2y = 2. Area =∫−32(6−y−y2)dy= \int_{-3}^{2} \left(6 - y - y^2\right)\,dy=[6y−y22−y33]−32= \left[6y - \frac{y^2}{2} - \frac{y^3}{3}\right]_{-3}^{2}=223+272= \frac{22}{3} + \frac{27}{2}=1256= \frac{125}{6}.

  • Answer: 1256\frac{125}{6} ≈ 20.833

Summary
