Unit 6 · Topic 6.7

The Fundamental Theorem and Definite Integrals

To evaluate ∫ from a to b of f(x) dx , find an antiderivative F and compute F(b) − F(a) .

Practice 1: Implementing Mathematical Processes

Part 1 · Hook

Why this matters

Finding an area by stacking millions of thin rectangles sounds hopeless by hand. Yet ∫13(3x2−2)dx\int_1^3 (3x^2 - 2)\,dx takes four lines: find a function whose derivative is 3x2−23x^2 - 2, plug in 3 and 1, subtract. That shortcut, the second half of the Fundamental Theorem, is why calculus can compute areas, distances and totals at all.

Part 5 · Step by step

How it works, step by step

  1. g(x)g(x)=∫axf(t)dt= \int_a^x f(t)\,dt and any FF are both antiderivatives of ff→they differ by a constant: g(x)=F(x)−F(a)g(x) = F(x) - F(a), since g(a)=0g(a) = 0
  2. Put x=bx = b→∫abf(x)dx\int_a^b f(x)\,dx=F(b)−F(a)= F(b) - F(a), the Fundamental Theorem, part 2
  3. Apply it to a rate f′f'→∫abf′(x)dx\int_a^b f'(x)\,dx=f(b)−f(a)= f(b) - f(a): the integral of a rate is the net change
  4. You know f(a)f(a) and the graph of f′f'→f(b)f(b)=f(a)= f(a)+∫abf′(x)dx+ \int_a^b f'(x)\,dx, with signed areas from the graph

Part 6 · Key ideas

Key ideas

  • FTC, part 2: if F′=fF' = f and ff is continuous on [a,b][a, b], then ∫abf(x)dx\int_a^b f(x)\,dx=F(b)−F(a)= F(b) - F(a)=F(x)|ab= F(x)\Big|_a^b.
  • Net change: ∫abf′(x)dx\int_a^b f'(x)\,dx=f(b)−f(a)= f(b) - f(a), so f(b)f(b)=f(a)= f(a)+∫abf′(x)dx+ \int_a^b f'(x)\,dx.
  • Going backward from a known value: f(0)f(0)=f(2)−∫02f′(x)dx= f(2) - \int_0^2 f'(x)\,dx.
  • With no elementary antiderivative, a calculator evaluates the integral; write the integral as the setup.

Part 7 · Misconception

A common mistake

The wrong idea: f(5)f(5)=∫25f′(x)dx= \int_2^5 f'(x)\,dx: the integral of the derivative gives the function.

What actually happens: The integral of f′f' gives the change in ff. Add the known starting value: f(5)f(5)=f(2)= f(2)+∫25f′(x)dx+ \int_2^5 f'(x)\,dx.

Part 9 · Summary

Summary

To evaluate ∫abf(x)dx\int_a^b f(x)\,dx, find an antiderivative FF and compute F(b)−F(a)F(b) - F(a). Read in context, the integral of a rate of change is the net change: f(b)f(b)=f(a)= f(a)+∫abf′(x)dx+ \int_a^b f'(x)\,dx. Use signed areas from a graph of f′f', an antiderivative from a formula, or a calculator when no formula exists.
