Area Between Curves (Functions of x)

The area between two curves is a sum of thin vertical rectangles whose heights are top minus bottom.

Practice 1: Implementing Mathematical Processes

Question set for this topic

Part 1 · Hook

Why this matters

A landscaper wants to know how much sod to buy for a curved lawn that sits between a winding path and a straight fence. Neither edge is the x-axis, so the area under one curve is not the answer. Slice the lawn into thin vertical strips, measure each from the lower edge to the upper edge, and add them up: that is one integral.

Part 5 · Step by step

How it works, step by step

  1. Slice the region into thin vertical rectangles of width Δx\Delta x→each one reaches from the bottom curve gg up to the top curve ff, so its height is f(x)−g(x)f(x) - g(x)
  2. Each rectangle has area (f(x)−g(x))Δx\left(f(x) - g(x)\right)\Delta x→adding them is a Riemann sum for f−gf - g
  3. Let the rectangles get thinner and thinner→the sum becomes ∫ab(f(x)−g(x))dx\int_a^b \left(f(x) - g(x)\right)\,dx
  4. The region starts and ends where the curves meet→the limits aa and bb are the solutions of f(x)=g(x)f(x) = g(x)
  5. Top minus bottom is never negative on [a,b][a, b]→the integral is positive, so a negative answer means the subtraction is backward

Part 6 · Key ideas

Key ideas

  • Area between curves: if f(x)≥g(x)f(x) \ge g(x) on [a,b][a, b], the area is ∫ab(f(x)−g(x))dx\int_a^b \left(f(x) - g(x)\right)\,dx, top minus bottom.
  • Find the limits by solving f(x)=g(x)f(x) = g(x). They are xx-values, because the integral is in dxdx.
  • Put the bottom function in parentheses before you subtract: (x+1)−(x2−1)(x + 1) - (x^2 - 1)=x+2−x2= x + 2 - x^2.
  • To tell which curve is on top, test one xx between the crossings in both functions.
  • The formula works wherever the curves are, above or below the xx-axis: only the difference matters.

Part 7 · Misconception

A common mistake

The wrong idea: The area between two curves is the area under the top curve, or the integral in whatever order you subtract.

What actually happens: Each strip runs from the bottom curve to the top curve, so the integrand is top minus bottom. The area under the top curve alone counts space below the region. Subtracting bottom minus top gives a negative number, and an area is never negative.

Part 9 · Summary

Summary
