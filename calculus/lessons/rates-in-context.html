Unit 4 · Topic 4.3

Rates of Change in Other Contexts

In any context, Q′ is the rate of change of Q in units of Q per input unit, and Q″ is the rate of change of that rate.

Practice 3: JustificationPractice 4: Communication and Notation

Part 1 · Hook

Why this matters

Doctors track how fast a medicine's concentration falls, economists how fast cost grows with each extra unit, ecologists how fast a population spreads. The derivative is the same in all of them; what changes is the story and the units. This topic practices reading rates, and rates of rates, in many settings.

Part 5 · Step by step

How it works, step by step

  1. Any quantity QQ that depends on an input→has a rate of change Q′Q' in units of QQ per unit of input
  2. The sign of Q′Q'→says whether the quantity is increasing or decreasing
  3. Q′Q' is itself a function, so Q″Q'' is the rate of change of the rate→measured in units of Q per input unit per input unit
  4. The sign of Q″Q''→says whether the rate Q′Q' is increasing or decreasing

Part 6 · Key ideas

Key ideas

  • An interpretation names the input value, the quantity, increasing or decreasing, and the rate with units.
  • When the function is itself a rate (liters per hour), its derivative is a rate of a rate (liters per hour per hour).
  • Q′<0Q' < 0 with Q″>0Q'' > 0: decreasing, but more slowly. Q′>0Q' > 0 with Q″<0Q'' < 0: increasing, but more slowly.
  • Chained quantities multiply rates, and their units cancel: (°F per foot)(feet per minute) = °F per minute.

Part 7 · Misconception

A common mistake

The wrong idea: If H″(t)>0H''(t) > 0, the temperature is going up.

What actually happens: H″H'' describes the rate H′H', not the temperature. With H′<0H' < 0 and H″>0H'' > 0 the temperature is still falling, just more slowly.

Check yourself

A bowl of soup cooling: H(t)=20+70e−0.04tH(t) = 20 + 70e^{-0.04t}

1. The temperature of a bowl of soup is H(t)=20+70e−0.04tH(t) = 20 + 70e^{-0.04t} degrees Celsius, tt minutes after it is served. Find H′(10)H'(10), with units.

Calculator allowed. Give the answer correct to three decimal places, then its unit.

Show the answer

H′(t)H'(t)=70(−0.04)e−0.04t= 70(-0.04)e^{-0.04t}=−2.8e−0.04t= -2.8e^{-0.04t}, so H′(10)H'(10)=−2.8e−0.4= -2.8e^{-0.4}≈−1.877\approx -1.877 °C per minute. (A calculator's numerical derivative at t=10t = 10 gives the same value.)

  • Answer: -1.877 degrees Celsius per minute

2. What are the units of H″(t)H''(t)?

  1. degrees Celsius per minute per minute
  2. degrees Celsius per minute
  3. degrees Celsius
  4. minutes per degree Celsius
Show the answer

Each derivative with respect to tt divides by minutes once more: H″(t)H''(t) is in degrees Celsius per minute per minute.

  • Correct: degrees Celsius per minute per minute: H″H'' is the rate of change of H′H', which is in °C per minute.
  • degrees Celsius per minute: Those are the units of H′(t)H'(t).
  • degrees Celsius: Those are the units of H(t)H(t).
  • minutes per degree Celsius: Rates put the output on top: °C per minute, then per minute again.

3. Find H″(10)H''(10), with units.

Calculator allowed. Give the answer correct to three decimal places, then its unit.

Show the answer

H″(t)H''(t)=(−2.8)(−0.04)e−0.04t= (-2.8)(-0.04)e^{-0.04t}=0.112e−0.04t= 0.112e^{-0.04t}, so H″(10)H''(10)=0.112e−0.4= 0.112e^{-0.4}≈0.075\approx 0.075 °C per minute per minute.

  • Answer: 0.075 degrees Celsius per minute per minute

4. At t=10t = 10, H′(10)<0H'(10) < 0 and H″(10)>0H''(10) > 0. What does this say about the soup?

  1. It is warming, because H″(10)H''(10) is positive
  2. It is cooling, and its rate of cooling is slowing down
  3. It is cooling faster and faster
  4. Its temperature is about to stop falling and start rising
Show the answer

H′(10)<0H'(10) < 0 means the temperature is decreasing at t=10t = 10. H″(10)>0H''(10) > 0 means H′H' is increasing, toward 0: the soup cools more and more slowly.

  • It is warming, because H″(10)H''(10) is positive: The sign of H′H', not of H″H'', says whether the temperature rises or falls.
  • Correct: It is cooling, and its rate of cooling is slowing down: H′<0H' < 0: cooling. H″>0H'' > 0: H′H' is rising toward 0, so the cooling gets gentler.
  • It is cooling faster and faster: Cooling faster would need H′H' to become more negative, that is H″<0H'' < 0.
  • Its temperature is about to stop falling and start rising: H′(t)=−2.8e−0.04tH'(t) = -2.8e^{-0.04t} is negative for every tt: the soup keeps cooling, more and more slowly.

5. W(t)W(t) is the number of gallons of water in a pool tt hours after filling starts. What are the units of W″(t)W''(t)?

  1. gallons per hour per hour
  2. gallons per hour
  3. gallons squared per hour
  4. hours per gallon per gallon
Show the answer

W″(t)=ddtW′(t)W''(t) = \frac{d}{dt}W'(t): (gallons per hour) per hour.

  • Correct: gallons per hour per hour: W′W' is in gallons per hour; its rate of change adds another per hour.
  • gallons per hour: Those are the units of W′(t)W'(t).
  • gallons squared per hour: Differentiating twice divides by hours twice; it does not square the gallons.
  • hours per gallon per gallon: Rates put the output on top.

6. The area A(t)A(t) of an oil slick satisfies A′(t)>0A'(t) > 0 and A″(t)<0A''(t) < 0 on an interval. What is happening to the slick?

  1. It is shrinking, but more and more slowly
  2. It is spreading faster and faster
  3. It is spreading, but more and more slowly
  4. It is shrinking faster and faster
Show the answer

The sign of A′A' says the area is growing; the sign of A″A'' says that growth rate is falling.

  • It is shrinking, but more and more slowly: A positive A′A' means the area is growing.
  • It is spreading faster and faster: That would need A″>0A'' > 0.
  • Correct: It is spreading, but more and more slowly: A′>0A' > 0: area increasing. A″<0A'' < 0: the rate of increase is decreasing.
  • It is shrinking faster and faster: With A′>0A' > 0 the slick is not shrinking.

Part 9 · Summary

Summary

In any context, Q′Q' is the rate of change of QQ in units of QQ per input unit, and Q″Q'' is the rate of change of that rate. Read the sign of Q′Q' for increasing or decreasing and the sign of Q″Q'' for whether that rate grows or shrinks. Always state units.
