Writing Differential Equations

A differential equation states how fast a quantity changes.

Practice 2: Connecting RepresentationsPractice 4: Communication and Notation

Question set for this topic

Part 1 · Hook

Why this matters

A hot cup of coffee cools fast at first, then more and more slowly as it nears room temperature. Nobody hands you a formula for its temperature. What you can say is how fast it cools: the hotter it is compared with the room, the faster it drops. That sentence, written with a derivative, is a differential equation.

Part 5 · Step by step

How it works, step by step

  1. A quantity changes at a rate that depends on its current value→write the rate as a derivative and the dependence as a formula: dPdt=kP\frac{dP}{dt} = kP
  2. "Proportional to" means "a constant times"→dydt=k⋅(that quantity)\frac{dy}{dt} = k \cdot (\text{that quantity}); "inversely proportional" puts the quantity in the denominator
  3. The signs of kk and of the other factor are known→the sign of dydt\frac{dy}{dt} is known, so you know where the quantity grows and where it shrinks
  4. One rate and the matching value are given→solve for kk, then predict the rate at any other value

Part 6 · Key ideas

Key ideas

  • A differential equation relates a derivative to the variables: dydt=0.4(50−y)\frac{dy}{dt} = 0.4(50 - y).
  • "The rate of change of yy is proportional to yy" is dydt=ky\frac{dy}{dt} = ky. The derivative is on the left; the quantity is on the right.
  • "Proportional to the difference between TT and 20" is dTdt=k(T−20)\frac{dT}{dt} = k(T - 20).
  • The sign of dydt\frac{dy}{dt} says where yy increases or decreases; you can read it before knowing any formula for yy.
  • kk has units: for dPdt=kP\frac{dP}{dt} = kP with tt in hours, kk is "per hour".

Part 7 · Misconception

A common mistake

The wrong idea: "A population grows at a rate proportional to its size" means P=ktP = kt.

What actually happens: The rate is proportional: dPdt=kP\frac{dP}{dt} = kP. The sentence is about dPdt\frac{dP}{dt}, and the size it mentions is PP, not tt.

3. What does the entry 91 in the table mean?

  1. After 350 hours, there are 91 cells.
  2. When there are 350 cells, the population is growing at 91 cells per hour.
  3. From now on, the population grows by 91 cells each hour.
  4. The population has grown by 91 cells since the first measurement.
Show the answer

The second row is dPdt\frac{dP}{dt}: at the moment P=350P = 350 cells, the population increases at 91 cells per hour.

  • After 350 hours, there are 91 cells.: The 350 is a number of cells, not a time, and 91 is a rate, not a count.
  • Correct: When there are 350 cells, the population is growing at 91 cells per hour.: It is the derivative, a rate, at the moment P = 350.
  • From now on, the population grows by 91 cells each hour.: The rate changes as P changes; 91 holds only at that moment.
  • The population has grown by 91 cells since the first measurement.: It grew by 150 cells between the first two columns; 91 is a rate, not a change in size.

4. The rate of change of y with respect to x is inversely proportional to the square of y. Which equation says this?

  1. dydx=ky2\frac{dy}{dx} = ky^2
  2. dydx=kx2\frac{dy}{dx} = \frac{k}{x^2}
  3. y=kx2y = \frac{k}{x^2}
  4. dydx=ky2\frac{dy}{dx} = \frac{k}{y^2}
Show the answer

"Inversely proportional to y2y^2" means ky2\frac{k}{y^2}; "the rate of change of yy with respect to xx" is dydx\frac{dy}{dx}.

  • dydx=ky2\frac{dy}{dx} = ky^2: That is directly proportional to the square of y.
  • dydx=kx2\frac{dy}{dx} = \frac{k}{x^2}: The sentence is about the square of y, not of x.
  • y=kx2y = \frac{k}{x^2}: The sentence describes the rate of change, so a derivative belongs on the left.
  • Correct: dydx=ky2\frac{dy}{dx} = \frac{k}{y^2}: Inversely proportional to y2y^2: a constant divided by y2y^2.

Part 9 · Summary

Summary

A differential equation states how fast a quantity changes. Translate words carefully: "the rate of change of yy" is dydt\frac{dy}{dt}, "proportional to" is "kk times", and the sign of the right side tells you where yy rises or falls. One known rate fixes kk.
