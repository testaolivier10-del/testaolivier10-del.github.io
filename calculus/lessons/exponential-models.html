Exponential Models

When the rate of change is proportional to the amount, dy/dt = ky and y = y_0e^kt .

Part 1 · Hook

Why this matters

Bacteria in a dish, money in an account, a medicine leaving the blood, a radioactive sample: each changes at a rate proportional to how much there is. One differential equation, dydt=ky\frac{dy}{dt} = ky, covers them all, and its solution y=y0ekty = y_0e^{kt} is worth knowing by heart, along with what kk means and how to find it from data.

Part 5 · Step by step

How it works, step by step

  1. A quantity changes at a rate proportional to its size→dydt=ky\frac{dy}{dt} = ky
  2. Separate, integrate and use y(0)→y=y0ekty = y_0e^{kt}, the exponential model
  3. k>0k > 0 or k<0k < 0→growth or decay; |k||k| is the rate as a fraction of the amount, per unit time
  4. A second data point y(t1)=y1y(t_1) = y_1 is known→ekt1=y1y0e^{kt_1} = \frac{y_1}{y_0}, so kk=1t1ln⁡y1y0= \frac{1}{t_1}\ln\frac{y_1}{y_0}

Part 6 · Key ideas

Key ideas

  • dydt=ky\frac{dy}{dt} = ky and y(0)=y0y(0) = y_0 give y=y0ekty = y_0e^{kt}. You may quote this result.
  • k=dy/dtyk = \frac{dy/dt}{y}: the rate of change as a fraction of the current amount, in "per unit time" (per hour, per year).
  • Doubling time (k>0k > 0) and half-life (k<0k < 0) are both ln⁡2|k|\frac{\ln 2}{|k|}; they do not depend on the starting amount.
  • To find kk from two values, divide first: y1y0=ekt1\frac{y_1}{y_0} = e^{kt_1}, then take ln⁡\ln.
  • Cooling toward room temperature TrT_r is exponential in T−TrT - T_r, not in TT.

Part 7 · Misconception

A common mistake

The wrong idea: A population goes from 400 to 1200 in 3 hours, so k=1200−4003k = \frac{1200 - 400}{3}.

What actually happens: That is an average rate, measured in cells per hour: a linear idea. For y=400ekty = 400e^{kt}: 1200=400e3k1200 = 400e^{3k}, so e3k=3e^{3k} = 3 and k=ln⁡33k = \frac{\ln 3}{3} per hour.

4. Which differential equation has the solution y=7e−2ty = 7e^{-2t}?

  1. dydt=7y\frac{dy}{dt} = 7y
  2. dydt=−2t\frac{dy}{dt} = -2t
  3. dydt=−14y\frac{dy}{dt} = -14y
  4. dydt=−2y\frac{dy}{dt} = -2y
Show the answer

For y=y0ekty = y_0e^{kt}, dydt=ky\frac{dy}{dt} = ky; here k=−2k = -2.

  • dydt=7y\frac{dy}{dt} = 7y: 7 is the starting amount, not the constant k.
  • dydt=−2t\frac{dy}{dt} = -2t: The rate is proportional to y, not to t.
  • dydt=−14y\frac{dy}{dt} = -14y: −14y=−98e−2t-14y = -98e^{-2t}, seven times too big.
  • Correct: dydt=−2y\frac{dy}{dt} = -2y: dydt=−14e−2t=−2y\frac{dy}{dt} = -14e^{-2t} = -2y.

5. A town's population PP satisfies dPdt=kP\frac{dP}{dt} = kP, with tt in years. What are the units of kk?

  1. people per year
  2. per year
  3. years
  4. people
Show the answer

k=dP/dtPk = \frac{dP/dt}{P}: (people per year) ÷ people = per year.

  • people per year: That is the unit of dPdt\frac{dP}{dt}; dividing by PP removes "people".
  • Correct: per year: dPdt\frac{dP}{dt} is people per year and PP is people, so kk is 1year\frac{1}{\text{year}}.
  • years: k⋅tk \cdot t must have no units, so kk is per year.
  • people: k multiplies P to give a rate, so it carries "per year".
