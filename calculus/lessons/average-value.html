Unit 8 · Topic 8.1

Average Value of a Function

The average value of f on [a, b] is 1/(b − a) ∫ from a to b of f(x) dx , the height of the equal-area rectangle, in the units of f .

Practice 1: Implementing Mathematical ProcessesPractice 4: Communication and Notation

Part 1 · Hook

Why this matters

A weather report says the average temperature yesterday was 19 °C. It was never "19 for the whole day"; it was colder at dawn and warmer at three. The average is the one steady temperature that would have built up the same total. For a function, that total is an integral, and the average value is the integral divided by the length of the interval.

Part 5 · Step by step

How it works, step by step

  1. ∫abf(x)dx\int_a^b f(x)\,dx is the signed area, or the total accumulated→spread it evenly over the interval to get one steady height
  2. Divide by the length b−ab - a→favgf_{\text{avg}}=1b−a∫abf(x)dx= \frac{1}{b - a}\int_a^b f(x)\,dx, in the units of ff
  3. ff is continuous on [a,b][a, b]→ff takes its average value at some cc in [a,b][a, b] (between its minimum and maximum, by the Intermediate Value Theorem)
  4. The integrand is f′f' instead of ff→1b−a∫abf′(x)dx\frac{1}{b - a}\int_a^b f'(x)\,dx=f(b)−f(a)b−a= \frac{f(b) - f(a)}{b - a}, the average rate of change of ff

Part 6 · Key ideas

Key ideas

  • Average value of ff on [a,b][a, b]: 1b−a∫abf(x)dx\frac{1}{b - a}\int_a^b f(x)\,dx. The integrand is ff itself.
  • Average value is a height, in the units of ff. Average rate of change, f(b)−f(a)b−a\frac{f(b) - f(a)}{b - a}, is a slope, in units of ff per unit of xx.
  • Areas below the axis count as negative, exactly as in the integral.
  • If ff is continuous, some cc in [a,b][a, b] has f(c)=favgf(c) = f_{\text{avg}}.
  • From a graph use geometry for the integral; from a table use a Riemann or trapezoidal sum; from a formula use the Fundamental Theorem or a calculator.

Part 7 · Misconception

A common mistake

The wrong idea: The average value of ff on [0,8][0, 8] is f(0)+f(8)2\frac{f(0) + f(8)}{2}, or f(8)−f(0)8\frac{f(8) - f(0)}{8}.

What actually happens: The first averages two values and ignores the rest; the second is the average rate of change. The average value uses every value: 18∫08f(x)dx\frac{1}{8}\int_0^8 f(x)\,dx.

6. For a function ff with continuous derivative, the average value of f′f' on [a,b][a, b] equals

  1. f′(b)−f′(a)b−a\frac{f'(b) - f'(a)}{b - a}
  2. f(b)−f(a)b−a\frac{f(b) - f(a)}{b - a}

  4. 1b−a∫abf(x)dx\frac{1}{b - a}\int_a^b f(x)\,dx
Show the answer

By the Fundamental Theorem, ∫abf′(x)dx\int_a^b f'(x)\,dx=f(b)−f(a)= f(b) - f(a); dividing by b−ab - a gives the average rate of change of ff.

  • f′(b)−f′(a)b−a\frac{f'(b) - f'(a)}{b - a}: That is the average rate of change of f′f', which is the average value of f″f''.
  • Correct: f(b)−f(a)b−a\frac{f(b) - f(a)}{b - a}: 1b−a∫abf′(x)dx\frac{1}{b - a}\int_a^b f'(x)\,dx=f(b)−f(a)b−a= \frac{f(b) - f(a)}{b - a} by the Fundamental Theorem.

Part 9 · Summary

Summary

The average value of ff on [a,b][a, b] is 1b−a∫abf(x)dx\frac{1}{b - a}\int_a^b f(x)\,dx, the height of the equal-area rectangle, in the units of ff. A continuous ff reaches it somewhere on the interval. Do not confuse it with the average rate of change f(b)−f(a)b−a\frac{f(b) - f(a)}{b - a}, which is the average value of f′f'.
