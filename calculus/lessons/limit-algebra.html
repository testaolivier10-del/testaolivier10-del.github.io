Limits by Algebra: Factor, Rationalize, Simplify

When direct substitution gives the indeterminate form 0/0 , rewrite: factor and cancel, multiply by a conjugate, or simplify a complex fraction.

Practice 1: Implementing Mathematical Processes

Part 1 · Hook

Why this matters

Substitute x=3x = 3 into x2−9x−3\frac{x^2 - 9}{x - 3} and you get 00\frac{0}{0}. It looks like a dead end, but it is the most common limit in calculus: every rate at an instant in Unit 2 starts out this way. Zero over zero is not an answer. It is a signal to do some algebra, and this topic shows exactly which algebra.

Part 5 · Step by step

How it works, step by step

  1. Direct substitution gives 00\frac{0}{0}→an indeterminate form: the limit is not yet known
  2. Top and bottom are both 0 at x=ax = a→they share a factor x−ax - a (for polynomials), possibly hidden behind a square root or a fraction
  3. Factor, rationalize or combine fractions, then cancel the shared factor→the new expression equals the old one for every x≠ax \ne a, so it has the same limit
  4. Substitute into the simplified expression→the limit appears (or a nonzero over zero shows there is no finite limit)

Part 6 · Key ideas

Key ideas

  • 00\frac{0}{0} is an indeterminate form: it means "rewrite first", never 0, 1 or "does not exist".
  • Canceling is legal in a limit because the limit never uses x=ax = a, and for x≠ax \ne a the canceled factor is not 0.
  • Toolkit: factor and cancel; multiply by a conjugate to clear a square root; combine a complex fraction; expand powers completely.
  • After simplifying, a nonzero number over 0 means no finite limit (unbounded); check one-sided signs if asked.
  • Keep "limx→a\lim_{x \to a}" on every line until you substitute; never write 00=…\frac{0}{0} = \ldots.

Part 7 · Misconception

A common mistake

The wrong idea: Substitution gives 00\frac{0}{0}, so the limit is 0 (or does not exist).

What actually happens: 00\frac{0}{0} is indeterminate: it gives no information about the limit. Rewrite the expression; limx→3x2−9x−3\lim_{x \to 3} \frac{x^2 - 9}{x - 3}=limx→3(x+3)= \lim_{x \to 3} (x + 3)=6= 6.

3. What can you conclude about limx→−2f(x)\lim_{x \to -2} f(x)?

  1. No finite limit: the top heads to −4-4, the bottom to 0.
  2. It equals 14\frac{1}{4}, found by factoring and then substituting x=−2x = -2.
  3. It equals 0, because x=−2x = -2 makes the denominator of ff equal to 0.
  4. It equals −12-\frac{1}{2}, the value of x+3x\frac{x + 3}{x} at x=−2x = -2.
Show the answer

At x=−2x = -2 the numerator is 4−2−6=−44 - 2 - 6 = -4, not 0, while the denominator heads to 0. A nonzero number over a number near 0 is unbounded, as the vertical asymptote shows.

  • Correct: No finite limit: the top heads to −4-4, the bottom to 0.: −4-4 over a quantity near 0 is unbounded.
  • It equals 14\frac{1}{4}, found by factoring and then substituting x=−2x = -2.: After canceling, x+3x+2\frac{x + 3}{x + 2} still has a zero denominator at −2-2.
  • It equals 0, because x=−2x = -2 makes the denominator of ff equal to 0.: A denominator near 0 makes the quotient large in size, not 0.

4. Which line of work is written correctly?

  1. limx→2(x+3)(x−2)(x+2)(x−2)\lim_{x \to 2} \frac{(x + 3)(x - 2)}{(x + 2)(x - 2)}=limx→2x+3x+2= \lim_{x \to 2} \frac{x + 3}{x + 2}=54= \frac{5}{4}

Each "=" joins equal things, and "lim" stays on every line until the number appears.

  • Correct: limx→2(x+3)(x−2)(x+2)(x−2)\lim_{x \to 2} \frac{(x + 3)(x - 2)}{(x + 2)(x - 2)}=limx→2x+3x+2= \lim_{x \to 2} \frac{x + 3}{x + 2}=54= \frac{5}{4}: Every equals sign is true.

  • limx→2x2+x−6x2−4\lim_{x \to 2} \frac{x^2 + x - 6}{x^2 - 4}=x+64= \frac{x + 6}{4}=2= 2: The x2x^2 terms were canceled; only factors can cancel.

7. What is limx→3|x−3|x−3\lim_{x \to 3} \frac{|x - 3|}{x - 3}?

  1. It does not exist: −1-1 from the left, 11 from the right.
  2. 11, because near x=3x = 3 the top and bottom have equal sizes, so they cancel.
  3. 00, because the top becomes 0.
  4. −1-1, because x−3x - 3 is negative.
Show the answer

For x>3x > 3, |x−3|=x−3|x - 3| = x - 3, so the quotient is 1. For x<3x < 3, |x−3|=−(x−3)|x - 3| = -(x - 3), so the quotient is −1-1. The one-sided limits differ.

  • Correct: It does not exist: −1-1 from the left, 11 from the right.: Left −1-1, right 11.
  • 11, because near x=3x = 3 the top and bottom have equal sizes, so they cancel.: Only for x>3x > 3; to the left the quotient is −1-1.
  • 00, because the top becomes 0.: The bottom also heads to 0.
  • −1-1, because x−3x - 3 is negative.: Only for x<3x < 3.

Part 9 · Summary

Summary

When direct substitution gives the indeterminate form 00\frac{0}{0}, rewrite: factor and cancel, multiply by a conjugate, or simplify a complex fraction. The simplified expression matches the original except at x=ax = a, so it has the same limit, which substitution now finds. Write every step with "lim" and true equals signs.
