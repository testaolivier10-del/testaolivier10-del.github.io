Accumulation in Context

With a rate in R and a rate out D , the amount satisfies A′(t) = R(t) − D(t) and A(t) = A(a) +∫ from a to t of (R − D) .

Question set for this topic

Part 1 · Hook

Why this matters

A bathtub fills from the tap while the drain is open. Whether the water rises or falls at any moment depends on one comparison: is more coming in than going out? How much water is in the tub at 7:10 depends on how much was there at 7:00, plus everything that came in, minus everything that drained, in between. That bookkeeping is one integral.

Part 5 · Step by step

How it works, step by step

  1. A quantity gains at a rate in and loses at a rate out→its net rate of change is A′(t)=R(t)−D(t)A'(t) = R(t) - D(t)
  2. Integrate the net rate over an interval→the net change in the amount: ∫ab(R(t)−D(t))dt\int_a^b (R(t) - D(t))\,dt
  3. Add the amount at the start→A(t)A(t)=A(a)= A(a)+∫at(R(s)−D(s))ds+ \int_a^t (R(s) - D(s))\,ds
  4. R−DR - D changes sign→the amount has a relative extremum there; compare it with the endpoints to find the absolute one

Part 6 · Key ideas

Key ideas

  • Amount now = amount at the start + ∫\int (rate in − rate out). The initial amount is part of the answer.
  • A′(t)=R(t)−D(t)A'(t) = R(t) - D(t): the amount increases when the rate in is larger and decreases when the rate out is larger.
  • An integral of a rate has the accumulated unit: gallons per hour integrated over hours gives gallons.
  • An interpretation names the quantity, its units and the time interval.
  • For the greatest or least amount on a closed interval: find where R − D changes sign, then compare with the endpoints (candidates test).

Part 7 · Misconception

A common mistake

The wrong idea: The tank holds 50 L at t=0t = 0, so at t=9t = 9 it holds ∫09(R(t)−D(t))dt\int_0^9 (R(t) - D(t))\,dt liters.

What actually happens: That integral is the change in the amount. The amount itself is 5050+∫09(R(t)−D(t))dt+ \int_0^9 (R(t) - D(t))\,dt.

Part 9 · Summary

Summary

With a rate in RR and a rate out DD, the amount satisfies A′(t)=R(t)−D(t)A'(t) = R(t) - D(t) and A(t)A(t)=A(a)= A(a)+∫at(R−D)+ \int_a^t (R - D). Integrals of rates carry accumulated units and need an interpretation with quantity, units and interval. The amount peaks or bottoms out where R−DR - D changes sign or at an endpoint.
