Unit 3 · Topic 3.1 Beta

The Chain Rule

For a composite function f(g(x)) , differentiate the outside function at the unchanged inside, then multiply by the derivative of the inside: d/dx f(g(x)) = f′(g(x))⋅g′(x) .

Practice 1: Implementing Mathematical Processes

Part 1 · Hook

Why this matters

Turn the pedals of a bike and the back wheel spins faster than your feet, because the gears multiply the rate. Many functions work the same way: one function feeds its output into another. The chain rule says the rates multiply, and it is the rule you will use most often for the rest of the course.

Part 5 · Step by step

How it works, step by step

  1. A function is built in layers, h(x)=f(g(x))h(x) = f(g(x))→xx feeds the inside function gg, and g(x)g(x) feeds the outside function ff
  2. When xx changes a little, gg changes g′(x)g'(x) times as much→and ff changes f′(g(x))f'(g(x)) times as much as its input gg does
  3. The two rates multiply, like gears→h′(x)=f′(g(x))⋅g′(x)h'(x) = f'(g(x)) \cdot g'(x)
  4. In Leibniz form, with u=g(x)u = g(x)→dydx=dydu⋅dudx\frac{dy}{dx} = \frac{dy}{du} \cdot \frac{du}{dx}

Part 6 · Key ideas

Key ideas

  • Spot the layers first: name the outside function and the inside function before you differentiate.
  • Differentiate the outside function and leave the inside alone: f′(g(x))f'(g(x)), not f′(x)f'(x) and not f′(g′(x))f'(g'(x)).
  • Then multiply by the derivative of the inside, g′(x)g'(x). Forgetting this factor is the most common lost point.
  • From a table, read f′f' at the inside value g(a)g(a), then multiply by g′(a)g'(a).

Part 7 · Misconception

A common mistake

The wrong idea: The derivative of sin⁡(x2)\sin(x^2) is cos⁡(x2)\cos(x^2).

What actually happens: That is only the outside derivative. The inside function x2x^2 changes too, at rate 2x2x, so ddxsin⁡(x2)\frac{d}{dx}\sin(x^2)=cos⁡(x2)⋅2x= \cos(x^2) \cdot 2x.

6. Which of these functions need the chain rule to be differentiated? Select all that apply.

  1. e3xe^{3x}
  2. (x2+1)10(x^2 + 1)^{10}
  3. x2exx^2 e^x
  4. cos⁡xx\frac{\cos x}{x}
Show the answer

The chain rule is needed when a function is applied to another function: e3xe^{3x} and (x2+1)10(x^2 + 1)^{10}.

  • Correct: e3xe^{3x}: The exponent 3x3x is an inside function: the derivative is 3e3x3e^{3x}.
  • Correct: (x2+1)10(x^2 + 1)^{10}: The tenth power acts on the inside function x2+1x^2 + 1.
  • x2exx^2 e^x: This is a product of x2x^2 and exe^x, neither with an inside function: the product rule is enough.
  • cos⁡xx\frac{\cos x}{x}: This is a quotient of two basic functions: the quotient rule is enough.

Part 9 · Summary

Summary

For a composite function f(g(x))f(g(x)), differentiate the outside function at the unchanged inside, then multiply by the derivative of the inside: ddxf(g(x))\frac{d}{dx} f(g(x))=f′(g(x))⋅g′(x)= f'(g(x)) \cdot g'(x). Name the layers before you start, and never drop the inside derivative.
