Unit 6 · Topic 6.6 Beta

Properties of Definite Integrals

Definite integrals add over adjacent intervals, change sign when the limits are reversed, are 0 over a single point, and split over sums and constant multiples (but not products).

Practice 1: Implementing Mathematical ProcessesPractice 2: Connecting Representations

Part 1 · Hook

Why this matters

A free-response question gives you three integrals in a table and asks for a fourth you were never told. No formula for ff, no graph, just numbers. The properties of definite integrals let you build the answer the way you would piece together a trip from its legs: adding, subtracting and reversing.

Part 5 · Step by step

How it works, step by step

  1. Riemann sums are built by adding width times height→anything true of sums (splitting a sum, pulling out a constant) is true of integrals
  2. Two intervals meet end to end→their integrals add: ∫abf\int_a^b f+∫bcf+ \int_b^c f=∫acf= \int_a^c f
  3. An interval is run backward→every width is negative, so ∫baf\int_b^a f=−∫abf= -\int_a^b f, and ∫aaf=0\int_a^a f = 0
  4. A constant k is integrated→the result is k times the width: ∫abkdx\int_a^b k\,dx=k(b−a)= k(b - a)

Part 6 · Key ideas

Key ideas

  • Additivity: ∫abf(x)dx\int_a^b f(x)\,dx+∫bcf(x)dx+ \int_b^c f(x)\,dx=∫acf(x)dx= \int_a^c f(x)\,dx, for any order of aa, bb, cc.
  • Reversed limits: ∫baf(x)dx\int_b^a f(x)\,dx=−∫abf(x)dx= -\int_a^b f(x)\,dx, and ∫aaf(x)dx\int_a^a f(x)\,dx=0= 0.
  • Linearity: ∫ab(kf(x)+g(x))dx\int_a^b (kf(x) + g(x))\,dx=k∫abf(x)dx= k\int_a^b f(x)\,dx+∫abg(x)dx+ \int_a^b g(x)\,dx, and ∫abkdx\int_a^b k\,dx=k(b−a)= k(b - a).
  • A bounded function with a jump or a hole can still be integrated: split at the break and use geometry on each piece.

Part 7 · Misconception

A common mistake

The wrong idea: ∫37(f(x)+4)dx\int_3^7 (f(x) + 4)\,dx=∫37f(x)dx= \int_3^7 f(x)\,dx+4+ 4.

What actually happens: The constant 4 is a height, so it is integrated over the interval too: ∫374dx\int_3^7 4\,dx=4⋅(7−3)= 4 \cdot (7 - 3)=16= 16.

5. For continuous functions ff and gg, which statement is not true in general?

  1. ∫baf(x)dx\int_b^a f(x)\,dx=−∫abf(x)dx= -\int_a^b f(x)\,dx
  2. ∫abfgdx\int_a^b fg\,dx=∫abfdx⋅∫abgdx= \int_a^b f\,dx \cdot \int_a^b g\,dx
  3. ∫ab(f(x)+g(x))dx\int_a^b \left(f(x) + g(x)\right)dx=∫abf(x)dx= \int_a^b f(x)\,dx+∫abg(x)dx+ \int_a^b g(x)\,dx
  4. ∫ab5f(x)dx\int_a^b 5f(x)\,dx=5∫abf(x)dx= 5\int_a^b f(x)\,dx
Show the answer

Sums and constant multiples pass through an integral; products do not. On [0,2][0, 2] with f=g=1f = g = 1: ∫021dx\int_0^2 1\,dx=2= 2, but 2⋅2=42 \cdot 2 = 4.

  • ∫baf(x)dx\int_b^a f(x)\,dx=−∫abf(x)dx= -\int_a^b f(x)\,dx: This is true: running the interval backward makes every width negative.
  • Correct: ∫abfgdx\int_a^b fg\,dx=∫abfdx⋅∫abgdx= \int_a^b f\,dx \cdot \int_a^b g\,dx: Correct, this is false: with f=g=1f = g = 1 on [0,2][0, 2] the left side is 2 and the right side is 4. Integrals split over sums, not over products.
  • ∫ab(f(x)+g(x))dx\int_a^b \left(f(x) + g(x)\right)dx=∫abf(x)dx= \int_a^b f(x)\,dx+∫abg(x)dx+ \int_a^b g(x)\,dx: This is true: every Riemann sum of f + g splits into a sum for f plus a sum for g.
  • ∫ab5f(x)dx\int_a^b 5f(x)\,dx=5∫abf(x)dx= 5\int_a^b f(x)\,dx: This is true: a constant factor comes out of every Riemann sum, so it comes out of the integral.

Part 9 · Summary

Summary

Definite integrals add over adjacent intervals, change sign when the limits are reversed, are 0 over a single point, and split over sums and constant multiples (but not products). Use these with geometry, splitting at jumps, to find integrals from tables and graphs.
