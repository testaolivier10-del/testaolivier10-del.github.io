Volumes with Square and Rectangle Cross Sections

A solid with known cross sections has volume ∫ from a to b of A(x) dx .

Practice 1: Implementing Mathematical Processes

Part 1 · Hook

Why this matters

Slice a loaf of bread and every slice is a flat shape with a tiny thickness. Know the area of each slice and you know the loaf: add up area times thickness. Calculus does exactly that, for solids built by standing squares on a region drawn on the floor.

Part 5 · Step by step

How it works, step by step

  1. Slice the solid perpendicular to the xx-axis→each slice is a thin slab: a cross section of area A(x)A(x) with thickness Δx\Delta x
  2. A slab's volume is about A(x)ΔxA(x)\,\Delta x→adding the slabs is a Riemann sum for AA
  3. Let the slabs get thin→the volume is VV=∫abA(x)dx= \int_a^b A(x)\,dx
  4. For a square cross section, the side s(x)s(x) is the length of the base across the slice→A(x)A(x)=s(x)2= s(x)^2=(f(x)−g(x))2= \left(f(x) - g(x)\right)^2

Part 6 · Key ideas

Key ideas

  • Volume by cross sections: VV=∫abA(x)dx= \int_a^b A(x)\,dx, where A(x)A(x) is the area of the slice at xx.
  • For squares standing on the base, the side is top minus bottom of the base: s=f(x)−g(x)s = f(x) - g(x), and A(x)=s2A(x) = s^2.
  • Square the whole difference: (f−g)2(f - g)^2 is not f2−g2f^2 - g^2.
  • Rectangles work the same way: A=s⋅hA = s \cdot h, with hh given (a number, or a multiple of ss).
  • Slices perpendicular to the yy-axis use right minus left and dydy.

Part 7 · Misconception

A common mistake

The wrong idea: To get the square's area, square the top function and the bottom function and subtract: f(x)2−g(x)2f(x)^2 - g(x)^2.

What actually happens: The square's side is the gap between the curves, f(x)−g(x)f(x) - g(x), so its area is (f(x)−g(x))2\left(f(x) - g(x)\right)^2. Squaring each curve first gives a different, wrong number.

2. Which integral gives the volume of the solid with this base and square cross sections perpendicular to the xx-axis?

  1. ∫−12(x+2−x2)2dx\int_{-1}^{2} \left(x + 2 - x^2\right)^2\,dx
  2. ∫−12[(x+2)2−(x2)2]dx\int_{-1}^{2} \left[(x + 2)^2 - (x^2)^2\right]\,dx
  3. ∫−12(x+2−x2)dx\int_{-1}^{2} \left(x + 2 - x^2\right)\,dx
  4. π∫−12(x+2−x2)2dx\pi \int_{-1}^{2} \left(x + 2 - x^2\right)^2\,dx
Show the answer

Volume is the integral of cross-sectional area: ∫−12A(x)dx\int_{-1}^{2} A(x)\,dx with A(x)=s2A(x) = s^2 and s=(x+2)−x2s = (x + 2) - x^2.

  • Correct: ∫−12(x+2−x2)2dx\int_{-1}^{2} \left(x + 2 - x^2\right)^2\,dx: Square the side, then integrate from −1-1 to 22.
  • ∫−12[(x+2)2−(x2)2]dx\int_{-1}^{2} \left[(x + 2)^2 - (x^2)^2\right]\,dx: This squares each curve separately. (a−b)2(a - b)^2 is not a2−b2a^2 - b^2.
  • ∫−12(x+2−x2)dx\int_{-1}^{2} \left(x + 2 - x^2\right)\,dx: This is the area of the base, not the volume.
  • π∫−12(x+2−x2)2dx\pi \int_{-1}^{2} \left(x + 2 - x^2\right)^2\,dx: A square's area is s2s^2, with no π\pi.

6. The volume of a solid with square cross sections has no π\pi, even when its base has a curved edge. Why?

  1. Each slice is a square with area s2s^2, and s2s^2 has no π\pi in it
  2. π\pi cancels out when the integral is evaluated
  3. π\pi is left out because the base lies flat in the xyxy-plane
  4. π\pi is needed when the base region has a curved edge
Show the answer

The volume is ∫A(x)dx\int A(x)\,dx, and AA is whatever the shape of a slice gives. A square's area is s2s^2. A curved edge only changes how ss depends on xx.

  • Correct: Each slice is a square with area s2s^2, and s2s^2 has no π\pi in it: The slice's area formula decides; for a square it is s2s^2.
  • π\pi cancels out when the integral is evaluated: There is no π\pi to cancel: it never enters.
  • π\pi is left out because the base lies flat in the xyxy-plane: Every base lies in the xyxy-plane; that does not decide whether π\pi appears.
  • π\pi is needed when the base region has a curved edge: A curved edge changes s(x)s(x), not the area formula of a square.

Part 9 · Summary

Summary

A solid with known cross sections has volume ∫abA(x)dx\int_a^b A(x)\,dx. When the slices are squares standing on a base, each side is the base's width across the slice, f(x)−g(x)f(x) - g(x), and A(x)=(f(x)−g(x))2A(x) = \left(f(x) - g(x)\right)^2. Square the difference, not each curve, and leave out π\pi.
