Washer Method About Other Lines

Revolving about a line other than an axis uses the same washer integral, V = π∫ (R² − r²) .

Practice 1: Implementing Mathematical Processes

Question set for this topic

Part 1 · Hook

Why this matters

Spin the same flat region about the xx-axis, then about a line one unit below it, and you get two different hollow solids: the second has a wider hole and more material. The washer method handles both. The whole difference is in measuring each radius from the right line.

Part 5 · Step by step

How it works, step by step

  1. The axis is the line y=ky = k and the region does not touch it→each slice is a washer centered on that line
  2. Draw a rectangle perpendicular to the axis→its far end gives RR and its near end gives rr, both measured from y=ky = k
  3. The axis is below the region→R=(top)−kR = (\text{top}) - k and r=(bottom)−kr = (\text{bottom}) - k
  4. The axis is above the region→the bottom curve is now the far edge: R=k−(bottom)R = k - (\text{bottom}) and r=k−(top)r = k - (\text{top})
  5. Square each radius and subtract→V=π∫ab(R2−r2)dxV = \pi\int_a^b \left(R^2 - r^2\right)\,dx

Part 6 · Key ideas

Key ideas

  • Washers about a line: V=π∫(R2−r2)V = \pi\int \left(R^2 - r^2\right) with both radii measured from the line y=ky = k or x=hx = h.
  • Axis below the region (y=ky = k, kk small): R=f(x)−kR = f(x) - k, r=g(x)−kr = g(x) - k. With k=−1k = -1, add 1 to each height.
  • Axis above the region: the far edge is the bottom curve, so R=k−g(x)R = k - g(x) and r=k−f(x)r = k - f(x).
  • Vertical axis x=hx = h: horizontal washers in dydy; the far edge may be the left curve if the axis is on the right.
  • Square each radius separately; (R−r)2(R - r)^2 is the region's height squared, not a washer's area.

Part 7 · Misconception

A common mistake

The wrong idea: To revolve about y=−1y = -1, subtract 1 from each radius, since −1-1 is one unit away.

What actually happens: A radius is (larger height) minus (smaller height). From y=−1y = -1 up to the curve is f(x)−(−1)=f(x)+1f(x) - (-1) = f(x) + 1: both radii grow by 1. Subtracting 1 instead measures toward the wrong side of the xx-axis.

1. RR is revolved about the line y=−1y = -1. What are the outer and inner radii of the washer at xx?

  1. R(x)=5R(x) = 5 and r(x)=x2+1r(x) = x^2 + 1
  2. R(x)=4R(x) = 4 and r(x)=x2r(x) = x^2
  3. R(x)=3R(x) = 3 and r(x)=x2−1r(x) = x^2 - 1
  4. R(x)=5R(x) = 5 and r(x)=x2−1r(x) = x^2 - 1
Show the answer

Measure from y=−1y = -1: to the far edge, 4−(−1)=54 - (-1) = 5; to the near edge, x2−(−1)=x2+1x^2 - (-1) = x^2 + 1.

  • Correct: R(x)=5R(x) = 5 and r(x)=x2+1r(x) = x^2 + 1: Both radii measured from the axis y=−1y = -1.
  • R(x)=4R(x) = 4 and r(x)=x2r(x) = x^2: Those are measured from the xx-axis, not from y=−1y = -1.
  • R(x)=3R(x) = 3 and r(x)=x2−1r(x) = x^2 - 1: Subtracting −1-1 adds 1: both radii grow by 1, they do not shrink.
  • R(x)=5R(x) = 5 and r(x)=x2−1r(x) = x^2 - 1: The inner radius needs the same shift as the outer one: x2−(−1)=x2+1x^2 - (-1) = x^2 + 1.

Show the answer

5. The region between y=xy = x and y=x2y = x^2 is revolved about the line y=1y = 1. Find the volume.

No calculator. Type the exact value (a fraction, π, e or a root is fine).

Show the answer

V=π∫01[(1−x2)2−(1−x)2]dx=π∫01(x4−3x2+2x)dx=π(15−1+1)=π5V = \pi\int_0^1 \left[(1 - x^2)^2 - (1 - x)^2\right]\,dx = \pi\int_0^1 \left(x^4 - 3x^2 + 2x\right)\,dx = \pi\left(\frac{1}{5} - 1 + 1\right) = \frac{\pi}{5}.

6. A region between y=f(x)y = f(x) (top) and y=g(x)y = g(x) (bottom), both positive, is revolved about the line y=−2y = -2. What is the outer radius?

  1. f(x)+2f(x) + 2
  2. f(x)−2f(x) - 2
  3. f(x)f(x)
  4. g(x)+2g(x) + 2
Show the answer

The axis is below the region, so the far edge is the top curve: f(x)−(−2)=f(x)+2f(x) - (-2) = f(x) + 2.

  • Correct: f(x)+2f(x) + 2: Top curve minus the axis height −2-2.
  • f(x)−2f(x) - 2: Subtracting −2-2 adds 2.
  • f(x)f(x): That is measured from the xx-axis.
  • g(x)+2g(x) + 2: g(x)+2g(x) + 2 is the inner radius, to the nearer curve.

7. A region between x=g(y)x = g(y) (left) and x=h(y)x = h(y) (right) is revolved about the line x=5x = 5, which lies to the right of the region. What is the outer radius?

  1. 5−g(y)5 - g(y)
  2. 5−h(y)5 - h(y)
  3. h(y)−5h(y) - 5
  4. h(y)−g(y)h(y) - g(y)
Show the answer

The axis is on the right, so the left curve is farther from it: R=5−g(y)R = 5 - g(y).

  • Correct: 5−g(y)5 - g(y): From an axis on the right, the left edge is the far edge.
  • 5−h(y)5 - h(y): 5−h(y)5 - h(y) is the inner radius, to the nearer (right) curve.
  • h(y)−5h(y) - 5: That is negative: h(y)<5h(y) < 5.
  • h(y)−g(y)h(y) - g(y): That is the width of the region, not a distance from the axis.

Part 9 · Summary

Summary

Revolving about a line other than an axis uses the same washer integral, V=π∫(R2−r2)V = \pi\int (R^2 - r^2). Draw one rectangle perpendicular to the axis; RR is the distance from the axis to its far end and rr to its near end. When the axis is above (or to the right of) the region, the far end is the bottom (or left) curve.
