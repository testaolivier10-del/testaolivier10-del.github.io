Introduction to Related Rates

When quantities linked by an equation change with time, differentiate the equation with respect to t .

Part 1 · Hook

Why this matters

Drop a stone in a pond and a ring of ripples spreads. The radius grows, so the area inside grows too, and their rates are linked: knowing one tells you the other. Whenever two changing quantities are tied by an equation, their rates of change are tied by the derivative of that equation.

Part 5 · Step by step

How it works, step by step

  1. Two or more quantities change with time and are linked by an equation→each one is a function of tt, even if the equation shows no tt
  2. Differentiate both sides of the equation with respect to tt→the chain rule attaches a rate to each variable: ddtr2=2rdrdt\frac{d}{dt}r^2 = 2r\frac{dr}{dt}
  3. The result is an equation among the rates→known rates and values give the unknown rate
  4. Substitute the values of one instant only after differentiating→the rates are not lost to derivatives of constants

Part 6 · Key ideas

Key ideas

  • Related rates: differentiate an equation linking quantities with respect to time tt.
  • Each variable that changes gets its own rate, dxdt\frac{dx}{dt}, drdt\frac{dr}{dt}, from the chain rule. Constants get none.
  • A decreasing quantity has a negative rate: shrinking at 2 cm/s means drdt=−2\frac{dr}{dt} = -2.
  • Products need the product rule: ddt(xy)=dxdty+xdydt\frac{d}{dt}(xy) = \frac{dx}{dt}y + x\frac{dy}{dt}.

Part 7 · Misconception

A common mistake

The wrong idea: For V=43πr3V = \frac{4}{3}\pi r^3, dVdt=4πr2\frac{dV}{dt} = 4\pi r^2.

What actually happens: 4πr24\pi r^2 is dVdr\frac{dV}{dr}. With respect to time, the chain rule adds the factor drdt\frac{dr}{dt}: dVdt=4πr2drdt\frac{dV}{dt} = 4\pi r^2\frac{dr}{dt}.

2. With drdt=2\frac{dr}{dt} = 2 cm per second, how fast is the volume increasing when r=3r = 3 cm? Include units.

No calculator. Type the exact value (a fraction, π, e or a root is fine), then its unit.

Show the answer

  • Answer: 72π72\,\pi ≈ 226.195 cubic centimeters per second

3. Now air is pumped in at dVdt=100\frac{dV}{dt} = 100 cubic centimeters per second. How fast is the radius increasing when r=5r = 5 cm? Include units.

No calculator. Type the exact value (a fraction, π, e or a root is fine), then its unit.

Show the answer

100=4π(5)2drdt=100πdrdt100 = 4\pi(5)^2\frac{dr}{dt} = 100\pi\frac{dr}{dt}, so drdt=1π≈0.318\frac{dr}{dt} = \frac{1}{\pi} \approx 0.318 centimeters per second.

  • Answer: 1π\frac{1}{\pi} ≈ 0.318 centimeters per second

4. Later, air leaks out and the radius shrinks by 0.5 cm every second. Which value goes into the related-rates equation?

  1. drdt=−0.5\frac{dr}{dt} = -0.5
  2. drdt=0.5\frac{dr}{dt} = 0.5
  3. dVdt=−0.5\frac{dV}{dt} = -0.5
  4. r=−0.5r = -0.5
Show the answer

A decreasing quantity has a negative derivative: drdt=−0.5\frac{dr}{dt} = -0.5 cm per second.

  • Correct: drdt=−0.5\frac{dr}{dt} = -0.5: The radius is decreasing, so its rate of change is negative.
  • drdt=0.5\frac{dr}{dt} = 0.5: A shrinking radius has a negative rate of change; the sign is part of the value.
  • dVdt=−0.5\frac{dV}{dt} = -0.5: 0.5 cm per second is a rate for the radius, not for the volume.
  • r=−0.5r = -0.5: A radius is a length; −0.5-0.5 is how fast it changes.

5. A circle's radius grows at drdt=3\frac{dr}{dt} = 3 cm per second. Write dAdt\frac{dA}{dt} in terms of rr, where A=πr2A = \pi r^2.

Type an expression for dAdt=\frac{dA}{dt} =.

Show the answer

dAdt=2πrdrdt=2πr⋅3=6πr\frac{dA}{dt} = 2\pi r\frac{dr}{dt} = 2\pi r \cdot 3 = 6\pi r.

  • Answer: dAdt=\frac{dA}{dt} = 6πr6\,\pi\,r

6. The area of a circular stain grows at a constant rate, dAdt=5\frac{dA}{dt} = 5 cm² per second. As the stain gets larger, what happens to drdt\frac{dr}{dt}?

  1. It increases
  2. It stays the same
  3. It becomes negative
  4. It decreases
Show the answer

dAdt=2πrdrdt\frac{dA}{dt} = 2\pi r\frac{dr}{dt}, so drdt=52πr\frac{dr}{dt} = \frac{5}{2\pi r}, which shrinks as rr grows.

  • It increases: Adding the same area each second to a larger circle widens it less.
  • It stays the same: A constant dAdt\frac{dA}{dt} does not mean a constant drdt\frac{dr}{dt}: they are linked by 2πr2\pi r.
  • It becomes negative: The radius still grows, so drdt>0\frac{dr}{dt} > 0.
  • Correct: It decreases: drdt=52πr\frac{dr}{dt} = \frac{5}{2\pi r}: a larger rr gives a smaller rate.

Part 9 · Summary

Summary

When quantities linked by an equation change with time, differentiate the equation with respect to tt. The chain rule gives every changing variable its rate, producing an equation among the rates. Substitute the values of one instant afterward, with signs that match increasing or decreasing.
