The Quotient Rule

The derivative of f/g is (f′g − fg′)/g² , derived from the product rule by writing f = f/g ⋅g .

Question set for this topic

Part 1 · Hook

Why this matters

Fuel economy is distance divided by fuel; a medicine's concentration is the amount of drug divided by the volume of blood it spreads through. When the top and the bottom of a fraction both change, the fraction changes in a way neither part shows alone. The quotient rule gives that rate.

Part 5 · Step by step

How it works, step by step

  1. Write q=fgq = \frac{f}{g} as f=qgf = q\,g→the product rule gives f′=q′g+qg′f' = q'g + qg'
  2. Solve for q′q' and replace qq with fg\frac{f}{g}→q′=f′g−fg′g2q' = \frac{f'g - fg'}{g^2}
  3. The numerator is a difference→the order matters: derivative of the top times the bottom comes first
  4. The denominator is a single power of xx→rewriting as a sum of powers is often faster than the quotient rule

Part 6 · Key ideas

Key ideas

  • The quotient rule: ddx(f(x)g(x))\frac{d}{dx}\left( \frac{f(x)}{g(x)} \right)=f′(x)g(x)−f(x)g′(x)(g(x))2= \frac{f'(x)g(x) - f(x)g'(x)}{\left( g(x) \right)^2}, where g(x)≠0g(x) \ne 0.
  • Memory aid: "low d-high minus high d-low, over low squared."
  • The order in the numerator matters: swapping it flips the sign of the answer.
  • The derivative of a quotient is NOT the quotient of the derivatives.
  • For 3x2−5x\frac{3x^2 - 5}{x}, divide first: 3x−5x−13x - 5x^{-1}, then use the power rule.

Part 7 · Misconception

A common mistake

The wrong idea: ddx(fg)=fg′−f′gg2\frac{d}{dx}\left( \frac{f}{g} \right) = \frac{fg' - f'g}{g^2}, or f′g′\frac{f'}{g'}.

What actually happens: The numerator starts with the derivative of the top: f′g−fg′f'g - fg'. Reversing it gives the negative of the right answer. And f′g′\frac{f'}{g'} ignores how each part's size affects the other: x2x=x\frac{x^2}{x} = x has derivative 1, but 2x1=2x\frac{2x}{1} = 2x.

4. Which is an equation of the line tangent to the graph of q(x)=f(x)g(x)q(x) = \frac{f(x)}{g(x)} at x=2x = 2?

  1. y=2+72(x−2)y = 2 + \frac{7}{2}(x - 2)
  2. y=2−72(x−2)y = 2 - \frac{7}{2}(x - 2)
  3. y=72+2(x−2)y = \frac{7}{2} + 2(x - 2)
  4. y=2−13(x−2)y = 2 - \frac{1}{3}(x - 2)
Show the answer

q(2)=42=2q(2) = \frac{4}{2} = 2 and q′(2)=72q'(2) = \frac{7}{2}.

  • Correct: y=2+72(x−2)y = 2 + \frac{7}{2}(x - 2): Height 2, slope 72\frac{7}{2}.
  • y=2−72(x−2)y = 2 - \frac{7}{2}(x - 2): The slope's sign comes from a reversed numerator.

5. A student writes ddx(xx+1)\frac{d}{dx}\left( \frac{x}{x + 1} \right)=(1)(x+1)−x(1)x+1= \frac{(1)(x + 1) - x(1)}{x + 1}=1x+1= \frac{1}{x + 1}. What is the error?

  1. The denominator should be (x+1)2(x + 1)^2.
  2. The numerator should be x(1)−(1)(x+1)x(1) - (1)(x + 1).
  3. The answer should be 11\frac{1}{1}, the derivatives of the top and bottom.
  4. There is no error.
Show the answer

The quotient rule divides by the square of the bottom: the derivative is 1(x+1)2\frac{1}{(x + 1)^2}.

  • Correct: The denominator should be (x+1)2(x + 1)^2.: The bottom is squared.
  • The numerator should be x(1)−(1)(x+1)x(1) - (1)(x + 1).: The student's numerator order is right: derivative of the top comes first.
  • The answer should be 11\frac{1}{1}, the derivatives of the top and bottom.: The quotient of the derivatives is not the derivative of the quotient.
  • There is no error.: The missing square changes the answer at every xx except where x+1=±1x + 1 = \pm 1.

6. Which formula is the quotient rule for ddx(f(x)g(x))\frac{d}{dx}\left( \frac{f(x)}{g(x)} \right)?

  1. f′(x)g(x)−f(x)g′(x)(g(x))2\frac{f'(x)g(x) - f(x)g'(x)}{\left( g(x) \right)^2}
  2. f(x)g′(x)−f′(x)g(x)(g(x))2\frac{f(x)g'(x) - f'(x)g(x)}{\left( g(x) \right)^2}
  3. f′(x)g(x)−f(x)g′(x)(f(x))2\frac{f'(x)g(x) - f(x)g'(x)}{\left( f(x) \right)^2}
  4. f′(x)g(x)+f(x)g′(x)(g(x))2\frac{f'(x)g(x) + f(x)g'(x)}{\left( g(x) \right)^2}
Show the answer

Low d-high minus high d-low, over low squared.

  • Correct: f′(x)g(x)−f(x)g′(x)(g(x))2\frac{f'(x)g(x) - f(x)g'(x)}{\left( g(x) \right)^2}: Derivative of the top times the bottom first; the bottom squared.
  • f(x)g′(x)−f′(x)g(x)(g(x))2\frac{f(x)g'(x) - f'(x)g(x)}{\left( g(x) \right)^2}: The numerator is reversed, which flips the sign.
  • f′(x)g(x)−f(x)g′(x)(f(x))2\frac{f'(x)g(x) - f(x)g'(x)}{\left( f(x) \right)^2}: The denominator is the bottom squared, not the top.
  • f′(x)g(x)+f(x)g′(x)(g(x))2\frac{f'(x)g(x) + f(x)g'(x)}{\left( g(x) \right)^2}: A quotient has a minus sign in the numerator; the plus belongs to the product rule.

Part 9 · Summary

Summary

The derivative of fg\frac{f}{g} is f′g−fg′g2\frac{f'g - fg'}{g^2}, derived from the product rule by writing f=fg⋅gf = \frac{f}{g} \cdot g. Keep the numerator in order and square the denominator. When the denominator is a single term, dividing first and using the power rule is often simpler.
