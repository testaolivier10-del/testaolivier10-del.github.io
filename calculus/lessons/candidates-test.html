The Candidates Test

On a closed interval, a continuous function has absolute extrema, and they sit at endpoints or critical points.

Practice 3: Justification

Question set for this topic

Part 1 · Hook

Why this matters

A water company must know the lowest level its reservoir will reach this month, not just a dip that looks low. A relative minimum answers "lower than nearby"; the company needs "lowest of all". On a closed interval there is a short, sure way to find it: list a few candidates and compare.

Part 5 · Step by step

How it works, step by step

  1. ff is continuous on [a,b][a, b]→by the Extreme Value Theorem, an absolute maximum and minimum exist
  2. An absolute extremum is either at an endpoint or inside→if inside, it is at a critical point, where f′f' is 0 or undefined
  3. So the endpoints and critical points are the only candidates→evaluate ff at each one and compare: the largest value is the absolute maximum, the smallest the absolute minimum
  4. You only showed a sign change of f′f'→that proves a relative extremum, a local fact, not an absolute one

Part 6 · Key ideas

Key ideas

  • Candidates Test (closed interval method): for ff continuous on [a,b][a, b], list the endpoints and the critical points in (a,b)(a, b), compute ff at each, and compare.
  • No derivative signs needed: just values. Show the table of candidates and values in your answer.
  • The absolute extrema can be at endpoints. A relative maximum can lose to an endpoint.
  • On a calculator problem, find critical points with the graph or solver, store them, and evaluate the model at each candidate.

Part 7 · Misconception

A common mistake

The wrong idea: "f′f' changes from positive to negative at x=cx = c, so f(c)f(c) is the absolute maximum."

What actually happens: A sign change is a local argument: it only compares f(c)f(c) with nearby values. For an absolute extremum on a closed interval, compare the values at the endpoints and every critical point. Chief readers see this lost point every year.

1. What is the absolute maximum value of ff on [0,6][0, 6]?

No calculator. Type the exact value (a fraction, π, e or a root is fine).

Show the answer

Candidates: endpoints x=0,6x = 0, 6 and critical points x=1,3,5x = 1, 3, 5. Values: 4,7,−2,1,54, 7, -2, 1, 5. The largest is f(1)=7f(1) = 7.

  • Answer: 7

2. Where does ff have its absolute minimum on [0,6][0, 6]?

  1. At x=0x = 0, the left endpoint
  2. At x=3x = 3
  3. At x=5x = 5, where f′(x)=0f'(x) = 0
  4. Nowhere, because f′(3)f'(3) is undefined
Show the answer

Of the candidate values 4,7,−2,1,54, 7, -2, 1, 5, the smallest is f(3)=−2f(3) = -2, so the absolute minimum is at x=3x = 3.

  • At x=0x = 0, the left endpoint: f(0)=4f(0) = 4 is not the smallest.
  • Correct: At x=3x = 3: f(3)=−2f(3) = -2 is the smallest of the five candidate values.
  • At x=5x = 5, where f′(x)=0f'(x) = 0: f(5)=1f(5) = 1 is larger than f(3)=−2f(3) = -2.
  • Nowhere, because f′(3)f'(3) is undefined: A critical point where f′f' is undefined is still a candidate, and here it wins.

3. Which list is the complete set of candidates for the absolute extrema of ff on [0,6][0, 6]?

  1. x=0,1,3,5,6x = 0, 1, 3, 5, 6
  2. x=1,3,5x = 1, 3, 5, the critical points
  3. x=0x = 0 and x=6x = 6, the endpoints
  4. x=1x = 1 and x=5x = 5, where f′(x)=0f'(x) = 0
Show the answer

On a closed interval, the candidates are the endpoints and the critical points (where f′f' is 0 or undefined): x=0,1,3,5,6x = 0, 1, 3, 5, 6.

  • Correct: x=0,1,3,5,6x = 0, 1, 3, 5, 6: Both endpoints and every critical point, including where f′f' is undefined.
  • x=1,3,5x = 1, 3, 5, the critical points: The endpoints are missing; an absolute extremum can sit at an end.
  • x=0x = 0 and x=6x = 6, the endpoints: The critical points are missing.
  • x=1x = 1 and x=5x = 5, where f′(x)=0f'(x) = 0: x=3x = 3, where f′f' is undefined, and both endpoints are missing.

4. Which justification earns credit for "the absolute maximum of ff on [0,6][0, 6] is 7"?

  1. "f′f' changes from positive to negative at x=1x = 1, so f(1)=7f(1) = 7 is the absolute maximum."
  2. "f′(1)=0f'(1) = 0, so f(1)=7f(1) = 7 is the absolute maximum on [0,6][0, 6]."
  3. "f(1)=7f(1) = 7 is greater than both endpoint values, f(0)=4f(0) = 4 and f(6)=5f(6) = 5."
  4. "The candidates x=0,1,3,5,6x = 0, 1, 3, 5, 6 give f=4,7,−2,1,5f = 4, 7, -2, 1, 5. The largest is 7, at x=1x = 1."
Show the answer

An absolute extremum needs a global argument. The Candidates Test lists every endpoint and critical point with its value and picks the largest.

  • "f′f' changes from positive to negative at x=1x = 1, so f(1)=7f(1) = 7 is the absolute maximum.": A sign change gives a relative maximum, a local fact. It does not compare f(1)f(1) with far-away values.
  • "f′(1)=0f'(1) = 0, so f(1)=7f(1) = 7 is the absolute maximum on [0,6][0, 6].": A zero of f′f' is only a candidate.
  • "f(1)=7f(1) = 7 is greater than both endpoint values, f(0)=4f(0) = 4 and f(6)=5f(6) = 5.": Close, but the other critical points must be compared too.
  • Correct: "The candidates x=0,1,3,5,6x = 0, 1, 3, 5, 6 give f=4,7,−2,1,5f = 4, 7, -2, 1, 5. The largest is 7, at x=1x = 1.": A candidates test: every endpoint and critical point, with values, compared.

Part 9 · Summary

Summary

On a closed interval, a continuous function has absolute extrema, and they sit at endpoints or critical points. The Candidates Test evaluates ff at each of those and compares. A sign change of f′f' shows only a relative extremum; an absolute one needs the comparison.
