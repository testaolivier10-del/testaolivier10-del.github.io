Disc Method About Other Lines

To revolve a region about a line y = k or x = h that it touches all the way across, use discs whose radius is the distance from that line to the curve: V = π∫ from a to b of (k − f(x))² dx (or the mirror image).

Part 1 · Hook

Why this matters

A lathe can spin a piece of wood around any center line the machinist chooses, not just the edge of the table. Revolving a region about a line like y=1y = 1 or x=3x = 3 works the same way as revolving it about an axis. The only change is where you measure the radius from.

Part 5 · Step by step

How it works, step by step

  1. The axis of rotation is the line y=ky = k (or x=hx = h)→every slice is a disc centered on that line
  2. The region touches that line all the way across→each rectangle reaches from the line to the curve and sweeps out a solid disc
  3. The radius is the distance from the line to the curve→r=k−f(x)r = k - f(x) if the line is above the curve, f(x)−kf(x) - k if it is below
  4. Square the whole radius and multiply by π\pi→VV=π∫ab(f(x)−k)2dx= \pi\int_a^b \left(f(x) - k\right)^2\,dx

Part 6 · Key ideas

Key ideas

  • Revolving about a line: the disc method still applies; only the radius changes.
  • The radius is a distance from the axis: larger value minus smaller value. With the line y=ky = k above the curve, r=k−f(x)r = k - f(x); below it, r=f(x)−kr = f(x) - k.
  • With a negative kk, subtract carefully: about y=−2y = -2, rr=f(x)−(−2)= f(x) - (-2)=f(x)+2= f(x) + 2.
  • A vertical line x=hx = h gives horizontal discs: r=h−x(y)r = h - x(y) or x(y)−hx(y) - h, integrated in dydy.
  • Square (k−f(x))(k - f(x)) as a whole; k2−f(x)2k^2 - f(x)^2 is a different number.

Part 7 · Misconception

A common mistake

The wrong idea: The radius is always the curve's height f(x)f(x), whatever the axis.

What actually happens: f(x)f(x) is the distance from the xx-axis. When the axis is y=ky = k, measure from that line: r=|f(x)−k|r = |f(x) - k|. For the region between y=x2y = x^2 and y=1y = 1 about y=1y = 1, the radius is 1−x21 - x^2, not x2x^2.

2. Which integral gives the volume of the solid formed when RR is revolved about the line y=2y = 2?

  1. π∫04(2−x)2dx\pi\int_0^4 \left(2 - \sqrt{x}\right)^2\,dx
  2. π∫04(22−(x)2)dx\pi\int_0^4 \left(2^2 - (\sqrt{x})^2\right)\,dx
  3. π∫04(x)2dx\pi\int_0^4 \left(\sqrt{x}\right)^2\,dx
  4. π∫02(2−x)2dx\pi\int_0^2 \left(2 - \sqrt{x}\right)^2\,dx
Show the answer

RR touches the line y=2y = 2 all the way across, so the slices are discs of radius 2−x2 - \sqrt{x}, for 0≤x≤40 \le x \le 4.

  • Correct: π∫04(2−x)2dx\pi\int_0^4 \left(2 - \sqrt{x}\right)^2\,dx: Square the distance from the axis to the curve.
  • π∫04(22−(x)2)dx\pi\int_0^4 \left(2^2 - (\sqrt{x})^2\right)\,dx: This squares each height separately. It measures from the xx-axis, which is not the axis here.
  • π∫04(x)2dx\pi\int_0^4 \left(\sqrt{x}\right)^2\,dx: That radius is measured from the xx-axis.
  • π∫02(2−x)2dx\pi\int_0^2 \left(2 - \sqrt{x}\right)^2\,dx: The disc's thickness is dxdx, so the limits are xx-values, 0 to 4.

5. When RR is revolved about the line y=2y = 2, where is the largest disc?

  1. At x=0x = 0, where the radius is 2
  2. At x=4x = 4, where x=2\sqrt{x} = 2
  3. At x=1x = 1, where x=1\sqrt{x} = 1
  4. At x=2x = 2, halfway across RR
Show the answer

The radius 2−x2 - \sqrt{x} is largest where x\sqrt{x} is smallest, at x=0x = 0: r=2r = 2.

  • Correct: At x=0x = 0, where the radius is 2: 2−0=22 - \sqrt{0} = 2, the farthest the curve gets from the axis.
  • At x=4x = 4, where x=2\sqrt{x} = 2: At x=4x = 4 the curve meets the axis, so the radius there is 0.
  • At x=1x = 1, where x=1\sqrt{x} = 1: There the radius is 2−1=12 - 1 = 1, smaller than at x=0x = 0.
  • At x=2x = 2, halfway across RR: There the radius is 2−2≈0.592 - \sqrt{2} \approx 0.59.

  1. 4−x24 - x^2

The curve is above the axis: radius =(2−x2)−(−2)= (2 - x^2) - (-2)=4−x2= 4 - x^2.

  • Correct: 4−x24 - x^2: Curve minus axis, with the minus sign on −2-2 handled.

Part 9 · Summary

Summary

To revolve a region about a line y=ky = k or x=hx = h that it touches all the way across, use discs whose radius is the distance from that line to the curve: VV=π∫ab(k−f(x))2dx= \pi\int_a^b \left(k - f(x)\right)^2\,dx (or the mirror image). Measure from the axis, subtract in the order that makes the radius positive, and square the whole radius.
