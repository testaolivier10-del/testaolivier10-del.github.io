Solving Related Rates Problems

To solve a related rates problem: draw and label, write an equation linking the quantities at every instant, differentiate it with respect to time, then substitute the instant's values and solve for the unknown rate.

Practice 1: Implementing Mathematical ProcessesPractice 3: Justification

Part 1 · Hook

Why this matters

Firefighters raise ladders, tanks fill and drain, shadows stretch as you walk away from a streetlight. In each case one rate is easy to measure and another is the one you care about. A related rates problem connects them with geometry and the chain rule, and the same five steps solve every one.

Part 3 · Prerequisite check

Quick check before you start

1. Differentiating x2+y2=100x^2 + y^2 = 100 with respect to tt gives

  1. 2xdxdt+2ydydt=02x\frac{dx}{dt} + 2y\frac{dy}{dt} = 0
  2. 2x+2y=02x + 2y = 0
  3. 2xdxdt+2ydydt=1002x\frac{dx}{dt} + 2y\frac{dy}{dt} = 100
  4. 2x+2ydydx=02x + 2y\frac{dy}{dx} = 0
Show the answer

Each variable depends on tt; the constant's derivative is 0.

  • Correct: 2xdxdt+2ydydt=02x\frac{dx}{dt} + 2y\frac{dy}{dt} = 0:
  • 2x+2y=02x + 2y = 0:
  • 2xdxdt+2ydydt=1002x\frac{dx}{dt} + 2y\frac{dy}{dt} = 100:
  • 2x+2ydydx=02x + 2y\frac{dy}{dx} = 0:

2. Similar triangles have legs 3 and 6 and rr and hh. Which is true?

  1. rh=36\frac{r}{h} = \frac{3}{6}
  2. r+h=9r + h = 9
  3. r⋅h=18r \cdot h = 18
  4. r−h=3r - h = 3
Show the answer

Corresponding sides are proportional.

  • Correct: rh=36\frac{r}{h} = \frac{3}{6}:
  • r+h=9r + h = 9:

Part 5 · Step by step

How it works, step by step

  1. Draw the situation and name every changing quantity→each named quantity is a function of time tt; fixed lengths stay numbers
  2. Find an equation linking the quantities at every instant (Pythagoras, similar triangles, a volume formula)→eliminate any extra variable with a second relation first, such as r = h/2
  3. Differentiate both sides with respect to tt→the chain rule gives an equation among the rates
  4. Substitute the values of the instant, with signs for increasing or decreasing→solve for the unknown rate and state it with units

Part 6 · Key ideas

Key ideas

  • The five steps: draw and label, relate, differentiate with respect to tt, substitute, answer with units.
  • Constants of the problem (a 10-foot ladder, a 6-foot person) stay numbers; quantities that change get letters.
  • Use similar triangles to remove an extra variable before differentiating, so only one unknown rate remains.
  • Shrinking or approaching means a negative rate. Report the answer in words too: "the top slides down at 1.5 ft/s".

Part 7 · Misconception

A common mistake

The wrong idea: Substitute the given values first, to make the equation simpler, then differentiate.

What actually happens: The values hold at one instant only. Substituted early they become constants with derivative 0, and the rates disappear. Differentiate the general relation first.

A ladder sliding down a wall

1. The foot of the 10-foot ladder slides away from the wall at 2 feet per second. How fast is the top moving when the foot is 6 feet from the wall? Include units.

No calculator. Type the exact value (a fraction, π, e or a root is fine), then its unit.

Show the answer

x2+y2=100x^2 + y^2 = 100 gives 2xdxdt+2ydydt=02x\frac{dx}{dt} + 2y\frac{dy}{dt} = 0. At x=6x = 6, y=8y = 8: 2(6)(2)+2(8)dydt=02(6)(2) + 2(8)\frac{dy}{dt} = 0, so dydt=−32\frac{dy}{dt} = -\frac{3}{2} feet per second. The top slides down at 1.5 ft/s.

  • Answer: −32\frac{-3}{2} ≈ -1.500 feet per second

2. How fast is the top of the ladder moving when the foot is 8 feet from the wall? Include units.

No calculator. Type the exact value (a fraction, π, e or a root is fine), then its unit.

Show the answer

At x=8x = 8, y=100−64=6y = \sqrt{100 - 64} = 6. Then 2(8)(2)+2(6)dydt=02(8)(2) + 2(6)\frac{dy}{dt} = 0, so dydt=−3212=−83\frac{dy}{dt} = -\frac{32}{12} = -\frac{8}{3} feet per second.

  • Answer: −83\frac{-8}{3} ≈ -2.667 feet per second

3. A student writes 62+y2=1006^2 + y^2 = 100 first and then differentiates. Why does that lose the answer?

  1. 626^2 is a constant, so the term with dxdt=2\frac{dx}{dt} = 2 disappears
  2. yy is a constant, so dydt=0\frac{dy}{dt} = 0
  3. The equation 62+y2=1006^2 + y^2 = 100 is false at this instant
  4. The derivative of 100 should be 10
Show the answer

The relation x2+y2=100x^2 + y^2 = 100 holds at every instant; x=6x = 6 holds at one. Differentiate the general relation, then substitute the instant.

  • Correct: 626^2 is a constant, so the term with dxdt=2\frac{dx}{dt} = 2 disappears: The foot's motion is what drives the top; substituting x=6x = 6 first throws it away.
  • yy is a constant, so dydt=0\frac{dy}{dt} = 0: yy still changes; it is xx that was frozen.
  • The equation 62+y2=1006^2 + y^2 = 100 is false at this instant: It is true at this instant (y=8y = 8); it is just not true at other instants.
  • The derivative of 100 should be 10: 100 is a constant, so its derivative is 0.

Water filling an inverted cone

4. Which relation between the water's radius rr and depth hh holds at every instant?

  1. h=r2h = \frac{r}{2}
  2. r=h−3r = h - 3
  3. r=h2r = \frac{h}{2}
  4. r=3r = 3
Show the answer

The water is a cone similar to the tank: rh=36\frac{r}{h} = \frac{3}{6}, so r=h2r = \frac{h}{2} at every depth.

  • h=r2h = \frac{r}{2}: The ratio is upside down: the depth is twice the radius, not half of it.
  • r=h−3r = h - 3: Similar triangles give a ratio, not a difference.
  • Correct: r=h2r = \frac{h}{2}: Similar triangles: rh=36\frac{r}{h} = \frac{3}{6}.
  • r=3r = 3: 3 ft is the radius of the full tank; the water's radius changes with hh.

5. Water is poured in at 4 cubic feet per minute. How fast is the water level rising when the water is 2 feet deep? Include units.

No calculator. Type the exact value (a fraction, π, e or a root is fine), then its unit.

Show the answer

V=πh312V = \frac{\pi h^3}{12}, so dVdt=πh24dhdt\frac{dV}{dt} = \frac{\pi h^2}{4}\frac{dh}{dt}. At h=2h = 2: 4=πdhdt4 = \pi\frac{dh}{dt}, so dhdt=4π≈1.27\frac{dh}{dt} = \frac{4}{\pi} \approx 1.27 feet per minute.

  • Answer: 4π\frac{4}{\pi} ≈ 1.273 feet per minute

6. In a related rates problem, which step comes right after writing an equation that links the changing quantities?

  1. Substitute the values given for that instant first
  2. Differentiate both sides with respect to time
  3. Solve the equation for one variable and stop
  4. Set each rate equal to 0
Show the answer

Draw and label, write the relation, differentiate with respect to tt, substitute the instant, solve, and answer with units.

  • Substitute the values given for that instant first: Substituting first turns changing quantities into constants.
  • Correct: Differentiate both sides with respect to time: Then substitute the values of the instant.
  • Solve the equation for one variable and stop: The question asks for a rate, which needs a derivative.
  • Set each rate equal to 0: Rates are what the problem is about; they are rarely 0.
