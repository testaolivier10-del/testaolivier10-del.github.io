Area Between Curves That Cross More Than Twice

When curves cross more than twice, top and bottom swap at each crossing.

Practice 1: Implementing Mathematical Processes

Part 1 · Hook

Why this matters

Two runners' speed graphs weave over and under each other during a race. The space between the graphs on a stretch where one runner is faster is real area, and so is the space where the other is faster. One integral would let those pieces cancel; to count all of the area, you have to cut the integral where the graphs cross.

Part 5 · Step by step

How it works, step by step

  1. Two graphs cross inside the interval→the curve on top changes at each crossing
  2. Where gg is on top, f(x)−g(x)<0f(x) - g(x) < 0→one integral of f−gf - g counts that piece as negative and cancels area
  3. Split the integral at every crossing and use top minus bottom on each piece→every piece is positive, and their sum is the area
  4. Top minus bottom on each piece is |f(x)−g(x)||f(x) - g(x)|→the area is also ∫ab|f(x)−g(x)|dx\int_a^b |f(x) - g(x)|\,dx, which a calculator can evaluate in one step

Part 6 · Key ideas

Key ideas

  • Splitting the integral: find every crossing inside the interval by solving f(x)=g(x)f(x) = g(x), then integrate top minus bottom on each piece and add.
  • One integral ∫ab(f−g)dx\int_a^b (f - g)\,dx is a net value: areas where gg is on top are subtracted.
  • Area =∫ab|f(x)−g(x)|dx= \int_a^b |f(x) - g(x)|\,dx. On a calculator, this is the fastest setup.
  • Crossings outside the interval do not create pieces, and the interval's ends are not crossings.
  • The same rule holds in yy: split where the right and left edges swap.

Part 7 · Misconception

A common mistake

The wrong idea: If the integral of f−gf - g comes out negative or zero, take its absolute value to get the area.

What actually happens: By the time you take the absolute value, the pieces have already canceled. Split at each crossing first (or put the absolute value inside the integral), then add the positive pieces.

The graphs of f(x)=x3−3x2f(x) = x^3 - 3x^2 and g(x)=−2xg(x) = -2x for 0≤x≤30 \le x \le 3

1. Solve f(x)=g(x)f(x) = g(x) to find where the graphs cross on [0,3][0, 3].

  1. x=0x = 0, x=1x = 1 and x=2x = 2
  2. x=0x = 0 and x=3x = 3
  3. x=0x = 0 and x=2x = 2
  4. x=1x = 1, x=2x = 2 and x=3x = 3
Show the answer

x3−3x2=−2xx^3 - 3x^2 = -2x gives x3−3x2x^3 - 3x^2+2x+ 2x=x(x−1)(x−2)= x(x - 1)(x - 2)=0= 0.

  • Correct: x=0x = 0, x=1x = 1 and x=2x = 2: All three roots of x(x−1)(x−2)x(x - 1)(x - 2) lie in [0,3][0, 3].
  • x=0x = 0 and x=3x = 3: At x=3x = 3, f(3)=0f(3) = 0 and g(3)=−6g(3) = -6: the graphs do not meet there. x=3x = 3 is a boundary of the region, not a crossing.
  • x=0x = 0 and x=2x = 2: This misses x=1x = 1, where f(1)=−2=g(1)f(1) = -2 = g(1).
  • x=1x = 1, x=2x = 2 and x=3x = 3: x=3x = 3 is not a crossing, and x=0x = 0 is: f(0)=g(0)=0f(0) = g(0) = 0.

6. On [a,c][a, c] the graphs of ff and gg cross once, at x=bx = b, with ff on top on [a,b][a, b]. Which expressions give the area between them on [a,c][a, c]? Select all that apply.

  1. ∫ac|f(x)−g(x)|dx\int_a^c \left|f(x) - g(x)\right|\,dx
  2. ∫ab(f−g)dx\int_a^b (f - g)\,dx+∫bc(g−f)dx+ \int_b^c (g - f)\,dx
  3. |∫ac(f(x)−g(x))dx|\left|\int_a^c \left(f(x) - g(x)\right)\,dx\right|
  4. ∫ac(f(x)−g(x))dx\int_a^c \left(f(x) - g(x)\right)\,dx
Show the answer

The absolute value inside the integral makes every height positive, which is what splitting at bb does by hand.

  • Correct: ∫ac|f(x)−g(x)|dx\int_a^c \left|f(x) - g(x)\right|\,dx: True: |f−g||f - g| is the height of every strip, whichever curve is on top.
  • Correct: ∫ab(f−g)dx\int_a^b (f - g)\,dx+∫bc(g−f)dx+ \int_b^c (g - f)\,dx: True: top minus bottom on each side of the crossing.

Part 9 · Summary

Summary

When curves cross more than twice, top and bottom swap at each crossing. Find the crossings, split the integral there, use top minus bottom on each piece, and add: the area is ∫ab|f(x)−g(x)|dx\int_a^b |f(x) - g(x)|\,dx. A single integral of f−gf - g gives a net amount, not the area.
