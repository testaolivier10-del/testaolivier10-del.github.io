Derivatives of Inverse Functions

The graph of an inverse is a reflection in y = x , which swaps rise and run, so slopes become reciprocals at matching points: (f⁻¹)′(a) = 1/(f′(f⁻¹(a))) .

Practice 1: Implementing Mathematical ProcessesPractice 2: Connecting Representations

Part 1 · Hook

Why this matters

If you know how fast a function climbs, you also know how fast its inverse climbs: undo the function and the steepness flips. A thermometer that converts a voltage to a temperature, or a calculator's square root key, is an inverse function, and this topic finds its slope without a formula for it.

Part 5 · Step by step

How it works, step by step

  1. The graph of g=f−1g = f^{-1} is the graph of ff reflected in y=xy = x→the point (a,b)(a, b) on ff becomes (b,a)(b, a) on gg
  2. Reflection in y=xy = x swaps rise and run→a tangent line of slope mm becomes one of slope 1m\frac{1}{m}
  3. So the slope of gg at bb is the reciprocal of the slope of ff at a=g(b)a = g(b)→g′(b)=1f′(g(b))g'(b) = \frac{1}{f'(g(b))}
  4. Where f′(a)=0f'(a) = 0→the tangent line of gg at bb is vertical and g′(b)g'(b) does not exist

Part 6 · Key ideas

Key ideas

  • The rule: g′(x)=1f′(g(x))g'(x) = \frac{1}{f'(g(x))} when gg is the inverse of ff and f′(g(x))≠0f'(g(x)) \ne 0.
  • Two steps: first find g(x)g(x), the input of ff that gives xx; then take the reciprocal of f′f' there.
  • From a table, find the given number in the f(x)f(x) row, read its xx, then read f′f' in that column.
  • It comes from the chain rule: differentiate f(g(x))=xf(g(x)) = x to get f′(g(x))⋅g′(x)=1f'(g(x)) \cdot g'(x) = 1.

Part 7 · Misconception

A common mistake

The wrong idea: If f(2)=4f(2) = 4 and g=f−1g = f^{-1}, then g′(4)=1f′(4)g'(4) = \frac{1}{f'(4)}.

What actually happens: The input of f′f' must be g(4)=2g(4) = 2, the xx where ff gives 4: g′(4)=1f′(2)g'(4) = \frac{1}{f'(2)}. Reading f′f' at 4 uses the wrong point of the graph.

1. The function gg is the inverse of ff. Find g′(4)g'(4).

No calculator. Type the exact value (a fraction, π, e or a root is fine).

Show the answer

g(4)=2g(4) = 2 because f(2)=4f(2) = 4. Then g′(4)g'(4)=1f′(g(4))= \frac{1}{f'(g(4))}=1f′(2)= \frac{1}{f'(2)}=12= \frac{1}{2}.

  • Answer: 12\frac{1}{2} ≈ 0.500

2. What is g′(7)g'(7)?

  1. 14\frac{1}{4}
  2. 44
  3. 17\frac{1}{7}
  4. 13\frac{1}{3}
Show the answer

Find g(7)g(7) from the table: f(3)=7f(3) = 7, so g(7)=3g(7) = 3. Then g′(7)=1f′(3)=14g'(7) = \frac{1}{f'(3)} = \frac{1}{4}.

  • Correct: 14\frac{1}{4}: g(7)=3g(7) = 3 and f′(3)=4f'(3) = 4, so g′(7)=14g'(7) = \frac{1}{4}.
  • 44: This is f′(3)f'(3); the slope of the inverse is its reciprocal.
  • 17\frac{1}{7}: This is 17\frac{1}{7}, the reciprocal of the input. The rule uses f′f' at g(7)g(7).
  • 13\frac{1}{3}: This is 1g(7)\frac{1}{g(7)}. The rule takes the reciprocal of a slope, f′(g(7))f'(g(7)), not of a value.

3. Which is an equation of the line tangent to the graph of gg at x=3x = 3?

  1. y−1=2(x−3)y - 1 = 2(x - 3)
  2. y−1=12(x−3)y - 1 = \frac{1}{2}(x - 3)
  3. y−3=12(x−1)y - 3 = \frac{1}{2}(x - 1)
  4. y−1=14(x−3)y - 1 = \frac{1}{4}(x - 3)
Show the answer

f(1)=3f(1) = 3 gives g(3)=1g(3) = 1, and g′(3)=1f′(1)=12g'(3) = \frac{1}{f'(1)} = \frac{1}{2}. So y−1=12(x−3)y - 1 = \frac{1}{2}(x - 3).

  • y−1=2(x−3)y - 1 = 2(x - 3): The slope 2 is f′(1)f'(1); the inverse needs its reciprocal.
  • Correct: y−1=12(x−3)y - 1 = \frac{1}{2}(x - 3): The point is (3,g(3))=(3,1)(3, g(3)) = (3, 1) and the slope is 1f′(1)=12\frac{1}{f'(1)} = \frac{1}{2}.
  • y−3=12(x−1)y - 3 = \frac{1}{2}(x - 1): This is a line through (1,3)(1, 3), a point of ff. On gg the coordinates swap: (3,1)(3, 1).
  • y−1=14(x−3)y - 1 = \frac{1}{4}(x - 3): This uses f′(3)=4f'(3) = 4. Read f′f' at g(3)=1g(3) = 1, not at 3.

6. A one-to-one differentiable function ff has f(2)=5f(2) = 5, f′(2)=−3f'(2) = -3, f(5)=1f(5) = 1 and f′(5)=8f'(5) = 8. What is (f−1)′(5)\left(f^{-1}\right)'(5)?

  1. 18\frac{1}{8}
  2. −13-\frac{1}{3}
  3. −3-3
  4. 12\frac{1}{2}
Show the answer

f(2)=5f(2) = 5 means f−1(5)=2f^{-1}(5) = 2. Then (f−1)′(5)\left(f^{-1}\right)'(5)=1f′(2)= \frac{1}{f'(2)}=−13= -\frac{1}{3}.

  • 18\frac{1}{8}: This is 1f′(5)\frac{1}{f'(5)}. The input 5 is an output of ff; the matching input is 2.
  • Correct: −13-\frac{1}{3}: f−1(5)=2f^{-1}(5) = 2, so the slope is 1f′(2)=−13\frac{1}{f'(2)} = -\frac{1}{3}.
  • −3-3: This is f′(2)f'(2); the inverse's slope is the reciprocal.
  • 12\frac{1}{2}: This is 1f−1(5)\frac{1}{f^{-1}(5)}, the reciprocal of a value instead of a slope.

Part 9 · Summary

Summary
