Unit 2 · Topic 2.6

Constant, Sum, Difference and Constant Multiple Rules

Constants added to a function disappear when you differentiate; constant factors stay.

Practice 1: Implementing Mathematical Processes

Part 1 · Hook

Why this matters

Two pumps fill a pool, one adding 30 liters a minute and the other 45. Together they add 75 liters a minute: rates add. Triple the first pump's output and its rate triples. These everyday facts are the sum and constant multiple rules, and with the power rule they differentiate every polynomial.

Part 5 · Step by step

How it works, step by step

  1. A constant function has a flat graph→its derivative is 0: ddx(c)=0\frac{d}{dx}(c) = 0
  2. Multiplying ff by kk stretches every rise by kk while runs stay the same→every slope is multiplied by kk: (kf)′=kf′(kf)' = kf'
  3. The change in f+gf + g is the change in ff plus the change in gg→rates add: (f+g)′=f′+g′(f + g)' = f' + g', and (f−g)′=f′−g′(f - g)' = f' - g'
  4. A polynomial is a sum of constant multiples of powers of xx→differentiate it term by term with the power rule

Part 6 · Key ideas

Key ideas

  • Constant rule: ddx(c)=0\frac{d}{dx}(c) = 0. Adding a constant shifts a graph up or down and changes no slope.
  • Constant multiple rule: ddx(kf(x))=kf′(x)\frac{d}{dx}\left( kf(x) \right) = kf'(x).
  • Sum and difference rules: ddx(f(x)±g(x))\frac{d}{dx}\left( f(x) \pm g(x) \right)=f′(x)±g′(x)= f'(x) \pm g'(x).
  • Together with the power rule: ddx(4x3−5x+2)\frac{d}{dx}\left( 4x^3 - 5x + 2 \right)=12x2−5= 12x^2 - 5.
  • These rules work on values too: if h=3f−2gh = 3f - 2g, then h′(2)=3f′(2)−2g′(2)h'(2) = 3f'(2) - 2g'(2).

Part 7 · Misconception

A common mistake

The wrong idea: ddx(3x2+7)=6x+7\frac{d}{dx}\left( 3x^2 + 7 \right) = 6x + 7.

What actually happens: The 7 is a constant term, and the derivative of a constant is 0: adding 7 lifts the graph without changing any slope. So the derivative is 6x6x. A constant factor stays (ddx(7x)=7\frac{d}{dx}(7x) = 7); a constant term disappears.

5. What is ddx(π2)\frac{d}{dx}\left( \pi^2 \right)?

  1. 00
  2. 2π2\pi
  3. π2\pi^2
  4. 2πx2\pi x
Show the answer

π2≈9.87\pi^2 \approx 9.87 is a constant. Its graph is a level line, so its derivative is 0.

  • Correct: 00: A constant has derivative 0.
  • 2π2\pi: That runs the power rule on π\pi, but π\pi is a number, not the variable xx.
  • π2\pi^2: That is the function itself, not its slope.
  • 2πx2\pi x: The variable is xx, and π2\pi^2 does not contain it.

6. A student writes ddx(3x2+7)=6x+7\frac{d}{dx}\left( 3x^2 + 7 \right) = 6x + 7. What is the correct derivative?

  1. 6x6x
  2. 6x+76x + 7
  3. 3x+73x + 7
  4. 6x+16x + 1
Show the answer

The constant term 7 has derivative 0. Adding 7 lifts the graph without changing a slope.

  • Correct: 6x6x: Power rule on 3x23x^2, constant rule on 7.
  • 6x+76x + 7: A constant term disappears; only constant factors stay.
  • 3x+73x + 7: The 2 comes down: 3⋅2x=6x3 \cdot 2x = 6x.
  • 6x+16x + 1: The derivative of a constant is 0, not 1.

Part 9 · Summary

Summary

Constants added to a function disappear when you differentiate; constant factors stay. Derivatives of sums and differences are sums and differences of derivatives. With the power rule, these rules differentiate any polynomial term by term, and they work on tables of values as well as formulas.
