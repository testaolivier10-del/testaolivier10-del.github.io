Choosing a Differentiation Rule

Choose the first rule from the last operation: a sum term by term, a product by the product rule, a quotient by the quotient rule (or a rewrite), a function of a function by the chain rule.

Part 1 · Hook

Why this matters

A mechanic does not grab a random wrench: one look at the bolt tells which tool fits. Differentiation is the same. You already own every rule you need; this topic trains the one look that tells you which rule to use first, so that long expressions stop being scary.

Part 5 · Step by step

How it works, step by step

  1. Ask what you would compute last to evaluate the expression→that last operation names the first rule: sum, product, quotient or composition
  2. Apply that rule→it leaves smaller pieces, each needing its own derivative
  3. Repeat the question on each piece→one rule per layer, until only basic derivatives remain
  4. Rewrite first when it removes a rule→5x3=5x−3\frac{5}{x^3} = 5x^{-3} needs only the power rule

Part 6 · Key ideas

Key ideas

  • The last operation decides the first rule: x2sin⁡(3x)x^2\sin(3x) is a product; sin2⁡(x3)\sin^2(x^3) is a composition (a square).
  • Inside a product or quotient, any factor may still need the chain rule.
  • Equations mixing xx and yy call for implicit differentiation; an inverse calls for 1f′(g(x))\frac{1}{f'(g(x))}.
  • Rewrite before differentiating when it saves a rule, but do not simplify more than the question asks.

Part 7 · Misconception

A common mistake

The wrong idea: The derivative of a product or a quotient is the product or quotient of the derivatives.

What actually happens: (fg)′=f′g+fg′(fg)' = f'g + fg' and (fg)′=f′g−fg′g2\left(\frac{f}{g}\right)' = \frac{f'g - fg'}{g^2}. Multiplying or dividing the derivatives gives a different, wrong function.

3. Let C(x)=f(g(x))C(x) = f(g(x)). What is C′(3)C'(3)?

  1. 3030
  2. −12-12
  3. 55
  4. 2020
Show the answer

The last operation is applying ff to g(x)g(x): chain rule. C′(3)C'(3)=f′(g(3))⋅g′(3)= f'(g(3)) \cdot g'(3)=f′(2)⋅6= f'(2) \cdot 6=5⋅6= 5 \cdot 6=30= 30.

6. What is ddxsin2⁡(x3)\frac{d}{dx}\sin^2\left(x^3\right)?

  1. 2sin⁡(x3)cos⁡(x3)2\sin(x^3)\cos(x^3)
  2. 6x2sin⁡(x3)cos⁡(x3)6x^2\sin(x^3)\cos(x^3)
  3. 6x2sin⁡(x3)6x^2\sin(x^3)
  4. −6x2sin⁡(x3)cos⁡(x3)-6x^2\sin(x^3)\cos(x^3)
Show the answer

Three layers: the square, the sine, and x3x^3. Multiply one derivative for each: 2sin⁡(x3)⋅cos⁡(x3)⋅3x22\sin(x^3) \cdot \cos(x^3) \cdot 3x^2.

  • 2sin⁡(x3)cos⁡(x3)2\sin(x^3)\cos(x^3): The innermost layer x3x^3 has derivative 3x23x^2, which is missing.
  • Correct: 6x2sin⁡(x3)cos⁡(x3)6x^2\sin(x^3)\cos(x^3): One factor per layer: 2sin⁡(x3)2\sin(x^3), cos⁡(x3)\cos(x^3), 3x23x^2.
  • 6x2sin⁡(x3)6x^2\sin(x^3): The middle layer, the sine, contributes cos⁡(x3)\cos(x^3), which is missing.
  • −6x2sin⁡(x3)cos⁡(x3)-6x^2\sin(x^3)\cos(x^3): The derivative of sine is cosine, with no minus sign.
