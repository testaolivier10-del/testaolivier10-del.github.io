The First Derivative Test

At a critical point, look at the sign of f′ on each side.

Practice 3: Justification

Part 1 · Hook

Why this matters

A cyclist on a hilly road knows she has reached the top of a climb when the road stops going up and starts going down. A flat stretch alone does not prove it: the road may level off and keep climbing. The First Derivative Test turns that common sense into a rule you can state and justify.

Part 5 · Step by step

How it works, step by step

  1. f′f' changes from positive to negative at a critical point cc (and ff is continuous at cc)→ff rises up to cc and falls after it, so f(c)f(c) is a relative maximum
  2. f′f' changes from negative to positive at cc→ff falls then rises, so f(c)f(c) is a relative minimum
  3. f′f' has the same sign on both sides of cc→ff keeps rising (or falling) through cc: no relative extremum, even if f′(c)=0f'(c) = 0
  4. You state the result→write the sentence: "f′f' changes from negative to positive at x=cx = c, so ff has a relative minimum there"

Part 6 · Key ideas

Key ideas

  • First Derivative Test: at a critical point cc where ff is continuous, if f′f' changes from ++ to −-, f(c)f(c) is a relative maximum; from −- to ++, a relative minimum; no change, neither.
  • It works where f′f' does not exist (corners, cusps) as well as where f′=0f' = 0.
  • A sign chart of f′f' organizes the work, but it is not a justification by itself: write the sentence.
  • On a graph of f′f': a relative maximum of ff is where the graph of f′f' crosses the axis going down; a minimum, crossing going up. Touching without crossing gives nothing.

Part 7 · Misconception

A common mistake

The wrong idea: f′(c)=0f'(c) = 0, so ff has a relative maximum or minimum at cc.

What actually happens: The scoring guidelines give no credit for "f′(c)=0f'(c) = 0" alone. The point needs a sign change, stated with its direction: "f′f' changes from positive to negative at x=cx = c." For x3x^3 at 0, f′=0f' = 0 but there is no extremum.

2. What happens to ff at x=2x = 2?

  1. A relative minimum, because f′(2)=0f'(2) = 0
  2. A relative maximum, because f′f' decreases down to 0 just before x=2x = 2
  3. A relative minimum, because f′f' has a low point at x=2x = 2
  4. No relative extremum, because f′f' does not change sign at x=2x = 2
Show the answer

f′(x)>0f'(x) > 0 on both sides of x=2x = 2 (the graph touches the axis there), so ff is increasing through x=2x = 2 and has no relative extremum.

  • A relative minimum, because f′(2)=0f'(2) = 0: f′(2)=0f'(2) = 0 makes 2 a critical point, not an extremum.
  • A relative maximum, because f′f' decreases down to 0 just before x=2x = 2: f′f' is positive on both sides; ff rises through x=2x = 2.
  • A relative minimum, because f′f' has a low point at x=2x = 2: A low point of f′f' says something about concavity, and f′f' never goes negative here.
  • Correct: No relative extremum, because f′f' does not change sign at x=2x = 2: f′>0f' > 0 just left and just right of 2: ff keeps rising.

3. Which justification earns credit for "ff has a relative minimum at x=0x = 0"?

  1. "f′(0)=0f'(0) = 0."
  2. "f′(x)<0f'(x) < 0 for −3<x<0-3 < x < 0."
  3. "f′f' changes from negative to positive at x=0x = 0."
  4. "The graph crosses the xx-axis at x=0x = 0."
Show the answer

First Derivative Test: f′f' changes from negative to positive at x=0x = 0, so ff has a relative minimum there.

  • "f′(0)=0f'(0) = 0.": A zero of f′f' alone does not decide: see x=2x = 2.
  • "f′(x)<0f'(x) < 0 for −3<x<0-3 < x < 0.": Half the story: f′f' must also be positive just after 0.
  • Correct: "f′f' changes from negative to positive at x=0x = 0.": It names f′f' and the direction of the sign change.
  • "The graph crosses the xx-axis at x=0x = 0.": Which graph, and crossing in which direction? Name f′f' and the signs.

For x3x^3, f′(x)=3x2≥0f'(x) = 3x^2 \ge 0 on both sides of 0, so ff rises through 0. A zero of f′f' without a sign change is not an extremum.

  • f(x)=x2f(x) = x^2: f′(x)=2xf'(x) = 2x changes from negative to positive: a minimum.
  • f(x)=x4−1f(x) = x^4 - 1: f′(x)=4x3f'(x) = 4x^3 changes from negative to positive: a minimum.

Part 9 · Summary

Summary

At a critical point, look at the sign of f′f' on each side. ++ to −-: relative maximum. −- to ++: relative minimum. No change: no extremum. The test works where f′f' is undefined too. Justify in words that name f′f' and the direction of its sign change; a sign chart alone does not earn the point.
