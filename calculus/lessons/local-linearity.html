Unit 4 · Topic 4.6

Local Linearity and Linearization

Near x = a , f(x) ≈ L(x) = f(a) + f′(a)(x − a) .

Practice 1: Implementing Mathematical ProcessesPractice 3: Justification

Part 1 · Hook

Why this matters

Zoom in far enough on a smooth curve and it looks straight. That is why the ground looks flat even though Earth is round. Near a point, a differentiable function is almost its tangent line, and that line gives quick, good estimates. Knowing whether such an estimate is too high or too low is a classic free-response point.

Part 5 · Step by step

How it works, step by step

  1. Near x=ax = a, a differentiable graph is nearly straight→it is close to its tangent line L(x)L(x)=f(a)+f′(a)(x−a)= f(a) + f'(a)(x - a)
  2. So for xx near aa→f(x)f(x)≈f(a)+f′(a)(x−a)\approx f(a) + f'(a)(x - a), the tangent line approximation
  3. If f″>0f'' > 0 between aa and xx, the graph is concave up and lies above the line→the approximation is an underestimate
  4. If f″<0f'' < 0 there, the graph is concave down and lies below the line→the approximation is an overestimate

Part 6 · Key ideas

Key ideas

  • The linearization at aa: L(x)L(x)=f(a)+f′(a)(x−a)= f(a) + f'(a)(x - a). Use the point closest to where you estimate.
  • Concave up (f″>0f'' > 0): tangent line below the graph, underestimate. Concave down (f″<0f'' < 0): overestimate.
  • Justify with the concavity on the interval between aa and xx, not with the sign of f′f'.
  • The closer xx is to aa, the better the approximation.

Part 7 · Misconception

A common mistake

The wrong idea: The tangent line approximation overestimates because ff is increasing.

What actually happens: Whether ff increases decides which way the line slopes, not which side of the graph it is on. Concavity decides: concave down means overestimate, concave up means underestimate.

  2. crosses the graph at x=ax = a

  4. gives exact values near aa
Show the answer

Concave up: the graph bends upward, away from the tangent line, so the line lies below it and tangent line approximations are underestimates.

  • lies above the graph near aa, so it overestimates f(x)f(x): That happens when the graph is concave down.
  • crosses the graph at x=ax = a: With constant concavity, the graph stays on one side of its tangent line near aa.
  • Correct: lies below the graph near aa, so it underestimates ff: Concave up graphs bend up away from their tangent lines.
  • gives exact values near aa: It matches ff exactly only at x=ax = a.

Part 9 · Summary

Summary

Near x=ax = a, f(x)f(x)≈L(x)\approx L(x)=f(a)+f′(a)(x−a)= f(a) + f'(a)(x - a). The tangent line lies below a concave up graph (f″>0f'' > 0), giving an underestimate, and above a concave down graph (f″<0f'' < 0), giving an overestimate. The estimate is best close to aa.
