Unit 5 · Topic 5.9

Connecting f, f' and f''

The three graphs carry one story.

Practice 2: Connecting RepresentationsPractice 3: Justification

Part 1 · Hook

Why this matters

A weather station logs temperature, its rate of change, and how fast that rate is changing. A forecaster reading the three together can say "still warming, but the warming is easing off, so the high is coming soon." Reading ff, f′f' and f″f'' together is the same skill, and the graph-of-f′f' question on the exam is built on it.

Part 5 · Step by step

How it works, step by step

  1. The sign of f′f'→tells whether ff is increasing or decreasing; a sign change of f′f' is a relative extremum of ff
  2. The sign of f″f'', or whether f′f' is rising or falling→tells the concavity of ff; a sign change of f″f'' is a point of inflection of ff
  3. f(a)f(a) and f′(a)f'(a)→give the tangent line, and the sign of f″f'' near aa says whether it overestimates or underestimates
  4. A justification on the exam→names the function whose sign or direction you read: "f′f' changes from positive to negative", "f′f' is decreasing"

Part 6 · Key ideas

Key ideas

  • Graphical analysis: given any one of ff, f′f', f″f'' (graph, table or formula), read the features of the others.
  • From a graph of f′f': sign of f′f' (above or below the axis) gives increasing or decreasing; direction of f′f' (rising or falling) gives concavity.
  • From a table: f′(a)=0f'(a) = 0 and the sign of f″(a)f''(a) give the Second Derivative Test; a continuous f″f'' that changes from positive to negative between two inputs gives a point of inflection between them.
  • Words: "increasing at a decreasing rate" means f′>0f' > 0 and f″<0f'' < 0.

Part 7 · Misconception

A common mistake

The wrong idea: On a graph of f′f', the points of inflection of ff are where the graph of f′f' crosses the xx-axis.

What actually happens: Crossings of f′f' are where ff has extrema. Inflection points of ff are where f′f' turns (its peaks and valleys), because that is where f″f'' changes sign. Chief readers see this swap every year.

Graph

The graph of f′(x)=x3−3x2+2f'(x) = x^3 - 3x^2 + 2

The graph shows the derivative of ff for −1≤x≤3.2-1 \le x \le 3.2. It crosses the xx-axis at x=1−3≈−0.73x = 1 - \sqrt{3} \approx -0.73, x=1x = 1 and x=1+3≈2.73x = 1 + \sqrt{3} \approx 2.73. Also, f(0)=4f(0) = 4.

1. At which value of xx does ff have a relative maximum?

  1. x=1x = 1
  2. x=0x = 0, where f′f' is highest
  3. x=2x = 2, where f′f' is lowest
  4. x≈−0.73x \approx -0.73, where f′=0f' = 0
Show the answer

f′f' crosses the axis going down at x=1x = 1 (positive before, negative after), so ff has a relative maximum there.

  • Correct: x=1x = 1: f′f' changes from positive to negative at x=1x = 1.
  • x=0x = 0, where f′f' is highest: A high point of f′f' is an inflection point of ff.
  • x=2x = 2, where f′f' is lowest: A low point of f′f' is an inflection point of ff.
  • x≈−0.73x \approx -0.73, where f′=0f' = 0: f′f' changes from negative to positive there: a relative minimum.

2. On which intervals is the graph of ff concave up?

  1. (0,2)(0, 2)
  2. (−0.73,1)(-0.73, 1) and (2.73,3.2)(2.73, 3.2)
  3. (−1,−0.73)(-1, -0.73) and (1,2.73)(1, 2.73)
  4. (−1,0)(-1, 0) and (2,3.2)(2, 3.2)
Show the answer

ff is concave up where f′f' is increasing: from x=−1x = -1 up to its high point at 0, and after its low point at 2.

  • (0,2)(0, 2): f′f' is decreasing there: concave down.
  • (−0.73,1)(-0.73, 1) and (2.73,3.2)(2.73, 3.2): There f′>0f' > 0: ff is increasing. That is direction, not concavity.
  • (−1,−0.73)(-1, -0.73) and (1,2.73)(1, 2.73): There f′<0f' < 0: ff is decreasing.
  • Correct: (−1,0)(-1, 0) and (2,3.2)(2, 3.2): f′f' is increasing there.

3. Use the graph and the formula to find the slope of the graph of f′f' at x=1x = 1, which is f″(1)f''(1).

No calculator. Type the exact value (a fraction, π, e or a root is fine).

Show the answer

f″(x)=3x2−6xf''(x) = 3x^2 - 6x, so f″(1)=−3f''(1) = -3: the graph of f′f' is falling at x=1x = 1, and ff is concave down there.

  • Answer: -3

4. Which is true of the graph of ff at x=2x = 2?

  1. It has a relative minimum
  2. It is decreasing and has a point of inflection
  3. It is increasing and concave up
  4. It has a point of inflection and a relative minimum
Show the answer

f′(2)f'(2)=8−12+2= 8 - 12 + 2=−2<0= -2 < 0, so ff is decreasing. f′f' has a low point at 2 (f″f'' changes from negative to positive), so the concavity of ff changes: a point of inflection.

  • It has a relative minimum: f′(2)=−2≠0f'(2) = -2 \ne 0: not a critical point.
  • Correct: It is decreasing and has a point of inflection: f′(2)=−2<0f'(2) = -2 < 0, and f′f' changes from decreasing to increasing at 2.
  • It is increasing and concave up: f′(2)<0f'(2) < 0 means decreasing.
  • It has a point of inflection and a relative minimum: A relative minimum would need f′f' to change sign at 2.

6. On (a,b)(a, b), f′(x)>0f'(x) > 0 and f″(x)<0f''(x) < 0. Which describes ff there?

  1. Increasing, at an increasing rate
  2. Decreasing, at a decreasing rate
  3. Decreasing, and concave down
  4. Increasing, at a decreasing rate
Show the answer

f′>0f' > 0 means ff increases; f″<0f'' < 0 means f′f' decreases, so the increase slows down: concave down.

  • Increasing, at an increasing rate: That would need f″>0f'' > 0.
  • Decreasing, at a decreasing rate: f′>0f' > 0 means increasing.
  • Decreasing, and concave down: f′>0f' > 0 means increasing.
  • Correct: Increasing, at a decreasing rate: f′>0f' > 0: increasing. f″<0f'' < 0: the rate f′f' is falling.

Part 9 · Summary

Summary

The three graphs carry one story. f′f' gives the direction of ff, and its sign changes give extrema. f″f'' (or whether f′f' rises or falls) gives the concavity of ff, and its sign changes give inflection points. Read from whichever representation you have, and name the function in every justification.
