Geometry for Calculus

Calculus word problems start with a geometric equation.

Practice 1: Implementing Mathematical Processes

Part 1 · Hook

Why this matters

A ladder slides down a wall; water pours into a cone; a shadow stretches as someone walks from a lamppost. Unit 4 asks how fast those quantities change, and Unit 8 builds solids from slices. Before any calculus happens, each problem needs one geometric equation linking its variables. This page collects the formulas and the two big tools: the Pythagorean theorem and similar triangles.

Part 4 · Step by step

How it works, step by step

  1. A right angle in the picture (wall and ground, two roads crossing)→the Pythagorean theorem links the sides: x2+y2=z2x^2 + y^2 = z^2
  2. Two triangles with the same angles (a shadow, water in a cone)→similar triangles: corresponding sides are in the same ratio, such as rh=410\frac{r}{h} = \frac{4}{10}
  3. Use the ratio to replace one variable by another→a formula like V=13πr2hV = \frac{1}{3}\pi r^2 h becomes a function of one variable, V=4π75h3V = \frac{4\pi}{75}h^3

Part 5 · Key ideas

Key ideas

  • Pythagorean theorem: in a right triangle with legs aa, bb and hypotenuse cc, a2+b2=c2a^2 + b^2 = c^2. The hypotenuse is opposite the right angle.
  • Similar triangles have equal angles, so their sides are in proportion: ab=AB\frac{a}{b} = \frac{A}{B}. Look for a small triangle inside a big one.
  • Know the area and volume formulas: circle πr2\pi r^2, semicircle 12πr2\frac{1}{2}\pi r^2, trapezoid b1+b22h\frac{b_1 + b_2}{2}h, cylinder πr2h\pi r^2 h, cone 13πr2h\frac{1}{3}\pi r^2 h, sphere 43πr3\frac{4}{3}\pi r^3.
  • Draw the picture, label what changes with letters and what is fixed with numbers, then write one equation.

Part 6 · Misconception

A common mistake

The wrong idea: In the cone problem, the radius of the water is the tank's radius, 4, so V=13π(4)2hV = \frac{1}{3}\pi(4)^2 h.

What actually happens: The water's radius changes with its depth. Similar triangles give r=410hr = \frac{4}{10}h; substitute before doing anything else: V=13π(25h)2hV = \frac{1}{3}\pi\left(\frac{2}{5}h\right)^2 h.

Part 7 · Check yourself

Check yourself

Exam-style questions. Anything you miss goes into your review queue.

1. A 13-foot ladder leans against a vertical wall. Its foot is 5 feet from the wall. How high up the wall does the top reach?

No calculator. Type the exact value (a fraction, π, e or a root is fine), then its unit.

Show the answer

The ladder, wall and ground form a right triangle with hypotenuse 13: 52+y2=1325^2 + y^2 = 13^2, so y2=144y^2 = 144 and y=12y = 12 feet.

  • Answer: 12 feet

2. A conical tank, point down, is 9 feet deep with a top radius of 6 feet. When the water is hh feet deep, its surface has radius rr. Which equation relates rr and hh?

  1. r=23hr = \frac{2}{3}h
  2. r=32hr = \frac{3}{2}h
  3. r=h−3r = h - 3
  4. r2+h2=36r^2 + h^2 = 36
Show the answer

The water forms a smaller cone similar to the tank, so the ratio of radius to depth is the same: rh=69=23\frac{r}{h} = \frac{6}{9} = \frac{2}{3}.

  • Correct: r=23hr = \frac{2}{3}h: Similar triangles: rh=69\frac{r}{h} = \frac{6}{9}.
  • r=32hr = \frac{3}{2}h: That is depth over radius; the ratio is rh=69\frac{r}{h} = \frac{6}{9}.
  • r=h−3r = h - 3: Similar shapes scale by a factor; they do not differ by a constant.
  • r2+h2=36r^2 + h^2 = 36: rr and hh are legs of the side-view triangle, not a radius and a slant length of 6.

4. A person 6 feet tall stands 10 feet from a lamppost 15 feet tall. How long is the person's shadow on the ground?

No calculator. Type the exact value (a fraction, π, e or a root is fine), then its unit.

Show the answer

Two similar right triangles share the angle at the shadow's tip: 6s=1510+s\frac{6}{s} = \frac{15}{10 + s}. Cross-multiply: 60+6s=15s60 + 6s = 15s, so 9s=609s = 60 and s=203≈6.67s = \frac{20}{3} \approx 6.67 feet.

  • Answer: 203\frac{20}{3} ≈ 6.667 feet

5. Write the area AA of an equilateral triangle in terms of its side length ss.

Show the answer

The height splits the base in half: h2+(s2)2=s2h^2 + \left(\frac{s}{2}\right)^2 = s^2, so h=32sh = \frac{\sqrt{3}}{2}s. Then AA=12⋅s⋅32s= \frac{1}{2} \cdot s \cdot \frac{\sqrt{3}}{2}s=34s2= \frac{\sqrt{3}}{4}s^2.

6. A box with a square base of side xx and height yy has volume 32 cubic inches. Which expresses yy in terms of xx?

  1. y=32x2y = \frac{32}{x^2}
  2. y=32xy = \frac{32}{x}
  3. y=32−x2y = 32 - x^2
  4. y=32−xy = \sqrt{32} - x
Show the answer

Volume =x⋅x⋅y=x2y=32= x \cdot x \cdot y = x^2 y = 32, so y=32x2y = \frac{32}{x^2}.

  • Correct: y=32x2y = \frac{32}{x^2}: x2y=32x^2 y = 32.
  • y=32xy = \frac{32}{x}: The base has area x2x^2, not xx.
  • y=32−x2y = 32 - x^2: Volume multiplies; it does not subtract.
  • y=32−xy = \sqrt{32} - x: x2y=32x^2 y = 32 does not give a square root.

7. A balloon rises straight up from a point 50 meters from an observer on level ground. When the balloon is hh meters high, the angle of elevation from the observer is θ\theta. Which equation is true?

  1. tan⁡θ=h50\tan\theta = \frac{h}{50}
  2. sin⁡θ=h50\sin\theta = \frac{h}{50}
  3. tan⁡θ=50h\tan\theta = \frac{50}{h}
  4. cos⁡θ=h50\cos\theta = \frac{h}{50}
Show the answer

In the right triangle, hh is opposite θ\theta and 50 is adjacent: tan⁡θ\tan\theta=oppositeadjacent= \frac{\text{opposite}}{\text{adjacent}}=h50= \frac{h}{50}.

  • Correct: tan⁡θ=h50\tan\theta = \frac{h}{50}: Opposite over adjacent.
  • sin⁡θ=h50\sin\theta = \frac{h}{50}: Sine uses the hypotenuse, h2+2500\sqrt{h^2 + 2500}, not 50.
  • tan⁡θ=50h\tan\theta = \frac{50}{h}: That is adjacent over opposite, which is cot⁡θ\cot\theta.
  • cos⁡θ=h50\cos\theta = \frac{h}{50}: Cosine is adjacent over hypotenuse.

Part 8 · Summary

Summary

Calculus word problems start with a geometric equation. Right angles call for the Pythagorean theorem; nested triangles call for similar triangles and a ratio; shapes call for their area or volume formulas. Label changing quantities with letters, fixed ones with numbers, and use a ratio to reduce a formula to one variable.
