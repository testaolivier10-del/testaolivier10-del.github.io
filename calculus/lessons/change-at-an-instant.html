Unit 1 · Topic 1.1 Beta

Can Change Happen at an Instant?

An average rate of change, (f(b) − f(a))/(b − a) , is a slope over an interval, with units of output per input.

Practice 2: Connecting RepresentationsPractice 3: Justification

Part 1 · Hook

Why this matters

A speedometer shows 64 miles per hour at this very instant. But a speed is distance divided by time, and in an instant no time passes and no distance is covered. Zero divided by zero is not a number. So what does the speedometer mean? Answering that question is where calculus begins.

Part 5 · Step by step

How it works, step by step

  1. Over an interval [a, b], divide the change in a quantity by the change in time→the average rate of change f(b)−f(a)b−a\frac{f(b) - f(a)}{b - a}, the slope of the line through two points of the graph
  2. At a single instant the interval has length 0→the quotient becomes zero divided by zero, which has no value
  3. Shrink the interval around the instant instead→the average rates close in on one number
  4. That number is what the average rates approach→it is the instantaneous rate of change: the speed the speedometer shows

Part 6 · Key ideas

Key ideas

  • The average rate of change of ff over [a,b][a, b] is f(b)−f(a)b−a\frac{f(b) - f(a)}{b - a}: the slope of the line through (a,f(a))(a, f(a)) and (b,f(b))(b, f(b)). Its units are output units per input unit.
  • A rate of change at one instant cannot be computed as a quotient, because the interval would have length 0.
  • The instantaneous rate of change at t=at = a is the number the average rates over shorter and shorter intervals around aa close in on.
  • From a table, estimate the rate at an instant with the shortest interval that contains it; from both sides, average the two closest rates.
  • A negative rate means the quantity is decreasing; its size tells how fast.

Part 7 · Misconception

A common mistake

The wrong idea: The average rate over [0,5][0, 5] tells how fast the quantity is changing at t=5t = 5.

What actually happens: An average rate describes a whole interval. The rate at one instant can be very different; estimate it from the shortest interval around that instant, and interpret averages with the words "on average over the interval".

2. Which sentence correctly interprets the value T(5)−T(0)5−0\frac{T(5) - T(0)}{5 - 0}=−5.4= -5.4?

  1. Over the first 5 minutes, the temperature fell by an average of 5.4 °F per minute.
  2. At exactly 5 minutes, the temperature is falling at 5.4 °F per minute.
  3. After 5 minutes, the coffee is 5.4 °F cooler than when it was poured.
  4. The temperature fell 5.4 °F in each of the first 5 minutes, minute by minute.
Show the answer

An average rate describes a whole interval: from t=0t = 0 to t=5t = 5 the coffee lost 27 °F, which is 5.4 °F per minute on average. It does not say how fast it cooled at any one moment.

  • Correct: Over the first 5 minutes, the temperature fell by an average of 5.4 °F per minute.: It names the interval, the quantity, the units and "on average".
  • At exactly 5 minutes, the temperature is falling at 5.4 °F per minute.: That describes one instant. An average over an interval does not tell the rate at its end.
  • After 5 minutes, the coffee is 5.4 °F cooler than when it was poured.: The total drop is 27 °F; 5.4 is the drop per minute.
  • The temperature fell 5.4 °F in each of the first 5 minutes, minute by minute.: An average does not say the cooling was steady; the table shows it slowed down.

3. Using the table, which is the best estimate of the rate at which the temperature is changing at t=9.5t = 9.5 minutes?

  1. −3 °F per minute, from the interval [9, 10]
  2. −3.8 °F per minute, from the interval [5, 10]
  3. −4 °F per minute, from the interval [5, 9]
  4. 137 °F, the temperature at t = 9
Show the answer

The narrowest interval in the table that contains t=9.5t = 9.5 is [9,10][9, 10]: 134−13710−9\frac{134 - 137}{10 - 9}=−3= -3 °F per minute. Shorter intervals give averages closer to the rate at an instant.

  • Correct: −3 °F per minute, from the interval [9, 10]: [9,10][9, 10] is the shortest interval around 9.5.
  • −3.8 °F per minute, from the interval [5, 10]: [5,10][5, 10] contains 9.5 but is five times longer, so its average blends in the faster cooling from earlier.
  • −4 °F per minute, from the interval [5, 9]: [5,9][5, 9] does not contain t=9.5t = 9.5.
  • 137 °F, the temperature at t = 9: That is a temperature, not a rate of change; a rate has units of °F per minute.

4. What do the average rates of change over [0,2][0, 2], [2,5][2, 5], [5,9][5, 9] and [9,10][9, 10] show about the coffee?

  1. It cools more and more slowly: the averages are −6, −5, −4 and −3 °F per minute.
  2. It cools faster and faster: the averages are −3, −4, −5 and −6 °F per minute.
  3. It cools at a steady rate, because the temperature falls on each interval.
  4. It warms up at the end, because −3 is greater than −6.
Show the answer

The averages are 168−1802=−6\frac{168 - 180}{2} = -6, 153−1683=−5\frac{153 - 168}{3} = -5, 137−1534=−4\frac{137 - 153}{4} = -4 and 134−1371=−3\frac{134 - 137}{1} = -3. They are all negative (cooling) and shrink in size, so the cooling slows down.

  • Correct: It cools more and more slowly: the averages are −6, −5, −4 and −3 °F per minute.: The averages go −6, −5, −4, −3: still cooling, but more slowly.
  • It cools faster and faster: the averages are −3, −4, −5 and −6 °F per minute.: That lists them in reverse time order.
  • It cools at a steady rate, because the temperature falls on each interval.: Falling on every interval does not mean falling at the same rate; the averages differ.
  • It warms up at the end, because −3 is greater than −6.: A rate of −3 still means the temperature is going down, just more slowly.

  • Correct: 64 feet per second, the speed at the instant t = 2: 64+16h64 + 16h heads to 64 as hh heads to 0.

7. A train passes mile marker 40 at 1:00 p.m. and mile marker 100 at 2:00 p.m. on a straight track. Which statement must be true?

  1. Its average speed from 1:00 to 2:00 was 60 miles per hour.
  2. At 1:30 p.m. it was traveling at 60 miles per hour.
  3. It traveled at 60 miles per hour for the entire hour.
  4. At 2:00 p.m. it was traveling at 100 miles per hour.
Show the answer

The data give an average over the interval: 100−401=60\frac{100 - 40}{1} = 60 miles per hour. They say nothing certain about the speed at any single instant, such as 1:30.

  • Correct: Its average speed from 1:00 to 2:00 was 60 miles per hour.: 60 miles1 hour\frac{60 \text{ miles}}{1 \text{ hour}}.
  • At 1:30 p.m. it was traveling at 60 miles per hour.: The train could have sped up and slowed down; the average says nothing sure about one moment.
  • It traveled at 60 miles per hour for the entire hour.: An average does not mean a steady speed.
  • At 2:00 p.m. it was traveling at 100 miles per hour.: The mile marker 100 is a position, not a speed.

Part 9 · Summary

Summary

An average rate of change, f(b)−f(a)b−a\frac{f(b) - f(a)}{b - a}, is a slope over an interval, with units of output per input. A rate at one instant cannot be a quotient, since nothing changes in zero time; instead, the averages over shrinking intervals close in on one number, the instantaneous rate of change. The next topic gives that closing in a name and a notation.
