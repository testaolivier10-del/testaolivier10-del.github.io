Washer Method About the Axes

When the region does not touch the axis, each slice is a washer.

Practice 1: Implementing Mathematical Processes

Share to Google Classroom (opens in a new tab)

Question set for this topic

Part 1 · Hook

Why this matters

A bagel, a pipe, a bundt cake: each is a solid of revolution with a hole down the middle. Slice one and you get a ring, a disc with a smaller disc cut out. That ring, called a washer after the metal ring under a bolt, is the slice you get whenever the spinning region does not reach the axis.

Part 5 · Step by step

How it works, step by step

  1. The region does not touch the axis along its length→each rectangle starts above the axis and sweeps out a ring, a washer
  2. The far edge of the rectangle is on one curve, the near edge on the other→outer radius RR = axis to the far curve; inner radius rr = axis to the near curve
  3. A washer is a big disc minus a small disc→its area is πR2−πr2=π(R2−r2)\pi R^2 - \pi r^2 = \pi\left(R^2 - r^2\right)
  4. Integrate the washer areas along the axis→V=π∫ab(R(x)2−r(x)2)dxV = \pi\int_a^b \left(R(x)^2 - r(x)^2\right)\,dx

Part 6 · Key ideas

Key ideas

  • Washer method: V=π∫ab(R2−r2)dxV = \pi\int_a^b \left(R^2 - r^2\right)\,dx, outer radius squared minus inner radius squared.
  • Both radii are measured from the axis: RR to the far curve, rr to the near one. About the xx-axis, R=f(x)R = f(x) (top) and r=g(x)r = g(x) (bottom).
  • Square each radius, then subtract: R2−r2R^2 - r^2, not (R−r)2(R - r)^2.
  • About the yy-axis, the washers are horizontal: write each curve as xx in terms of yy, and the farther curve from the yy-axis is the outer radius.
  • Discs are washers with r=0r = 0: use washers whenever the region leaves a gap at the axis.

Part 7 · Misconception

A common mistake

The wrong idea: A washer's area is π(R−r)2\pi(R - r)^2, since R−rR - r is the width of the region.

What actually happens: The washer is a big disc with a small disc removed, so its area is πR2−πr2\pi R^2 - \pi r^2. With R=3R = 3 and r=2r = 2 that is 5π5\pi, while π(3−2)2=π\pi(3 - 2)^2 = \pi is far too small: the ring sits far from the axis, where it sweeps out a lot of area.

1. RR is revolved about the xx-axis. What are the outer radius R(x)R(x) and the inner radius r(x)r(x) of the washer at xx?

  1. R(x)=2xR(x) = 2x and r(x)=x2r(x) = x^2
  2. R(x)=x2R(x) = x^2 and r(x)=2xr(x) = 2x
  3. R(x)=2x−x2R(x) = 2x - x^2 and r(x)=0r(x) = 0
  4. R(x)=2xR(x) = 2x and r(x)=2x−x2r(x) = 2x - x^2
Show the answer

Both radii start at the xx-axis. The far edge of the region is the line (2x2x); the near edge is the parabola (x2x^2), which leaves a hole.

  • Correct: R(x)=2xR(x) = 2x and r(x)=x2r(x) = x^2: Outer to the far curve, inner to the near curve.
  • R(x)=x2R(x) = x^2 and r(x)=2xr(x) = 2x: On (0,2)(0, 2), 2x>x22x > x^2: the line is farther from the axis, so it gives the outer radius.
  • R(x)=2x−x2R(x) = 2x - x^2 and r(x)=0r(x) = 0: That treats the region's height as a solid disc. The region does not reach the axis, so each slice has a hole of radius x2x^2.
  • R(x)=2xR(x) = 2x and r(x)=2x−x2r(x) = 2x - x^2: The inner radius runs from the axis to the near curve, x2x^2, not across the region.

5. When RR is revolved about the yy-axis, what are the radii of the washer at height yy?

  1. R=yR = \sqrt{y} and r=y2r = \frac{y}{2}
  2. R=y2R = \frac{y}{2} and r=yr = \sqrt{y}
  3. R=2xR = 2x and r=x2r = x^2
  4. R=y−y2R = \sqrt{y} - \frac{y}{2} and r=0r = 0
Show the answer

Horizontal slices: write each curve as xx in terms of yy. The parabola x=yx = \sqrt{y} is farther from the yy-axis than the line x=y2x = \frac{y}{2}.

  • Correct: R=yR = \sqrt{y} and r=y2r = \frac{y}{2}: Outer to the far curve, inner to the near one, both in yy.
  • R=y2R = \frac{y}{2} and r=yr = \sqrt{y}: At y=1y = 1, y2=0.5<1=y\frac{y}{2} = 0.5 < 1 = \sqrt{y}: the line is the nearer curve.
  • R=2xR = 2x and r=x2r = x^2: Those are the radii about the xx-axis, in xx.
  • R=y−y2R = \sqrt{y} - \frac{y}{2} and r=0r = 0: That is the width of the region, not a radius from the yy-axis.

6. The region bounded by y=xy = \sqrt{x}, y=1y = 1 and x=4x = 4 is revolved about the xx-axis. Find the volume.

No calculator. Type the exact value (a fraction, π, e or a root is fine).

Show the answer

From x=1x = 1 (where x=1\sqrt{x} = 1) to x=4x = 4: R=xR = \sqrt{x}, r=1r = 1, so V=π∫14(x−1)dx=π(152−3)=9π2V = \pi\int_1^4 (x - 1)\,dx = \pi\left(\frac{15}{2} - 3\right) = \frac{9\pi}{2}.

7. A washer has outer radius 3 and inner radius 2. What is the area of its face?

  1. 5π5\pi
  2. π\pi
  3. 25π25\pi
  4. 13π13\pi
Show the answer

Big disc minus small disc: π(3)2−π(2)2=9π−4π=5π\pi(3)^2 - \pi(2)^2 = 9\pi - 4\pi = 5\pi.

  • Correct: 5π5\pi: π(R2−r2)\pi(R^2 - r^2).
  • π\pi: π(3−2)2\pi(3 - 2)^2 squares the difference; the ring is wider than that.
  • 25π25\pi: π(3+2)2\pi(3 + 2)^2 is a disc of radius 5.
  • 13π13\pi: π(32+22)\pi(3^2 + 2^2) adds the hole instead of removing it.

Part 9 · Summary

Summary

When the region does not touch the axis, each slice is a washer. Measure both radii from the axis: the outer radius RR to the far curve and the inner radius rr to the near one. Then V=π∫ab(R2−r2)dxV = \pi\int_a^b \left(R^2 - r^2\right)\,dx: the difference of squares, never the square of the difference.
