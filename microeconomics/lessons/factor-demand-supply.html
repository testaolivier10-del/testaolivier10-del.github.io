Unit 5 · Topic 5.2

Changes in Factor Demand and Factor Supply

A labor market works like any market, with the wage as its price.

Practice 3: Manipulation

Part 1 · Hook

Why this matters

In one summer an invented beach town loses a third of its lifeguards when a nearby water park offers better pay, while a heat wave fills the beaches. Every lifeguard who stays gets a raise. Two shifts are happening at once in one labor market, and both push the wage the same way.

Part 5 · Step by step

How it works, step by step

  1. The demand for a product rises→its price rises
  2. The product price rises→each worker's MRP rises
  3. MRP rises at every quantity of labor→labor demand shifts right
  4. Labor demand shifts right→the wage and the number of workers hired both rise

Part 6 · Key ideas

Key ideas

  • Labor demand shifts with the demand for (and price of) the product, worker productivity, the prices of other inputs and the number of firms.
  • A cheaper input that replaces workers lowers labor demand; a cheaper input used with workers raises it.
  • Labor supply shifts with the number of qualified people (population, migration, training rules, age rules), pay in other jobs, and how people feel about the work.
  • A change in the wage itself is a movement along both curves. Read the new equilibrium wage and quantity of labor exactly as in any market.

Part 7 · Misconception

A common mistake

The wrong idea: When a product becomes more popular, workers want to make it, so the supply of labor to that industry rises.

What actually happens: More demand for the product raises its price and each worker's MRP, so firms want more workers: labor demand shifts right. The wage rises, and more workers are hired as a movement along labor supply.

Part 8 · Check yourself

Check yourself

Exam-style questions. Anything you miss goes into your review queue.

1. Builders in an invented region win many new orders for houses. On the graph of the market for carpenters, shift the right curve and mark the new equilibrium.

Work on the graph (it needs JavaScript), then check.

Show the answer

More demand for houses raises the price of houses and so each carpenter's MRP, so the demand for carpenters (DL) shifts right. The wage and the quantity of carpenters hired both rise.

  • Key: the demand for labor shifts right

2. A new rule requires every electrician to complete an extra year of training before working. On the graph of the market for electricians, shift the right curve and mark the new equilibrium.

Work on the graph (it needs JavaScript), then check.

Show the answer

Fewer people qualify to work at each wage, so labor supply (SL) shifts left. The wage rises and the quantity of electricians hired falls.

  • Key: the supply of labor shifts left

3. Put the links in order: the demand for solar panels rises, and the market for panel installers changes.

  1. The price of solar panel installations rises
  2. Each installer's MRP rises (MP × the higher price)
  3. The demand for installers (DL) shifts right
  4. The wage of installers and the number hired both rise
Show the answer

Name every link: product price, MRP, labor demand, then the new wage and employment.

  • Correct order: 1. The price of solar panel installations rises 2. Each installer's MRP rises (MP × the higher price) 3. The demand for installers (DL) shifts right 4. The wage of installers and the number hired both rise

4. A country raises the minimum age at which people may work in factories. What happens in the market for factory workers?

  1. Labor demand shifts left: the wage and employment fall
  2. Labor supply shifts right: the wage falls and employment rises
  3. Labor demand shifts right: the wage and employment rise
  4. Labor supply shifts left: the wage rises and employment falls
Show the answer

Rules on who may work shift labor supply. A higher minimum age shifts it left.

  • Labor demand shifts left: the wage and employment fall: Factories still want the same workers; the rule changes who may sell their labor.
  • Labor supply shifts right: the wage falls and employment rises: The rule removes workers, so supply falls.
  • Labor demand shifts right: the wage and employment rise: Nothing here makes workers more valuable to factories.
  • Correct: Labor supply shifts left: the wage rises and employment falls: Right: fewer people may work at each wage.

5. On a graph of the market for plumbers, how is a higher wage shown when nothing else about the market has changed?

  1. Labor supply shifts right
  2. A movement along labor supply and labor demand, not a shift of either
  3. Labor demand shifts left
  4. Both curves shift left
Show the answer

As with any market, a change in the curve's own price (here the wage) is a movement along the curve.

  • Labor supply shifts right: The wage is on the axis; its change moves along the supply curve.
  • Correct: A movement along labor supply and labor demand, not a shift of either: Right: a change in the wage itself moves along the curves.
  • Labor demand shifts left: A higher wage lowers the quantity of labor demanded along the curve.
  • Both curves shift left: Only a change in a shifter, not in the wage, shifts a curve.

6. Which of these would shift the labor supply curve for truck drivers to the right? Select all that apply.

  1. A cheaper, shorter path to a commercial driver's license
  2. Higher pay in warehouse jobs that drivers could take
  3. Safer trucks that make the job less stressful
  4. A rise in the demand for shipping
  5. A rise in drivers' wages
Show the answer

Labor supply shifts with the number of people who can do the job, their other options and how they feel about the work.

  • Correct: A cheaper, shorter path to a commercial driver's license: Right: more people can qualify at each wage.
  • Higher pay in warehouse jobs that drivers could take: That pulls drivers away, shifting supply left.
  • Correct: Safer trucks that make the job less stressful: Right: more people are willing to drive at each wage.
  • A rise in the demand for shipping: That shifts labor demand, not supply.
  • A rise in drivers' wages: A wage change is a movement along supply.

Part 9 · Summary

Summary

A labor market works like any market, with the wage as its price. Labor demand shifts with the demand for the product, productivity and the prices of other inputs (substitutes lower it when they get cheaper; complements raise it), because each changes MRP or how firms combine inputs. Labor supply shifts with the number of qualified people, pay in other jobs and the appeal of the work. Then read the new wage and employment from the graph: a demand shift moves them the same way; a supply shift moves them in opposite ways.
