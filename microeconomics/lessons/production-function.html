Unit 3 · Topic 3.1

The Production Function

A production function shows how much output a firm gets from its inputs.

Practice 1: Principles and ModelsPractice 2: Interpretation

Part 1 · Hook

Why this matters

A food truck with one grill hires a second cook and the line moves twice as fast. A third cook helps a little. By the fifth cook, people are bumping elbows and waiting for a turn at the grill. The truck has not changed, so each new cook adds less food than the last. That pattern shows up in every kitchen, farm and factory.

Part 5 · Step by step

How it works, step by step

  1. The firm adds workers while its kitchen, field or factory stays fixed→each new worker has less of the fixed input to work with
  2. Each new worker has less of the fixed input→the marginal product of each new worker falls: diminishing marginal returns
  3. Marginal product falls below average product→average product falls too
  4. Marginal product falls toward zero→total product rises more and more slowly and stops rising when marginal product reaches zero

Part 6 · Key ideas

Key ideas

  • A production function links inputs to output. In the short run at least one input is fixed; in the long run every input can change.
  • Total product (TP) is all the output. Marginal product: MP=ΔTPΔL\text{MP} = \frac{\Delta \text{TP}}{\Delta L}. Average product: average product=TPL\text{average product} = \frac{\text{TP}}{L}.
  • MP often rises at first (specialization), then falls: diminishing marginal returns, from adding a variable input to a fixed one.
  • When MP is above average product, average product rises; when MP is below average product, average product falls. TP is at its peak where MP is zero.

Part 7 · Misconception

A common mistake

The wrong idea: Diminishing marginal returns means total output falls when another worker is hired.

What actually happens: It means each new worker adds less than the one before. Total product still rises as long as marginal product is positive; it falls only if marginal product turns negative.

Part 8 · Check yourself

Check yourself

Exam-style questions. Anything you miss goes into your review queue.

1. What makes a period "the short run" for a firm?

  1. It lasts less than one year
  2. The firm can change each input it uses
  3. At least one input is fixed
  4. The firm produces no output yet
Show the answer

In the short run at least one input is fixed; in the long run every input can change. The length in calendar time depends on the industry.

  • It lasts less than one year: The short run is not a set length of time; it ends when every input can change.
  • The firm can change each input it uses: That describes the long run.
  • Correct: At least one input is fixed: Right: the short run is defined by a fixed input, not by a number of months.
  • The firm produces no output yet: A firm produces in the short run; it just cannot change every input.

2. A call center's total product rises from 300 to 340 calls a day when it goes from 10 to 12 workers. What is the marginal product per added worker, in calls a day?

Type a number. A four-function calculator is allowed, as on the exam.

Show the answer

Marginal product is the change in total product divided by the change in labor: 340−30012−10\frac{340 - 300}{12 - 10}=20= 20 calls per added worker.

  • Answer: 20

3. Why do diminishing marginal returns occur in the short run?

  1. Later workers hired are less skilled than the first ones
  2. The price of the product falls as output rises
  3. The firm grows so large that it is hard to manage
  4. A variable input is added to a fixed input
Show the answer

Diminishing marginal returns are a short-run idea: adding workers to a fixed kitchen, field or factory eventually leaves each new worker less of the fixed input.

  • Later workers hired are less skilled than the first ones: The idea assumes workers are alike; returns diminish even with identical workers.
  • The price of the product falls as output rises: Diminishing returns are about physical output, not prices.
  • The firm grows so large that it is hard to manage: That is a long-run problem of size, when no input is fixed.
  • Correct: A variable input is added to a fixed input: Right: that is the source of diminishing marginal returns.

4. In the figure, after marginal product reaches its peak, with which worker does it come down to equal average product?

  1. The second worker, where marginal product is highest
  2. The fifth worker, where total product is 48 loaves
  3. The fourth worker, where total product is 44 loaves
  4. The third worker, where both are 12 loaves
Show the answer

MP is 10, 14, 12, 8, 4, 0 and average product is 10, 12, 12, 11, 9.6, 8. As MP falls from its peak it meets average product at the third worker (12 and 12); after that MP is below average product and average product falls.

  • The second worker, where marginal product is highest: Marginal product (14) is above average product (12) for the second worker.
  • The fifth worker, where total product is 48 loaves: Marginal product (4) is far below average product (9.6) for the fifth worker.
  • The fourth worker, where total product is 44 loaves: Marginal product (8) is already below average product (11) for the fourth worker, so the two met earlier.
  • Correct: The third worker, where both are 12 loaves: Right: the third worker adds 12 loaves and average product with three workers is 36 / 3 = 12.

5. In the figure, diminishing marginal returns begin with the third worker, yet total product keeps rising through the fifth worker. Why?

  1. Average product is rising, which pulls total product up
  2. Marginal product falls but stays above zero
  3. The bakery buys more ovens as it hires
  4. Total product rises as long as marginal product rises
Show the answer

Diminishing marginal returns means each worker adds less than the one before, not that output falls. Total product rises while marginal product is positive and stops rising when MP reaches zero (the sixth worker).

  • Average product is rising, which pulls total product up: Average product is falling after the third worker, and it does not drive total product.
  • Correct: Marginal product falls but stays above zero: Right: falling MP slows the rise in TP; TP falls only if MP turns negative.
  • The bakery buys more ovens as it hires: The oven is fixed in the short run; that is why marginal product falls.
  • Total product rises as long as marginal product rises: Marginal product is already falling here, yet total product still rises.

6. Which statements does the figure support? Select all that apply.

Read MP as the change in TP for each worker and compare it with average product: 14 for the second worker, and average product falls once MP drops below it.

  • Correct: The second worker adds 14 loaves: True: TP rises from 10 to 24.

Part 9 · Summary

Summary

A production function shows how much output a firm gets from its inputs. In the short run at least one input is fixed, so adding workers eventually brings diminishing marginal returns: each worker adds less than the one before. Marginal product is the change in total product divided by the change in labor, and average product is total product divided by labor. Marginal product above average pulls the average up; below it, the average falls; total product peaks where marginal product reaches zero.
