Unit 6 · Topic 6.1

Socially Efficient and Inefficient Market Outcomes

A quantity is socially efficient where marginal social benefit equals marginal social cost: every unit worth more than it costs is made, and no other.

Practice 2: Interpretation

Part 1 · Hook

Why this matters

A town pool could stay open every evening of the week. Each extra hour gives swimmers some benefit and costs the town a lifeguard, heat and light. Open too few hours and good evenings go to waste; open too many and the town pays for empty lanes. There is one best number of hours, and it is found at the margin.

Part 5 · Step by step

How it works, step by step

  1. A unit's MSB is greater than its MSC→making it adds to total surplus
  2. Units are added until MSB = MSC→total surplus is as large as it can be: the efficient quantity
  3. A policy or market power moves quantity away from that point→units are lost (or wasted) whose benefit and cost do not match
  4. Those units' net benefit is lost→deadweight loss appears: a market failure if the market itself causes it

Part 6 · Key ideas

Key ideas

  • Marginal social benefit (MSB) is the benefit of one more unit to everyone; marginal social cost (MSC) is its cost to everyone.
  • The socially efficient quantity is where MSB = MSC. There total surplus is largest.
  • A competitive market whose demand and supply capture every benefit and cost has D = MSB and S = MSC, so its equilibrium is efficient.
  • Any other quantity leaves deadweight loss, a triangle between MSB and MSC pointing toward the efficient quantity. A market failure is a market outcome that is not efficient.

Part 7 · Misconception

A common mistake

The wrong idea: A market equilibrium is always the socially efficient outcome.

What actually happens: It is efficient only when demand captures every benefit and supply every cost, and buyers and sellers are price takers. Market power, and benefits or costs that fall on others, make the market quantity differ from MSB = MSC.

Part 8 · Check yourself

Check yourself

Exam-style questions. Anything you miss goes into your review queue.

1. Where is the socially efficient quantity of a good?

  1. Where total benefit is largest
  2. Where the price is as low as possible
  3. Where producers earn the most profit
  4. Where marginal social benefit equals marginal social cost
Show the answer

Below the efficient quantity, each added unit is worth more than it costs; above it, less. Total surplus is largest at MSB = MSC.

  • Where total benefit is largest: Total benefit keeps rising while MSB is positive; the efficient point weighs cost too.
  • Where the price is as low as possible: A low price says nothing about whether each unit is worth its cost.
  • Where producers earn the most profit: Profit is the firm's goal, not society's; a monopoly's profit-maximizing quantity is too small.
  • Correct: Where marginal social benefit equals marginal social cost: Right: MSB = MSC.

2. What is a market failure?

  1. A market outcome that is not socially efficient
  2. A market in which some firms lose money
  3. A market in which prices rise quickly
  4. A market in which the government sets prices
Show the answer

A market fails when it leaves deadweight loss: too much or too little is made compared with MSB = MSC.

  • Correct: A market outcome that is not socially efficient: Right: the market produces a quantity other than the one where MSB = MSC.
  • A market in which some firms lose money: Losses and exit are a normal part of competitive markets.
  • A market in which prices rise quickly: Fast price changes can be efficient responses to shifts.
  • A market in which the government sets prices: Government policy can cause inefficiency, but that is not the definition.

6. Total surplus in a market is largest when

  1. as many units as buyers want at a price of zero are made
  2. consumer surplus is as large as possible
  3. producer surplus is as large as possible
  4. every unit with MSB at least MSC is made, and no other
Show the answer

Total surplus = consumer surplus + producer surplus, and it is largest at the efficient quantity. Any other quantity leaves deadweight loss.

  • as many units as buyers want at a price of zero are made: Units past MSB = MSC cost more than they are worth.
  • consumer surplus is as large as possible: A price ceiling can raise consumer surplus while lowering total surplus.
  • producer surplus is as large as possible: A monopoly can raise producer surplus while creating deadweight loss.
  • Correct: every unit with MSB at least MSC is made, and no other: Right: that is the quantity where MSB = MSC.

Part 9 · Summary

Summary

A quantity is socially efficient where marginal social benefit equals marginal social cost: every unit worth more than it costs is made, and no other. Total surplus is largest there. A competitive market in which demand and supply reflect all benefits and costs reaches that point on its own. Taxes, subsidies, price controls and monopoly move the quantity away from it, and the lost net benefit is deadweight loss, a triangle between MSB and MSC that points toward the efficient quantity. A market outcome that is not efficient is a market failure.
