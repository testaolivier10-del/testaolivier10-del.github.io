Supply

Supply shows how much sellers offer at each price.

Practice 3: ManipulationPractice 4: Graphing and Visuals

Part 1 · Hook

Why this matters

A strawberry farm sells 20 boxes at the weekend market when the price is $4, and digs up a back field to sell more when the price hits $5. The next spring, fertilizer doubles in price and the farm offers fewer boxes even at $5. One change moved the farm along its supply curve; the other moved the curve itself.

Part 5 · Step by step

How it works, step by step

  1. The price of a good rises, other things equal→each unit sells for more
  2. A higher price covers the rising cost of producing extra units→sellers offer more: the quantity supplied rises
  3. The quantity supplied rises because of the good's own price→sellers move up along the same supply curve; the curve does not shift

Part 6 · Key ideas

Key ideas

  • Law of supply: other things equal, a higher price means a larger quantity supplied. The curve slopes up.
  • A change in the good's own price is a movement along the curve: a change in quantity supplied.
  • A change in a determinant shifts the whole curve: input prices, technology, the prices of other goods the seller could make, taxes and subsidies, expectations, the number of sellers.
  • A shift left is a decrease in supply (less at each price); a shift right is an increase.
  • Market supply is the horizontal sum of every seller's quantity at each price.

Part 7 · Misconception

A common mistake

The wrong idea: An increase in supply moves the supply curve up.

What actually happens: An increase in supply moves the curve to the right: more is offered at each price. Because supply slopes up, a rightward shift also sits lower on the graph, so "up" for a curve that slopes up means less supply, not more. Say right and left.

4. The market price of strawberries rises from $3 to $5 a box. What happens in the market?

  1. Supply shifts right from 30 to 50 boxes
  2. Quantity supplied falls from 50 to 30 along the curve
  3. Quantity supplied rises from 30 to 50 along the supply curve
  4. Supply shifts left because buyers pay more
Show the answer

A change in the good's own price moves sellers along supply. The market quantity supplied rises from 30 to 50 boxes; supply itself does not shift.

  • Supply shifts right from 30 to 50 boxes: A change in the own price does not shift supply.
  • Quantity supplied falls from 50 to 30 along the curve: That is the change for a price fall from $5 to $3.
  • Correct: Quantity supplied rises from 30 to 50 along the supply curve: Right: the own price changed, so sellers move along the curve.
  • Supply shifts left because buyers pay more: What buyers pay does not move the sellers' curve.

Scenario

Four events for the furniture workshops of Larkfield

Larkfield is an invented town where small workshops make wooden chairs. Four things happen over one year:

  1. A storm closes forests in the region and the price of lumber jumps.
  2. Workshops start using a new laser cutter that halves the time it takes to shape each chair.
  3. The town starts paying each workshop $20 for every chair it produces.
  4. Workshop owners hear that chair prices will rise sharply next month.

5. Event 1: the price of lumber jumps. What happens to the supply of chairs?

  1. Supply shifts right
  2. Supply shifts left
  3. Quantity supplied rises along the curve
  4. Demand for chairs shifts left
Show the answer

Lumber is an input. A higher input price raises the cost of each chair, so workshops offer fewer chairs at each price: supply shifts left.

  • Supply shifts right: Costlier inputs make each chair more expensive to produce.
  • Correct: Supply shifts left: Right: a higher input price lowers supply.
  • Quantity supplied rises along the curve: The chair price did not change; an input price did.
  • Demand for chairs shifts left: Lumber is an input for sellers, so supply changes.

6. Event 2: workshops start using a laser cutter. What happens to the supply of chairs?

  1. Supply shifts left
  2. Supply shifts right
  3. Quantity supplied falls along the curve
  4. Demand for chairs shifts right
Show the answer

Better technology lowers the cost of producing each chair, so workshops offer more chairs at each price: supply shifts right.

  • Supply shifts left: A faster way to make chairs makes producing them cheaper.
  • Correct: Supply shifts right: Right: better technology raises supply.
  • Quantity supplied falls along the curve: The price of chairs did not change.
  • Demand for chairs shifts right: The cutter changes how chairs are made, not how many buyers want.

Part 9 · Summary

Summary

Supply shows how much sellers offer at each price. By the law of supply, a higher price raises the quantity supplied, because a higher price covers the rising cost of producing more. A change in the good's own price moves sellers along the curve; a change in input prices, technology, the prices of other goods the seller could make, taxes or subsidies, expectations or the number of sellers shifts the whole curve. Market supply adds every seller's quantity at each price.
