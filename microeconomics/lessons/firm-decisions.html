Unit 3 · Topic 3.6

Firms' Short-Run Decisions to Produce and Long-Run Decisions to Enter or Exit a Market

In the short run a firm compares the price with average variable cost: if P is at least the lowest point of AVC it produces where MR = MC, even at a loss, because closing would lose the whole fixed cost; if P is below that point it shuts down.

Practice 2: InterpretationPractice 3: Manipulation

Part 1 · Hook

Why this matters

A ski rental shop makes a loss in a warm winter. Should it lock its doors tomorrow? It still owes rent on its lease whether it opens or not. If the rentals it can make bring in more than the staff and cleaning they cost, staying open shrinks the loss. Next year, when the lease ends, the question changes: stay in the business at all, or leave?

Part 5 · Step by step

How it works, step by step

  1. The market price falls below the lowest point of ATC→firms make economic losses
  2. In the long run, firms with losses can get out of their fixed commitments→some firms exit the market
  3. Fewer firms sell in the market→market supply shifts left and the price rises
  4. The price rises back to the lowest point of ATC→the remaining firms earn zero economic profit, so exit stops

Part 6 · Key ideas

Key ideas

  • Short run, shutdown rule: produce (where MR = MC) if P is at least the lowest point of AVC; shut down if P is below it. Shut down, the loss is the fixed cost.
  • Fixed costs do not change MC or AVC, so they do not change the short-run output choice.
  • Above the shutdown point, the MC curve is the firm's short-run supply curve.
  • Long run: economic profit draws firms in (entry), shifting market supply right; losses push firms out (exit), shifting it left, until economic profit is zero.

Part 7 · Misconception

A common mistake

The wrong idea: A firm making a loss should close at once.

What actually happens: In the short run a firm that covers its variable costs (P at least the lowest AVC) loses less by producing than by closing, because closing still leaves the fixed cost. It exits only in the long run, if the losses last.

Check yourself

A firm's cost curves with a price line

1. In the graph, the firm sells at the price shown. What should it do in the short run?

  1. Close, because the price is under ATC
  2. Keep producing, because it earns an economic profit
  3. Keep producing, as the price is above AVC
  4. Shut down, because MC is rising
Show the answer

The price is below the lowest point of ATC (a loss) but above the lowest point of AVC. Producing covers all variable costs and part of the fixed cost, so the loss is smaller than the fixed cost it would lose by closing.

  • Close, because the price is under ATC: A price under ATC means a loss, but closing would make the loss larger: the whole fixed cost.
  • Keep producing, because it earns an economic profit: The price is below ATC at every output, so the firm makes a loss.
  • Correct: Keep producing, as the price is above AVC: Right: the price sits between the lowest points of AVC and ATC.
  • Shut down, because MC is rising: Rising MC is normal; the shutdown test compares price with AVC.

2. In the graph, if the firm shut down in the short run, its loss would equal

  1. its total fixed cost
  2. zero, because it produces nothing
  3. its total variable cost
  4. its total cost at the best output
Show the answer

A firm that shuts down in the short run loses its total fixed cost: no revenue, no variable cost, but the rent and other fixed costs remain.

  • Correct: its total fixed cost: Right: with no output it has no revenue and no variable cost, but still pays fixed cost.
  • zero, because it produces nothing: Fixed costs are still due when output is zero.
  • its total variable cost: Variable cost falls to zero when the firm stops producing.
  • its total cost at the best output: That includes variable costs the firm avoids by closing.

3. In the graph, as the price falls, what is the lowest price at which the firm still produces in the short run?

  1. The price at the lowest point of ATC
  2. The price at the lowest point of MC
  3. A price of zero, because fixed cost is sunk
  4. The price at the lowest point of AVC
Show the answer

The shutdown point is the lowest point of AVC. Above it, the MC curve is the firm's short-run supply curve; below it, the firm produces nothing.

  • The price at the lowest point of ATC: That is the break-even price; between it and the AVC minimum the firm still produces at a loss.
  • The price at the lowest point of MC: The MC minimum is below the AVC minimum; there the firm cannot cover variable cost.
  • A price of zero, because fixed cost is sunk: At very low prices revenue would not cover variable cost, so producing adds to the loss.
  • Correct: The price at the lowest point of AVC: Right: below that, revenue does not cover variable cost.

4. A firm should shut down in the short run when the price is

  1. below the lowest point of average total cost
  2. below marginal cost at its current output
  3. below the lowest point of AVC
  4. equal to average total cost
Show the answer

The shutdown rule: shut down in the short run if P is below the lowest point of AVC; then the loss is just the fixed cost.

  • below the lowest point of average total cost: Between the two minimums the firm makes a loss but still covers variable cost, so it keeps producing.
  • below marginal cost at its current output: That says to produce less, not to stop.
  • Correct: below the lowest point of AVC: Right: it cannot cover variable cost at any output.
  • equal to average total cost: At P = ATC the firm earns normal profit.

Part 9 · Summary

Summary

In the short run a firm compares the price with average variable cost: if P is at least the lowest point of AVC it produces where MR = MC, even at a loss, because closing would lose the whole fixed cost; if P is below that point it shuts down. Fixed costs do not change that choice. Above the shutdown point, MC is the firm's short-run supply curve. In the long run, economic profit draws new firms in and losses push firms out; entry shifts market supply right and exit shifts it left, moving the price until economic profit is zero.
