Unit 3 · Topic 3.5

Profit Maximization

Marginal revenue is the change in total revenue from one more unit.

Practice 2: InterpretationPractice 3: Manipulation

Part 1 · Hook

Why this matters

A pottery sells every mug it makes for $20; one small shop cannot change the market price. It could make three mugs a day, or seven. Making more brings in more revenue, but each extra mug also costs more to make. The best output is found one mug at a time: keep going while the next mug adds more to revenue than to cost.

Part 5 · Step by step

How it works, step by step

  1. Marginal revenue is greater than marginal cost for the next unit→producing it adds MR - MC to profit, so the firm expands
  2. Output rises and diminishing marginal returns push MC up→the gap between MR and MC shrinks
  3. MR equals MC→one more unit would add nothing to profit, so profit is at its largest
  4. The firm knows its profit-maximizing output q*→it measures profit as (P - ATC) x q*, the rectangle from the price down to ATC

Part 6 · Key ideas

Key ideas

  • Marginal revenue: MR=ΔTRΔQ\text{MR} = \frac{\Delta \text{TR}}{\Delta Q}, the extra revenue from one more unit.
  • The profit-maximizing rule: produce where MR = MC. From a table: the last unit whose MR is at least its MC.
  • A firm that sells every unit at one market price has MR = AR = P: its demand curve is horizontal, d = MR = AR = P.
  • Profit = TR - TC = (P - ATC) x q, at the profit-maximizing q: the rectangle between the price and ATC.

Part 7 · Misconception

A common mistake

The wrong idea: A firm maximizes profit by producing where its average total cost is lowest, because each unit is cheapest there.

What actually happens: Profit depends on revenue too. The firm produces where MR = MC; that output is at the lowest point of ATC only when the price happens to equal that lowest cost.

4. Use the table. Why should the firm not make a sixth mug?

  1. Total cost rises when the sixth mug is made
  2. Average total cost is above the price at six mugs
  3. Its MC ($24) is above its MR ($20)
  4. Marginal cost is rising at six mugs
Show the answer

Compare the margin: the sixth mug adds $20 to revenue and $24 to cost, so profit falls by $4. Stop where MR = MC, or at the last unit with MR ≥ MC.

  • Total cost rises when the sixth mug is made: Total cost rises with every mug; the test is whether it rises by more than revenue.
  • Average total cost is above the price at six mugs: ATC at six mugs is about $19.33, below the $20 price; the test is MR against MC.
  • Correct: Its MC ($24) is above its MR ($20): Right: the sixth mug lowers profit by $4.
  • Marginal cost is rising at six mugs: MC is already rising from the third mug, and mugs four and five still add profit.

7. At a firm's current output, marginal revenue is $15 and marginal cost is $11. To raise profit, the firm should

  1. produce more, as MR exceeds MC
  2. produce less, because cost is rising
  3. keep output the same, because it earns a profit
  4. raise its price above $15
Show the answer

While MR > MC, each extra unit adds MR - MC to profit. The firm expands until MR = MC.

  • Correct: produce more, as MR exceeds MC: Right: MR > MC, so more output raises profit.
  • produce less, because cost is rising: Rising cost matters only when it passes MR; here MR is still larger.
  • keep output the same, because it earns a profit: Profit is not yet as large as it can be while MR > MC.
  • raise its price above $15: The question is about output at the given price.

Part 9 · Summary

Summary

Marginal revenue is the change in total revenue from one more unit. A firm maximizes profit by producing where MR = MC: from a table, the last unit whose MR is at least its MC. A firm that sells every unit at a market price it cannot change has MR = AR = P, so its demand curve is a horizontal line, d = MR = AR = P. Profit is TR - TC = (P - ATC) x q at the profit-maximizing output: on a graph, the rectangle from the price down to ATC, out to that output.
