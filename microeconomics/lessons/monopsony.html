Unit 5 · Topic 5.4

Monopsonistic Markets

A monopsony is the only buyer of a kind of labor.

Practice 2: Interpretation

Part 1 · Hook

Why this matters

In an invented mountain county there is one hospital, and nearly every nurse in the county works there. The hospital knows that offering more pay would bring in more nurses, but it would have to pay the raise to every nurse already on staff. That extra cost changes how many nurses it hires and what it pays them.

Part 5 · Step by step

How it works, step by step

  1. The firm is the only buyer of this labor→it faces the upward-sloping labor supply curve
  2. Hiring one more worker means a higher wage for every worker→MFC lies above labor supply
  3. The firm hires where MRP = MFC→it hires fewer workers than where MRP meets labor supply
  4. Fewer workers are hired→the wage read on labor supply is lower than the competitive wage

Part 6 · Key ideas

Key ideas

  • A monopsony is a single buyer of a factor. It faces the market labor supply curve, so to hire more it must raise pay for everyone.
  • So MFC lies above SL. The monopsonist hires where MRP = MFC, then pays the wage read down on SL at that quantity.
  • Compared with a competitive market: fewer workers, a lower wage, and deadweight loss between MRP and SL. The wage bill is W × L, the rectangle under the wage.
  • A minimum wage between the monopsony wage and the competitive wage makes MFC flat at the minimum wage, so both the wage and employment rise.

Part 7 · Misconception

A common mistake

The wrong idea: The single employer pays the wage where MRP crosses MFC, because that is the point it chose.

What actually happens: MRP = MFC gives only the number of workers. The wage is the lowest pay that brings in that many workers: read straight down to the labor supply curve.

Part 8 · Check yourself

Check yourself

Exam-style questions. Anything you miss goes into your review queue.

1. What is a monopsony?

  1. A market with a single seller of a product
  2. A labor market with many employers that each take the wage
  3. A market with a single buyer, such as one employer of a kind of labor
  4. A firm that hires workers in many different towns
Show the answer

Mono-psony: one buyer. A town where one firm hires nearly all workers of one kind is the classic case.

  • A market with a single seller of a product: That is a monopoly.
  • A labor market with many employers that each take the wage: That is a perfectly competitive labor market.
  • Correct: A market with a single buyer, such as one employer of a kind of labor: Right: one buyer faces the whole upward-sloping labor supply curve.
  • A firm that hires workers in many different towns: Having many workplaces does not make a firm the single buyer.

2. Why does a monopsonist's MFC curve lie above the labor supply curve?

  1. It pays each new worker more than the workers already hired
  2. Its workers produce more output than workers elsewhere
  3. It pays each worker the worker's MRP
  4. To hire one more worker it must raise the wage for every worker
Show the answer

A single employer faces the upward-sloping labor supply curve. A higher quantity needs a higher wage paid to all, so MFC > W.

  • It pays each new worker more than the workers already hired: Every worker is paid the same wage; the extra cost comes from raising everyone's pay.
  • Its workers produce more output than workers elsewhere: Output is about MRP; MFC is about cost.
  • It pays each worker the worker's MRP: It pays the wage on labor supply, which is below MRP at its quantity.
  • Correct: To hire one more worker it must raise the wage for every worker: Right: so the added cost is the new worker's wage plus the raise for all the others.

Part 9 · Summary

Summary

A monopsony is the only buyer of a kind of labor. Because it must raise everyone's pay to hire one more worker, its MFC lies above labor supply. It hires where MRP = MFC and pays the wage read on the labor supply curve at that quantity, so it hires fewer workers and pays less than a competitive market would, with deadweight loss between MRP and labor supply. The wage bill is the wage times the workers hired. A minimum wage set between the monopsony wage and the competitive wage makes MFC flat and raises both the wage and employment.
