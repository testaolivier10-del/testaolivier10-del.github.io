Demand

Demand shows how much buyers want at each price.

Part 1 · Hook

Why this matters

A coffee shop near campus drops its iced latte price from $5 to $4 for exam week, and the line runs out the door. A month later a new bubble tea shop opens next door, and the coffee shop sells fewer lattes at the same $4. Both stories are about demand, but only one of them moves the demand curve.

Part 5 · Step by step

How it works, step by step

  1. The price of a good falls, other things equal→the good is cheaper than its substitutes and buyers' money goes further
  2. Buyers switch toward it and can afford more (the substitution and income effects)→the quantity demanded rises
  3. The quantity demanded rises because of the good's own price→buyers move down along the same demand curve; the curve does not shift

Part 6 · Key ideas

Key ideas

  • Law of demand: other things equal, a higher price means a smaller quantity demanded. The curve slopes down.
  • It slopes down because of the substitution effect, the income effect and diminishing marginal utility.
  • A change in the good's own price is a movement along the curve: a change in quantity demanded.
  • A change in a determinant shifts the whole curve: tastes, income (normal or inferior), prices of substitutes and complements, expectations, the number of buyers.
  • Market demand is the horizontal sum of every buyer's quantity at each price.

Part 7 · Misconception

A common mistake

The wrong idea: When a good gets cheaper, its demand increases, so the demand curve shifts right.

What actually happens: A lower own price raises the quantity demanded: a movement down along the same curve. Demand shifts only when something other than the good's own price changes, such as income or the price of a related good.

4. The cafe cuts the smoothie price from $4 to $3. What happens in the market for its smoothies?

  1. Demand shifts right because the smoothies are cheaper
  2. A movement along demand: quantity demanded falls from 12 to 8
  3. A movement along demand: quantity demanded rises from 8 to 12
  4. Demand shifts left because each smoothie earns the cafe less
Show the answer

A change in the good's own price is a movement along the demand curve. The market quantity demanded rises from 8 to 12; demand itself does not change.

  • Demand shifts right because the smoothies are cheaper: A lower own price does not shift demand; it moves buyers along it.
  • A movement along demand: quantity demanded falls from 12 to 8: That is the change for a price rise from $3 to $4.
  • Correct: A movement along demand: quantity demanded rises from 8 to 12: Right: the own price changed, so buyers move along the curve.
  • Demand shifts left because each smoothie earns the cafe less: The cafe's earnings do not move the buyers' curve.

Scenario

Four news items about e-scooter rentals in Porthaven

Porthaven is an invented city where riders rent e-scooters by the trip. A local paper ran four items in one month:

  1. The rental company raised its price per trip from $3 to $4.
  2. The city raised bus fares by 50 cents.
  3. A survey found that riders expect the rental price to jump next month.
  4. A factory closed, and many residents earn less. Riders who can afford a car or a bike tend to stop renting scooters.

5. Item 1 reports a higher price per trip. How does it affect the market for scooter rentals?

  1. Demand for rentals shifts left
  2. Quantity demanded falls along the demand curve
  3. Demand for rentals shifts right
  4. Quantity demanded rises along the demand curve
Show the answer

The rental price is the good's own price. A rise moves riders along the demand curve: quantity demanded falls. Demand does not shift.

  • Demand for rentals shifts left: A change in the own price is a movement along demand, not a shift.
  • Correct: Quantity demanded falls along the demand curve: Right: the own price changed.
  • Demand for rentals shifts right: A higher price does not raise demand.
  • Quantity demanded rises along the demand curve: By the law of demand, a higher price lowers quantity demanded.

6. Item 2 reports higher bus fares. How does it affect the demand for scooter rentals?

  1. Demand shifts left, because buses are a complement
  2. Demand shifts right, because buses are a substitute
  3. Quantity demanded rises along the demand curve
  4. No change, because bus fares are a different market
Show the answer

Buses and scooters are substitutes for getting around town. When the bus costs more, some riders switch to scooters, so demand for rentals shifts right.

  • Demand shifts left, because buses are a complement: Riders use a bus or a scooter for a trip, so the two are substitutes.
  • Correct: Demand shifts right, because buses are a substitute: Right: a higher price of a substitute raises demand.
  • Quantity demanded rises along the demand curve: The rental price did not change; another good's price did, which shifts demand.
  • No change, because bus fares are a different market: Prices of related goods are a determinant of demand.

Part 9 · Summary

Summary

Demand shows how much buyers want at each price. By the law of demand, a higher price lowers the quantity demanded, because of the substitution and income effects and diminishing marginal utility. A change in the good's own price moves buyers along the curve; a change in tastes, income, the prices of substitutes or complements, expectations or the number of buyers shifts the whole curve. Market demand adds every buyer's quantity at each price.
