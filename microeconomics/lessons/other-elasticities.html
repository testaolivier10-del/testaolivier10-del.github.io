Unit 2 · Topic 2.5

Other Elasticities

Income elasticity of demand divides the percent change in quantity demanded by the percent change in income; cross-price elasticity divides the percent change in quantity demanded of one good by the percent change in the price of another.

Practice 3: Manipulation

Part 1 · Hook

Why this matters

When a city's factories hire again and paychecks rise, the steakhouse downtown fills up and the bus company sells fewer monthly passes. When a cafe raises the price of coffee, it sells more tea and less cream. Two more elasticities measure these responses, and their signs tell you what kind of good you are looking at.

Part 5 · Step by step

How it works, step by step

  1. The price of coffee rises→coffee drinkers look for a cheaper drink
  2. Some switch to tea, a substitute→the quantity of tea demanded rises at each tea price
  3. Tea demand rises as the coffee price rises→the cross-price elasticity is positive: the goods are substitutes

Part 6 · Key ideas

Key ideas

  • Income elasticity of demand = percent change in quantity demanded divided by percent change in income. Keep the sign.
  • Positive: normal good (above 1, demand grows faster than income). Negative: inferior good.
  • Cross-price elasticity of demand = percent change in quantity demanded of X divided by percent change in the price of Y. Keep the sign.
  • Positive: substitutes. Negative: complements. Near zero: unrelated.
  • Elasticity can be measured for any determinant of demand; each is a percent change in quantity over a percent change in the cause.

Part 7 · Misconception

A common mistake

The wrong idea: A cross-price elasticity of −0.3-0.3 is small and negative, so the two goods are substitutes that barely affect each other.

What actually happens: For cross-price elasticity the sign carries the meaning: negative means complements (a higher price of one lowers demand for the other). Substitutes have a positive cross-price elasticity.

  • It rises by about 3.6%: Multiply 1.8 by 5%, which gives 9%.

Part 9 · Summary

Summary

Income elasticity of demand divides the percent change in quantity demanded by the percent change in income; cross-price elasticity divides the percent change in quantity demanded of one good by the percent change in the price of another. Unlike price elasticity of demand, both keep their sign. A positive income elasticity marks a normal good and a negative one an inferior good. A positive cross-price elasticity marks substitutes, a negative one complements, and one near zero unrelated goods.
