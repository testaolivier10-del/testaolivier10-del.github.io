Unit 5 practice test: Factor Markets

twelve questions, every option explained

12 exam-style questions from the unit, free, with an explanation for every option. Most are multiple choice, as on the real exam; a few ask for a number or have you shift a curve on a graph, and sets share one data table, graph or scenario. Start at question 1

Scenario

A year in the invented town of Fenmore

Fenmore has a large market for nurses. During one year: (1) the town's population ages and hospitals treat more patients; (2) a nearby city raises its nurses' pay well above Fenmore's; (3) Fenmore's hospitals buy monitoring equipment that lets each nurse care for more patients; (4) a new nursing school in Fenmore graduates its first large class.

1. In the scenario, how does event (2), higher pay for nurses in the nearby city, affect the market for nurses in Fenmore?

  1. Labor demand in Fenmore increases
  2. Labor supply in Fenmore increases
  3. Nothing changes, because Fenmore's wage is the same
  4. Labor supply in Fenmore decreases
Show the answer

Wages in other jobs a worker could take shift labor supply. Better pay elsewhere shifts Fenmore's supply of nurses left.

  • Labor demand in Fenmore increases: Fenmore's hospitals have not changed how many nurses they want; the nurses' other options changed.
  • Labor supply in Fenmore increases: Better pay elsewhere pulls nurses away from Fenmore, not toward it.
  • Nothing changes, because Fenmore's wage is the same: The wage in other places is a shifter of labor supply here.
  • Correct: Labor supply in Fenmore decreases: Right: some nurses leave for the better-paid jobs, so fewer offer to work in Fenmore at each wage.

2. In the scenario, events (1) and (4) happen together. What can you say about the equilibrium in Fenmore's market for nurses?

  1. Employment rises, and the wage change is indeterminate
  2. The wage rises, and the employment change is indeterminate
  3. The wage and employment both rise for certain
  4. The wage falls and employment is unchanged
Show the answer

Demand right and supply right: quantity rises for sure; the wage rises if the demand shift is larger and falls if the supply shift is larger.

  • Correct: Employment rises, and the wage change is indeterminate: Right: more demand and more supply both raise quantity, but they push the wage in opposite directions.
  • The wage rises, and the employment change is indeterminate: Both shifts raise employment, so employment is certain; the wage is the uncertain one.
  • The wage and employment both rise for certain: The supply increase pushes the wage down, so the wage change depends on the sizes of the shifts.
  • The wage falls and employment is unchanged: Both shifts raise the number of nurses hired.

3. In the scenario, event (4) happens alone. What happens to the wage and employment of nurses in Fenmore?

  1. The wage rises and employment rises
  2. The wage falls and employment falls
  3. The wage falls and employment rises
  4. The wage rises and employment falls
Show the answer

More qualified nurses means more labor offered at each wage: a lower wage and more nurses hired.

  • The wage rises and employment rises: That is what a rise in labor demand would do.
  • The wage falls and employment falls: That is a fall in labor demand.
  • Correct: The wage falls and employment rises: Right: labor supply shifts right, so the equilibrium slides down labor demand.
  • The wage rises and employment falls: That is a fall in labor supply.

Scenario

A minimum wage for Caddock County nurses

Use the Caddock County table. The county sets a minimum wage of $14 an hour for nurses. The hospital may pay more but not less. At $14 an hour, up to 4 nurses are willing to work; a fifth would need $16.

4. In the scenario, what is the hospital's marginal factor cost of the second, third and fourth nurses after the minimum wage?

  1. $12, $16 and $20, as before
  2. $14 each, because every nurse already earns $14
  3. $10, $12 and $14, the wages in the table
  4. $26, $22 and $18, their MRPs
Show the answer

A minimum wage set above the monopsony wage turns the firm into a wage taker up to the labor supply curve.

  • $12, $16 and $20, as before: Those MFCs came from raising everyone's pay to hire one more; at a fixed $14 that no longer happens.
  • Correct: $14 each, because every nurse already earns $14: Right: hiring one more nurse no longer raises anyone else's pay, so MFC equals the minimum wage up to labor supply.
  • $10, $12 and $14, the wages in the table: Every nurse must now be paid at least $14.
  • $26, $22 and $18, their MRPs: Those are revenues the nurses add, not their cost.

5. In the scenario, how many nurses does the hospital hire after the minimum wage?

  1. 3, the same as before
  2. 2, down from 3
  3. 5, up from 3
  4. 4, up from 3
Show the answer

The minimum wage lowers MFC for the nurses the hospital was not hiring, so it hires more of them.

  • 3, the same as before: MFC for the fourth nurse falls from $20 to $14, below her $18 MRP.
  • 2, down from 3: In a monopsony, a minimum wage between the monopsony and competitive wages raises employment.
  • 5, up from 3: A fifth nurse would need $16, paid to all five, so she would add $80 - $56 = $24 to cost and only $14 to revenue.
  • Correct: 4, up from 3: Right: the fourth nurse adds $18 and costs $14, so she is hired; a fifth would raise every wage to $16, an MFC of $24, and adds only $14.

6. In the scenario, what happens to nurses' wage and employment compared with no minimum wage?

  1. Both rise, to $14 and 4 nurses
  2. The wage rises and employment falls
  3. The wage rises and employment stays at 3
  4. Both fall
Show the answer

Unlike in a competitive labor market, a minimum wage in a monopsony between the monopsony wage and the competitive wage raises the wage and employment together.

  • Correct: Both rise, to $14 and 4 nurses: Right: a minimum wage set between the monopsony wage and the competitive wage can raise both.
  • The wage rises and employment falls: That happens with a minimum wage above the equilibrium in a competitive labor market, not here.
  • The wage rises and employment stays at 3: The fourth nurse now costs less than she adds, so she is hired.
  • Both fall: The wage cannot fall below $14, and employment rises.

7. Hiring one more worker raises a firm's output by 8 units. Each extra unit adds $5 to the firm's revenue. What is that worker's marginal revenue product, in dollars?

Type a number. A four-function calculator is allowed, as on the exam.

Show the answer

MRP = MP × MR = 8 × $5 = $40.

  • Answer: 40

8. A firm's total labor cost is $180 with 2 workers and $270 with 3 workers. What is the marginal resource cost of the third worker, in dollars?

Type a number. A four-function calculator is allowed, as on the exam.

Show the answer

MRC = change in total labor cost ÷ change in workers = ($270 - $180) ÷ 1 = $90. For a wage taker it equals the wage.

  • Answer: 90

9. A competitive firm's workers have MRPs of $70, $60, $50, $40 and $30. The market wage is $50. How many workers does it hire?

Type a number. A four-function calculator is allowed, as on the exam.

Show the answer

Hire while MRP ≥ wage: $70, $60 and $50 all cover the $50 wage (the third worker breaks even), while the fourth adds $40. Hire 3.

  • Answer: 3

10. A single employer pays 10 workers $15 an hour. To hire an 11th worker it must raise the wage to $16 for all 11. What is the MFC of the 11th worker, in dollars per hour?

Type a number. A four-function calculator is allowed, as on the exam.

Show the answer

Total labor cost goes from 10 × $15 = $150 to 11 × $16 = $176, so MFC = $176 - $150 = $26: the new worker's $16 plus a $1 raise for each of the other 10.

  • Answer: 26

11. What is a factor market?

  1. A market where households buy finished goods from firms
  2. A market where a government sets the price of each input
  3. A market where firms sell goods to other countries
  4. A market where firms buy labor, land and capital from their owners
Show the answer

In a factor market, households (and other owners of resources) sell labor, land and capital, and firms buy them. Payments are wages, rent and interest.

  • A market where households buy finished goods from firms: That is a product market.
  • A market where a government sets the price of each input: Prices in factor markets are usually set by buyers and sellers; who sets them is not what defines the market.
  • A market where firms sell goods to other countries: Selling abroad is trade in goods, which happens in product markets.
  • Correct: A market where firms buy labor, land and capital from their owners: Right: factor markets trade the inputs used to make goods and services.

12. A country raises the minimum age at which people may work in factories. What happens in the market for factory workers?

  1. Labor demand shifts left: the wage and employment fall
  2. Labor supply shifts right: the wage falls and employment rises
  3. Labor demand shifts right: the wage and employment rise
  4. Labor supply shifts left: the wage rises and employment falls
Show the answer

Rules on who may work shift labor supply. A higher minimum age shifts it left.

  • Labor demand shifts left: the wage and employment fall: Factories still want the same workers; the rule changes who may sell their labor.
  • Labor supply shifts right: the wage falls and employment rises: The rule removes workers, so supply falls.
  • Labor demand shifts right: the wage and employment rise: Nothing here makes workers more valuable to factories.
  • Correct: Labor supply shifts left: the wage rises and employment falls: Right: fewer people may work at each wage.
