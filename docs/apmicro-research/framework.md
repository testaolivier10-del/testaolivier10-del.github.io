## 3. Topic content notes (scope, our words)

### From the CED text (Units 1-2, Topics 3.1-3.3)
- **1.1:** scarcity forces trade-offs; most factors of production are scarce, but some (such as established
  knowledge, because it is non-rival) may not be.
- **1.2:** every allocation answers what, how and for whom; command, market and mixed systems answer through
  different institutions and coordinating mechanisms.
- **1.3:** the PPC shows trade-offs, scarcity, unused resources and growth; its shape reflects constant, increasing
  or **decreasing** opportunity cost (decreasing is listed, unlike most textbooks); shifts come from changes in
  resources or productivity/technology; growth is an outward shift; calculate opportunity cost from PPC data or
  tables.
- **1.4:** absolute advantage (more output from the same resources) vs comparative advantage (lower opportunity
  cost); specializing lets both consume beyond their PPCs; calculate mutually beneficial terms of trade.
- **1.5:** economic cost includes **explicit and implicit** costs; total benefit is utility for consumers and total
  revenue for firms; net benefit is largest at the optimal choice; some choices need marginal comparisons, others
  totals.
- **1.6:** consumers maximize total utility under constraints with diminishing marginal utility; the optimum
  equalizes **marginal utility per dollar** across goods; marginal analysis compares extra benefit with extra cost;
  sunk costs and fixed benefits do not change the optimal quantity; optimum where MB = MC (or total benefit is
  largest). **Exclusion (official): indifference curves are beyond the scope; MU/P ratios are in scope.**
- **2.1:** well-defined property rights let markets work; agents respond to incentives under constraints (income,
  time, law); law of demand is a movement along the curve; downward slope from income and substitution effects
  and/or diminishing marginal utility; market demand is the horizontal sum of individual demands; determinants shift
  the curve.
- **2.2:** law of supply (movement along the curve); market supply is the sum of individual supplies; determinants
  shift it.
- **2.3:** price elasticity of demand = % change in Qd / % change in P; elasticity changes along a straight-line
  demand curve, so slope is not elasticity; elastic (>1), inelastic (<1), unit elastic (=1) in absolute value;
  depends on substitutes and similar factors; the total revenue test (price change and revenue direction by
  elasticity); calculate from graphs or tables. Whether the midpoint formula is required was **not seen** in the
  readable text (flag).
- **2.4:** price elasticity of supply, same benchmarks; depends on factors such as alternative input prices.
- **2.5:** elasticity for any determinant; **income elasticity** (sign gives normal vs inferior) and
  **cross-price elasticity** (sign gives substitutes, complements, unrelated).
- **2.6:** equilibrium clears the market in perfect competition; price signals allocate resources; consumer and
  producer surplus measure gains and efficiency; with no market failure, equilibrium maximizes total surplus;
  calculate CS and PS at equilibrium.
- **2.7:** market forces close shortages and surpluses; shifts change P, Q, CS, PS and total surplus; the size of
  the effect depends on the elasticities; calculate the changes.
- **2.8:** price floors and ceilings and other price or quantity rules change incentives and outcomes in all market
  structures; taxes and subsidies shift supply or demand and change government revenue or cost; in an efficient
  market intervention can only lower allocative efficiency; deadweight loss defined; **tax and subsidy incidence
  depends on the elasticities**; calculate outcome changes.
- **2.9:** opening to trade moves the domestic price to the world price (above or below autarky), with imports or
  exports filling the gap; effects on CS, PS, total surplus; tariffs change domestic price, quantity, government
  revenue, CS and total surplus; quotas change quantities and so price and surpluses. **Exclusion (official):
  graphing quotas is beyond the scope; knowing how quotas change quantities is in scope.**
- **3.1:** the production function links inputs and output; total, marginal and average product; **diminishing
  marginal returns** come from adding one input with others fixed in the short run.
- **3.2:** fixed plus variable cost gives total cost; marginal, average, total and variable costs change with output,
  but total fixed cost is the same at every output, **including zero**; diminishing marginal returns make MC slope
  up; specialization and division of labor lower MC; cost curves shift with input prices or productivity.
- **3.3:** in the long run all costs are variable; returns to scale (increasing, constant, decreasing) describe
  input-output links; LRATC shows economies, constant returns (efficient scale) and diseconomies of scale; minimum
  efficient scale shapes the number of firms and the market structure. CED teacher note: long-run cost questions
  are hard for students but **not a large share of the exam**.
  https://apcentral.collegeboard.org/media/pdf/ap-microeconomics-course-and-exam-description.pdf

### From CR reports and scoring guidelines (Topics 3.4-6.5) [CR]/[SG] plus [ours]
- **3.4 Types of profit:** accounting profit = TR minus explicit costs; economic profit also subtracts implicit
  costs, so accounting profit is larger when implicit costs are positive; normal profit = zero economic profit
  [CR 2023 set 2; formulas ours].
- **3.5 Profit maximization:** produce where MR = MC (with tables, the last unit where MR is at least MC, compared
  for the next unit); profit = TR minus TC = (P minus ATC) x Q [SG 2026 Q2; CR 2023 set 1].
- **3.6 Short-run and long-run decisions:** shut down when price is below minimum AVC; fixed costs do not change MC
  or the output choice; positive economic profit draws entry, losses cause exit, shifting market supply until
  economic profit is zero [CR 2023 sets 1-2, 2025 set 2; SG 2026 Q2].
- **3.7 Perfect competition:** side-by-side market and firm graphs; the firm is a price taker with a horizontal
  d = MR = AR = P at the market price; long-run equilibrium at minimum ATC; constant-cost industries return to the
  same price [CR 2023 set 2, 2024 set 1, 2025 set 2]. Increasing- and decreasing-cost industries: not seen in any
  report (flag; check CED).
- **4.1:** barriers to entry and market power; imperfect competitors face downward-sloping demand with MR below it
  [CR 2023 set 2, 2024 set 2; ours].
- **4.2 Monopoly:** profit max at MR = MC, price read from the demand curve above that quantity; ATC placement shows
  profit, zero profit or loss; deadweight loss between D and MC from QM to the socially optimal Q; revenue is
  largest where MR = 0 (unit-elastic point); a monopolist produces on the elastic part of demand; **natural
  monopoly** (ATC falling over the whole relevant demand range) and regulation (fair-return price where P = ATC,
  socially optimal price where P = MC, which may need a subsidy) [CR 2023 sets 1-2, 2024 set 2, 2025 set 1].
- **4.3 Price discrimination:** a perfectly price-discriminating monopolist's MR is the demand curve, so it produces
  where D = MC (the efficient quantity), consumer surplus falls to zero and deadweight loss disappears [CR 2024
  set 2].
- **4.4 Monopolistic competition:** downward demand from product differentiation; short-run profit or loss; entry
  or exit shifts each firm's demand until long-run demand is tangent to ATC (zero economic profit), with excess
  capacity and price above MC [ours; CR 2025 set 1 tells teachers to revisit elasticity here]. No FRQ in
  2023-2026 centered on this topic (from the CR/SG list).
- **4.5 Oligopoly and game theory:** payoff matrices for two players; best responses, dominant strategies (may not
  exist), Nash equilibrium (may be one, two or none in pure strategies), the effect of a payoff change (tax,
  subsidy, cost change), joint profit maximization by a merged firm or cartel [CR 2024 set 1, 2025 set 1; SG
  2026 Q1]. Mixed strategies and sequential games: not seen (flag).
- **5.1-5.2:** factor demand is derived demand; MRP = MR x MP (in a competitive product market P x MP); labor demand
  shifts with product price or demand and with productivity; labor supply shifts with population, preferences,
  rules on who can work (CR 2025 set 1: a higher minimum working age cuts labor supply) [CR 2023 set 1, 2025 set 1].
- **5.3:** competitive firm hires where MRP = MFC = wage; wage set in the market graph and taken by the firm;
  fixed costs do not change hiring; least-cost input mix: MP_L / w = MP_K / r (CR 2023 set 1 notes students used
  the utility rule instead of cost minimization) [CR 2023 sets 1-2, 2024 set 2].
- **5.4 Monopsony:** single employer; MFC above labor supply; hire where MRP = MFC and pay the wage read from the
  supply curve at that quantity (lower wage, fewer workers than competition); a minimum wage set between the
  monopsony wage and the competitive wage can raise both wage and employment [CR 2025 set 2].
- **6.1:** efficiency where MSB = MSC; deadweight loss from any market outcome that differs from it [CR 2024 set 1].
- **6.2 Externalities:** negative production (MSC above MPC: market overproduces), positive production (MSC below
  MPC), positive consumption (MSB above MPB: market underproduces), negative consumption; correct with per-unit
  (Pigouvian) taxes or subsidies equal to the external cost or benefit at the efficient quantity; **lump-sum taxes
  do not change MC, price or quantity** [CR 2023 sets 1-2, 2024 set 1; SG 2026 Q1].
- **6.3 Public and private goods:** rival vs non-rival, excludable vs non-excludable; free-rider problem; public
  goods and common resources [ours; no FRQ 2023-2026 on this topic].
- **6.4:** government intervention in different structures: price ceilings on a monopoly (can raise quantity),
  subsidies, lump-sum vs per-unit taxes, minimum wage in monopsony, regulation of natural monopoly [CR 2023 set 1,
  2025 set 2].
- **6.5 Inequality:** Lorenz curve and Gini coefficient as measures of income or wealth inequality; sources of
  inequality; progressive, proportional and regressive taxes; transfer programs [ours; no FRQ 2023-2026; verify the
  CED's exact list].

## 5. Exam format, May 2027

- **Date: Tuesday, May 4, 2027, Session 2 (12 p.m. local time in most US schools).** AP Macroeconomics is Friday,
  May 7, 2027 (https://apcentral.collegeboard.org/exam-administration-ordering-scores/exam-dates). Late-testing date
  is on a separate page, not checked.
- **Mode: hybrid digital.** MCQ answered in Bluebook; FRQs shown in Bluebook and **handwritten (graphs drawn by
  hand) in a paper booklet** returned for scoring (exam page).
- **Total 2 h 10 min** (student assessment page).
- **Section I: 60 MCQ, 1 h 10 min, 66% of score** (exam page). Number of answer choices **not stated** on the exam
  or student page and the CED exam section was not readable (flag; older paper exams used five).
- **Section II: 3 FRQs, 1 h including a 10-minute reading/planning period, 33% of score** (exam page):
  - **Q1 long FRQ, 10 points, 50% of the section.** Suggested ~25 min.
  - **Q2 and Q3 short FRQs, 5 points each, 25% of the section each.** Suggested ~12 min each.
  - Students may move between questions; the clock turns red at 5 minutes with no further warnings; only booklet
    work is scored; label parts (A, B, i, ii); a correctly labeled graph has every axis and curve labeled and shows
    directional changes; "calculate" means show how the answer was reached (2026 FRQ directions page).
- **Calculator:** four-function only on **both sections**; Bluebook has a built-in **Desmos four-function**
  calculator; scientific and graphing calculators are not allowed (calculator policy page).
- 2023-2025 used two FRQ sets; 2026 posted one set (past exam questions page).

### FRQ scoring rules (2026 SG and CR reports)
- Graphs: label **every axis and curve**; mark the required prices and quantities on the axes (PM, QM, PF, QF, Q1,
  P1, $20); the firm's demand in perfect competition as **d = MR = AR = P** with PF on the axis; equilibrium shown
  with dotted lines to the axes (hash marks lost points in 2024 and 2025); one clear ATC minimum with MC passing
  through it; areas fully shaded (partial shading loses the point).
- "State/identify" needs an explicit answer; "explain" needs the causal link (2026 SG; CR 2025 set 1 on subsidy
  answers that skip MC/MR).
- "Calculate" needs the formula or the numbers used, not just the answer (2026 SG Q2; CR 2023 set 1 asks for
  formula, numbers, answer).
- Game theory answers need **the numeric comparisons from the matrix**, both comparisons for a dominant-strategy
  claim (CR 2024 set 1, 2025 set 1).
- Each point is scored independently; later answers can follow from earlier ones (2026 SG notes).

## 7. Where students lose points (CR reports 2023-2025; 2026 SG)

Paraphrased; percentages are the share of students earning that point.

1. **Cost curve geometry: ATC placement and MC through its minimum (every year).** ATC drawn on, above or tangent to
   demand when profit is positive, or below it when losses are asked; MC not passing through the single ATC minimum;
   flat-bottomed ATC. ATC point: 44% (2024 set 2), 47% (2025 set 1), 50% (2023 set 1), 52% (2023 set 2).
   https://apcentral.collegeboard.org/media/pdf/ap24-cr-report-microeconomics-set-2.pdf ;
   https://apcentral.collegeboard.org/media/pdf/ap25-cr-report-microeconomics-set-1.pdf
2. **The competitive firm's demand curve.** Drawing it downward sloping, not linking it to the market price
   (PF = PM), or writing "MRDARP"/"DARP" as a label. Firm d = MR point: 47.5% (2024 set 1), 48% (2025 set 2), 49%
   (2023 set 2). Readers ask for "MR = d = AR = P" with PF on the axis and the market on the left, firm on the right.
   https://apcentral.collegeboard.org/media/pdf/ap25-cr-report-microeconomics-set-2.pdf ;
   https://apcentral.collegeboard.org/media/pdf/ap24-cr-report-microeconomics-set-1.pdf
3. **Shading areas.** Government subsidy cost **8%** (2025 set 2: shaded only part, or shaded tax revenue after a
   leftward shift); profit rectangle 40% (2023 set 2); DWL in monopoly 53-56% (shaded to ATC instead of between D and
   MC from QM to QS); CS in monopoly 61% (shaded below MR or past Q*).
   https://apcentral.collegeboard.org/media/pdf/ap23-cr-report-micro-set-1.pdf ;
   https://apcentral.collegeboard.org/media/pdf/ap23-cr-report-micro-set-2.pdf
4. **Monopoly points from the wrong intersection.** Q where MC meets D, or P read from MR; revenue-maximizing Q
   (MR = 0) 38%; unit elastic there 42%; perfect price discrimination price where D = MC 42% and CS falls to zero
   29% (2024 set 2). Natural monopoly identified from ATC over the demand range 30%; regulated price points 30-49%
   (2023 set 2).
5. **Factor-market chains.** Higher product demand raises labor demand **through price, MR and MRP**: 11% (2025 set
   1); MRP rises with explanation 35% (2025 set 2); hiring by MRP vs MFC 29-37% (2023 set 2, 2024 set 2); monopsony
   wage read from MRP instead of labor supply, or hiring at S = MRP (wage point 51%, wage bill 41%, 2025 set 2);
   fixed cost does not change hiring 27% (2023 set 2); writing MRP = MR x P.
6. **Marginal cost and average cost arithmetic.** MC as change in TC without dividing by change in output: **9%**
   (2024 set 2); AFC divided by workers instead of output 30%; TFC multiplied by quantity 46% (2023 set 1); LRATC vs
   change in total cost; economies of scale confused with returns to scale (2025 set 2, diseconomies point 48%;
   2024 set 2, 18%).
7. **Taxes, subsidies and controls.** Lump-sum vs per-unit taxes (lump-sum leaves MC, P and Q unchanged: 24% in
   2023 set 1); subsidy shifts supply right (consumer price after subsidy 34%, subsidy cost using the new quantity
   35%, DWL rises past the efficient quantity **6%**, 2024 set 2); a nonbinding floor has no effect (57%, 2025 set
   1); a binding ceiling cuts quantity via quantity supplied (42%, 2024 set 2); "supply greater than demand"
   instead of quantity supplied vs quantity demanded (2025 set 2, price floor point 36%).
8. **Externalities.** Treating a production externality as a consumption one, MSC vs MPC reversed, assuming a
   competitive equilibrium is always efficient, policy not matched to the cause (subsidize consumers for a positive
   consumption externality). Market Q vs efficient Q with MPC > MSC: **11%** (2023 set 2); inefficiency by marginal
   analysis 18% (2023 set 1); subsidy choice 36% (2024 set 1).
9. **Game theory comparisons.** Picking the highest payoff without the rival's choice; claiming a dominant strategy
   that does not exist (no-dominant-strategy point 43-46%); finding only one of two Nash equilibria (48%) or
   confusing Nash with dominant strategy (29%); summing all cells for joint profit (2024 set 1, 2025 set 1).
10. **Elasticity signs and uses.** Calling demand inelastic because the coefficient is negative; dividing the wrong
    way; calling goods complements when cross-price elasticity is positive; classifying normal vs inferior from
    price elasticity (cross-price elasticity 42%, 2024 set 1; substitutes 35-36%, 2023 set 1 and 2025 set 2).
11. **Long-run adjustment chain.** Entry or exit explained by "demand" instead of profit; exit not tied to lower
    market supply (28%, 2023 set 1); entry from positive profit 53% (2024 set 1); new long-run firm position 38%
    (2023 set 2).
12. **Utility maximization.** Ignoring MU/P, swapping prices, stopping at the first unit; the MU/P bundle point 29%
    (2025 set 2).
13. **Surplus arithmetic.** Wrong triangle, missing the one-half, original quantity after a change; total surplus
    56-58%; trade direction from comparing Qs and Qd at the world price (2025 set 1, 58%); comparative advantage of
    the wrong good or absolute advantage (2023 set 1).

### Reader advice to build into the course
- Side-by-side market and firm graphs, market left, firm right, price carried across with a dotted line.
- Practice monopoly graphs with positive, zero and negative profit; MC through ATC's minimum every time.
- DWL as the area between D (or MSB) and MC (or MSC) from the actual to the efficient quantity; shade fully.
- Write the formula before the numbers (MC = change in TC / change in Q; profit = TR minus TC).
- Vary table layouts so output is not always the first column (CR 2024 set 2).
- Four-step long-run chain: profit, entry/exit, market supply shift, new price and quantity.
- Product-to-factor chain: product demand, price, MR, MRP, labor demand, wage.
- Matrix reading drills with both comparisons stated; role-play best responses.
- Introduce MPB/MPC alongside demand and supply in Unit 2 (CR 2024 set 1).

## 9. Every formula and calculation

| Calculation | Formula (standard; [ours] unless noted) | Topic |
|---|---|---|
| Opportunity cost, comparative advantage, terms of trade | from PPC tables (output and input methods) | 1.3-1.4 (CED) |
| Economic vs accounting cost | explicit + implicit; accounting profit = TR − explicit; economic = TR − explicit − implicit | 1.5, 3.4 |
| Utility maximization | MU_x / P_x = MU_y / P_y with the budget fully spent; total utility = sum of MU | 1.6 (CED) |
| Price elasticity of demand / supply | % change in Q / % change in P (absolute value for demand) | 2.3-2.4 (CED) |
| Income, cross-price elasticity | % change in Qd / % change in income; % change in Qd of X / % change in P of Y | 2.5 (CED) |
| Total revenue test | TR = P x Q; direction by elastic/inelastic | 2.3 (CED) |
| Consumer, producer surplus | triangle ½ x base x height (or trapezoid) | 2.6 (CED) |
| Shortage / surplus | Qd − Qs at the controlled price | 2.7, 2.8 |
| Tax revenue, subsidy cost | per-unit amount x new quantity | 2.8 (CR) |
| Deadweight loss | ½ x (change in Q) x (price wedge) | 2.8, 6.2 (CR) |
| Tariff revenue | tariff x imports | 2.9 |
| Product | MP = change in TP / change in L; AP = TP / L | 3.1 (CED) |
| Costs | TC = TFC + TVC; AFC = TFC/Q; AVC = TVC/Q; ATC = TC/Q; MC = change in TC / change in Q | 3.2 (CED, CR) |
| LRATC | LRTC / Q | 3.3 (CR) |
| Profit | TR − TC = (P − ATC) x Q | 3.4-3.5 (SG) |
| Shutdown | produce in the short run only if P ≥ min AVC | 3.6 |
| MRP, MFC | MRP = MP x MR (= MP x P in competition); MFC = change in TC / change in L | 5.1-5.4 (CR) |
| Least-cost input rule | MP_L / w = MP_K / r | 5.3 (CR 2023) |
| Wage bill | wage x workers | 5.4 (CR) |
| Pigouvian tax/subsidy | external cost or benefit per unit at the efficient Q | 6.2 (CR) |
| Joint profit, payoff change | sum of the two payoffs in one cell; add or subtract a tax or cost in every affected cell | 4.5 (CR) |
| Gini coefficient | area between equality line and Lorenz curve / total area under the equality line (flag: CED coverage unconfirmed) | 6.5 |

### Graphs: what `mac-graph.js` already draws vs what is new

The engine (`macroeconomics/assets/mac-graph.js`, `docs/apmacro-architecture.md` "Graphs") has eight kinds:
`ppc`, `sd`, `adas`, `money`, `reserves`, `lf`, `phillips`, `forex`. All curves are straight lines of slope ±1 (or
vertical, or the PPC arc, or the reserve-demand kink) on a 0-10 grid; tasks are `label`, `shift` (whole steps),
`equilibrium` and `point`. **It has no area shading or area picking, no curved cost curves, no second panel, no
horizontal price lines and no table/matrix visual** (grep of `mac-graph.js` for shade, polygon, area, surplus,
deadweight finds nothing).

| Micro graph | Status | What is needed |
|---|---|---|
| PPC (bowed out, straight) | **reuse as is** (`ppc`, `linear`) | add a bowed-in shape for decreasing opportunity cost (1.3 EK) |
| Supply and demand shifts, equilibrium | **reuse as is** (`sd`) | none |
| Double shifts, indeterminate outcome | **reuse as is** (`indeterminate`) | none |
| Competitive labor market (wage, quantity of labor) | reuse with a new kind config | a `labor` kind like `sd` with axis and curve labels (W, QL, D_L = MRP, S_L) |
| Consumer and producer surplus | **new** | area task: shade or pick the region between curve, price line and axis |
| Price ceilings and floors | **new** (extends `sd`) | a draggable horizontal price line, read Qs and Qd, DWL area |
| Per-unit tax and subsidy with surplus areas | **new** (extends `sd`) | shift by the tax wedge (slope 1 makes a vertical shift equal a whole step), buyer and seller prices, revenue or subsidy cost rectangle, DWL triangle |
| Tariffs (and quotas, not graphed) with surplus areas | **new** (extends `sd`) | world price line, imports gap, tariff line, CS, PS, revenue, two DWL triangles |
| Short-run cost curves (MC, ATC, AVC) | **new** | curved U shapes with MC through both minima; ATC/AVC gap narrowing |
| LRATC | **new** | U or flat-bottomed long-run curve; regions for economies/diseconomies |
| Perfect competition firm and market side by side | **new** | two linked panels, price carried across; horizontal d = MR = AR = P; QF at MR = MC; profit/loss rectangle; long-run tangency at min ATC |
| Monopoly (incl. natural monopoly, price discrimination) | **new** | D with MR at twice the slope; points from two intersections (MR = MC, then up to D); profit, CS, DWL areas; MR = 0 point |
| Monopolistic competition | **new** | as monopoly plus long-run D tangent to ATC |
| Monopsony | **new** | S_L, MFC above it, MRP; hire at MRP = MFC, wage down on S_L; minimum wage line |
| Externalities | **new** (extends `sd`) | four curve ids (MPB, MSB, MPC, MSC), gap between private and social curves, DWL triangle, tax/subsidy closing the gap |
| Lorenz curve | **new** | equality diagonal, bowed curve, shift toward/away from equality |
| Game theory payoff matrices | **new** (not a graph) | 2x2 matrix widget: circle best responses, mark dominant strategies and Nash cells, edit payoffs after a tax or cost change |

Grader rules (`ApMacroGrade.graph`, point by point, never by a model) carry over; the new work is the geometry
(curves that are not slope ±1, a second panel) and an `area` task graded by which region the student shaded.
