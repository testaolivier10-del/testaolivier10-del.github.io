## 2. Exam format

Source: https://apcentral.collegeboard.org/courses/ap-chemistry/exam ; course overview PDF
https://apcentral.collegeboard.org/media/pdf/ap-chemistry-course-overview.pdf

- Total time 3 h 15 min.
- **Section I, MCQ:** 60 questions, 90 min, 50% of score. Discrete items and item sets built on data/stimulus.
  4 answer choices (A–D) (CB moved AP Classroom questions to 4 options in 2024 to match the exam; updates-2024-25 page).
  MCQ assesses Science Practices 1, 2, 4, 5, 6 (not Practice 3, which is FRQ only) (course overview PDF).
- **Section II, FRQ:** 7 questions, 105 min, 50% of score. **3 long** (10 points each) + **4 short** (4 points each)
  = 70 raw points. Assesses all 6 practices. On released exams Q1–Q3 are the long questions and Q4–Q7 the short ones
  (consistent with 2023–2025 scoring statistics and CR reports).
- **Calculator:** allowed on both sections. Scientific or graphing handheld, plus the built-in Desmos scientific and
  graphing calculators in Bluebook; four-function allowed but not recommended
  (https://apstudents.collegeboard.org/exam-policies-guidelines/calculator-policies). No 2027 change listed for Chemistry.
- **Digital (hybrid) format:** students answer MCQ and *view* FRQs in Bluebook, then **handwrite** FRQ answers in paper
  booklets that are returned for scoring. Started with the **May 2025** exam (Chemistry was among the 28 exams moved to
  digital/hybrid in 2025; e.g. https://www.fwcd.org/news/2024-12-04/28-ap-exams-go-digital-in-may-2025 ; exam page).
- **Reference materials:** periodic table plus "AP Chemistry Equations and Constants" sheet, provided in print (mailed to
  schools) and inside Bluebook (exam page).
- **2027 exam date:** Thursday, May 6, 2027 (exam page).

### Equations and constants sheet (current, reorganized 2024)
Source: https://apcentral.collegeboard.org/media/pdf/ap-chemistry-equations-sheet.pdf
- Unit symbols: g, mol, L, m, s, Hz, atm, mm Hg, °C, K, J, V, C, A.
- Conversions: 1 Hz = 1 s⁻¹; 1 atm = 760 mm Hg = 760 torr; K = °C + 273.15; 1 V = 1 J/C; 1 A = 1 C/s.
- Metric prefixes table (giga to pico).
- Atomic structure: Coulomb's law F ∝ q₁q₂/r² (added 2024); E = hν; c = λν; h = 6.626 × 10⁻³⁴ J·s;
  c = 2.998 × 10⁸ m/s; Avogadro 6.022 × 10²³ mol⁻¹.
- Gases, liquids, solutions: P₁V₁/T₁ = P₂V₂/T₂ (combined gas law); PV = nRT; Dalton P_total = P_A + P_B + …;
  P_A = X_A·P_total; molar mass M = m/n; D = m/V; KE = ½mv²; Beer's law A = εbc; R = 8.314 J mol⁻¹ K⁻¹ and
  0.08206 L atm mol⁻¹ K⁻¹; STP = 273.15 K and 1.0 atm; 22.4 L/mol at STP.
- Kinetics (no zero-order integrated law listed): first order ln[A]t − ln[A]0 = −kt and [A]t = [A]0e^(−kt); second order
  1/[A]t − 1/[A]0 = kt; t½ = 0.693/k (first order).
- Equilibrium: generic Kc and Kp expressions; Kw = [H₃O⁺][OH⁻] = 1.0 × 10⁻¹⁴ at 25 °C; pH + pOH = 14;
  pH, pOH, pKa, pKb definitions; Kw = Ka × Kb and pKw = pKa + pKb (added 2024); Henderson-Hasselbalch.
- Thermodynamics/electrochemistry: ΔH°rxn, ΔS°rxn, ΔG°rxn from products minus reactants; q = mcΔT;
  ΔG° = ΔH° − TΔS°; ΔG° = −RT ln K; ΔG° = −nFE°; E = E° − (RT/nF) ln Q (Nernst); I = q/t; F = 96,485 C/mol e⁻.
- **Not included:** a standard reduction potential table, solubility rules, Arrhenius equation, zero-order integrated law.
  (Course content should teach these as reasoning, or give values in the question as the exam does.)

## 3. Science practices and labs

Source: CED (practices and skills); course overview PDF (MCQ uses practices 1, 2, 4, 5, 6).

| # | Practice | Skills |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Models and Representations | 1.A, 1.B (describe components/quantities at particulate and macroscopic levels) |
| 2 | Question and Method | 2.A–2.F (testable question, hypothesis, procedure, observations, error sources, modifications) |
| 3 | Representing Data and Phenomena | 3.A graph, 3.B particulate/visual model, 3.C cross-scale representation |

### Labs
- Requirement (CED, course overview): at least 25% of instructional time in hands-on lab work; **minimum 16 hands-on
  labs, at least 6 of them inquiry-based**; students keep a lab notebook. The CB does **not** mandate specific titles.
- Reference set: CB lab manual "AP Chemistry Guided-Inquiry Experiments: Applying the Science Practices" (2013), 16
  investigations (titles confirmed in CB sample syllabus 2, https://apcentral.collegeboard.org/media/pdf/ap-chemistry-sample-syllabus-2.pdf):
  1. Concentration vs transmitted light (spectrophotometry)
  2. Mass percent of copper in brass by color (spectrophotometry)
  3. What makes hard water hard? (gravimetric)
  4. How much acid is in fruit juices and soft drinks? (titration)
  5. Sticky question: separating molecules that attract each other (chromatography)
  6. What's in that bottle? (bonding in solids)
  7. Applying green chemistry to purification (stoichiometry)
  8. Analysis of hydrogen peroxide (redox titration)
  9. Can components of quick ache relief resolve consumer complaints? (separation/analysis)
  10. How long will that marble statue last? (kinetics)
  11. Rate law of the fading of crystal violet using Beer's law (kinetics)
  12. Hand warmer design challenge: where does the heat come from? (thermochemistry)
  13. Can we make the colors of the rainbow? (Le Châtelier)
  14. Structure and concentration of acid/base vs pH during titration
  15. Buffering activity of common household products
  16. Preparation and testing of an effective buffer
- The "6 guided inquiry" figure is the minimum count, not a fixed list of six; nearly all 16 manual labs are written
  as guided inquiry. Recent FRQs lean on lab skills: calorimetry, titration curves, solution prep with volumetric
  glassware, chromatography, Beer's law, analog-instrument reading.

### Changes

  - Equation sheet reorganized: Coulomb's law, metric prefixes, Kw = Ka×Kb and pKw = pKa + pKb added.
  - AP Classroom: MCQs standardized to 4 choices; new practice exams.
- **2025 (2024-25 exam):** first hybrid digital administration in Bluebook (see section 2).

### Exclusion statements (what the exam will not test)
Verified in the current CED text (Units 1–2):
- 1.2: interpreting mass spectra of samples with multiple elements, or peaks from species other than singly charged
  monatomic ions.
- 1.5: assigning quantum numbers to electrons in subshells.
- 1.7: writing electron configurations of aufbau exceptions.
- 2.3: knowledge of specific crystal structures.
- 2.7: derivation and depiction of hybrid orbitals; hybridization involving d orbitals; molecular orbital theory
  (no MO diagrams, MO filling, or bonding/nonbonding/antibonding distinction assessed explicitly).
- 3.1 (note, not an exclusion): "London dispersion forces" should not be used as a synonym for "van der Waals forces".

Units 3–9 **[UNVERIFIED verbatim; the CED text beyond topic 3.1 could not be extracted]**, widely reported and
consistent with the 2019 CED as I recall it:
- 3.7: calculations with molality, percent by mass, percent by volume not assessed; colligative properties not
  assessed (reported by Fiveable/TutorChase study notes).
- 8.11 (old 7.13): computations of solubility as a function of pH not assessed (only qualitative reasoning)
  (https://fiveable.me/ap-chem/unit-7/ph-solubility/study-guide/qd1vmgubfqj1rcw5ifov).
- 9.8: labeling electrodes as positive or negative not assessed (recalled from 2019 CED; not found online).
- 9.10: Nernst equation is on the sheet, but the exam emphasizes qualitative use (sign/direction from Q vs 1);
  study sites say exact Nernst calculations are not expected. Treat as "qualitative focus", verify exact wording.
- Older (2013–2014 framework) exclusions frequently repeated online (Arrhenius calculations, Lewis acid-base,
  polyprotic titration numerics, phase diagrams) come from the old curriculum framework
  (https://adriandingleschemistrypages.com/ap/beyond-the-scope-of-this-course-and-the-ap-exam/). Do not treat as
  current without checking the 2024 CED.
- **In scope (not excluded), contrary to some assumptions:** photoelectron spectroscopy (topic 1.6), Beer-Lambert law
  (3.13, on the equation sheet; appeared in 2026 FRQ 6), spectroscopy/EM spectrum (3.11), Coulomb's law reasoning.

## 6. Chief Reader Reports: common FRQ mistakes and weak skills

2024 https://apcentral.collegeboard.org/media/pdf/ap24-cr-report-chemistry.pdf ;
2023 https://apcentral.collegeboard.org/media/pdf/ap23-cr-report-chemistry.pdf ;
2022: CR report URL (ap22-cr-report-chemistry.pdf) did not resolve; summary from ChemEd X
https://www.chemedx.org/article/using-information-2022-ap-chemistry-reading-improve-exam-performance [secondary].

(https://teachchemistry.org/periodical/issues/september-2026/extended-feature-the-2026-ap-chemistry-debrief).

### FRQ means
- 2025 (scoring statistics): Q1 4.78/10, Q2 4.29/10, Q3 3.61/10, Q4 2.32/4, Q5 1.88/4, Q6 1.34/4 (galvanic cell,
  lowest), Q7 2.00/4.
- 2024: Q1 4.43, Q2 5.84, Q3 4.42 (/10); Q4 2.60, Q5 2.07, Q6 2.26, Q7 2.03 (/4).
- 2023: Q1 5.38, Q2 5.22, Q3 4.51 (/10); Q4 1.66 (buffer prep, lowest), Q5 2.54, Q6 1.95, Q7 2.23 (/4).

### Recurring errors by theme (paraphrased)
**Significant figures and units**
- Sig-fig errors are the single most common point loss in calculation parts (2022 Q1, 2023 Q2, 2025 Q3: heat value
  reported to wrong sig figs; addition/subtraction rule confused with multiplication/division rule).
- Missing or wrong units, kJ vs J slips, °C not converted to K in gas law, wrong R value (J vs L·atm) (2023 Q5,
  2024 Q2, 2025 Q5). Readers want full dimensional-analysis setups shown.
- Algebra/log slips: ln used instead of log in Henderson-Hasselbalch (2024 Q2, 2025 Q2); dividing molarity by volume
  instead of multiplying (2024 Q7); computing pOH and calling it pH (2025 Q1); dilution with solvent volume instead of
  total volume (2025 Q1); decimal transcription (0.043 vs 0.43).

**Justifications / argumentation**
- Restating the prompt, vague generalities, no comparison of both species, no cited data. Readers repeatedly
  recommend Claim-Evidence-Reasoning structure (2023 Q3, Q5, Q7; 2025 Q5).
- Periodicity/IMF arguments that cite only molar mass or "bigger atom" instead of number of occupied shells,
  polarizability, Coulomb's law distance (2023 Q6, 2024 Q3, 2025 Q5). Vocabulary mix-ups: shell vs subshell vs orbital.
- Entropy explained as "disorder" without particle-level reasoning (microstates, dispersal of matter/energy)
  (2024 Q2, 2025 Q3).
- Coulomb's law: using ionic radius as r rather than distance between interacting species (2025 Q1).

**Particulate diagrams**
- Wrong orientation of water dipoles around ions (O toward cation, H toward anion) (2023 Q5).
- H-bond drawn to wrong atom or within the same species; H-bond criteria unclear (2025 Q4).
- Counting particles in equilibrium diagrams for K (2024 Q5, 2025 Q3); using limiting-reactant logic where an
  equilibrium expression is needed (2024 Q5); representing species at points along a titration curve (2024 Q1, 2025 Q2).

**Intermolecular vs intramolecular**
- Saying covalent bonds break on vaporization; omitting dipole-dipole for polar molecules; calling ion-dipole
  "dipole-dipole" (2022, 2023 Q6, 2025 Q2).
- Belief that a liquid below its boiling point has no vapor pressure (2025 Q5).

**Equilibrium**
- Using parentheses or concentrations in Kp; wrong exponents; forgetting to square [OH⁻] in Ksp (2023 Q2, Q7; 2025 Q1).
- Saying K or Ksp changes with concentration/common ion; describing shifts via "reverse rate" language instead of
  Q vs K (2023 Q7, 2024 Q5). Static view of equilibrium.
- Suggesting a catalyst or inert gas to shift equilibrium (2024 Q5); misreading ΔH sign for temperature effects
  (2024 Q5, 2025 Q3).
- Relationships among Ka, Kb, Kw: computing Ka as 1/Kb or Kw − Kb (2025 Q7).

**Acids, bases, buffers, titrations**
- Confusing equivalence point with half-equivalence point (pH = pKa) (2022 Q1, 2024 Q1, 2025 Q2); reading total
  volume instead of equivalence volume from a curve.
- Substituting Kb (not Ka of the conjugate acid) into Henderson-Hasselbalch for a weak-base buffer (2023 Q4).
- Not understanding that buffer pH depends on the ratio, not absolute concentrations (2023 Q4).
- Protonating the wrong site of an organic acid/base (2025 Q7). Weak acid described only as "weak Ka" without
  "mostly un-ionized"/Ka < 1 (2023 Q5).

**Kinetics**
- Order justified by "graph is linear" without naming the plot (1/[A] vs t for second order) (2024 Q6).
- Ignoring stoichiometry in relative rates of appearance/disappearance (2024 Q6).
- Catalyst vs intermediate confusion (catalyst consumed then regenerated; intermediate formed then consumed)
  (2025 Q7). Rate vs amount of product conflated (2024 Q2).

**Thermochemistry and thermodynamics**
- Calorimetry: wrong ΔT or mass picked from a table, negative specific heat, missing sign for exothermic molar ΔH,
  q not converted to per-mole (2024 Q1, Q4). Heat vs temperature vocabulary.
- Hess's law: only one ΔH adjusted when reversing/scaling (2023 Q2).
- "Endothermic is favorable at all temperatures"; sign logic of ΔG = ΔH − TΔS (2024 Q2, 2025 Q3).

**Electrochemistry**
- Multiplying E° by coefficients (E° is intensive) (2022, 2023 Q1, 2024 Q3, 2025 Q6).
- Combining three half-reactions; writing reduction at the anode; not recognizing negative E°cell means electrolytic
  (2023 Q1, 2024 Q3, 2025 Q6).
- Faraday calculations missing mol e⁻ per mol substance (2024 Q3). Mass comparisons that ignore molar mass (2025 Q6).

**Lab and experimental design**
- Vague procedures: not naming a volumetric flask or "dilute to the mark", not rinsing a buret with titrant
  (2023 Q4, 2024 Q7).
- Misreading analog instruments (thermometer scale divisions) (2024 Q4); choosing wrong values from data tables;
  outlier claims without comparing specific trials (2023 Q3).

**Other content**
- Net ionic equations: dissociating solids (2023 Q3); fractional coefficients/unbalanced charge (2025 Q6).
- Electron configurations: 4d vs 3d for first-row transition metals; removing 3d before 4s for cations (2023 Q1).
- Lewis/VSEPR: missing lone pairs on central atom (2025 Q3); adding instead of removing an electron for a cation;
  odd-electron species (2024 Q6); hybridization confused with electron configuration (2025 Q4).

### Implications for course design
