# Brief for AP® Human Geography accuracy reviewers (Phase 4)

You review one unit group, the tools or the exams, independently of the author. Read: `docs/aphug-spec.md`,
`docs/aphug-authoring-guide.md`, `docs/aphug-research/framework.md` (sections 3, 7 and 8) and, above all,
`docs/aphug-research/accuracy-risks.md`, which is binding. Write findings to `docs/aphug-reviews/<scope>.md`; fix
only clear factual errors yourself and list every fix; everything else goes back to the author.

## Checklist (every page, item, stimulus, FRQ and trainer screen)

1. Facts: every statement matches the CED essential knowledge (framework.md section 3) and standard textbook
   content; models are labeled as models with assumptions and at least one limit (skill 1.E).
2. Only CED-named models keyed (spec decision 8). Lee, Zelinsky, Griffin-Ford, McGee, de Blij, heartland,
   rimland, organic state and named projections appear only as asides, never as the route to an answer.
3. Real figures: a source and a year on each (UN WPP 2024, World Bank FY27, UNDP with data and report years,
   PRB 2026, Pew 2025 with 2020 data, Ethnologue 29th edition); no CIA World Factbook; "about" and rounding for
   anything that moves; no "currently" or "today" without a year. Log every dated figure for the yearly pass.
4. Contested places: control, claim and recognition with a date; both names where names differ (Gulf of Mexico /
   Gulf of America, Denali / Mount McKinley, Falkland Islands / Islas Malvinas); no answer that depends on a
   disputed status or a membership count; maps carry the border note.
5. Peoples and religions: their own names, capitalized; ethnicity never treated as biology; serious accusations
   attributed to a named body; the AP universalizing/ethnic classification taught as the AP classification.
6. Neutral: no country called backward or primitive; no "Third World"; "unauthorized" or "undocumented", never
   "illegal", for people; both sides of gentrification, urban renewal, population policies and immigration,
   attributed, with no editorial verdict.
7. Task verbs: each FRQ part's sample does what its verb demands; rubrics are one point each and checkable.
8. Scale: the scale of analysis named as the scale of the data, never the size of the map; map scale taught
   apart from it.
9. Stimulus questions need the stimulus; patterns are named with a place ("clustered" alone earns nothing).
10. Plain words: every term defined before use; sentences a 9th grader reads at first pass; no em dashes.
11. Legibility: every map, pyramid and model readable at 360 px with no overlapping words (screenshots).

Score each finding: error (wrong or misleading), risk (stale, undated, one-sided), polish. One pass; a second only
if serious problems turn up.
