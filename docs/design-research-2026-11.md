# Trim LevlPrep down to one clear next step

The best study sites do fewer things on each screen than LevlPrep does, and they make the next step obvious: a small global header, about five sections inside a course, one primary button per screen, a question on screen fast, and a returning student dropped straight back into their own course. LevlPrep already matches them on the hard parts that take years to build (one consistent structure across 13 courses, an explanation on every question, a guided path, a dashboard built around five plain questions, a non renewing price), so this redesign is mostly about **removing and merging, not adding**. The measured problems trace back to a few choices: **10 course tabs** (phones hide Review, Exams and the dashboard behind "More"), a **full cover band with a drawing and a handwritten line on every page** (on a phone, a notes page shows zero lines of notes on its first screen, and a practice question pushes its answer options off screen), **course homes of nine blocks and about 1,000 words with four competing buttons**, and **six names for reading content**. Usability research is clear on the direction: people read about 20% of a page's words, hidden navigation cuts discoverability by more than 20%, tab bars work up to about five items, and "More" tabs hide content. Learning research is equally clear that answering questions with feedback beats rereading, so practice, not reading, should be the default action. The evidence for motivation and pricing favors restraint: forgiving streaks, few reminders, a generous free tier and upgrade prompts at moments of proven value, which LevlPrep's one payment season pass already suits. The report ends with 14 ranked decisions, each tagged by how strong its evidence is.

A note on sources: LevlPrep facts come from a measured run of the repo at commit ee246f0 (2026-11-04) at phone (390px) and desktop (1280px) widths, signed out with no progress ([LevlPrep baseline](research notes, not in the repo)). Signed in states were not captured, and the live site may lag the repo. Competitor facts come from pages fetched on 2026-11-04 and from published research. Where something is my judgment rather than a finding, the text says so.

## Ten tabs and many doors: how the best sites organize many courses

### The proven pattern is a small global layer plus a short menu inside each course

Serious study products use two levels. A small global layer (home, my courses, catalog, account) stays the same everywhere, and inside a course a local menu shows where you are. On desktop that local menu is usually a **left sidebar**: Coursera lists the weeks of a course down the left ([Coursera Learner Guide 2020](https://cei.hkust.edu.hk/sites/default/files/content/coursera-learner-guide-2020.pdf)), Khan Academy opens a left sidebar with assignments at the top and course content below ([Khan for Educators: Student navigation](https://rosetta.to/u/khanacademy/student-navigation-using-course-mastery-on-khan-academy-khan-for-educators-khan-)), and AMBOSS gives its learning mode just four sidebar items (Qbank, Study plans, Analysis, Library), with a dropdown at top left that switches the whole sidebar, which is the closest real example of a course switcher ([AMBOSS Platform overview](https://support.amboss.com/hc/en-us/articles/360034825692-Platform-overview)). Question bank products organize the menu around tools (UWorld: Create Test, Previous Tests, Performance, Flashcards, My Notebook) ([UWorld USMLE features](https://medical.uworld.com/usmle/features/)), while course products organize it around the unit tree. LevlPrep is both, which explains why its tab row mixes content (Learn), tools (Practice, Review, Flashcards, Exams, Tools, Glossary) and guidance (Path, Dashboard).

The research behind these choices is unusually firm. NN/g's eyetracking found **users look at the left half of the screen 80% of the time**, and a left vertical menu handles "broad or fast growing" structures without shrinking labels ([NN/g, Left-Side Vertical Navigation](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/vertical-nav/)). In a 179 person study, **hidden menus were used 27% of the time on desktop versus 48% for visible ones, cut content discoverability by more than 20%, and made desktop tasks at least 39% slower** ([NN/g, Hamburger Menus and Hidden Navigation](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/hamburger-menus/)). For phones, Material 3 sets a bottom bar at **2 to 5 destinations** and points to a drawer beyond that ([SAP Fiori for Android, M3 Navigation bar](https://www.sap.com/design-system/fiori-design-android/v25-8/components/m3-standard-components/navigation-bar/usage)), and Apple's guidelines say "Avoid overflow tabs," because a More tab "makes it harder for people to reach and notice content," and advise a default of five or fewer, single word labels, and never hiding or disabling tabs when content is missing ([Apple HIG: Tab bars](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/tab-bars)). NN/g adds that local navigation must stay quieter than the global header, or users miss the way out ([NN/g, Local Navigation](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/local-navigation/)).

### LevlPrep's structure is consistent but has too many doors

LevlPrep's skeleton is sound: a hub of 13 courses, one shared header, and the **same tab set in every course**, which is exactly what Apple and NN/g ask for. The trouble is count and overlap. Every course has **10 tabs** (Home, Path, Learn, Practice, Review, Flashcards, Exams, Glossary, Tools, Dashboard). On phones the bar shows Home, Path, Learn, Practice and More, so **Review, Exams and Dashboard, the pages that matter most near an exam, sit behind the overflow tab** Apple warns against ([LevlPrep baseline](research notes, not in the repo)). The course home alone offers at least four routes into Practice, Flashcards and Exams appear both as tabs and in the practice page's side rail, and "Practice this lesson" appears twice on a notes page. Reading content goes by **notes, textbook, Learn, Read, lesson and video lesson**, and the progress area is called Dashboard in the tab, "Where you stand today" in its headline and "progress" in the docs. A first time visitor cannot easily tell that notes are free and interactive lessons past Unit 2 are Premium: a Unit 4 AP® Biology lesson opens as a Premium gate even though the buttons leading to it read as open ([bio lesson screenshot](research notes, not in the repo)).

### Verdict on hub with top tabs versus a sidebar

No study compares top tabs and a left sidebar for course sites directly, so this is a reasoned call. **Keep the hub and the top header, cut the course sections to five, and use a left sidebar only where there is a tree to show.** Five sections fit comfortably in a top row on desktop and exactly fill a phone bottom bar with no More tab. The unit and topic tree, which a top row cannot hold, belongs in a left sidebar inside Learn and on notes pages, where LevlPrep already has a contents sidebar. A full left sidebar for the whole course would also work (NN/g favors it for growing structures), but it costs width on notes pages and duplicates what five tabs already do. For returning students, the evidence points to landing inside the course: Coursera's Resume button returns you "even partway through a video," and Khan's 2022 change meant students "no longer need to visit a course homepage" to find work ([Coursera Learner Guide 2020](https://cei.hkust.edu.hk/sites/default/files/content/coursera-learner-guide-2020.pdf); [Khan for Educators](https://rosetta.to/u/khanacademy/student-navigation-using-course-mastery-on-khan-academy-khan-for-educators-khan-)). Duolingo shows the risk of going too far: its single path drew public backlash for removing free choice ([UX Collective critique](https://uxdesign.cc/down-the-wrong-path-the-disaster-of-the-latest-duolingo-ui-update-a4cdd1e6ea1c)), so "browse all units" must stay one tap away.

## First screens: winners use one line, one button and a real question

### Short heroes and compact catalogs lead the field

The leanest consumer homepage, Quizlet, has **six sections and a five word headline** ("Simple tools for learning anything.") and shows real study sets rather than a category grid ([Quizlet homepage](https://quizlet.com)). Brilliant uses a seven word headline, two audience buttons, and product screenshots instead of illustration ([Brilliant homepage](https://brilliant.org)). The closest analog to LevlPrep's AP catalog, Fiveable, lists subjects as **compact text links in seven groups under one shared promise line** ("Every subject has unit guides, practice for every topic, and FRQs you can get scored") and offers an optional diagnostic for anyone "not sure where to start" ([Fiveable homepage](https://fiveable.me)). Multi exam brands like Pocket Prep and UWorld lead with "find your exam" search or grouped lists ([Pocket Prep homepage](https://www.pocketprep.com); [UWorld homepage](https://www.uworld.com)). NN/g's homepage principles match: a concise value statement, specific examples rather than generic categories, the most important content on the first screen, and specific link labels instead of "Explore" ([NN/g, 5 Principles of Homepage Design](https://nngroup.com/articles/homepage-design-principles/)).

LevlPrep's homepage has already moved most of the way. The October audit's long paragraph is now **one sentence and one button** ("Choose your course"), and the founder line ("By Olivier Testa, an EMT who passed the NREMT with it") is the kind of craft signal that works for a site without a big user count. What remains is the catalog: **13 large cover cards in five rows**, each with its own description and the identical "Free to start" tag, with **no course card visible on a phone's first screen** ([home at 390px](research notes, not in the repo)). A grouped list by goal (EMT, college science, AP®) with one shared promise line would put every course within one screen and replace 13 descriptions with one.

### Let people answer before asking for an account

The best documented onboarding result in learning products is Duolingo's: **simply moving the signup screen back a few steps, so people try a lesson first, raised daily active users about 20%**, and later tuning of soft (dismissible) versus hard walls added 8.2%, with hard walls alone performing "significantly worse" ([First Round Review, Duolingo A/B testing](https://review.firstround.com/the-tenets-of-a-b-testing-from-duolingos-master-growth-hacker)). Brilliant also runs its onboarding quiz before signup ([Embeddables Brilliant example](https://embeddables.com/examples/brilliant-onboarding)), and Pocket Prep asks for an exam date to drive a study plan ([Pocket Prep, 6 Effective Ways to Study](https://www.pocketprep.com/posts/6-effective-ways-to-study-with-pocket-prep)). Duolingo's onboarding also shows the cost of length: a 2026 walkthrough measured about 8 minutes to account creation, and flagged the longer placement test being pre marked "recommended" as a dark pattern ([vc.ru Duolingo onboarding analysis](https://vc.ru/design/3017207-onboarding-duolingo-analiz-ux-i-temnyh-patternov)).

LevlPrep already lets signed out visitors practice, which is the right foundation. On 2026-11-04 the owner removed the "try one question" block from the homepage and every course home as clutter ([LevlPrep baseline](research notes, not in the repo)). The evidence does not require a question on the homepage itself, but it does favor **a real question within one or two taps of arriving**, before any signup prompt, and then a soft "save your progress" prompt once there is progress worth saving. The course home's path card ("Create your personalized path," three question chips) is a reasonable place for a one question setup such as exam date or "new to this or reviewing."

### Deep landings and trust

Visitors from search and social usually land on an inner page. NN/g recommends one specific "next" link at the end of each page, specific related links, and an explicit mention of the product that solves the visitor's problem; one site cut bounce rate from 30% to 2.5% with such a redesign (older, 2008 data) ([NN/g, Fight for the Second Click](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/reduce-bounce-rates/)). For trust, NN/g found people believe outside reviews over on site testimonials and lose trust over typos, vague labels and login walls ([NN/g, Trustworthiness in Web Design](https://nngroup.com/articles/trustworthy-design/)). Competitors that make outcome claims do it best when they disclose method: Fiveable labels its claim that 96.5% of AP® scores its students reported in 2026 were a 3 or higher as "Voluntary, self-reported survey results... Not a randomized study," while Albert.io relies only on "Created by human experts since 2011" ([Fiveable homepage](https://fiveable.me); [Albert homepage](https://www.albert.io)). Contradictions hurt: Princeton Review shows two different ratings on one page and Kaplan shows a testimonial heading with nothing under it ([Princeton Review homepage](https://www.princetonreview.com); [Kaplan homepage](https://www.kaptest.com)). For LevlPrep, the honest signals are true counts (19,971 questions, 13 courses), who builds it and how questions are checked; the AP® Biology footer line that the course "has not yet been reviewed by an AP® Biology teacher" is honest, but it should sit with a plain explanation of the accuracy process rather than alone at the bottom.

## Inside a course: one Continue button beats nine blocks

### Course home

The clearest products answer "what should I do next" with one action. Duolingo replaced its skill tree with a path because learners were unsure whether they were using the app "the correct or best way," and it folded review into the path because "Practice is also forward progress" ([Duolingo Blog, new home screen design](https://blog.duolingo.com/new-duolingo-home-screen-design)). Khan Academy shows one course mastery percentage and, before any quiz, a card with the number of questions and roughly how long others took ([Khan Academy: Course and Unit Mastery](https://support.khanacademy.org/hc/articles/115002552631-What-are-Course-and-Unit-Mastery-)). NN/g's progressive disclosure research explains why fewer options work: "the very fact that something appears on the initial display tells users that it's important," and designs deeper than two levels "typically have low usability" ([NN/g, Progressive disclosure](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/progressive-disclosure/)).

LevlPrep's AP® Biology home stacks **nine blocks, about 1,000 words, and is 6,211px tall on a phone**: cover band, path card, unit list beside a "Ways to study" set, exam facts, videos, tools, two plan cards, nine FAQs and a footer note. **Four buttons compete above the fold on desktop** (Start the first lesson, All units, Create my path, Open full path), the path card is presented as the main idea but is not the primary button, and on a phone the sticky "Start the first lesson" repeats the hero button right above the tab bar ([bio home full page](research notes, not in the repo); [bio home at 390px](research notes, not in the repo)). The primary button also differs by course ("Start the first lesson," "Start practicing," "Open the textbook"). My judgment: the course home should become the path, with one Continue button on top, the unit list with progress underneath, and plans and FAQs moved to the Premium page.

### Practice screen

Top question banks converge on **two modes**: a study or tutor mode with instant explanations, and an exam mode that copies the real test (UWorld "Tutor" versus "Timed," AMBOSS Study versus Exam mode) ([UWorld USMLE features](https://Medical.UWorld.com/usmle/features/); [AMBOSS Help: Study Mode and Exam Mode](https://support.amboss.com/hc/en-us/articles/360036038991-Using-Study-Mode-Exam-Mode)). Exam mode tools stay minimal and match the real exam; College Board's Bluebook offers a timer you can hide until 5 minutes remain, mark for review, an option eliminator, highlighting and Desmos where allowed ([Bluebook Testing Tools](https://bluebook.collegeboard.org/students/tools)). AMBOSS layers its explanation: a short "Key Learning Point" first, then "Show all Explanations" on request, and it records hints as "answered with help" so they do not inflate progress ([AMBOSS Help](https://support.amboss.com/hc/en-us/articles/360036038991-Using-Study-Mode-Exam-Mode)). Pocket Prep replaces the filter form with named presets (Question of the Day, Quick 10, Missed Questions, Weakest Subject, Build Your Own, Mock Exam) ([Pocket Prep: Quiz modes explained](https://help.pocketprep.com/en/articles/14548723-quiz-modes-explained-how-to-study-with-pocket-prep)).

LevlPrep's question screen does several things right: explanations on every answer, icon and text verdict pills (so feedback no longer relies on color alone), Previous and Next, and misses feeding a review queue. The practice setup also already offers presets ("My weakest topics," "Questions I missed") next to "Build a set." The flaw is layout: **during a session the cover band ("In session, with the why.") stays on top, about 300px on a phone, and for an AP® Biology data question the setup text fills the rest of the screen, so the answer options start below the first screen** ([practice question at 390px](research notes, not in the repo)). A session screen should drop the band entirely.

### Dashboard and readiness

Products keep progress screens small: UWorld shows percentile and percent correct by subject and gives a **predicted score only after a full length self assessment** ([UWorld USMLE features](https://Medical.UWorld.com/usmle/features/)); Khan turns progress into four levels per skill (Attempted, Familiar, Proficient, Mastered) that can drop after misses ([Khan Academy: Mastery levels](https://support.khanacademy.org/hc/tr/articles/5548760867853)). NN/g defines a dashboard as a single page users absorb "without having to think," best drawn with sorted bars and lines rather than donuts or gauges ([NN/g, Dashboards](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/dashboards-preattentive/)). LevlPrep's five question dashboard (What should I do now, Am I getting better, Will I be ready, Where am I losing points, What have I built) is **well aligned with this research** and is one of the site's strongest designs. Its weakness is again the wrapper: a **444px band on an 800px desktop screen** with a drawing, a handwritten line and a "Level 1 Water Watcher, 50 XP to 2" badge before the first question appears, and section labels prefixed "1 · right now" ([dashboard screenshot](research notes, not in the repo)).

### What learning science says the default action should be

A major review of ten study techniques rated **practice testing and spaced practice "high utility"** and rereading, highlighting and summarizing "low" ([Dunlosky et al. 2013, PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/26173288/)). In Roediger and Karpicke's study, testing beat restudy at one week by **about 61% versus 40% retention**, even though restudy made students feel more confident ([Roediger and Karpicke 2006](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/16507066/)). Interleaved practice felt worse during practice but scored 77% a day later, well above blocked practice ([Dunlosky et al. 2013 full text](https://www.whz.de/fileadmin/lehre/hochschuldidaktik/docs/dunloskiimprovingstudentlearning.pdf)). Worked examples help beginners but become redundant as skill grows ([Wikipedia: Worked-example effect](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Worked-example_effect)). Two implications follow. Every lesson and notes page should end in questions, and readiness should be judged from delayed, mixed question performance, never from pages read. For a true beginner in AP® Biology or organic chemistry, a short read or worked example first is fine; for a student reviewing for an exam, the first tap should be a question.

## Visual design: keep the color, lose the banner on every page

### People read about a fifth of each page

NN/g's reading studies found users read **about 20% of a page's words, and read half only on pages of about 111 words or fewer** ([NN/g, How Little Do Users Read?](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/how-little-do-users-read/)). In a separate test, text that was concise, scannable and objective improved measured usability by **124%** over promotional text ([NN/g, Concise, Scannable, and Objective](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/concise-scannable-and-objective-how-to-write-for-the-web/)). Readers scan headings by their first two words, so numbered prefixes such as "1 · right now" push the meaning to the right ([NN/g, F-Shaped Pattern](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/f-shaped-pattern-reading-web-content/)). GOV.UK caps sentences at 25 words, citing data that readers understand over 90% of a 14 word sentence and under 10% of a 43 word one ([Inside GOV.UK, sentence length](https://insidegovuk.blog.gov.uk/2014/08/04/sentence-length-why-25-words-is-our-limit/)). A practical rule follows: a heading should not need a sentence under it. If it does, rewrite the heading.

### Readable notes need plain pages

Long text reads best at **50 to 75 characters per line** ([Baymard, Line Length Readability](https://baymard.com/blog/line-length-readability)). GOV.UK, one of the most tested design systems, uses **19px body text with 25px line height on phone and desktop** ([GOV.UK type scale](https://design-system.service.gov.uk/styles/type-scale/)). WCAG 2.2 requires 4.5:1 contrast for normal text and targets of at least 24 by 24px, and its "Focus Not Obscured" rule means sticky bars must not cover the element a keyboard user is on ([W3C, What's new in WCAG 2.2](https://www.w3.org/WAI/standards-guidelines/wcag/new-in-22/)). Common touch guidance is 44px or larger ([mobiForge, Designing for Touch](https://mobiforge.com/design-development/designing-touch-thumb-and-finger-sized-design)), and Core Web Vitals pass only with the main content painted within 2.5 seconds and layout shift under 0.1 on mobile ([web.dev, Web Vitals](https://web.dev/articles/vitals)), which every extra web font works against.

### LevlPrep's look is cohesive but loud everywhere

The "light channel look" (pale grid paper, very heavy two tone headlines, one handwritten line per block, saturated full width cover bands, navy and yellow buttons) is deliberate and cohesive. The problem is that **every page repeats the big band, the course drawing and the course's handwritten tagline**, so the notes page for topic 4.1 still says "every unit, data questions." On a phone, the notes page's entire first screen is band (drawing, breadcrumb, title, handwritten line, button, three chips, share row) with **no line of the notes visible**; bands run 586 to 663px on course homes; and the sticky button plus tab bar take about 136px at the bottom, so header and footer chrome use roughly a quarter of the screen ([notes at 390px](research notes, not in the repo); [LevlPrep baseline](research notes, not in the repo)). The site loads **four type families** (Inter Display, Nunito, Kalam handwriting, IBM Plex Mono) and some buttons still fall back to Arial; the bottom bar labels are 10.5px; the CSS runs past 6,000 shared lines with more than a dozen breakpoints; and the Learn page is **49,850px tall on a phone** ([LevlPrep baseline](research notes, not in the repo)). On the positive side, no page scrolls sideways at 390px, and the October audit's crowded phone header is fixed.

Duolingo is a useful model for keeping a loud brand: its identity rests on one saturated green and a custom rounded bold typeface ([Creative Bloq](https://creativebloq.com/news/feather-bold)), yet the loudness sits in a few elements (logo, main button, mascot) on otherwise plain screens. That reading of its screens is mine, not a measured finding. My judgment for LevlPrep: keep the course colors and drawings on the hub and course home (at about half the current height on phones), use a slim title row on every inner page, keep display type and the handwritten font out of study content, and check the yellow on green and gold button text with a contrast tool.

## Motivation and pricing: forgiving habits and a generous free tier

### What actually moves retention

The best published numbers are Duolingo's, and they reward patience rather than gimmicks. Its growth model found **retention of already active users had 5 times the impact of the next best lever**, and raising it cut daily churn among the best users by over 40% ([Lenny's Newsletter, How Duolingo reignited user growth](https://lennysnewsletter.com/p/how-duolingo-reignited-user-growth)). Separating the streak from the daily goal so one lesson keeps it alive raised **Day 14 retention 3.3%** ([Duolingo blog, Improving the streak](https://blog.duolingo.com/improving-the-streak)); two streak freezes beat one, three did no better, and a free "earn back" lets users repair a lost streak ([Lenny's Podcast summary](https://www.recall.it/summary/lennys-podcast/behind-the-product-duolingo-streaks-or-jackson-shuttleworth-group-pm-retention-team)). Copying a casual game mechanic did nothing ([Lenny's Newsletter](https://lennysnewsletter.com/p/how-duolingo-reignited-user-growth)), and an XP display that won its test was not shipped because it clashed with simplicity ([Lenny's Podcast summary](https://www.recall.it/summary/lennys-podcast/behind-the-product-duolingo-streaks-or-jackson-shuttleworth-group-pm-retention-team)). The academic picture is similar: a meta analysis found gamification helps learning modestly (**g = .42 in rigorous studies**) but its motivation and behavior effects lose significance in rigorous designs, and story themes had no effect ([Sailer and Homner 2020](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10648-019-09498-w)). A 2026 field trial with about 60,000 students found highlighting streaks deepened engagement among existing users while personalized reminders worked better at reviving inactive ones ([Aulagnon et al. 2026](https://www.ipr.northwestern.edu/documents/working-papers/2026/wp-26-05.pdf)). On the other side, **Khan Academy retired streaks in 2021 because over 99% of learners did not use them** and teachers called them demotivating when illness or lost Wi Fi broke them ([Khan Academy support](https://support.khanacademy.org/hc/bg/community/posts/360075847492/comments/360013900252)), and consumer research finds broken streaks especially demotivating and streak holders overconfident ([University of Delaware release](https://www.marketscreener.com/quote/stock/DUOLINGO-INC-125228573/news/University-of-Delaware-HOW-STREAKS-MOTIVATE-US-Lerner-research-sheds-light-on-the-psychology-behi-46127947/)).

For reminders, Duolingo sends one practice reminder **23.5 hours after the last session**, rotates fresh copy (a two week test raised new user Day 7 retention 2.0%), and requires strong justification to send more, because people who opt out rarely come back ([Yancey and Settles, KDD 2020](https://research.Duolingo.com/papers/yancey.kdd20.pdf); [Lenny's Newsletter](https://lennysnewsletter.com/p/how-duolingo-reignited-user-growth)). Referrals are weak: Duolingo's free month referral program raised new users only 3%, and the team moved to friend features instead ([Lenny's Newsletter](https://lennysnewsletter.com/p/how-duolingo-reignited-user-growth)). LevlPrep shows a streak flame and level badge in the header once there is progress, a named level with XP on the dashboard, and an invite program of gift days ([LevlPrep baseline](research notes, not in the repo)). For an EMT and AP® audience the evidence favors keeping the streak quiet and forgiving, tying the main progress display to the exam (topics mastered, readiness), and treating XP level names as optional decoration rather than a headline element.

### Pricing that fits how students buy

Education apps charge more and convert slower than any other category in RevenueCat's 2025 data: **median $44.99 a year or $9.99 a month, and only 28.5% of conversions on the first day** ([RevenueCat State of Subscription Apps 2026, Education](https://www.revenuecat.com/state-of-subscription-apps-2026-education)). Hard paywalls convert more across categories (10.7% versus 2.1% by day 35), but would clash with LevlPrep's free positioning and search traffic. Competitors gate tools, not reading: Pocket Prep's free tier is **25 to 50 questions**, with the full bank and modes like Missed Questions and Weakest Subject behind Premium ([Pocket Prep FAQ](https://www.pocketprep.com/frequently-asked-questions/)), at about $15.99 a month or $95.99 a year for EMS per App Store data whose storefront was not confirmed ([App Pricing Lab](https://www.apppricinglab.com/app/apple/1503584128)). Albert.io sells **$79 per AP subject for a fixed term with no auto renewal** ([Albert.io student pricing](https://albert.io/pricing-students)), and UWorld AP offers a 7 day trial without a card ([UWorld College Prep AP](https://collegeprep.uworld.com/ap/)).

LevlPrep's model, **one payment that never renews** ($25 per AP® course, $29 for a 90 day NREMT pass or an ochem or A&P semester, $49 for a full year, all 30% off as founding prices until 31 January 2027, refundable within 7 days), already matches how AP® students and parents buy and sits below Pocket Prep for EMT ([premium page](research notes, not in the repo)). Its free bank is far larger than Pocket Prep's sample, which the pricing page should say plainly. The funnel numbers from 2026-09-24 to 2026-10-08 (about **6,700 page views, 644 gate views, 10 interest clicks, 4 checkout starts, 0 paid**) are too small to judge price, but they do show that gates are seen far more than they persuade ([LevlPrep trust and conversion plan](../docs/trust-conversion-plan.md)). The education data favors upgrade prompts at moments of shown value (after a mock exam score, at a locked review mode, near the exam date) over two plan cards on every course home.

## The teacher side: four class tabs and an Assign button

Teachers are short on time (Pew 2024: **84% of public K to 12 teachers** lack enough work hours for grading and planning) and juggle many tools ([Learning Counsel summary](https://thelearningcounsel.com/articles/districts-are-reducing-app-counts-teachers-are-still-building-instruction/)). The tool they all know, Google Classroom, uses **four class tabs** (Stream, Classwork, People, Grades), and its July 2026 redesign moved to one home hub with actionable insights and assignment completion ([CustomGuide: Google Classroom navigation](https://www.customguide.com/course/google-classroom/google-classroom-navigation); [Google Workspace Updates, 2026-07-21](https://workspaceupdates.googleblog.com/2026/07/redesigned-google-classroom-homepage-with-tailored-views-based-on-users-role.html)). Edpuzzle shows the shortest assign flow: pick content, Assign, pick class, optional dates, then only the toggles that apply, with the whole class (including late joiners) as the default ([Edpuzzle Help](https://support.edpuzzle.com/hc/articles/360007501931)). Results converge on a **student by question grid** with color bands and a class average row, where clicking a question shows how many picked each answer (Formative) ([Formative Help](https://help.formative.com/en/articles/6198532-view-and-score-responses)), and IXL's Trouble Spots flags three misses of the same item type, ranked by how many students it affects ([IXL blog](https://blog.ixl.com/2020/11/04/3-popular-teacher-reports-on-ixl/)). Live modes stay simple: one options screen, a big join code, safe auto nicknames, and a report button at the end ([Gimkit Help: Hosting](https://help.gimkit.com/en/category/hosting-1lmb5bt)). Two cautions: a study of 21 teachers found new dashboard data "did not necessarily lead to immediate action," so each insight needs its action beside it ([Springer, teacher dashboards](https://www.springerprofessional.de/en/do-teachers-find-dashboards-trustworthy-actionable-and-useful-a-/19111564)), and Khan's 2026 simplification drew complaints when it removed a detailed view, so keep detail one click away rather than deleting it ([Khan Academy community post](https://support.khanacademy.org/hc/de/community/posts/47404589461261/comments/47750016839821)).

LevlPrep's teacher class view has **seven tabs** (Class, Assignments, Students, Gradebook, Skills for AP® only, Live, Settings) plus a "+ Assign" button, and it reuses the student side's cover band and handwritten line ([assets/class/teach.js](../assets/class/teach.js)). Its feature set is broad (question bank, printable A/B tests, extra time, tab leave counts, co teachers, a department view, an AP® score band labeled "Estimate" and "Not a College Board score") and v2 is merged and live ([docs/teacher-classes.md](../docs/teacher-classes.md)). The "+ Assign" button is the right instinct. The tab count is not: Students and Gradebook overlap, Skills and Live are views and actions rather than places, and Apple's rule against tabs that appear and disappear argues for keeping Skills as a view inside the gradebook rather than a tab that only AP® classes get. One housekeeping note: the status file still says teacher entry is "footer link and a line on Premium; nothing on the homepage," while the latest commit added a header link on every page and a homepage teacher strip, so the file needs updating.

## Redesign decisions, ranked

Exact sizes and counts are always inferences.

Two process decisions sit alongside these. First, before building, run a five student first click test on the new labels and five sections; no outside research covers label confusion on study sites, and LevlPrep's own analytics (gate views, question tallies, read depth) can measure before and after. Second, change one shared component first: the cover band and course nav live in shared code, so decisions 1, 2 and 9 can ship together across all 13 courses in one pass.

## Conclusion

The research reframes LevlPrep's problem. The site does not lack features; it has a surplus of doors and a single loud wrapper repeated on every page, and both are cheap to fix because the structure is already shared code. The bigger lesson from Duolingo's numbers is that, once the screens are clear, growth comes from bringing active students back (practice first, a forgiving habit cue, one well timed reminder) far more than from homepage polish, so the redesign should be judged by whether a student who answered ten questions today comes back tomorrow, not by how the hub looks.

Several questions remain open and should be treated as experiments rather than settled: no study compares top tabs with a sidebar for course sites, no source gives a safe number of cards or blocks, nobody has published whether asking for an exam date improves retention, and LevlPrep's own conversion sample (four checkout starts) is too small to say whether price, trust or gate placement is the barrier. Measure each change with the analytics already in place, and keep the decisions that move return visits and questions answered.
