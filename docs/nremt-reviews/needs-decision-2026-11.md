# NREMT lessons and question bank: points for a person to decide (2026-11-10)

From the independent reviews of the 76 new lessons and the full question bank audit. Each lesson uses careful wording ("per local protocol") on these points and no check question is keyed on them. Review logs: `docs/nremt-reviews/`.

## Lessons and notes

- **Ch 1 (ch1-history):** Did the first documented EMS (France, 1790s) give no care on the battlefield? The standard EMT text says it only carried the wounded away; historical accounts of Larrey's flying ambulances describe surgeons bringing aid onto the battlefield itself. Lesson says: Now: the service was built to carry the wounded off the field to the surgeons, and 'as the EMT textbooks tell it, the job was transport, not care at the scene'. Question 1 asks for its main job (transport to care) and no longer has 'treated them at the scene' as a wrong option. Options: (a) Keep the textbook line in the notes as it is, since exams follow the textbook. (b) Soften the notes sentence 'No care was given at the scene' to 'Its purpose was transport to the surgeons' to match the lesson. Reviewer suggests (b).
- **Ch 2 (ch2-provider-wellness):** The notes' disease table calls TB 'highly contagious'. CDC describes TB as spread through the air but usually needing prolonged close contact. Keep, or reword? Lesson says: TB is airborne; treat any productive cough as possible TB; N-95 or HEPA respirator. It does not call TB highly contagious and no question depends on it. Options: (a) Leave the notes. (b) Change the table note to 'Airborne; assume any productive cough = possible TB'. Not checked against a fetched CDC page in this review.
- **Ch 4 (ch4-scope-consent):** Is it a flat rule that an intoxicated patient cannot refuse, or is it a question of decision-making capacity (alcohol or drugs can remove capacity, but one drink does not)? Texts teach the flat rule; current guidance and many protocols use capacity. Lesson says: 'A patient whose judgment is impaired by alcohol, drugs, illness or injury cannot give a valid informed refusal.' The check question uses a patient who is plainly impaired and disoriented with a head injury, so it is right under either reading. Options: (a) Keep the notes' flat rule. (b) Reword the notes to 'a patient whose decision-making capacity is impaired by alcohol or drugs cannot give a valid informed refusal; assess capacity and involve medical direction per local protocol'. Reviewer suggests (b).
- **Ch 4 (ch4-dnr-ethics):** Valid DNR in hand, family demands resuscitation: does the EMT start CPR while contacting medical direction or withhold while contacting? And who may revoke a DNR (patient, health care agent, any family member) differs by state. Lesson says: 'Contact medical direction. The physician may need to speak with the family directly.' It does not say whether to begin CPR meanwhile. The check question keys only on contacting medical direction. Options: (a) Leave as is (silent, per local protocol). (b) Add 'follow local protocol on whether to begin resuscitation while you call'. Owner or medical reviewer to choose.
- **Ch 4 (ch4-dnr-ethics):** The notes define a DNR as stating that 'the patient has a terminal illness' and as signed by patient and physician. Out-of-hospital DNR rules (who qualifies, who signs) are set by each state. Lesson says: 'A legal document, typically signed by the patient and a physician, saying the patient does not want resuscitative efforts.' Terminal illness is not mentioned and not tested. Options: (a) Leave the notes. (b) Change to 'typically signed by the patient (or their decision maker) and a physician, stating that the patient does not want resuscitative efforts; it is often written for a patient with a terminal illness'.
- **Ch 6 (ch6-anatomy):** Acidosis 'impairs the blood's ability to carry oxygen' (chapter 6) and 'in an acidotic environment hemoglobin carries less oxygen' (chapter 7, ch7-pathophysiology). Physiologically, acidosis lowers hemoglobin's affinity for oxygen, which mostly helps unloading at the tissues; the harm of acidosis is to cell and enzyme function. Some EMT texts use the simplified wording. Lesson says: Both lessons say only that anaerobic metabolism produces lactic acid, which causes acidosis and damages cells. Neither repeats the oxygen-carrying claim and no question touches it. Options: (a) Keep the textbook simplification. (b) Change both notes sentences to 'acidosis injures cells and disrupts their function; untreated, it worsens shock'. Needs a clinician's call.
- **Ch 6 (ch6-anatomy):** 'Never take a blood pressure on the affected arm of a patient who has had a mastectomy' is standard textbook teaching, but the evidence that cuff pressure causes lymphedema is weak and some services allow it when no other limb is available. Lesson says: 'Do not use the arm on the affected side ... Use the other arm.' It is a step, not a check question. Options: (a) Keep as the exam answer. (b) Add 'when you have a choice' or 'per local protocol'.
- **Ch 8 (ch8-lifespan):** The age tables for heart rate, breathing rate and blood pressure follow one EMT text and do not match AHA PALS tables in places. Also 'heart disease is the major killer after age 40': current CDC data put cancer first in middle adulthood and heart disease first from about 65. Lesson says: The lesson tests the trend only (rates fall and pressure rises with age) and no exact range. Its opening example (9 months old, heart rate 130, 30 breaths a minute) sits inside the notes' ranges. It does not repeat the heart disease line. Options: (a) Keep one table and name its source, adding 'ranges vary by source'. (b) Replace with the PALS table. For the mortality line: reword to 'heart disease and cancer become the leading causes of death' or leave. Not checked against a fetched source in this review.
- **Ch 9 (ch9-suction):** Which maximum suction time should the course teach for an adult: 15 seconds or 10 seconds? Lesson says: About 15 seconds at most in an adult, about 10 in a child and about 5 in an infant; "some texts teach 10 seconds for adults, so follow local protocol". No check question keys on the number. Options: (a) Keep 15/10/5 with the hedge (Red Cross suctioning sheet and the AAOS text teach 15/10/5). (b) Teach 10 seconds or less for everyone (AHA course materials and the Brady text). The 2025 AHA guideline text itself gives no suction time limit.
- **Ch 9 (ch9-bvm):** When the chest does not rise with a bag-valve mask, is the first check the mask seal or the head position? Lesson says: Start with the two common causes, re-seal the mask and reposition the head and jaw, then suction, then check for an obstruction. The check question asks what comes first but does not offer repositioning as an option. Options: (a) Seal first, as the notes say ("in this order"). (b) Reposition the head first, then the seal, as the Brady text and AHA course materials list it. (c) Teach both as the first pair without ranking them (what the lesson now does); if chosen, soften "in this order" in the ch9-bvm notes.
- **Ch 10 (ch10-oxygen-equipment):** Should the notes keep "open the valve fully, then back it off slightly"? Lesson says: Nothing. The lesson leaves the point out. Options: (a) Keep it (some EMT texts teach a half turn back). (b) Replace with "open the valve slowly and fully, per the manufacturer and local protocol" (compressed gas guidance warns a backed-off valve can be mistaken for a closed one).
- **Ch 10 (ch10-delivery-devices):** Which saturation targets should the course state: a floor only (94% or above) or a range, and should it mention the lower thresholds AHA uses for specific conditions? Lesson says: "About 94% or above" for most patients and "about 88 to 92%" with known COPD, "local protocol sets the actual numbers". The COPD check question keys on titrating rather than withholding or aiming for 100%, with the number marked as commonly taught. Options: (a) Keep as is. (b) State a range, 94 to 98%, to make the "no higher than needed" point explicit. Note for chapters 20 and 21: the 2025 AHA post-cardiac arrest algorithm titrates to 90 to 98%, and the AHA/ACC ACS guidance gives oxygen only below 90%, so one number will not fit every chapter.
- **Ch 11 (ch11-scene-size-up):** Are there four components of the scene size-up or five? Lesson says: Four: scene safety, Standard Precautions, nature of the call (MOI or nature of illness), and number of patients with the resources needed. The check question no longer says "four"; it asks for "the components". Options: (a) Keep four (the notes fold resources into number of patients). (b) Teach five, as the Brady text and the NREMT assessment skill sheets list additional resources separately. Content is the same either way.
- **Ch 14 (ch14-principles-assessment):** Should the notes tell an EMT to auscultate bowel sounds before palpating the abdomen? Lesson says: Inspect, then palpate the quadrants, painful area last. Auscultation of bowel sounds is left out. Options: (a) Remove it from the notes (current EMT texts say bowel sounds are not worth scene time). (b) Keep it as a nursing and physician exam sequence with a note that it is not a field priority.
- **Ch 15 (ch15-secondary-assessment):** Should the notes keep "soft collars have no role in prehospital care", and how firmly should routine collar use be taught? Lesson says: "Use a rigid collar, applied per local protocol and sized to the length of the neck"; manual stabilization "when a spinal injury is suspected". The soft collar statement is not repeated and no question keys on it. Options: (a) Keep the notes sentence (classic NREMT skill sheet teaching). (b) Soften to "a rigid, correctly sized collar is the standard teaching; some systems use soft collars or selective spinal motion restriction per protocol" (2018 ACS-COT/ACEP/NAEMSP position statement).
- **Ch 16 (ch16-reassessment):** Is "FIRR" a mnemonic the owner wants taught? The reviewer could not find it in the National EMS Education Standards, the NREMT outline or the standard texts. Lesson says: FIRR sums up care after the assessment: Field impression, Interventions, Reassessments, Report. One check question asks what the field impression is "in FIRR"; the keyed answer (a working impression: body system and probable cause) is true with or without the mnemonic. Options: (a) Keep it as the course's own memory aid. (b) Drop the label and keep the content; if so, reword the stem of check question 5 to "What is a field impression?".
- **Ch 21 (ch21-special):** Hypothermic cardiac arrest: are shocks repeated by the standard sequence, or held after one unsuccessful shock until the core reaches 30 C (86 F)? Lesson says: Full resuscitation during rewarming, AED used; repeat shocks after a first unsuccessful one follow local protocol. States that 2025 AHA allows deferring them and 2020 kept the standard sequence. No question is keyed on shock count. Options: (a) Teach the 2025 AHA wording (one shock, then defer until 30 C; class 2b). (b) Keep the 2020 standard-algorithm wording that current EMT textbooks and many protocols still use. (c) Keep the neutral per-protocol wording now in the lesson. An EMT usually cannot measure core temperature, which favors (c).
- **Ch 21 (ch21-steps):** AED analysis time: the notes say about 15 to 20 seconds. Lesson says: No number; nobody touches the patient during analysis. Options: Remove the figure from the notes, or replace it with a device-dependent phrase (commonly about 5 to 15 seconds). Not verified against a primary source, so no notes fix was written.
- **Ch 21 (ch21-arrest-pathophys):** Statistics in the notes: sudden arrest is more than 85% of all arrests; about 20% of pediatric arrests are from a sudden dysrhythmia; survival above 50% for witnessed arrest against a national average under 15%. Lesson says: No percentages: adult arrests are usually sudden and cardiac, pediatric arrests mostly asphyxial, high-performance systems report far higher survival than the national average. Options: Owner to decide whether to keep the textbook figures, replace them with CARES registry figures (overall survival about 10%; above 50% only for witnessed shockable arrest in the best systems), or drop the numbers.
- **Ch 20 (ch20-acs):** How long after an erectile dysfunction medication is nitroglycerin withheld? Lesson says: Within the window in your protocol (often 24 to 48 hours, up to 72 in some protocols). Both check questions use a dose taken last night or this morning, which every window excludes. Options: Notes say 48 to 72 hours (textbook teaching). ACC/AHA: 24 hours for sildenafil and vardenafil, 48 hours for tadalafil. Pick one for the notes or keep the range with 'per protocol'.
- **Ch 20 (ch20-acs):** Oxygen threshold in ACS: 94% or 90%? Lesson says: Oxygen to reach an SpO2 of 94% or higher and no higher than needed, per protocol. The check question uses a patient at 98% in no distress, where no threshold would give oxygen. Options: EMT texts and the National Model EMS Clinical Guidelines use 94%; the 2025 ACC/AHA ACS guideline gives oxygen only below 90%. Keep 94% per protocol (consistent with the pilot chapters) or change course-wide.
- **Ch 20 (ch20-acs):** Is aspirin withheld because the patient already took aspirin today, or is on a prescribed blood thinner? Lesson says: Follow your protocol for a patient who already took aspirin today or who takes a prescribed blood thinner. Not keyed in any question. Options: The notes list 'already took aspirin earlier today' as a flat reason to withhold. Many protocols give the full 162 to 325 mg regardless of a daily low dose. Owner or clinician to choose the notes wording.
- **Ch 22 (ch22-ams-diabetes):** Does oral glucose require a known history of diabetes? Lesson says: History of diabetes, altered mental status and able to swallow safely, 'per protocol' and 'the usual criteria'. The check question uses a known diabetic with a measured low glucose, so the key does not depend on this. Options: Keep the three textbook criteria, or teach measured or suspected hypoglycemia in any patient awake and able to swallow (National Model EMS Clinical Guidelines; 2024 AHA and Red Cross first aid guidelines).
- **Ch 23 (ch23-allergic):** Timing around the auto-injector: reassess at 2 minutes (notes), repeat dose from 5 minutes (label) or 5 to 10 minutes (first aid guidelines); and whether to teach the 0.1 mg infant device (7.5 to 15 kg). Lesson says: Reassess at 2 minutes; a second dose, which the labeling allows from 5 minutes after the first, needs another injector and medical direction; a smaller infant follows medical direction. No question is keyed on the repeat interval. Options: Keep as is, or name the 0.1 mg infant auto-injector and a single repeat interval chosen by the owner or medical reviewer.
- **Ch 19 (ch19-conditions):** Interval between metered-dose inhaler puffs: the notes say 15 to 30 seconds. Lesson says: No interval given. Options: Albuterol labeling and most texts say about 1 minute between puffs; some EMT texts give shorter intervals. Choose the figure for the notes or remove it.
- **Ch 19 (ch19-conditions):** Croup: the notes attach 'don't put anything in the mouth, risk of vocal cord spasm' to croup; that is the classic epiglottitis rule. Lesson says: Groups epiglottitis and croup: do not look in the throat or put anything in the mouth; transport calmly. The check question describes epiglottitis only. Options: Keep the shared rule (safe), or give croup its own care line (calm, position of comfort, cool humidified oxygen per protocol).
- **Ch 18 (ch18-pharmacology):** Should the notes give a number for the minimum systolic pressure before nitroglycerin? Lesson says: Check the pressure as your protocol requires; no number. Options: Protocols use 90 or 100 mmHg (some 110). The notes fix adds the rule without a number; the owner can add one.
- **Ch 27 (ch27-behavioral-assessment):** OWNER APPROVAL NEEDED: may the suicide risk addition proposed in notes-fixes-4.json (last ch27-behavioral-assessment entry) go into the notes, and should the lesson then be extended to teach it? The chapter title and intro promise suicide, and neither section nor either lesson covers it. Lesson says: Nothing on suicide. The lesson was deliberately not extended, because a lesson may only teach what its notes section says. Options: (a) Approve the proposed text as written: ask directly and without judgment, warning signs and risk factors, scene safety, medical care first after an attempt, stay with the patient, transport, family secures means, respectful language, 988; no method detail. (b) Approve it as a separate third topic or section titled "Suicide risk" (cleaner than appending to the de-escalation topic) and add a matching lesson step, key idea and one check question. (c) Have a clinician or EMS instructor reword it first. Two sentences rest on the standard EMT texts and not on a fetched source: "is not left alone" and "is transported for evaluation"; local protocol decides refusal handling. Until a decision, the chapter title overpromises; an interim option is to leave "and Suicide" in the title only once the content is in.
- **Ch 27 (ch27-restraint):** How many rescuers for a physical restraint: the notes say a minimum of 4 (one per limb) plus one EMT talking; many texts and protocols say 5 (one per limb and one at the head). The NAEMSP position statement gives no number. Lesson says: "Assemble enough help: at least 4 rescuers, one per limb, with one EMT assigned to talk to and reassure the patient throughout (the exact number is set by local protocol)." The check question that was keyed on "four" was replaced with a scene safety question. Options: Keep "at least 4" with the protocol caveat (current), or change the notes and lesson to "at least four, ideally five, per local protocol".
- **Ch 27 (ch27-restraint):** Restrained patient position: the notes and lesson say face-up only. NAEMSP only forbids prone and hobble (hands and feet tied behind the back) restraint and anything that constricts the neck or chest; some protocols also allow a side-lying position, which protects the airway of a patient who may vomit. Lesson says: "Position the patient face-up. Never prone, never hog-tied, never in any position that restricts the chest wall." The check question keys face-up against two prone options and a side-lying option with a tight chest strap, so it does not turn on supine versus lateral. Options: Keep "face-up" (matches the standard EMT texts and the exam), or soften to "face-up, or on the side if your protocol allows; never prone".
- **Ch 27 (ch27-behavioral-assessment):** Two psychosis points need a textbook citation before they go into the notes: (1) the usual EMT text advice not to argue with a delusion or hallucination and not to pretend to share it; (2) the notes tie acute psychosis to schizophrenia only, though it also occurs with bipolar disorder, severe depression, substance use and medical illness. Lesson says: The author's line "Do not argue with the beliefs or pretend to share them" was not in the notes and I could not confirm it in a fetched primary source, so the lesson now says: "What the patient hears or believes is real to the patient, so stay calm, respectful and non-judgmental, acknowledge how the patient feels, and keep assessing". The lesson names no diagnosis. Options: (a) Add both points to the notes with a page reference from the course text (Limmer or AAOS), then restore the "do not argue, do not play along" line in the lesson. (b) Leave as is.
- **Ch 25 (ch25-substance-abuse):** The notes and a takeaway say flatly that an intoxicated patient cannot make an informed refusal. EMT texts list intoxication as something that can remove capacity; most protocols require a capacity assessment and medical direction contact, not an automatic rule. Lesson says: "Alcohol and drugs can take away a patient's ability to make an informed refusal. Do not simply accept a refusal from an intoxicated patient; assess their capacity and follow your local protocol and medical direction." No check question is keyed on it. Options: (a) Change the notes and takeaway to the lesson's capacity wording. (b) Keep the flat rule because that is how the course text and the exam state it, and add "per local protocol".
- **Ch 26 (ch26-abdominal):** The notes say tearing pain can be produced by only two structures, the aorta and the stomach (perforating ulcer). Standard EMT texts tie tearing pain to the aorta; the stomach claim and the word "only" have no source. Lesson says: "Tearing pain points first to the aorta, classically as tearing pain felt through to the back." The stomach claim and the author's phrase "the notes also link it to" were removed. The check question keys tearing pain through to the back to an aortic aneurysm, which is not disputed. Options: (a) Drop "only" and the stomach from the notes table and paragraph. (b) Keep it if the course text says so, with the page reference.
- **Ch 26 (ch26-abdominal):** The notes say a ruptured abdominal aortic aneurysm may give unequal pulses between the limbs. Unequal arm pulses are the sign of a thoracic aortic dissection; with an abdominal aneurysm the usual finding is weak or absent femoral or leg pulses. Lesson says: "Pulses in the legs may be weak or unequal." Not keyed in any question. Options: (a) Change the notes to "leg pulses may be weak, absent or unequal". (b) Keep the notes wording if the course text uses it.
- **Ch 26 (ch26-abdominal):** The notes say appendicitis "classically begins with nausea, followed by pain". The classic sequence in the standard texts is pain around the umbilicus first, then nausea and vomiting, then pain settling in the right lower quadrant. I did not fetch a primary source for the order. Lesson says: "nausea and pain around the umbilicus that moves to become steady pain in the right lower quadrant" (no order given, not keyed). Options: (a) Reword the notes to "pain around the umbilicus with nausea, then steady pain in the right lower quadrant". (b) Keep if the course text gives nausea first.
- **Ch 26 (ch26-assessment):** "Mortality for the same abdominal condition can be up to 9 times higher" in older patients has no source. Lesson says: "can have far higher mortality from the same condition" (no number, not keyed). Options: (a) Cite the source in the notes. (b) Change the notes to "much higher".
- **Ch 28 (ch28-renal):** The notes say cardiac arrest in an end-stage renal disease patient "may not respond to standard defibrillation". Read alone, that could suggest an EMT should change or doubt the usual arrest sequence, which no guideline supports at the BLS level. Lesson says: "Start CPR and use the AED exactly as you would for any arrest. Tell ALS and the hospital that the patient missed dialysis, because high potassium can make the arrest harder to reverse and needs treatment they can give." The check question keys high potassium as the likely cause, which is not disputed. Options: (a) Change the notes to the lesson wording. (b) Keep the notes sentence and add "give standard CPR and AED care regardless".
- **Ch 24 (ch24-communicable):** Respirator and reporting details the author flagged and I could not settle from a fetched source: (1) the table calls measles, influenza, croup, pertussis and TB all "airborne droplets", while CDC isolation guidance separates airborne (TB, measles, chickenpox: N-95) from droplet (influenza, pertussis, mumps, meningococcal disease: surgical mask); (2) after a TB exposure the notes say to report to the receiving hospital, while the Ryan White rules route it through the service's designated infection control officer, and the baseline test may be a blood test or a skin test. Lesson says: N-95 is tied to suspected TB only, as in the notes, and the lesson says "Report the exposure and get a baseline TB test, with a repeat test later" without naming who receives the report or which test. No question is keyed on the airborne versus droplet split. Options: (a) Rework the notes table into airborne and droplet groups with the mask for each, add N-95 for measles and chickenpox, and name the designated officer; then extend the lesson. (b) Leave the simplified table.
- **Ch 25 (ch25-ingested-inhaled):** The notes call cherry-red skin in carbon monoxide poisoning "a myth". It is usually described as a rare, late finding, not a myth. The CDC clinical guidance page does not mention it, so I could not verify either wording from a primary source. Lesson says: "Do not wait for cherry-red skin, which is not typically seen." Not keyed. Options: (a) Change the notes to "a rare, late sign that is not typically seen, so do not wait for it". (b) Keep "myth".
- **Ch 30 (ch30-burns):** How much active cooling of a thermal burn should the EMT lesson teach, and with what size limit? Lesson says: Cool a fresh thermal burn with cool running water, per local protocol (first aid guidance is at least 10 minutes); limit cooling of a large burn and keep the patient warm; never ice. A cool wet dressing only for a small burn, with the notes' 10% caution. No check question is keyed on cooling time or the 10% figure. Options: (a) Keep as is. (b) Teach the first aid figure outright (cool or cold running water at least 10 minutes, 2024 AHA and Red Cross; Red Cross advisory says ideally 20). (c) Teach the older EMT text rule only (dry sterile dressing, moist only under 10% BSA). EMS protocols differ on the size cut-off (often 10% to 20%).
- **Ch 30 (ch30-wounds):** Amputated part: dry or saline-moistened sterile dressing? Lesson says: Wrap in a sterile dressing (dry or moistened with saline, per local protocol), seal in a plastic bag, keep the bag cool, never the part directly on ice or in water. The check question is keyed only on wrapped, bagged, cool. Options: (a) Keep "dry or moist per protocol". (b) Moist only (many hand surgery and trauma center protocols; the notes' original wording). (c) Dry only (wording in some editions of the EMT texts).
- **Ch 31 (ch31-chest):** Open (sucking) chest wound: which dressing does the course teach as the answer: fully occlusive, taped on three sides, or a vented chest seal? Lesson says: Cover at once with a gloved hand, then an occlusive dressing reaching 2 inches past the wound or a vented chest seal, per local protocol; whether one side is left untaped is set by protocol; lift an edge if tension pneumothorax signs develop. The check question is keyed on "gloved hand, then a chest seal or occlusive dressing" against clearly wrong options (probe, pack, wait for ALS). Options: (a) Keep the protocol-neutral wording. (b) Occlusive on all sides plus burping (AAOS, NREMT-style). (c) Three-sided flutter dressing (older texts, some state protocols). (d) Vented chest seal preferred (TCCC). Note the 2024 AHA and Red Cross first aid guideline lets lay rescuers leave the wound open or use a non-occlusive dry dressing, so a "dry gauze" distractor was removed.
- **Ch 31 (ch31-chest):** Flail chest definition: 2 or more ribs, or 3 or more ribs, each broken in 2 or more places? (b) 3 or more (AAOS). Pick the one that matches the textbook the course tracks; keep the note about the other.

- **Ch 31 (ch31-abdomen):** Evisceration: is an occlusive layer over the moist sterile dressing taught as standard? - **Ch 33 (ch33-head-neck-spine):** Spinal motion restriction: should the course still present long backboard application as the default method, and what age gets shoulder padding? - **Ch 35 (ch35-cold):** How long to check for a pulse in severe hypothermia?
