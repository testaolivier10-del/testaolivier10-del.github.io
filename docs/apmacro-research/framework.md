## 3. Topic content notes (scope, our words)

### From the CED text (Units 1-2, Topics 3.2-3.6)
- **1.2-1.3:** opportunity cost from PPC data or tables; absolute and comparative advantage from PPC data or tables;
  mutually beneficial terms of trade (calculate).
- **1.6:** calculate a shortage or surplus in disequilibrium.
- **2.1:** GDP measured by expenditure, income and value-added approaches; calculate nominal GDP.
- **2.3:** unemployment rate (share of the labor force out of work), labor force participation rate (share of the
  adult population in the labor force), both calculated; frictional, structural, cyclical; **natural rate =
  frictional + structural**; cyclical = actual minus natural; natural rate can change; measured rate understates
  joblessness (discouraged and part-time workers).
- **2.4:** CPI = cost of a fixed basket relative to a base year; inflation = percentage change in a price index (CPI
  or GDP deflator); deflation and disinflation; calculate CPI, inflation and changes in real variables; substitution
  bias can overstate inflation. **Exclusion (official, CED p. 50): calculating the producer price index (PPI) is
  beyond the scope of the course and exam.**
- **2.6:** nominal vs real GDP; real GDP at base-year prices (agencies use other methods because this can overstate
  growth); GDP deflator converts nominal to real.
- **2.7:** phases are contraction and expansion (corrected EK).
- **3.2:** expenditure (spending) multiplier and tax multiplier both depend on MPC; MPC = change in consumption /
  change in disposable income; MPC + MPS = 1. The **balanced-budget multiplier was not seen** in the readable text
  (flag).
- **3.3-3.6:** SRAS slopes up (sticky wages and prices) and shifts with input prices and inflation expectations;
  LRAS vertical at full-employment output, linked to the PPC; short-run vs long-run equilibrium; recessionary and
  inflationary output gaps; positive and negative AD and SRAS shocks; demand-pull vs cost-push inflation.
  Suggested skills 4.A-4.C and 1.A.
  https://apcentral.collegeboard.org/media/pdf/ap-macroeconomics-course-and-exam-description.pdf

### From CR reports and scoring guidelines (Topics 3.7-6.6) [CR]/[SG] plus [ours]
- **3.7 Self-adjustment:** the long-run fix comes from **SRAS shifting** as nominal wages/input prices and
  inflation expectations adjust, not from AD or automatic stabilizers [CR 2023-2025].
- **3.8-3.9:** discretionary fiscal policy; automatic stabilizers (progressive taxes, unemployment compensation as a
  transfer that supports disposable income and consumption) [CR 2023 set 2; 2025 set 2 explicitly tells teachers to
  cover Topic 3.9].
- **4.2:** real interest rate = nominal minus expected inflation; unexpected inflation helps fixed-rate borrowers and
  hurts lenders and fixed-income earners [CR 2024 set 2; formula ours].
- **4.4:** fractional reserve banking, balance sheets, money (deposit) multiplier = 1 / required reserve ratio
  [ours; limited-reserves framework, verify CED wording].
- **4.5-4.6:** money market graph (nominal interest rate vs quantity of money; vertical money supply in the limited
  framework); central bank tools. **Ample reserves:** the central bank sets administered rates (interest on
  reserves) to move the policy rate; the **reserve market graph** has reserve supply crossing reserve demand in its
  flat (ample) range, and policy shifts the administered-rate bounds rather than reserve supply [CR 2024 sets 1-2,
  2025 sets 1-2]. **Limited reserves:** open market operations (buy/sell bonds), discount rate, reserve requirement
  [CR 2023 set 1; 2026 SG Q1 still scores "sell bonds" with a money market graph]. **Both frameworks are tested.**
- **4.7:** loanable funds (real interest rate vs quantity of loanable funds); government borrowing raises demand (or
  lowers supply) [SG 2025 set 2].
- **5.2:** SRPC (downward), LRPC (vertical at the natural rate); expected inflation; points on the SRPC [SG 2025 set
  2; 2026].
- **5.3:** money growth and inflation in the long run (quantity theory, MV = PQ) [ours; verify CED].
- **5.4-5.5:** budget deficit (outlays exceed revenue) vs national debt; crowding out via higher real interest rates
  lowering private investment and long-run growth [CR 2023 set 1].
- **5.6-5.7:** growth as PPC and LRAS shifting out; physical and human capital, technology; policies that promote
  growth [CR 2023 set 1].
- **6.1:** current account and capital and financial account (CFA) with **CA + CFA = 0** [CR 2023-2025].
- **6.2-6.6:** exchange rates expressed as one currency per unit of the other; FX market graph; determinants
  (tastes, relative incomes, relative price levels, relative real interest rates); appreciation lowers net exports;
  higher relative real interest rates draw financial capital inflows and appreciate the currency [CR 2023-2025].

## 5. Exam format, May 2027

- **Date: Friday, May 7, 2027, Session 2 (12 p.m. local time in most US schools).** AP Microeconomics is Tuesday,
  May 4, 2027, Session 2 (https://apcentral.collegeboard.org/exam-administration-ordering-scores/exam-dates).
  Late-testing date on a separate page, not checked.
- **Mode: hybrid digital.** MCQ answered in Bluebook; FRQs shown in Bluebook and **handwritten (graphs drawn by
  hand) in a paper booklet** returned for scoring (exam page).
- **Total about 2 h 10 min** (70 + 60 min; our sum; Cognito also says ~2 h 10
  https://cognito.org/blog/how-to-study-for-ap-macroeconomics [3P]).
- **Section I: 60 MCQ, 1 h 10 min, 66% of score** (exam page). Number of answer choices not confirmed this pass
  (5 on older exams; digital exams in other subjects use 4) (flag).
- **Section II: 3 FRQs, 1 h including a 10-minute reading/planning period, 33% of score** (exam page):
  - **Q1 long FRQ, 10 points, 50% of the section.** Suggested ~25 min.
  - **Q2 and Q3 short FRQs, 5 points each, 25% of the section each.** Suggested ~12 min each.
  - Students may move between questions; the clock turns red at 5 minutes; no proctor warnings; only booklet work
    is scored; label parts (A, B, i, ii) (2026 FRQ directions page).
- **Calculator:** four-function only (add, subtract, multiply, divide, square root, percent) on **both sections**;
  Bluebook has a built-in **Desmos four-function** calculator; scientific and graphing calculators are not allowed
  (calculator policy page).
- 2023-2025 used two FRQ sets (US time zones); 2026 posted one set (past exam questions page).

### FRQ scoring rules (2023 and 2026 SG; 2026 directions)
- Graphs: label **every axis and curve**, mark required points/values (PL1, Y1, YF, etc.), and show the direction of
  any change. A wrongly labeled graph loses the graph point and usually the shift point.
- "State/identify" needs an explicit statement; "explain" needs the causal link, not the conclusion alone.
- "Calculate" needs the work shown (formula or steps), not just the answer.
- Each listed point is scored independently; later points can follow from an earlier answer (consistency).

### FRQ task patterns (2023-2026, paraphrased for coverage only)
- **Long FRQ (10 pts):** an AD-AS scenario (gap, self-adjustment) linked to policy (fiscal or monetary in either
  reserves framework), a second graph (money market, reserve market, loanable funds, Phillips curve or FX), then
  effects on interest rates, bond prices, investment, capital flows, currency, unemployment.
- **Short FRQs (5 pts):** usually one calculation cluster (unemployment rates, GDP deflator/real GDP, multiplier to
  close a gap) or one market (reserve market, FX market, Phillips curve) with 1-2 explanations.
Sources: CR reports and SG 2023-2026 (past exam questions page).

## 7. Where students lose points (CR reports 2023-2025; 2026 SG)

Paraphrased; part-level percentages are the share of students earning that point.

1. **Ample-reserves monetary policy (every year, worst single area).** Students propose limited-reserve tools (buy
   or sell bonds, change the reserve requirement) or fiscal policy when the question sets an ample-reserves system;
   confuse the action (change interest on reserves / administered rates) with the target (policy rate); draw a
   money market or AD-AS graph instead of the **reserve market**, label curves "money demand/supply", omit reserve
   supply, put supply outside the ample range, or shift the whole demand curve instead of the bounds. 2023 set 2:
   only 14% chose the right tool; 2025: reserve market graph 18-19%.
   https://apcentral.collegeboard.org/media/pdf/ap25-cr-report-macroeconomics-set-2.pdf ;
   https://apcentral.collegeboard.org/media/pdf/ap23-cr-report-macro-set-1.pdf
2. **Foreign exchange graph labeling and shifts.** Inverting the currency ratio on the vertical axis (e.g. the price
   of currency X must be in units of Y per X), missing the initial equilibrium, shifting the wrong curve or the wrong
   way, not stating appreciation/depreciation, and asserting a currency change without tying it to demand or supply
   of the currency. 2025 set 1: FX shift point 25%.
   https://apcentral.collegeboard.org/media/pdf/ap25-cr-report-macroeconomics-set-1.pdf ;
   https://apcentral.collegeboard.org/media/pdf/ap23-cr-report-macro-set-2.pdf
3. **Balance of payments: CA + CFA = 0.** Treating the CFA as two accounts or as unrelated to the current account;
   not explaining that higher real interest rates draw financial capital inflows (CFA surplus); confusing a
   government budget deficit with a trade or CFA deficit. 2025 set 1 CFA point: 12%; set 2: 28%.
   https://apcentral.collegeboard.org/media/pdf/ap24-cr-report-macroeconomics-set-2.pdf
4. **Multiplier math.** Multiplying the multiplier by the output gap instead of dividing; recommending the wrong
   direction of spending change; mixing the tax multiplier with the spending multiplier; confusing MPC with APC or
   MPS. 2025 set 1: 27% correct; set 2: 40%.
   https://apcentral.collegeboard.org/media/pdf/ap23-cr-report-macro-set-2.pdf
5. **Self-adjustment mechanism.** Saying SRAS shifts without the reason (nominal wages/input prices, inflation
   expectations), or attributing the fix to AD or automatic stabilizers. 2023 set 1: 28%; 2025 set 1: 31%.
6. **Explanations that skip links in the chain.** Interest rate change, then interest-sensitive spending
   (investment, consumption), then AD, then output/price level/unemployment; investment raising AD; higher real
   rates lowering investment and long-run growth (2023 set 1: 15%); automatic stabilizers slowing consumption
   (2025 set 2: 10%).
7. **Unemployment calculations.** Natural rate and actual = natural + cyclical (2024 set 1: 39%; 2025 set 2: 32%);
   number unemployed (2023 set 1: 51%); retirees and discouraged workers leave the labor force (not counted as
   unemployed) (2026 SG).
8. **Real vs nominal.** Using nominal GDP per capita for living standards; inverting the deflator (value below 100)
   or writing it as a percent or dollars; not seeing the deflator as a price index for inflation; real GDP
   calculation 31% (2025 set 1); real vs nominal in base year 52%.
   https://apcentral.collegeboard.org/media/pdf/ap24-cr-report-macroeconomics-set-1.pdf
9. **Phillips curve plotting.** SRPC alone is easy (60-70%), but the full graph with LRPC at the natural rate, the
   expected inflation rate and a correctly placed point drops to 24% (2025 set 2).
10. **Net exports.** Believing net exports rise with domestic real income; attributing the change to the price level;
    mixing exports, imports and net exports across two countries (2023 set 2, 2024 set 2).
11. **Crowding out / loanable funds.** Wrong direction of the real interest rate after expansionary fiscal policy
    or no loanable funds link (2024 set 1).

**Not in CR reports for 2023-2025:** a money (deposit) multiplier error. Money-multiplier questions were not a CR
focus in these years (possibly because ample reserves displaced them); keep it for MCQ (flag: check MCQ practice
exams).

### Reader advice to build into the course
- Teach both reserve frameworks side by side and when each applies (every report).
- Flowcharts linking loanable funds, CFA and FX markets; simple CA + CFA = 0 numeric examples.
- Graph peer review and saying the graph aloud; label everything.
- Multiplier routine: find MPC/MPS, compute the multiplier, divide the gap, state the direction.
- Error analysis of explanations; keep ceteris paribus.
- Do not rush Unit 6 at the end of the year (2023 set 2).

## 9. Every formula and calculation

| Calculation | Formula (standard; [ours] unless noted) | Topic |
|---|---|---|
| Opportunity cost, comparative advantage, terms of trade | from PPC tables (output and input methods) | 1.2-1.3 (CED) |
| Shortage / surplus | Qd minus Qs at a given price | 1.6 (CED) |
| GDP, expenditure approach | C + I + G + (X − M) | 2.1 |
| Unemployment rate | unemployed / labor force x 100 | 2.3 (CED) |
| Labor force | employed + unemployed | 2.3 |
| Labor force participation rate | labor force / adult (working-age) population x 100 | 2.3 (CED) |
| Natural rate; actual rate | frictional + structural; natural + cyclical | 2.3 (CED) |
| CPI | cost of basket in year t / cost in base year x 100 | 2.4 (CED) |
| Inflation rate | (index_new − index_old) / index_old x 100 | 2.4 (CED) |
| Real value of a nominal variable | nominal / price index x 100 | 2.4, 2.6 |
| GDP deflator | nominal GDP / real GDP x 100 | 2.6 (CED) |
| Real GDP | nominal GDP / deflator x 100, or quantities at base-year prices | 2.6 (CED) |
| GDP growth / per capita | % change in real GDP; real GDP / population | 2.6, 5.6 |
| MPC, MPS | ΔC / ΔDI; ΔS / ΔDI; MPC + MPS = 1 | 3.2 (CED) |
| Spending multiplier | 1 / (1 − MPC) = 1 / MPS | 3.2 |
| Tax multiplier | −MPC / MPS | 3.2 |
| Required change to close a gap | gap / multiplier | 3.2, 3.8 (CR) |
| Balanced-budget multiplier | 1 (flag: CED mention not confirmed) | 3.2 |
| Real interest rate | nominal rate − (expected) inflation | 4.2 |
| Required reserves; excess reserves | deposits x reserve ratio; total reserves − required | 4.4 |
| Money (deposit) multiplier | 1 / required reserve ratio | 4.4 |
| Max change in money supply | excess reserves x money multiplier (plus initial deposit effects when cash is deposited; teach both cases) | 4.4 |
| Quantity theory | M x V = P x Y | 5.3 |
| Budget balance | tax revenue − government outlays (deficit if negative); debt = accumulated deficits | 5.4 |
| Balance of payments | current account + capital and financial account = 0 | 6.1 (CR) |
| Exchange rate conversion | price in Y per X ↔ 1 / (price in X per Y) | 6.2 |

Four-function calculator only, so all arithmetic must be hand-friendly (calculator policy page).
