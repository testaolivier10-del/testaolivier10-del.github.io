# AP Physics 1 market and student needs (research as of 2026-10-12)

Purpose: inform a new AP Physics 1 course on LevlPrep (free notes plus a one-time Premium pass, same model as AP Chem).
All content paraphrased. Reddit was read through public mirror front ends (redlib/libreddit) because reddit.com and
Google endpoints are blocked from the shell; sample is ~5 threads plus r/APPhysics front page, not a full scrape.
Search interest is from Google autocomplete only (no volumes). Several prices come from competitor-written comparison
pages (flagged).

## 1. Size, timing, exam facts

- ~170-175k takers a year (174,401 in 2025). After the 2025 redesign the pass rate jumped from ~47% to ~67% and 5s
  doubled to ~20% (2026: 19% 5s, 68% 3+). Students still call it "the hardest AP" from the pre-2025 reputation.
  https://apstudents.collegeboard.org/about-ap-scores/score-distributions/ap-physics-1
- **Exam: Wednesday, May 5, 2027, afternoon.** 42 MCQ / 85 min + 4 FRQ / 95 min (new for 2027; 2025-26 was 40/80 and
  4/100). https://apcentral.collegeboard.org/courses/ap-physics-1/exam
- Usually a first physics course for juniors, often taught by non-specialist teachers (several Reddit posts open with
  "my teacher has never taught physics").
- **Demand modes** (autocomplete + Reddit):
  1. Unit-by-unit through the year: "ap physics 1 unit 1 practice problems / review / frq / test / progress check mcq",
     "unit 2 practice problems", "how to study for ap physics 1 midterm / final / kinematics". October = Units 1-2.
  2. Spring cram (April to May 5): "practice exam", "practice test pdf", "score calculator", "formula sheet",
     "score distribution 2026" (top suggestion after release in July).
  3. Pre-course worry (summer): "is ap physics 1 hard", "is it worth it", "algebra based or calculus", "1 vs C".

### Top search phrases (Google autocomplete, 2026-10-12)
- "ap physics 1": score distribution 2026, formula sheet, equation sheet, units, frq, score calculator, reference sheet, ced

## 3. Student pain points (recurring themes)

1. **It doesn't "click" for months.** Students describe frustration with conceptual reasoning over plug-and-chug;
   advice is to master forces/FBDs first. Trig (SOH-CAH-TOA, components) and solving systems flagged as skill gaps.
   https://redlib.groet-infra.nl/r/APStudents/comments/1vfj1k9/ap_physics_1_help/ (Reddit ID 1vfj1k9)
2. **Weak or new teachers; self-teaching.** "Teacher has never taught physics, class is lost" posts; replies point to
   Khan, OpenStax, Flipping Physics. https://lr.eu.psf.lt/r/apphysics/comments/1f8fah9/ap_physics_1_help (ID 1f8fah9)
3. **Rotation is the hardest content.** Fiveable's own practice data: lowest accuracy on 6.6 orbiting satellites (43%),
   5.5 rotational equilibrium (47%), 4.2 impulse (48%). Torque/ladder problems recur on r/APPhysics.
   https://fiveable.me/ap-physics-1-revised/ap-physics-1-exam/ap-physics-1-is-it-hard/study-guide/ap-physics-1-is-it-hard
4. **Fluids has little practice material** (new to Physics 1 in 2025, many books/old exams lack it), and the 2025 CR
   report says classes rushed Unit 8. r/APPhysics front page: https://red.applefritter.com/r/APPhysics
5. **FRQ format anxiety:** partial credit questions, how to show derivations for formulas not on the sheet, drawing
   best-fit lines, and the lab question's "what to plot so the slope gives X". Old released FRQs don't match the 4 new
   types, so students hunt for "2024-25 format" practice.
6. **Written justification:** FRQ rubrics reward functional-dependence reasoning and naming conditions (zero net
   external force), which students rarely practise (see framework.md section 8).
7. **Practice tests feel harder than the class** ("can't answer most Princeton Review questions"); books are noted as
   off-format or harder than the real exam.

### What students recommend / dislike
- Love: Flipping Physics, Allan Tsao, Ultimate Review Packet, official past FRQs, AP Classroom progress checks
  (students hunt for the FRQ versions), PR/Barron's for MCQ volume, the free CB AP Physics 1 workbook.
- Mixed or negative: AP Daily videos and Khan called unhelpful or shallow by some; older MIT/Giancoli material good but
  outdated; 5 Steps to a 5 editions may be pre-2025.

## 4. Gaps a new interactive course with simulations could fill

1. **FBD builder with instant feedback** keyed to the CR errors (extra "net force" or "centripetal force" vectors,
   components drawn as forces, wrong point of application, normal force direction at the top of a loop).
2. **Representation-linked sims**: drag a cart/spring/pendulum and see x-t, v-t, a-t, energy bar charts and
   momentum-time graphs update together; directly trains TBR (diagram, equation, graph, consistency).
3. **Lab-question trainer**: simulated apparatus that generates noisy data; student picks axes to linearize, plots,
   draws best-fit, reads slope. No competitor does this interactively; it is a 10-point FRQ with ~49% mean.
4. **QQT practice with derivation scaffolds** (two-column words + math) and a functional-dependence checker ("if ρ
   doubles, a ...").
5. **Rotation and fluids depth**: the weakest units in practice data and the thinnest in existing materials.
6. **Current-format accuracy**: 42/85 + 4/95 timing, 8-point QQT, single-select 4-choice MCQ, g = 10 vs 9.8 handling,
   strict CED boundaries (no 2D momentum, no simultaneous equations, no torque direction). Many paid books still mix
   old formats.
7. **Unit-by-unit SEO pages** matching "unit N practice problems / review / frq" queries, which dominate autocomplete
   from September to March, plus a reference-sheet explainer and an honest score-distribution page for the spring.
8. **Free core + one-time pass** undercuts Fiveable's $79/yr and UWorld's $149 while competing with free Khan/Flipping
   Physics on interactivity rather than video volume.
