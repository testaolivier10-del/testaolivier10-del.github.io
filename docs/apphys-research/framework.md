## 1. What changed in the 2024-25 revision (first exam May 2025)

Source: https://apcentral.collegeboard.org/courses/ap-physics/revisions-2024-25 ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AP_Physics_1
- Fluids moved in from AP Physics 2 as Unit 8 (10-15%). Course went from 7 to 8 units.
- Mechanical waves and sound moved out to AP Physics 2. (Circuits had already left AP Physics 1 in 2021.)
  Do not teach waves, sound, or circuits.
- Added: explicit links between rotational and translational motion (Unit 6 rolling), power learning objectives,
  SHM equations of motion (x = A cos(2πft)).
- Science practices cut from 7 to 3, and no longer tied to specific LOs: any LO can be assessed with any practice.
- Exam: multi-select MCQs removed; 50 MCQ became 40; FRQs went from 5 to 4, all four of a new type.
- Old (2015-2024) FRQs (Paragraph Argument Short Answer, 7-point short answers) and old released exams do not match the
  current format. CB's past-exam page flags this.

## 3. Per-topic learning objectives, essential knowledge, exclusions

Format: LO ids with a short paraphrase; key EK points; **Boundary** = the CED's exclusion or limit statements (short
quotes where wording matters). "None" = the CED prints no boundary for that topic.

### Unit 1 Kinematics (from CED)
- **1.1** 1.1.A scalar vs vector (magnitude/direction); distance and speed are scalars; position, displacement, velocity,
  acceleration are vectors; in 1D the sign gives direction. 1.1.B vector sums in 1D (opposite directions, opposite signs).
  Boundary: none.
- **1.2** 1.2.A change in position; object model ignores size/shape/internal structure. 1.2.B average velocity
  (Δx/Δt) and average acceleration (Δv/Δt); acceleration if speed or direction changes; small-interval averages
  approximate instantaneous values. Boundary: none.
- **1.3** 1.3.A represent motion with diagrams, graphs, equations, narratives; 3 constant-acceleration equations;
  free-fall g downward ≈ 10 m/s²; slope of x-t = v, slope of v-t = a, area under v-t = Δx, area under a-t = Δv.
  Boundary: no **quantitative** analysis of nonuniform acceleration (qualitative graphs and discussion only); g = 10 m/s²
  is the course value, but 9.8 or 9.81 is not penalized.
- **1.4** 1.4.A reference frame of an observer. 1.4.B convert between inertial frames: observed velocity = object
  velocity plus frame velocity; acceleration is the same in all inertial frames.
  Boundary: relative-velocity vector addition **restricted to one dimension**; frames assumed inertial unless stated.
- **1.5** 1.5.A resolve vectors into perpendicular components with trig in a chosen coordinate system. 1.5.B 2D motion
  as two independent 1D motions; projectile = zero acceleration in one direction, constant in the other. Boundary: none.

### Unit 2 Force and Translational Dynamics (from CED)
- **2.1** 2.1.A system properties come from internal interactions; treat a system as one object when internals don't
  matter; systems can exchange mass/energy. 2.1.B center of mass: on symmetry lines; x_cm = Σmx/Σm; model the system as a
  point at its CM. Boundary: calculate CM only for **five or fewer particles** in a 2D arrangement or highly symmetric
  systems.
- **2.2** 2.2.A a force is an interaction between two objects; an object cannot exert a net force on itself; contact
  forces are macroscopic electric forces. 2.2.B free-body diagrams: every force the environment exerts, drawn from a dot
  at the CM; choosing an axis along the acceleration simplifies algebra.
  Boundary: FBDs show **forces, not force components**.
- **2.3** 2.3.A third-law pairs act on different objects; internal forces don't change CM motion; tension; ideal string
  (massless, inextensible, uniform tension); ideal pulley (massless, frictionless).
  Boundary: tension in a string/cable/chain **with mass only qualitatively**; the only action-at-a-distance force in
  AP Physics 1 is **gravity**.
- **2.4** 2.4.A net force = vector sum; translational equilibrium (ΣF = 0) means constant velocity; forces can balance in
  one dimension and not another; inertial frame = where the first law holds. Boundary: none.
- **2.5** 2.5.A unbalanced forces give a_cm proportional to and in the direction of F_net; CM velocity changes only with
  nonzero net external force. Boundary: none.
- **2.6** 2.6.A Newton's gravitation: ∝ m1m2/r², attractive, along the line of centers, acts at the CM; field g = F/m;
  weight = gravitational force from a planet. 2.6.B gravity treated as constant near the surface (g ≈ 10 N/kg).
  2.6.C apparent weight = normal force; it differs from mg when accelerating; weightless when gravity is the only force;
  equivalence principle. 2.6.D inertial vs gravitational mass, experimentally equal. Boundary: none.
- **2.7** 2.7.A kinetic friction μ_k·F_N, opposes relative sliding, independent of contact area; normal force is
  perpendicular to and away from the surface. 2.7.B static friction takes whatever value prevents slipping, up to
  μ_s·F_N; usually μ_s > μ_k. Boundary: none.
- **2.8** 2.8.A ideal spring (massless), Hooke's law F = -kΔx, force always toward equilibrium. Boundary: none.
- **2.9** 2.9.A centripetal acceleration v²/r toward the center; supplied by one force, several forces, or components
  (loop-the-loop top: minimum speed when gravity alone supplies it; banked curves; conical pendulum); tangential
  acceleration changes speed; net a = vector sum; period and frequency (T = 2πr/v). 2.9.B circular orbits and Kepler's
  third law (T² vs r³ via the central mass).
  Boundary: **quantitative banked curves only when no friction is needed**; friction-required banked curves
  qualitative only; **Kepler's first and second laws not required**.

### Unit 3 Work, Energy, and Power [CED via physicslearn]
- **3.1** 3.1.A K = ½mv², scalar, depends on the reference frame. Boundary: none (1.4's 1D limit applies).
- **3.2** 3.2.A work = energy transferred by a force; scalar (+, -, 0); constant force uses the component parallel to
  displacement; work-energy theorem ΔK = ΣW; work = area under F_parallel vs x.
  Boundary: only **mechanical** energy transfer is analyzed (no heating or thermodynamics).
- **3.3** 3.3.A potential energy belongs to a system of interacting objects (conservative forces only), not one object;
  zero point is chosen; U_s = ½k(Δx)², ΔU_g = mgΔy, U_G = -Gm1m2/r; sum over pairs for 3+ objects. Boundary: none.
- **3.4** 3.4.A single-object systems have only K. 3.4.B mechanical energy = K + U; changes are balanced inside the
  system or by transfer across the boundary. 3.4.C energy is always conserved; mechanical energy is constant only with no
  net external work and no internal nonconservative forces.
  Boundary: mechanical energy can be "dissipated as thermal energy or sound" (qualitative); only mechanical energy is
  analyzed quantitatively.
- **3.5** 3.5.A average power = ΔE/Δt = W/Δt; instantaneous power = F_parallel·v. Boundary: none.

### Unit 4 Linear Momentum [CED via physicslearn]
- **4.1** 4.1.A p = mv, vector along v; collision model (internal forces ≫ external), explosion model.
  Boundary: "momentum" means linear momentum unless stated.
- **4.2** 4.2.A impulse J = F_avg·Δt, vector, = area under F-t; F_net = slope of p-t. 4.2.B impulse-momentum theorem
  J = Δp; Newton's second law follows for constant mass.
  Boundary: **no quantitative analysis of systems whose mass changes over time** (rockets, leaking carts).
- **4.3** 4.3.A CM velocity constant with no net external force; total p = vector sum; internal impulses equal and
  opposite. 4.3.B system choice decides whether p changes; p constant when net external force is zero.
  Boundary: **no questions requiring simultaneous equations**; quantitative momentum conservation in **one dimension**,
  2D only semiquantitative. (This is why fully elastic "find both final velocities" problems are out.)
- **4.4** 4.4.A elastic: total K unchanged (individual K can change); inelastic: total K decreases, converted by
  nonconservative forces; perfectly inelastic: objects stick, common final velocity. Boundary: none of its own
  (4.3's simultaneous-equations limit applies).

### Unit 5 Torque and Rotational Dynamics [CED via physicslearn]
- **5.1** 5.1.A angular displacement (radians), angular velocity, angular acceleration about a fixed axis; rigid
  system; the 3 constant-α equations; graphs analogous to 1D kinematics.
  Boundary: rotation direction described only as **clockwise or counterclockwise** about a given axis (no vector
  directions).
- **5.2** 5.2.A s = rθ, v = rω, a_T = rα; all points on a rigid body share ω and α. Boundary: same CW/CCW limit.
- **5.3** 5.3.A only the force component perpendicular to r causes torque; lever arm = perpendicular distance to the line
  of action. 5.3.B force diagrams (like FBDs but showing where each force acts); τ = rF sinθ.
  Boundary: **direction of the torque vector beyond scope** (sign conventions only).
- **5.4** 5.4.A rotational inertia depends on mass and its distribution; I = mr² per point mass; sum for systems.
  5.4.B I is minimum about the CM; parallel-axis theorem I' = I_cm + Md².
  Boundary: calculate I for **five or fewer objects**; I of extended rigid bodies (disk, rod, sphere) **will be
  provided**, not memorized.
- **5.5** 5.5.A rotational and translational equilibrium are independent; Στ = 0 means constant ω; unbalanced torque
  means ω changes. Boundary: **no simultaneous analysis of rotation in multiple planes**.
- **5.6** 5.6.A α = τ_net / I, same direction as net torque; a full description may need separate translational and
  rotational analyses. Boundary: none.

### Unit 6 Energy and Momentum of Rotating Systems [CED via physicslearn]
- **6.1** 6.1.A K_rot = ½Iω², scalar; a body can have K_rot with its CM at rest. Boundary: none.
- **6.2** 6.2.A work by a torque W = τΔθ; area under τ vs θ. Boundary: none.
- **6.3** 6.3.A L = Iω (rigid body); L = rmv sinθ (point object about a point; depends on chosen axis).
  6.3.B angular impulse = τΔt = area under τ-t. 6.3.C angular impulse = ΔL; τ_net = ΔL/Δt (= Iα when I constant);
  τ_net = slope of L-t. Boundary: **direction of angular momentum and angular impulse beyond scope**.
- **6.4** 6.4.A total L is the sum of parts; changing shape changes ω at constant L (skater). 6.4.B L conserved when net
  external torque is zero; system choice matters. Boundary: none of its own (6.3 applies).
- **6.5** 6.5.A total K = translational + rotational. 6.5.B rolling without slipping links CM motion to rotation
  (v_cm = rω, Δx_cm = rΔθ); static friction does no dissipative work. 6.5.C rolling while slipping: no direct link,
  kinetic friction dissipates energy.
  Boundary: **rolling friction beyond scope**; rolling-while-slipping is **qualitative only** (explain how linear and
  angular quantities change, not model them).
- **6.6** 6.6.A satellite with much smaller mass than the central body; circular orbit: E, U, K, L all constant;
  elliptical orbit: E and L constant, U and K change; U_G = 0 at infinity; escape speed from total energy = 0.
  Boundary: none of its own (2.9's "no Kepler 1st/2nd law" applies).

### Unit 7 Oscillations [CED via physicslearn; no boundary statements in this unit]
- **7.1** 7.1.A SHM is periodic motion with a restoring force proportional to displacement and opposite it; equilibrium =
  zero net force; small-angle pendulum approximates SHM (restoring torque ∝ angle).
- **7.2** 7.2.A T = 1/f; spring-mass period depends only on m and k (T = 2π√(m/k)); small-angle pendulum on ℓ and g
  (T = 2π√(ℓ/g)).
- **7.3** 7.3.A x(t) as a sine or cosine; locate maxima, minima, zeros of x, v, a; period independent of amplitude;
  analyze SHM from graphs.
- **7.4** 7.4.A E = K + U constant; K max where U min and vice versa; minimum K is zero; amplitude sets total energy
  (E = ½kA² for a spring).

### Unit 8 Fluids [CED via physicslearn]
- **8.1** 8.1.A states of matter differ by particle interactions; fluid has no fixed shape; ρ = m/V; ideal fluid is
  incompressible and nonviscous. Boundary: none.
- **8.2** 8.2.A P = F_perp/A, scalar; incompressible fluid density constant. 8.2.B fluid pressure from particle
  collisions; absolute P = P0 + ρgh; gauge pressure = ρgh. Boundary: none.
- **8.3** 8.3.A Newton's laws apply to fluid parcels. 8.3.B buoyant force is the net upward force from fluid particles;
  F_b = ρ_fluid·V_displaced·g (Archimedes). Boundary: none.
- **8.4** 8.4.A conservation of mass: volume flow rate = Av, continuity A1v1 = A2v2. 8.4.B Bernoulli's equation as
  energy conservation; Torricelli v = √(2gΔy) derivable.
  Boundary: unless stated, **fluids are ideal and pipes completely filled** (no viscosity, turbulence, compressibility).

### Exclusions checklist (what the course must NOT teach as assessed content)
Quantitative nonuniform acceleration; 2D relative velocity; FBDs with components; quantitative tension in massive
ropes; forces at a distance other than gravity (no electric/magnetic); friction-required banked curves (quantitative);
Kepler 1 and 2; heat/thermal energy quantitatively; variable-mass systems; simultaneous equations (e.g. full 1D elastic
collision solutions); quantitative 2D momentum; torque/angular momentum vector directions (right-hand rule); multi-plane
rotation; memorizing I of extended bodies; rolling friction; quantitative slipping-while-rolling; nonideal fluids;
waves, sound, circuits (all moved out of the course).

## 4. Science practices

Source: CED pp. framework intro (read directly); course overview PDF.

| # | Practice | Skills |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Creating Representations | 1.A diagrams/tables/charts/schematics; 1.B quantitative graphs with scales, units, plotted data; 1.C qualitative sketched graphs of a model's behavior |
| 2 | Mathematical Routines | 2.A derive a symbolic expression from known quantities; 2.B calculate or estimate a quantity with units; 2.C compare quantities across scenarios/times/locations; 2.D predict new values or factors of change from functional dependence |
| 3 | Scientific Questioning and Argumentation | 3.A create experimental procedures for a question; 3.B apply a law/definition/model to make a claim; 3.C justify a claim with evidence, representations, or principles |

Practices are not tied to LOs; any LO can be tested with any skill (revisions page). Every exam item aligns to one LO and
one skill (CED).

Exam weighting by practice **[UNVERIFIED; the CED exam-overview pages were past the extraction cutoff]**. Wikipedia's
AP Physics 1 article gives: MCQ: Mathematical Routines 55-75%, Scientific Questioning/Argumentation (labelled
"Experimental Design and Analysis") 25-35%, Creating Representations not listed for MCQ; FRQ: Creating Representations
20-35%, Mathematical Routines 30-40%, Questioning/Argumentation 35-45% (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AP_Physics_1).
The same article for Physics C: Mechanics shows MCQ 65-85% / 20-35% and FRQ 20-35% / 40-45% / 30-35%, which suggests the
physics courses share a similar table. Takeaway either way: MCQ is mostly Mathematical Routines; representations (FBDs,
graphs, bar charts) are FRQ-only skills.

### Labs
25% of instructional time in hands-on, inquiry-based lab work; students keep notebooks and reports (CED, course
overview). No mandated lab list in the extracted text. The 2025 CR report points teachers to the CED's "Laboratory
Investigations" section for what each piece of equipment measures (meterstick, spring scale, stopwatch, motion
detector, force sensor, etc.).

## 5. Exam format

Sources: exam page; course overview PDF; CED clarifications PDF; 2025/2026 FRQ booklets.

| | 2025 and 2026 exams | **May 2027 exam (current)** |
|---|---|---|
| Section I MCQ | 40 Q, 80 min, 50% | **42 Q, 85 min, 50%** |
| Section II FRQ | 4 Q, 100 min, 50% | **4 Q, 95 min, 50%** |
| Total | 3 h | 3 h (plus a 10-min break) |

- **MCQ:** single-select only (multi-select removed for the 2025 exam), 4 answer choices (A-D), discrete items and
  stimulus-based sets. No penalty for wrong answers. (https://www.applerouth.com/blog/ap-physics-overhaul-what-students-need-to-know-about-the-may-2025-exam-changes ;
  revisions page.)
- **Hybrid digital (since May 2025):** MCQs answered in Bluebook; FRQs viewed in Bluebook but **handwritten** in a paper
  booklet (pencil or dark ink, ruler/straightedge allowed). So graphs, FBDs, and bar charts are hand-drawn on the exam.
- **Calculator:** allowed on both sections: four-function, scientific, or graphing; up to 2 handhelds plus Bluebook's
  built-in Desmos (scientific and graphing). No 2027 change for physics.
- **Reference sheet:** "AP Physics 1: Algebra-Based Exam Reference Information", printed and in Bluebook.

### The four FRQs (fixed order; total 40 raw points)
Points from the course overview PDF and the 2025 and 2026 scoring guidelines (both years match).

| Q | Type (official name) | Points | Suggested time | What it asks |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Mathematical Routines (MR) | 10 | ~25 min | Multi-part quantitative analysis: sketch a graph (e.g. momentum or velocity vs time), symbolic derivation from a principle, then a claim with justification. 2025: collision + friction; 2026: water fountain projectile + flow rate. |
| 2 | Translation Between Representations (TBR) | 12 | ~30 min | (A) draw a representation (FBD, energy bar chart, or momentum vectors), (B) derive an equation consistent with it, (C) sketch a graph, (D) compare/justify consistency across the representations. 2025: energy with spring on incline; 2026: disk collision and CM. |
| 3 | Experimental Design and Analysis (LAB) | 10 | ~25 min | (A) procedure with measured quantities and a way to reduce uncertainty, (B) choose what to plot for a linear graph and how the slope gives the target, (C) plot given data: axis labels and units, linear scale, best-fit line, (D) use the slope to compute the quantity. 2025: meterstick torque to find mass; 2026: ramp to find μ_k. |
| 4 | Qualitative/Quantitative Translation (QQT) | **8** | ~20 min | (A) make a qualitative claim and justify without equations, (B) derive an expression from a named principle, (C) use the derived functional dependence to check the part A claim. 2025: buoyancy and acceleration in fresh vs salt water; 2026: rotational toys with different I. |

Note: the pre-2025 exam had a 12-point QQT and a 12-point Experimental Design; under the current format **QQT is 8
points** and TBR is the 12-pointer.

### Equation sheet (2025 version, "© 2025 College Board")
Official PDF text extraction only yielded headers, conventions, units and prefixes; the equations below are from a
transcription at https://www.physicslearn.org/formulas/ap-physics-1 that matches the official section headers and
variable lists. Verify against the PDF.

- **Constants:** G = 6.67 × 10⁻¹¹ N·m²/kg²; 1 atm = 1.0 × 10⁵ Pa; g = 9.8 m/s² (acceleration at Earth's surface);
  g = 9.8 N/kg (field strength). **Conflict:** the CED says the course uses g = 10 m/s² while the sheet prints 9.8;
  both are accepted. Teach "10 for estimates, 9.8 is fine, show which you use".
- **Prefixes** tera to pico; **units** Hz, J, kg, m, N, Pa, s, W.
- **Trig table** for 0°, 30°, 37°, 45°, 53°, 60°, 90° (37/53 = 3-4-5 triangle values).
- **Geometry:** rectangle, triangle, circle (A, C, arc length s = rθ), rectangular solid, cylinder, sphere (V, S);
  right-triangle trig and Pythagorean theorem.
- **Conventions printed on the sheet:** frames inertial, air resistance negligible, springs and strings ideal, fluids
  ideal and pipes full, unless stated.
- **Translational mechanics:** v_x = v_x0 + a_x t; x = x0 + v_x0 t + ½a_x t²; v_x² = v_x0² + 2a_x(x - x0);
  x_cm = Σm_i x_i / Σm_i; a_sys = ΣF/m_sys; |F_g| = Gm1m2/r²; |F_f| ≤ |μF_N|; F_s = -kΔx; a_c = v²/r; K = ½mv²;
  W = F_∥d = Fd cosθ; ΔK = ΣW_i; U_s = ½k(Δx)²; U_G = -Gm1m2/r; ΔU_g = mgΔy; P_avg = W/Δt = ΔE/Δt;
  P_inst = F_∥v = Fv cosθ; p = mv; F_net = Δp/Δt = mΔv/Δt; J = F_avg Δt = Δp; v_cm = Σp_i/Σm_i.
- **Rotation, oscillation, fluids:** ω = ω0 + αt; θ = θ0 + ω0t + ½αt²; ω² = ω0² + 2α(θ - θ0); v = rω; a_T = rα;
  τ = r_⊥F = rF sinθ; I = Σm_i r_i²; I' = I_cm + Md²; α_sys = Στ/I_sys; K = ½Iω²; W = τΔθ; L = Iω; L = rmv sinθ;
  ΔL = τΔt; Δx_cm = rΔθ; T = 1/f; T_s = 2π√(m/k); T_p = 2π√(ℓ/g); x = A cos(2πft); x = A sin(2πft); ρ = m/V;
  P = F_⊥/A; P = P0 + ρgh; P_gauge = ρgh; F_b = ρVg; A1v1 = A2v2; P1 + ρgy1 + ½ρv1² = P2 + ρgy2 + ½ρv2².
- **Not on the sheet:** I formulas for disk, rod, hoop, sphere (given in the question); escape speed; v_cm = rω for rolling
  is there only as Δx_cm = rΔθ and v = rω; Torricelli (derive from Bernoulli); Kepler's third law (derive from
  F_g = ma_c); minimum loop speed √(gr) (derive).

## 8. Common student errors (Chief Reader Reports)

### 2025 (current format) - https://apcentral.collegeboard.org/media/pdf/ap25-cr-report-physics-1.pdf
**Q1 MR, momentum (collision, then friction between objects):**
- Momentum-vs-time graph drawn sloped, or with a drop at the collision, instead of one constant horizontal line.
- Using energy conservation to find the post-collision speed of a sticky collision; not starting from momentum
  conservation.
- Using the combined mass for both before and after; wrong ΔK expression; fraction/algebra simplification errors; not
  substituting the derived v_f.
- Saying "momentum is constant because of conservation of momentum" (circular) instead of "net external force on the
  system is zero"; calling friction internal without linking that to momentum.

**Q2 TBR, energy (block, spring, incline):**
- Putting kinetic energy into the equation at points where the block is at rest.
- Algebra errors with squared terms that carry coefficients (e.g. ½k(2x)²).
- Assuming a block always slows on touching a spring (depends on k and height change).
- Justifying with only U_g or only U_s instead of comparing both.
- Total mechanical energy graph drawn sloping instead of horizontal; U_g graph curved, increasing, or mirroring U_s
  instead of linear decrease; plotting vs time instead of position.
- Wrong height from incline distance and angle (d sinθ); bar charts whose K and U_g don't add to the total.

**Q3 LAB, torque (meterstick on a pivot, spring scale):**
- Assuming the spring-scale reading equals the hanging block's weight regardless of position.
- Thinking a spring scale measures torque (it measures force; torque needs the lever arm).
- Axis with only a label or only a unit; plotting inverse quantities that give a curve; connect-the-dots instead of a
  best-fit line.
- Mixing up similar distances (scale-to-pivot vs block-to-pivot); only ~20% related the slope to the stick's mass.

**Q4 QQT, buoyancy (blocks in fresh vs salt water):**
- Treating buoyant force as the only force (omitting gravity); thinking gravity changes with fluid density.
- Starting from Bernoulli when the prompt said Newton's second law.
- Reading ρ as p (pressure or momentum); equating pressure with force.
- Drawing buoyant force and gravity in the same direction; misreading inequality symbols so the claim contradicts
  the chosen answer; "denser fluid resists more" without noticing buoyancy also increases.
- CR advice: teach task verbs; two-column derivations (words beside math); functional-dependence language; actually
  schedule time for Unit 8 (many classes rushed fluids).

### 2024 (old format, still relevant content) - https://apcentral.collegeboard.org/media/pdf/ap24-cr-report-physics-1.pdf
- **FBDs in a vertical loop:** normal force drawn upward at the top; a separate "centripetal force" or net force vector
  drawn as an extra force; extraneous vectors (friction, "motion", velocity, KE, momentum) on the FBD.
- **Equilibrium FBD (hinged rod):** hinge force drawn horizontal only; extra normal force; tension drawn as components
  instead of one vector; forces at the wrong points; unlabeled vectors.
- Assuming equal vertical tension components means equal tensions; "shorter string means smaller tension".
- Using only translational equilibrium and ignoring the hinge; wrong trig function; leaving answers in F_g instead of
  given M and L.
- Energy bar charts split evenly by default; energy described as "lost"; height errors (3R vs 4R).
- ΔK written as ½m(Δv)²; momentum written as p = Ft; timing a spring from top to bottom instead of one full period;
  using the spring period formula for a pendulum.
- Saying "gravity" instead of gravitational force/field/acceleration; arguing from F = mg that a more massive planet has
  smaller g; claiming an object's mass changes between planets; addressing only g or only ℓ.
- Elastic collision graphs: object A shown stopping or reversing incorrectly; CM line slope changing after a collision;
  not recognizing sticking = inelastic.
- Struggled to linearize (plot 4π²m vs T² to get k as slope).

### Cross-year patterns to design against
1. Free-body diagrams: extra forces (net force, "centripetal force", motion), components drawn as forces, wrong
   points of application, missing labels.
2. Mixing up momentum and energy conservation (which is conserved in inelastic collisions, and why).
3. Circular justifications ("conserved because of conservation"); naming the principle without the condition (zero net
   external force/torque, no nonconservative work).
4. Graph conventions: total energy should be flat; best-fit lines are not connect-the-dots; axes need label and unit;
   linearization so the slope means something.
5. Functional dependence: going from a derived expression to "if ρ doubles, a ..." and checking it against the claim.
6. Symbol literacy (ρ vs p, ℓ vs L, I vs current) and reading the variable list on the reference sheet.
7. Sign handling: g as negative vs positive in a chosen axis; mass vs weight (mg) confusion; ΔK vs ½m(Δv)².
8. Lab equipment literacy: what a spring scale, force sensor, motion detector, photogate actually measure.
