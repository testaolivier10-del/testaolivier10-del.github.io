## Named studies checked against the papers

- **Roediger & Karpicke (2006), Experiment 1** (`Psychological Science, 17`, 249-255; full text read). The paper
  reports 120 Washington University undergraduates, ages 18-24, and two TOEFL prose passages ("The Sun", "Sea
  Otters"). There were 7-minute study, restudy and test periods with no feedback. Learning condition was within
  subjects and the retention interval (5 min, 2 days, 1 week) between subjects. Recall of idea units: restudy vs
  test was 81 vs 75 at 5 min, 54 vs 68 at 2 days and 42 vs 56 at 1 week. The EBQ Source 1 and the
  `retrieving-memories` notes match on every number and on the design. The reference is correct.
- **Ebbinghaus (1885) savings.** The figure plots 58.2, 44.2, 35.8, 33.7, 27.8, 25.4 and 21.1% at 20 min, 1 h,
  8.8 h, 1, 2, 6 and 31 days. I checked them against Ebbinghaus's own Section 29 text (psychclassics, Ruger &
  Bussenius translation). After 1 hour "one half the amount of the original work had to be expended". After 8
  hours "two thirds". After 24 hours "about one third was always remembered" (he cites 33.7 again). After 6 days
  "about one fourth" and after a month "fully one fifth". I also checked a secondary table (58, 44, 36, 33, 28,
  25, 21%) and the interval list in Murre & Dros (2015, PLoS ONE e0120644): 20 min, 1 h, 9 h (8.75 in their fits),
  1, 2, 6 and 31 days. All agree. I could not read the exact table, which is printed as an image in the source,
  so the decimals at 20 min, 2 days and 6 days are confirmed only to the nearest percent. The point at 0 is
  labeled "just learned (100%)", which follows from the definition of savings and is not a data point. The
  Murre & Dros replication claim in the notes is accurate.
- **Loftus & Palmer (1974), Experiment 2.** 150 students in groups of 50. Broken glass was reported by 16/50
  (smashed), 7/50 (hit) and 6/50 (control). Correct, and so is the reference.
- Simons & Chabris (1999): "about half" missed the gorilla, which is correct. Godden & Baddeley (1975): divers'
  context effect, correct. Steele & Aronson (1995): Black students on a verbal test framed as diagnostic, correct,
  with the replication caution in place. The H.M. (1953) case is checked below.

## Checked and fine

- **Science.** I read every stem, option, explanation, glossary entry, lesson and notes page in the eight topics.
  The authoring guide's content traps are taught correctly:
  - proactive and retroactive interference (direction and examples)
  - recall and recognition
  - episodic, semantic and procedural memory
  - retrograde and anterograde amnesia
  - maintenance and elaborative rehearsal
  - context-, state- and mood-dependent memory
