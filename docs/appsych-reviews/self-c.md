## For needs-author (contested or uncertain)

- **Ebbinghaus numbers.** The forgetting-curve figure uses the commonly reported savings: 58.2, 44.2, 35.8, 33.7,
  27.8, 25.4 and 21.1% at 20 min, 1 h, about 9 h, 1, 2, 6 and 31 days. I confirmed the 20-minute, 1-day and
  31-day values against a secondary source. The others are the standard textbook table; I could not open the
  primary source (PMC4492928 was blocked).
- **Stereotype threat.** It is taught as real but smaller and less consistent than early studies showed, citing
  Steele & Aronson (1995) and later large replications. A teacher should agree the wording (replication entry).
- **Priming.** Word priming is taught as robust; behavioral priming (the slow-walking study) as not replicated.
- **Growth mindset.** Taught as a small average effect on grades, larger for struggling students.
- **Repression.** Taught as the psychodynamic account with weak evidence. The CED lists it as a concept; the
  wording should be confirmed.
- **Multiple intelligences.** Gardner is not named (the framework says 2.8 asks only g versus several
  abilities); the notes say the evidence for independent intelligences is limited.
- **Short-term memory capacity.** The notes give "about seven, newer estimates about four chunks". The figure and
  glossary say "a few items, about 20 to 30 seconds".
- **2.8 exclusion.** "Labeling or describing cognitive abilities and disabilities" is respected: no
  intellectual-disability categories or cutoffs appear.
