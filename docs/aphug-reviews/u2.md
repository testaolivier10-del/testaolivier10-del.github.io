## Errors fixed (before, after)

1. TFR map `population-dynamics-s1`: Yemen in 2.1-3.9 (data about 4.6). After: 4.0 or more; the map label now names
   Yemen with Afghanistan.
2. TFR map: South Sudan in 4.0 or more (data about 3.9 in 2023, 3.8 in 2024). After: 2.1-3.9.
3. TFR map: Gambia shaded 4.0 or more at about 4.0, on the class line. After: unshaded, as the legend's rule says.
4. TFR map: Kuwait, Georgia, Ecuador, Tunisia (all under 2.1) and Malawi (about 3.6) were unshaded though far from a
   line, against the legend's "no data, or too close to a class line". After: shaded.
5. `population-distribution` item 1: "the East Asia and South Asia clusters, where more than half of the world's
   people live" (about 45%). After: "more than four in ten (UN WPP 2024)".
6. `population-distribution` item 7: "far from the capital" is not supported by the map (on the base map the South Coast
   is close to the Capital Region). After: "one of the most crowded provinces, though it is not the capital".
   Item 6: the Central Valley tied the South Coast in the second class, so the keyed description was not the only
   defensible one. After: Central Valley moved to the class below (invented data).
7. `malthusian-theory` item 13 (model): steps 1 and 2 ("population grows geometrically", "food grows
   arithmetically") are parallel premises, so either order is defensible, but only one was keyed. After: one piece
   for the two ratios, then "outruns food", "food per person falls", "checks".
8. `malthusian-theory`: "moral restraint" was added in later editions (from 1803), not the 1798 essay (notes now say
   so). The Bengal 1943 claim that food was not unusually scarce is Sen's finding and is disputed by some historians;
   item 11's stem and the lesson's misconception now attribute it to Sen.
9. China (`population-policies` hook, notes, `population-policies-s2`): "In 2021 ... began paying families to help
   with child care". The national payment began in 2025. After: 2021 three children and fines ended; 2025 payment of
   3,600 yuan a year per child under 3; table title "1980 to 2025".
10. France (`aging-populations` notes and item 12): "raised the minimum age ... from 62 to 64 in 2023". The rise was
    phased and was suspended by the 2026 social security financing law. After: "passed a law in 2023 ... in steps;
    in late 2025 parliament paused the rise". The keyed answer (a political effect) is unchanged.
11. `aging-populations` item 2: the key "The oldest provinces are rural ones far from the capital" needed facts not on
    the map (rural, distance). After: "Highest in the Northern Hills and Southern Forest; lowest in the Capital Region
    and South Coast".
12. Remittances (`migration-effects-s1`, items 1 to 3, hook, notes): brought up to the latest World Bank brief (Brief
    41, 2024 estimates). Item 2's "more than four times" became "nearly four times" (129 / 33 = 3.9).
13. Sudan (`forced-voluntary-migration` item 14): "(UNHCR, 2024)" for the largest displacement. After: about 14
    million by the end of 2024, UNHCR Global Trends (end-2024 data, published 2025).
14. `transition-model` notes: the DTM had limits but no stated assumption; added one sentence (industry and cities
    lower deaths, then families choose fewer children; migration left out). The epidemiological stages now match the
    DTM one to one (degenerative with stage 3, delayed degenerative with stage 4), as standard texts pair them.
15. `aging-populations` hook: "some villages have more residents over 70 than under 40" had no source. After: "many
    villages now have far more older residents than children".
16. Polish: `population-composition` item 3 "the many many" typo; item 14 slip text ("come first" contradicted the
    key) now says "come before it".

## What a teacher should still confirm

- Refugee definition: the course says a refugee "has been recognized as needing protection". Under the 1951
  Convention, recognition declares status rather than creating it, and UNHCR counts people in refugee-like
  situations. The course's wording matches common AP usage and the asylum seeker contrast; a teacher may want to add
  that nuance.
- Great Migration "mostly voluntary" (item 12) is keyed with a note that violence and segregation pushed hard; some
  teachers frame it differently.
- Two figure legibility requests from author B (flow maps as map renders; tap-to-enlarge figures at 360 px) are
  builder work, still open.
