# AP Psychology market and student needs (research as of 2026-10-27)

Purpose: inform a new AP Psychology course on LevlPrep (free notes plus a one-time $25 Premium pass, as with the other
AP® courses). Paraphrased. Prices come from vendor pages unless flagged as retailer or competitor-reported. Evidence is
labelled thin where it is. Framework facts and sources: `docs/appsych-research/framework.md`.

## 1. Size and timing

- **The largest audience of any LevlPrep AP course so far.** 334,038 takers in 2025 (334,960 on the College Board
  PDF); 2026 reported near 379,000 (Wikipedia only, unverified). APES, by comparison, is about 245,000.
  https://apstudents.collegeboard.org/about-ap-scores/score-distributions/ap-psychology ;
  https://apcentral.collegeboard.org/media/pdf/ap25-psychology-score-distributions.pdf ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AP_Psychology
- **Pass rate is high and rising**: 61.7% (2024, old exam), 70.5% (2025, first redesigned exam), 74% (2026). But 5s
  fell from 19.2% (2024) to 14.4% (2025) and 15% (2026), so **the 4-to-5 step is where students need help**, not
  passing. Same score page.
- **Exam: Friday 14 May 2027, Session 2** (12:00 p.m. local), Bluebook, 75 four-option MCQ in 90 min (66.7%), AAQ +
  EBQ in 70 min (33.3%), no calculator. https://apcentral.collegeboard.org/courses/ap-psychology/exam
- **Content is stable**: no 2026-27 revision; only the October 2025 clarifications (AAQ Part D wording, minor EK edits).
  Material built now should hold through at least May 2028 (no future change listed).
  https://apcentral.collegeboard.org/courses/how-ap-develops-courses-and-exams/course-changes-overview
- **Who takes it**: no prerequisites; offered to sophomores through seniors, sometimes juniors and seniors only
  (Cognito, https://cognito.org/blog/ap-psychology-guide, [third party]); about a quarter of courses are one semester
  (College Board revisions page). Many takers are non-science-track; thin evidence beyond that.
- **Old materials are a real trap**: the format changed in 2025 (100 five-option MCQ and two old-style FRQs became 75
  four-option MCQ plus AAQ/EBQ; 9 units became 5). Pre-2024 books, decks and videos teach excluded content (Maslow's
  hierarchy, psychosexual stages, named emotion theories) and the retired FRQ format. Cognito warns about this too
  (https://cognito.org/blog/ap-psychology-frq-guide).

## 2. What students struggle with

1. **The EBQ is the weaker FRQ.** 2025 means out of 7: EBQ 4.07 (set 1) and 4.91 (set 2) vs AAQ 4.97 and 5.10.
   The weakest single part in set 1 was the second reasoning part, C(ii) (0.99 of 2).
   https://apcentral.collegeboard.org/media/pdf/ap25-psychology-scoring-statistics-set-1.pdf ;
   https://apcentral.collegeboard.org/media/pdf/ap25-cr-report-psychology-set-1.pdf
2. **Reasoning that names no concept, or a concept with no link.** Readers report: explaining the evidence with no
   psychology concept (1 point max), a dropped-in definition, the same concept reused in C(ii), or a restricted
   research term (random assignment, statistical significance, mean) given as the concept. Both 2025 reports; 2026 SG.
3. **Citing and choosing evidence.** No source citation, a method instead of a result, or the same source used twice.
   Same reports.
4. **AAQ research-method basics.** Wrong or multiple methods; a conceptual definition instead of the operational one;
   restating means instead of interpreting them; sample size offered as generalizability; ethics not in the summary.
   AAQ part means in 2025 set 1 were lowest for statistic interpretation (0.66) and method (0.68). Same reports.
5. **Applying vs defining.** Scoring guidelines say a definition alone earns nothing; Cognito and Fiveable both
   name "treating it as a vocab test" as the top MCQ mistake ([third party]). About 65% of MCQs are concept
   application in a scenario ([third party], framework section 4).
6. **Volume of terms.** About 750 key terms (College Board). Most MCQs ask for the right term in a scenario, so
   discrimination between close pairs (negative reinforcement vs punishment, proactive vs retroactive interference,
   availability vs representativeness, schedules of reinforcement) matters. Evidence that these specific pairs cost
   the most is thin (teacher folklore, prep-site lists); no official MCQ item statistics exist.

## 4. Interactive tools that exist

- Fiveable: FRQ practice "with scoring guidance" and score calculators; whether the AAQ/EBQ grading is AI or
  self-check was not visible (thin).
- Cognito: rubric-structured practice; grading method not shown.
- Score calculators (Num8ers and others) with illustrative, not official, cutoffs.
- Nothing found that drills the specific AAQ parts one at a time with instant, rubric-based feedback (pick the
  method, write an operational definition, interpret a difference, pick and describe an ethical safeguard, argue
  generalizability from participant tables). The CR reports explicitly recommend partial-AAQ practice. One search
  each; thin evidence of absence.
- No interactive simulators found for neural firing, reinforcement schedule graphs, signal detection, or
  distribution/percentile reading tied to AP items.

## 5. Gaps LevlPrep can fill

1. **Part-by-part AAQ trainer** on original study summaries in the official layout (Introduction, Participants
   tables, Method, Results graph): method picker (one answer only), operational-definition writer checked against
   the study's measure, "what does this difference mean" with direction checks, ethics "identify + describe the
   application" (new 2026 wording), generalizability from participant data with a population named. Directly
   mirrors the CR reports' advice.
2. **EBQ scaffold with checks** for the exact point-losers: claim vs topic sentence, citation present, result not
   method, different source in C, different concept in C(ii), concept not on the restricted-term list, and an
   evidence-concept-claim link. The AI FRQ grader (`docs/frq-grader.md`) is the natural engine; even a rule-based
   checklist would beat what was found.
3. **Scenario MCQs that test application and close-pair discrimination**, with exclusions enforced (no Maslow
   hierarchy, no psychosexual stages, no emotion-theory names) so the course is visibly "2027-correct" against
   outdated free decks.
4. **Data-interpretation drills without a calculator**: mean/median/mode, SD meaning, percentile, significance and
   effect size wording (3.B, 3.C, and the 2025 clarification).
5. **Low-cost position**: free notes and unit tests, $25 one-time Premium against $99-169 UWorld and $79 Albert or
   Fiveable, close to a $24-28 book. Khan has no course, so the free tier fills a real hole.

Risks
- **Crowded and cheap**: 334k+ takers draw many free resources (Cognito, Fiveable free guides, Quizlet), and a 74%
  pass rate plus an "accessible" reputation may mean low willingness to pay. Lead with the 4-to-5 step and the FRQ
  trainers, not with passing.
- **Sensitive content**: Topic 3.3 (gender and sexual orientation) was the trigger for Florida's 2023 dispute with
  College Board (Wikipedia). Keep 3.3 to CED scope and neutral wording.
- **Mental-health topics**: Unit 5 disorders and treatments can be read as advice; pages need a "not medical advice"
  note and crisis resources where symptoms are discussed.
- **Copyright**: the clarifications PDF bars uploading AP Classroom material; all study summaries and items must be
  original, and released FRQs only linked, never reproduced.
- **Third-party EK data**: exclusions for 2.7-5.5 come from a non-official copy; one wrong exclusion (e.g. teaching
  something excluded, or skipping something tested) is a credibility risk. Verify against the CED PDF before launch.
