## 1. Revised for 2026-27? (answer: no; clarifications only)

- **Course changes overview**: AP Psychology row says the CED to use in 2026-27 is the Fall 2025 CED plus its
  clarifications document; most recent change "Fall 2025, clarified CED released"; no future change listed.
  Psychology is not in the 2026-27 change list (that list includes AP Statistics, APES, Physics, Calculus exam
  updates and others) or the 2027-28 list (AP Seminar, AP CSP). The page notes non-assessable updates go into the
  clarifications PDF without changing the CED cover date.
- **Background**: the big redesign was 2024-25 (first exam May 2025): nine units became five (aligned to the APA
  introductory psychology pillars), the old Scientific Foundations unit folded into the science practices, about
  750 key terms, Argumentation added as a practice, MCQ cut from 100 to 75 with **4 options instead of 5**, time
  raised to 90 min, FRQ replaced by AAQ + EBQ (revisions pages). A teacher quoted by a student paper said many
  names were removed, so the exam is less about matching names to ideas (https://www.tjtoday.org/38464/showcase/ap-psychology-curriculum-changes-starting-this-school-year/, [3P]).
- **What the clarifications PDF changes** (implemented October 2025):
  - Practice 1.A: adds a list of psychological perspectives; Practice 3.C: adds effect size and statistical
    significance.
  - EK wording: 1.3.B.1 term updated to "all-or-none principle"; 1.4.A.2 adds alertness as a reticular activating
    system function; 1.5.A.3 spelling of hypnagogic; 3.7.A.2.iii corrects the spontaneous recovery description;
    3.8.A.5.ii hyphenates the reinforcement schedule names (fixed-ratio etc.).
  - **AAQ Part D**: the source summary will describe several ethical safeguards; the student picks one, identifies
    it and describes how the researchers applied it. AAQ scoring guidelines (CED pp. 169-172) updated to match.
  - **EBQ scoring** (CED pp. 174-178): evidence must be "accurate" (with a note on what that means); the note on
    using a psychological perspective, theory, concept or research finding in reasoning is clarified.
  - Forthcoming (summer 2026): resource links moved to AP Classroom / Online Teacher Community; Progress Checks
    described as editable in the Question Bank; AP Classroom items may not be uploaded to other sites (so LevlPrep
    must never copy AP Classroom questions).
- **Spec impact**: content is stable; build the AAQ Part D trainer on the "identify + describe application" form,
  not "name a guideline".

## 3. Learning objectives, essential knowledge and exclusions per topic

Source per line: **[CED]** read in the CED extract; **[3P]** Lumisource pages that reproduce CED LO/EK codes
(https://www.lumisource.io/ap/psychology/unit4/topic4-4/practice-test and sibling URLs, pattern
`/unitN/topicN-M/practice-test`), cross-checked against Fiveable unit pages (https://fiveable.me/ap-psych-revised/unit-4.md etc.).
"Excl." = exclusion statement (paraphrased).

Unit 1 [CED; 1.4 and 1.6 LO codes 3P]
- 1.1: heredity-environment interaction, evolutionary perspective (and its misuse in eugenics), twin/family/adoption
  studies. **Excl.: genetics vocabulary (genotype, phenotype, DNA, chromosomes, dominant/recessive) out of scope.**
- 1.2: CNS vs PNS; autonomic (sympathetic, parasympathetic) vs somatic.
- 1.3: neurons and glia, reflex arc, all-or-none principle, depolarization, refractory period, resting potential,
  threshold, reuptake; neurotransmitters, hormones, psychoactive drugs (agonists, antagonists, reuptake inhibitors,
  tolerance/withdrawal); MS and myasthenia gravis. **Excl.: sodium-potassium pump; only the listed neurotransmitters
  (dopamine, serotonin, norepinephrine, glutamate, GABA, endorphins, substance P, acetylcholine) and hormones
  (adrenaline, leptin, ghrelin, melatonin, oxytocin); endocrine glands except the pituitary.**
- 1.4 (LO 1.4.A): brain stem/medulla, reticular activating system (now incl. alertness), reward center,
  cerebellum, cortex and lobes, limbic structures (thalamus, hypothalamus, pituitary, hippocampus, amygdala), corpus
  callosum, split-brain and contralateral organization, Broca's/Wernicke's areas and aphasia, plasticity, EEG,
  fMRI, case studies, lesioning.
- 1.5: consciousness, circadian rhythm, NREM/REM stages, sleep and dream theories (activation-synthesis,
  consolidation, restoration). **Excl.: psychoanalytic dream theory; only the listed sleep disorders (insomnia,
  narcolepsy, REM sleep behavior disorder, sleep apnea, somnambulism).**
- 1.6 (LOs 1.6.A-G): thresholds, JND, Weber's law, adaptation, sensory interaction, synesthesia; vision (rods,
  cones, trichromatic and opponent-process theories, afterimages, color vision deficiency, prosopagnosia,
  blindsight); hearing (place, volley, frequency theories; conduction vs sensorineural deafness); smell and taste
  (umami, oleogustus, supertasters); touch; pain (gate control, phantom limb); vestibular and kinesthesis.

Unit 2 [2.1-2.6 CED; 2.7-2.8 3P]
- 2.1 (2.1.A internal/external influences; 2.1.B visual perception): top-down/bottom-up, schemas, perceptual set,
  Gestalt (closure, figure-ground, proximity, similarity), cocktail party effect, change blindness, binocular cues
  (retinal disparity, convergence), monocular cues, constancies, apparent movement. **Excl.: only the monocular cues
  listed in EK 2.1.B.2.**
- 2.2: prototypes, schemas, assimilation/accommodation, algorithms, heuristics (representativeness, availability),
  mental set, priming, framing, gambler's fallacy, sunk-cost fallacy, executive functions, creativity, divergent
  thinking, functional fixedness.
- 2.3: explicit (episodic, semantic) vs implicit (procedural); prospective memory; long-term potentiation; working

- 2.4: mnemonics, method of loci, chunking, hierarchies, spacing effect, massed vs distributed practice, serial
  position (primacy, recency).
- 2.5: memory stores, maintenance vs elaborative rehearsal, superior autobiographical memory, retrograde and
  anterograde amnesia, Alzheimer's, infantile amnesia.
- 2.6: recall vs recognition, context-, mood- and state-dependent memory, retrieval practice, testing effect,
  metacognition.
- 2.7 (2.7.A): forgetting curve, encoding failure, proactive/retroactive interference, tip of the tongue,
  repression (psychodynamic), misinformation effect, source amnesia, constructive memory, imagination inflation.
- 2.8 (2.8.A theories, B measurement, C systemic issues, D achievement): g vs multiple abilities, IQ ratio,
  standardization, validity (construct, predictive), reliability (test-retest, split-half), stereotype threat and

Unit 3 [all 3P]
- 3.1 (3.1.A themes; 3.1.B designs): stability vs change, nature vs nurture, continuous vs discontinuous;

- 3.2 (A prenatal, B infancy/childhood, C adolescence, D adulthood): teratogens, motor milestones, rooting

- 3.5 (A components, B development): phonemes, morphemes, semantics, grammar, syntax, generativity; cooing,

- 3.6 (3.6.A): ecological systems theory, parenting styles (authoritarian, authoritative, permissive), attachment

### Named researchers: required vs excluded

- **The CED EK names almost no people.** In the read part (1.1-2.6) no researcher is named in EK; Loftus and Joshua
  Foer appear only in sample classroom activities. [3P] EK pages name Piaget and Vygotsky (3.4), Lewin (4.6) and
  nobody else; Erikson's stages, Ainsworth-type attachment styles, Harlow-type monkey studies, Baumrind-type parenting
  styles, Pavlov, Skinner, Bandura, Tolman, Kohler, Rogers, Selye, Beck and Ellis appear as **concepts without names**.
  So MCQs test the idea, not the name; teach names as memory hooks only.
- **Excluded (exclusion statements)**: Freud's psychosexual stages (3.6 and 4.4), Maslow's hierarchy (4.4), names of
  emotion theories (4.7), psychoanalytic dream theory (1.5), sodium-potassium pump, unlisted neurotransmitters/
  hormones/glands, genetics vocabulary, prenatal stage names, pragmatics, timing variants of classical conditioning
  and expectancy theory, unlisted sleep disorders, unlisted monocular cues, unlisted psychological disorders.
- **Absent (not excluded, just not in EK, [3P])**: Kohlberg's moral stages, Zimbardo, Sherif, Darley and Latane by
  name, Ekman by name, Gardner/Sternberg by name (2.8 only asks g vs several abilities), Binet/Wechsler/Terman.
  Low priority; do not build items on them.
- **EBQ restricted terms**: scoring guidelines list terms that cannot earn the application point because they are
  research or generic terms, not psychology concepts. 2025 set 1: confederate, statistically significant, operant
  conditioning (that prompt's own topic). 2026: distractor, mean, random assignment, rehearsal, retrieval, standard
  deviation, statistical significance. Pattern: methods/statistics vocabulary and the prompt's own topic word do
  not count as the concept.

Four practices (Course at a Glance, CED). Note a naming mismatch: the Course at a Glance prints Practice 1 as
"Concept Understanding"; the CED body and revisions page use "Concept Application". Use "Concept Application".

## 5. Exam format, May 2027

- **Date**: Friday 14 May 2027, Session 2 (exam page, student page). Session 2 starts 12:00 p.m. local time in most
  US schools (start-times page).
- **Mode**: fully digital in Bluebook, both sections, auto-submitted. 2 h 40 min total. **No calculator.**
- **Section I MCQ**: 75 questions, 90 min, 66.7% of score; **4 options (A-D)** (revisions exam page, official); more
  graphs, charts, tables and figures than before; standalone items plus sets sharing one study description or data
  display ([3P] Fiveable/Achievable). No guessing penalty. Assesses Practices 1-3.
- **Section II FRQ**: 2 questions, 70 min, 33.3% of score, 7 points each (14 raw).
  - **Q1 Article Analysis Question (AAQ)**, suggested 25 min including 10 min reading. One summarized
    peer-reviewed study. 2026 layout: introduction, Participants (with demographic tables), Method, Results and
    Discussion (with a graph of group means), plus footnotes and a reference line (2026 FRQ).
    - A Research method (1): name the one method (e.g. experiment).
    - B Research variable (1): give the measurable operational definition as used in the study.
    - C Statistic interpretation (1): explain what a reported statistic or difference means for these groups.
    - D Ethical guideline (1): from guidelines the summary describes, identify one and describe how the
      researchers applied it (new wording, clarifications PDF; 2026 SG options were IRB review, informed consent,
      debriefing).
    - E Generalizability (1): claim whether results generalize to a named population, supported by specific
      participant characteristics; "partly" needs both sides.
    - F Argumentation (2): use a specific, accurately read result to explain whether the study supports or refutes
      the named concept/hypothesis.
  - **Q2 Evidence-Based Question (EBQ)**, suggested 45 min including 15 min reading. Three summarized peer-reviewed
    sources on one topic, labeled Source 1, 2, 3, each with the same headings plus a graph or table.
    - A Claim (1): a defensible claim that answers the prompt (not a topic sentence or question).
    - B(i) Evidence (1): one specific, accurate result from one source, **cited** ("(Source 1)" or "According to
      Source 1"; any clear style accepted). Exact numbers not required if the direction is right.
    - B(ii) Reasoning (2): 1 point for explaining how the evidence supports the claim; 2 points for also applying a
      psychological perspective, theory, concept or research finding (not a restricted term, not a bare definition).
    - C(i) Evidence (1): different result from a **different source**.
    - C(ii) Reasoning (2): as B(ii) but with a **different concept** from B(ii); reusing it caps at 1.
- **General scoring rules** (2025 and 2026 SG): definitions alone earn nothing; responses must apply to the
  source's context; wrong information costs a point only if it contradicts an otherwise correct answer; evidence
  must be consistent with the claim; uncited evidence loses the evidence point but reasoning can still score.

Recurring errors (both 2025 sets unless noted):

AAQ
- **Method (A)**: naming the wrong method (correlation, survey, naturalistic observation, meta-analysis), listing
  several methods, or describing the topic; mentioning random assignment or a questionnaire without naming the
  method. 2026 SG also rejects hybrids like "experimental correlation".
- **Variable (B)**: describing the concept in general (e.g. what the misinformation effect is) instead of how the
  study measured it; vague feelings; naming "a Likert scale" without the score; incomplete list of coded behaviors
  (set 2).
- **Statistic (C)**: restating the means or difference without saying what it shows; defining "mean" or
  "statistically significant" in general; reversing the direction; comparing the wrong pair of groups (set 2);
  saying "not significant" without explaining it means likely due to chance (set 2).
- **Ethics (D)**: naming guidelines not in the summary (confidentiality, debriefing, IRB when absent) or mixing in
  non-ethical design features (random assignment). From 2026 must also describe how it was applied.
- **Generalizability (E)**: using sample size, design flaws, reliability or validity instead of participant
  characteristics; no named population; "partly" with only one side argued.
- **Argumentation (F)**: evidence without explanation, explanation without a study result, misreading results, or
  claiming the result proves the idea.

EBQ
- **Claim (A)**: topic description, a question, off-topic or oversimplified statement, or a bare source summary.
- **Evidence (B(i), C(i))**: **no citation**; citing a method or participant detail instead of a result; vague or
  inaccurate finding; **reusing the B source in C**.
- **Reasoning (B(ii), C(ii))**: explaining the evidence without a psychology concept (1/2 max); dropping in a
  definition without integrating it; naming a concept without linking it to the evidence and claim; generic
  "helpful vs harmful" talk; **reusing the B(ii) concept in C(ii)**; using a restricted term (research/statistics
  words or the prompt's own topic word) as the concept.

Reader advice that maps to trainer design: match the task verb; mine the summary (Participants and Method
sections hold most A, B, D, E answers); read all three EBQ sources and note results and conflicts before writing;
use an evidence-then-concept-then-link structure for every reasoning part.
