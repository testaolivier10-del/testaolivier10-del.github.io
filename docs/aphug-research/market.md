# AP Human Geography market and student needs (research as of 2026-11-09)

Purpose: inform a new AP Human Geography course on LevlPrep (free notes plus a one-time $25 Premium pass, as with the
other AP® courses). Paraphrased. Prices come from vendor pages unless flagged as retailer. Evidence is labelled thin
where it is. Framework facts and sources: `docs/aphug-research/framework.md`.

## 1. Size, timing, who takes it

- **Large audience**: 282,781 US takers in 2025 (283,512 global), up from 262,253 (2024) and 247,043 (2023); 2026
  count not posted (Wikipedia gives about 300,000, unverified). Ninth-largest AP exam in 2025 by volume, behind
  Psychology (334,038) and ahead of Macroeconomics (176,356).
  https://apstudents.collegeboard.org/about-ap-scores/score-distributions/ap-human-geography ;
  https://apcentral.collegeboard.org/media/pdf/ap25-human-geography-score-distributions.pdf ;
  https://apcentral.collegeboard.org/media/pdf/program-summary-report-2025.pdf ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AP_Human_Geography
- **Mostly 9th graders, often their first AP.** The 2025 program summary row for Human Geography reads (as extracted)
  7,266 / 201,751 / 43,583 / 18,564 / 18,139 / 54 across its grade columns. The extractor labelled these "9th, 10th,
  11th, 12th, not in HS, under 9th", but the column sum exceeds the total and 201,751 tenth graders is implausible; the
  likely reading is under-9th 7,266, **9th grade about 201,751 (about 71%)**, 10th 43,583, 11th 18,564, 12th 18,139,
  not in high school 54. Treat the exact split as unconfirmed until someone opens the PDF. Cognito calls APHG a first
  AP for freshmen and sophomores (https://cognito.org/blog/is-ap-human-geography-hard, [3P]); Wikipedia says the
  course is usually taken by freshmen ([3P]).
  Spec impact: write for 14-year-olds: short sentences, worked examples of every task verb, no assumed world-map
  knowledge, and teach "how an AP FRQ works" from zero.
- **Pass rate jumped in the digital era**: 54.4% (2023), 56.1% (2024), 64.7% (2025), 66% (2026). 1s fell from about 30%
  to 10%, but 2s rose to about 25%, so **a quarter of students now land one step below passing**. Same score page.
- **Exam: Monday 3 May 2027, Session 1** (8:00 a.m. local), Bluebook, 60 MCQ in 60 min (50%), 3 FRQs in 75 min (50%,
  7 points each), first exam week. https://apcentral.collegeboard.org/courses/ap-human-geography/exam
- **Content is stable**: CED effective Fall 2020, no change for 2026-27 and none announced; June 2026 clarifications are
  non-content. https://apcentral.collegeboard.org/courses/how-ap-develops-courses-and-exams/course-changes-overview

## 2. What students struggle with (official reports)

1. **FRQs are where points go missing.** Question means 2023-2025 ran 2.12 to 4.44 of 7; the no-stimulus Q1 was the
   lowest question in four of six sets. Single parts as low as 6% (comparative advantage) and 18% (redlining) in 2025
   set 2. https://apcentral.collegeboard.org/media/pdf/ap25-cr-report-human-geography-set-2.pdf
2. **Task verbs.** Naming when asked to describe, describing when asked to explain, one side when asked to compare,
   no degree when asked "to what degree". Every CR report 2023-2025 repeats this (framework section 7).
3. **Scale of analysis.** Only 38% could identify the scale of a subnational map (2025 set 2 Q3A); wrong-scale answers
   cost points in several sets. https://apcentral.collegeboard.org/media/pdf/ap25-cr-report-human-geography-set-2.pdf
4. **Vague spatial description and no example.** "Clustered" with no place earns nothing (2024 set 1, 2025 set 1).
   https://apcentral.collegeboard.org/media/pdf/ap24-cr-report-human-geography-set-1.pdf
5. **Model knowledge.** Galactic city model unknown or confused (2023 set 1 Q3D); Rostow mixed with DTM (2023 set 2
   Q2G); Malthus weakness not given (2024 set 1 Q1E).
   https://apcentral.collegeboard.org/media/pdf/ap23-cr-report-human-geography-set-1.pdf
6. **Reading stimuli.** Legends, choropleth maps, comparing two maps, citing table values, saying what a source cannot
   show (2025 set 1 advice). https://apcentral.collegeboard.org/media/pdf/ap25-cr-report-human-geography-set-1.pdf
7. **Vocabulary load.** Cognito estimates 300 to 400 terms and says vague references to places score lower
   (https://cognito.org/blog/is-ap-human-geography-hard, [3P]). Thin evidence on which terms cost the most beyond the CR
   lists.

## 4. Interactive tools that exist

- **AI FRQ scoring** now exists at UWorld (paid, $99+) and Fiveable claims part-by-part rubric scoring; how specific the
  per-point feedback is was not visible. Thin.
- Score calculators (Albert and others).
- Flashcards and mock exams everywhere (Knowt, Quizlet, Fiveable).
- **Not found** in one search each (thin evidence of absence): a tool where students build or label a model (drag
  zones onto a Burgess, Hoyt or galactic diagram, place von Thünen rings, sort DTM stages from birth and death rate
  curves) and get graded; a scale-of-analysis trainer that shows one dataset at several scales and asks which scale
  a source uses; a map-reading drill on legends, choropleth classes and "what this map cannot show"; an FRQ trainer
  that checks the task verb (identify vs describe vs explain vs compare vs degree) part by part.

## 5. Gaps LevlPrep can fill

1. **Task-verb FRQ trainer with per-point feedback.** Seven 1-point parts per question make rule checks practical:
   explain parts need a cause-effect link (because, so, therefore); compare parts need both sides named; degree parts
   need high, moderate or low stated first; describe parts need a located pattern, not "clustered". The AI FRQ grader
   (`docs/frq-grader.md`) fits here; even a checklist beats what was found free. Mirrors every CR report.
2. **Graded model builders.** Drag-and-drop zones for Burgess, Hoyt, Harris-Ullman, galactic and the three regional
   city models; von Thünen rings with a transport-cost slider; bid-rent curves; DTM and epidemiological stage sorter;
   gravity model and rank-size calculators with real city data (dated). Then a "where does this model fail" prompt
   for skill 1.E.
3. **Scale-of-analysis practice.** Same indicator mapped at global, national and local scales; ask which scale the
   data use (the 38% item), what pattern appears only at one scale, and what a regional source cannot prove about a
   nation.
4. **Map and data reading drills.** Legends, choropleth classes, pyramids, two-map comparison, table citation, and
   "what can't this show". Five MCQ options or four: confirm first (framework section 10).
5. **No-stimulus Q1 practice.** The lowest FRQ in most sets. Prompt students to supply their own named example for
   every concept (an example bank by unit, dated and sourced, see accuracy-risks.md).
6. **Freshman-first design.** Most takers are about 14 and new to AP: a "how FRQs are scored" primer, short pages,
   world-region map warm-ups (no prior map knowledge assumed).
7. **Price position**: free notes and unit tests, $25 one-time Premium vs UWorld $99-169, Fiveable or Albert $79,
   Knowt Ultra $96/yr, close to a $23-28 book. No Khan course, so the free tier fills a real hole.

Risks
- **Crowded and cheap**: high volume brings many free resources and a 66% pass rate; parents of 9th graders may not
  pay. Lead with the FRQ trainer and the "2 to 3" step (a quarter of takers score 2).
- **Sensitive content**: borders, ethnic conflict, religion, migration, gender roles and population policy are
  politically live; neutrality and dated sources matter more here than in any other LevlPrep course
  (see `docs/aphug-research/accuracy-risks.md`).
- **Stale facts**: country data change every year and the CIA World Factbook was discontinued in February 2026, so
  many free study sites now cite a dead source (accuracy-risks.md section 1).
- **Copyright**: AP Classroom items cannot be reposted; all stimuli (maps, tables) must be original or built from
  openly licensed data, and released FRQs only linked.
- **CED reading gap**: Units 4.9 to 7 EK were read through a model paraphrase of the CED; verify before launch.
