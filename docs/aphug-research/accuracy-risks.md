# AP Human Geography accuracy and neutrality risks (as of 2026-11-09)

Purpose: tell authors which facts go stale or are contested, and give one rule of thumb for each so pages stay
accurate and neutral. Applies to notes, MCQ stems, stimuli, FRQ prompts and video scripts. The exam itself tests
concepts, not current statistics; real data in our items are there to practice reading, so **a dated, cited source
matters more than the newest number**.

## 0. General rules (apply everywhere)

1. **Every real figure carries a source and a year**: "(UN WPP 2024, 2024 estimate)", "(World Bank, FY27
   classification)". A figure without a year is a bug.
2. **Prefer these sources, in this order**: UN agencies (UN DESA World Population Prospects, UNHCR, UNDP), World Bank,
   PRB World Population Data Sheet, national statistics offices, Pew (religion), Ethnologue (languages). Do not cite
   the CIA World Factbook as a live source (section 1).
3. **Round and say "about"** for anything that moves yearly (population, GDP, shares). Never write "currently" or
   "today" without a year.
4. **Use hypothetical places** ("Country A") when the skill is reading a pattern, as College Board itself did in 2025
   set 2 Q2 (https://apcentral.collegeboard.org/media/pdf/ap25-cr-report-human-geography-set-2.pdf). Use real places
   when the point is a real example students can cite on Q1.
5. **Contested things get attribution, not a verdict**: "X is claimed by A and B", "the UN describes...", "A calls it
   P, B calls it Q". No item may hinge on a disputed fact.
6. **Log every dated figure** in a per-course data table (path to be set in the spec) with value, source URL, year
   and date checked, so one yearly pass can refresh them.

## 2. Population figures

- **World population**: UN WPP 2024 gives 8.2 billion in 2024 and a projected peak near 10.3 billion in the mid-2080s.
  https://population.un.org/wpp/assets/Files/WPP2024_Key-Messages.pdf . Rule: say "about 8.2 billion (UN, 2024)";
  never give a single "current" number.
- **Largest countries**: India passed China as the most populous country in 2023 (UN estimate) [ours, from WPP; not
  re-read this pass, verify in WPP 2024 before use]. Older materials still say China; check any ranking against WPP.
- **Fertility and replacement**: global TFR and the count of countries below replacement change every edition. Rule:
  quote from PRB 2026 or WPP with year; teach replacement as about 2.1 in low-mortality settings, higher where child
  mortality is high [ours].
- **Japan, South Korea, Italy etc. as aging examples**: safe as patterns, but exact TFR and % 65+ need a dated cite.
  (2025 set 1 Q2 used a 2021 Japanese pyramid: https://apcentral.collegeboard.org/media/pdf/ap25-cr-report-human-geography-set-1.pdf .)
- **China's population policy**: the one-child policy (1980-2015) was replaced by two-child (2016) and three-child
  (2021) limits and later pronatalist incentives [ours, not verified this pass]. Rule: always date each stage; never
  describe China as still antinatalist without a year.
- **Refugees and displaced people**: UNHCR totals change twice a year. Rule: cite UNHCR Global Trends with year;
  use "refugee", "asylum seeker", "internally displaced person" exactly as the CED defines (2.11), and never
  "illegal" for people; prefer "unauthorized" or "undocumented" with attribution when discussing US policy [ours].
- **City populations (megacity, metacity, primate city)**: depend on the definition used (city proper, urban
  agglomeration, metro area). Rule: name the definition and source (UN World Urbanization Prospects or national
  census) and year; the CED's megacity and metacity thresholds are not stated in the EK read, and 2024 set 1 Q3C
  docked an "incorrect population threshold" (https://apcentral.collegeboard.org/media/pdf/ap24-cr-report-human-geography-set-1.pdf),
  so teach the common thresholds (10 million megacity, 20 million metacity) as convention with a source [ours, verify].

## 3. Country classifications and development terms

- **Core, semiperiphery, periphery** are Wallerstein's model categories (CED 7.5), not an official list. Rule: say
  "often classed as semiperiphery in world-system terms" and never present a country list as fact.
- **Developed / developing / LDC / MDC**: CR reports use MDC and LDC (e.g. 2023 set 1 Q1E,
  https://apcentral.collegeboard.org/media/pdf/ap23-cr-report-human-geography-set-1.pdf), and the UN keeps a formal
  "Least Developed Countries" list that is different from "less developed" in AP usage [ours; UN LDC list not read this
  pass]. Rule: use "more developed / less developed" as AP does, and if you mean the UN LDC category, say so and cite
  the UN list with year. Avoid "Third World" except in a historical sentence.
- **Newly industrialized countries, BRICS, Global South**: membership lists change (BRICS expanded after 2023) [ours].
  Rule: avoid membership lists in items unless dated and sourced.
- **Supranational bodies** (EU, NATO, ASEAN, African Union, Arctic Council, Mercosur, OPEC, WTO, all named in CED 4.9
  and 7.6): membership changes (e.g. UK left the EU in 2020; Finland and Sweden joined NATO in 2023 and 2024) [ours,
  verify]. Rule: write "as of [year], N members (source)", and test functions, not membership counts.
- **GDP vs GNP vs GNI, nominal vs PPP**: different numbers for the same country. Rule: name the measure and year; the
  World Bank income groups use Atlas GNI per capita (section 1).
- **Gender Inequality Index, HDI, SDG scores** are composites revised with each report, and back-years get
  recalculated. Rule: compare countries only within one edition.

## 4. Disputed borders, territories and names

Rule of thumb for all: **describe who controls, who claims, and who recognizes, with a date; do not state sovereignty
as settled; use the name the UN uses and mention the other name with its users.** Never make an item's answer depend
on a disputed status. Use contested cases only to teach concepts the CED names (boundary disputes 4.5, irredentism
4.8, devolution 4.9, stateless nations 4.1, shatterbelts and choke points 4.3).

| Case | Neutral pattern (our wording) | Concept it serves |
|---|---|---|
| Kashmir | administered in parts by India, Pakistan and China; claimed in whole or part by India and Pakistan | boundary dispute, superimposed boundary legacy |
| Taiwan | governed by the authorities in Taipei; the People's Republic of China claims it; most states do not formally recognize it as a separate state; not a UN member | sovereignty, recognition |
| Israel and Palestine (West Bank, Gaza, East Jerusalem) | Palestine is a UN non-member observer state and is recognized by many but not all states; borders and control are disputed and changed during recent conflict; date any description | self-determination, stateless nation, boundary dispute |
| Crimea and other Russian-occupied areas of Ukraine | annexed or claimed by Russia; the UN General Assembly and most states treat them as Ukrainian; describe control as of a date | irredentism, sovereignty |
| Western Sahara | claimed and largely administered by Morocco; the Polisario Front seeks independence; on the UN list of non-self-governing territories [ours, verify] | decolonization, self-determination |
| Kosovo | declared independence from Serbia in 2008; recognized by many but not all states; not a UN member | devolution, recognition |
| South China Sea (Spratly, Paracel) | overlapping claims by China, Viet Nam, the Philippines, Malaysia, Brunei and Taiwan; a 2016 arbitral ruling went against China's broad claim, which China rejects [ours, verify] | UNCLOS, EEZs, choke points |
| Falkland Islands / Islas Malvinas | British Overseas Territory claimed by Argentina; give both names | maritime boundary, relict colonial claim |
| Cyprus, Northern Cyprus | the north is recognized only by Türkiye | partition, devolution |
| Kurdistan | Kurds as a large stateless nation across Türkiye, Iraq, Iran and Syria; Iraq's Kurdistan Region is autonomous | stateless nation, multistate nation |
| Sudan and South Sudan, the former Soviet Union | named in CED 4.9 as state breakups; South Sudan became independent in 2011 [ours] | devolution |
| Gulf of Mexico / Gulf of America | US federal name since 2025 is Gulf of America (EO 14172, GNIS); Mexico and most international bodies use Gulf of Mexico (sources in section 1) | toponyms, political power over naming |
| Denali / Mount McKinley | US federal name reverted to Mount McKinley in 2025; the national park remains Denali; Denali is the Koyukon Athabascan name | toponyms, Indigenous naming |
| Persian Gulf / Arabian Gulf; Sea of Japan / East Sea | name used varies by state; give both with users | toponyms |

Naming conventions (use the official short names and note the old one once): Türkiye (Turkey), Czechia (Czech
Republic), Eswatini (Swaziland), North Macedonia (2019), Myanmar (Burma; usage varies), Kyiv (not Kiev), Côte d'Ivoire,
Timor-Leste, Cabo Verde, Viet Nam (UN and World Bank spelling; "Vietnam" is fine in prose) [ours; the World Bank and
UNDP tables above use Türkiye, Viet Nam, Eswatini, Czechia, confirming the current forms:
https://hdr.undp.org/data-center/country-insights].

Number of states: the UN has 193 members and two non-member observer states (Holy See, State of Palestine) [ours; not
re-read this pass; https://www.un.org/en/about-us/member-states returned no count]. Rule: say "193 UN member states"
rather than "195 countries in the world", and explain that counts differ by who recognizes whom.

## 5. Ethnic, religious and Indigenous group terms

- **Use the group's own preferred name**, capitalized: Indigenous, Inuit (not Eskimo), Roma (not the slur), Sámi,
  Māori, First Nations, Native American or American Indian (both used in the US; the CED uses "Native American
  reservations" in 4.1) [ours; CED wording from https://apcentral.collegeboard.org/media/pdf/ap-human-geography-course-and-exam-description.pdf].
- **Hispanic vs Latino vs Latine**: follow the source's term when quoting data (the US Census uses "Hispanic or
  Latino"); otherwise "Latino" [ours].
- **Ethnicity vs race vs nationality**: the CED treats ethnicity, language and religion as cultural traits; do not
  describe ethnic groups as biological categories. Avoid "tribe" for African ethnic groups unless the group uses it
  [ours].
- **Persecuted groups (Rohingya, Uyghurs, Yazidis, etc.)**: report events with attribution to UN bodies, courts or
  governments ("the UN fact-finding mission described...", "the US government has called it genocide; China
  rejects this"). Use "ethnic cleansing" (a CED term in 4.8) only for cases where a named authoritative body used it,
  and attribute it [ours].
- **Religion figures**: Pew's 2025 report (2020 data): Christians about 2.3 billion (28.8% of world population),
  Muslims 25.6%, religiously unaffiliated 24.2%, Hindus 14.9%, Buddhists 4.1%, Jews under 1%; Muslims grew fastest
  2010-2020. https://www.pewresearch.org/religion/2025/06/09/how-the-global-religious-landscape-changed-from-2010-to-2020/ .
  Rule: cite "Pew 2025, 2020 data"; "Christianity is the largest religion" is safe; "Islam is the fastest-growing" is
  safe with that date.
- **Religion classification**: the CED classes Christianity, Islam, Buddhism and Sikhism as universalizing and
  Hinduism and Judaism as ethnic (3.7). Teach this as the AP classification; note that adherents may not use these
  labels, and that Hinduism and Judaism do accept converts in some forms [ours]. Describe beliefs only as "adherents
  believe"; never rank religions; use Sunni and Shia (or Shi'a) consistently; use "The Church of Jesus Christ of
  Latter-day Saints" on first mention [ours].
- **Languages**: Ethnologue's 29th edition lists 7,170 living languages
  (https://www.ethnologue.com/ethnoblog/welcome-29th-edition/). Rule: cite edition; speaker counts differ between first
  language and total speakers, so say which. Language families: CED names Indo-European (3.7); classification of some
  families (e.g. Altaic) is disputed [ours], so avoid items on contested families.

## 6. Development indices and model claims

- **HDI, GII, GNI ranks** go stale yearly and are revised backwards: compare within one edition, cite it (section 1).
- **Rostow, Wallerstein, DTM, Malthus, von Thünen, Burgess etc.** are models, not facts. Pages must state assumptions
  and limits (skill 1.E) and never say a country "is in stage 3" as a fact; say "data are consistent with stage 3 of
  the DTM (birth rate X, death rate Y, source, year)". The 2024 set 2 Q3 placed negative-RNI countries in stage 4 or 5
  (https://apcentral.collegeboard.org/media/pdf/ap24-cr-report-human-geography-set-2.pdf).
- **Green Revolution dates**: a CR report dates it roughly 1961 to 1989 and puts commercial biotech crops in the late
  1990s (https://apcentral.collegeboard.org/media/pdf/ap24-cr-report-human-geography-set-1.pdf). Never list GMOs as a
  Green Revolution technology.
- **Redlining**: define it as refusing services or offering worse terms based on neighborhood location (CR 2025 set 2);
  its US history was tied to race, so explain both the mechanism and the history without conflating them.
- **Gentrification, urban renewal, population policies, immigration**: CED says these have positive and negative
  consequences (6.10, 2.7). Every page gives both, attributed, with no editorial verdict.

## 7. Review checklist for authors (copy into the authoring guide)

- [ ] Every real number has a source and year; no CIA Factbook citations.
- [ ] Income group says "World Bank FY27"; HDI says the data year and report year.
- [ ] Disputed places use the control/claim/recognition pattern with a date; both names given where names differ.
- [ ] No item's correct answer depends on a contested fact or a membership count.
- [ ] Group names are the groups' own, capitalized; serious accusations are attributed.
- [ ] Models are labelled as models, with at least one limit.
- [ ] Hypothetical places are used where only the pattern matters.
