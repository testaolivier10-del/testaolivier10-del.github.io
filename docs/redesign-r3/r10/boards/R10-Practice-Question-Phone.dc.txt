<p class="qstem">An alert, oriented adult patient with decision-making capacity refuses transport against your medical advice after a thorough assessment and explanation of the risks. What is the appropriate action?</p>
<div class="opts" role="list"><div class="opt bad"><span class="k">A</span><span>Call law enforcement to force the patient into the ambulance regardless of his stated wishes</span><span class="mark bad">Your answer</span><span class="why">Involving law enforcement to force transport would generally be an unlawful restriction of his rights, absent a specific legal basis such as a lack of capacity.</span></div><div class="opt ok"><span class="k">B</span><span>Respect the refusal, document the risk discussion clearly, and offer to return</span><span class="mark ok">Correct</span><span class="why">A patient with intact decision-making capacity has the right to refuse care and transport once he has been informed of the risks.</span></div><div class="opt "><span class="k">C</span><span>Transport the patient against his will, any refusal after an EMS call being overridden for the patient’s safety</span><span></span><span class="why">Transporting him against his will would generally be an unlawful restriction of his rights; a refusal from a patient with capacity is not overridden.</span></div><div class="opt "><span class="k">D</span><span>Ignore the refusal and simply leave without any further discussion or documentation</span><span></span><span class="why">Walking away without proper documentation leaves both the patient and the crew without an adequate record of what was discussed and decided.</span></div></div>
<div class="verdict"><b>Not quite.</b><span>It goes to your review queue.</span><p class="hand">every option, explained</p></div>
<div class="explain"><b>Answer: B) Respect the refusal, document the risk discussion clearly, and offer to return</b>A patient with intact decision-making capacity has the legal and ethical right to refuse care and transport, even against medical advice, once he’s been informed of the risks. The EMT’s role is to respect that decision, thoroughly document the assessment, the risks explained, and the refusal itself, and let him know he can call back if he changes his mind. Transporting him against his will, or involving law enforcement to force transport, would generally constitute an unlawful restriction of his rights absent a specific legal basis (such as a lack of capacity or an imminent danger under applicable law), and simply walking away without proper documentation leaves both the patient and the crew without an adequate record of what was discussed and decided.</div>
