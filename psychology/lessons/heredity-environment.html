Unit 1 · Topic 1.1

Interaction of Heredity and Environment

Behavior and mental processes come from heredity and environment working together.

Practice 1: Concept ApplicationPractice 2: Research Methods and Design

Part 1 · Hook

Why this matters

Identical twins Jim Lewis and Jim Springer were adopted by different families as babies and did not meet until they were 39. Reports of their reunion listed striking matches: similar habits, hobbies and even pets’ names. Some of those matches were surely coincidence, and twins raised apart differ in plenty of ways too. But large studies of twins and adopted children, not single stories, are how psychology measures what heredity and environment each contribute.

Part 5 · Step by step

How it works, step by step

  1. People inherit tendencies from their biological parents (heredity)→relatives who share more genes tend to be more alike on many traits
  2. The environment, from nutrition to schooling to culture, shapes how those tendencies develop→the same inheritance can lead to different outcomes in different environments
  3. Twin, family and adoption studies compare relatives who share different amounts of genes and environment→researchers can estimate heritability: how much of the variation in a group is linked to genetic differences

Part 6 · Key ideas

Key ideas

  • Nature and nurture interact: heredity sets tendencies, and the environment shapes how they develop. Behavior comes from both, never one alone.
  • Heritability is the share of variation in a trait, within one group and environment, linked to genetic differences. It says nothing about one person, or about differences between groups.
  • Twin studies compare identical twins (all genes shared) with fraternal twins (about half); adoption studies compare a child with biological and adoptive parents; family studies track a trait across relatives.
  • Evolutionary psychology explains shared human traits by natural selection. Its misuse to rank people led to eugenics and forced sterilization.

Part 7 · Misconception

A common mistake

The wrong idea: A heritability of 0.60 means 60% of a person’s trait comes from genes, and a heritable trait cannot be changed.

What actually happens: Heritability describes differences among people in a group, not the makeup of one person. An inherited condition such as PKU can be transformed by the environment.

Part 8 · Check yourself

Check yourself

Exam-style questions. Anything you miss goes into your review queue.

1. Two brothers inherit a tendency to grow tall. One grows up with plenty of food; the other, raised during a long famine, ends up much shorter. Which idea does this best illustrate?

  1. Heredity and environment interact to shape a trait
  2. Height is set by heredity, so the famine had little effect
  3. Height is set by environment, so inheritance plays no part
  4. The brothers are probably fraternal twins with different inheritance
Show the answer

Inherited potential is expressed through the environment. Nature and nurture work together rather than one alone.

  • Correct: Heredity and environment interact to shape a trait: Right: the same inherited potential led to different heights in different environments.
  • Height is set by heredity, so the famine had little effect: The famine clearly changed the outcome, so heredity alone does not set height.
  • Height is set by environment, so inheritance plays no part: Both brothers inherited a tendency to be tall; inheritance still matters.
  • The brothers are probably fraternal twins with different inheritance: The stem says both inherited the same tendency; the environment differed.

2. Researchers find that a trait has a heritability of 0.60 in a large group of adults. What does this mean?

  1. In each adult, 60% of the trait comes from genes and 40% from environment
  2. About 60% of the differences among these adults are linked to genes
  3. The environment has little power to change the trait in these adults
  4. 60% of the adults inherited the trait from a parent
Show the answer

Heritability is the share of the variation among people in a particular group and environment that is linked to genetic differences. It says nothing about how much of one person’s trait comes from genes.

  • In each adult, 60% of the trait comes from genes and 40% from environment: Heritability is not about the makeup of any one person’s trait.
  • Correct: About 60% of the differences among these adults are linked to genes: Right: heritability describes variation within a group.
  • The environment has little power to change the trait in these adults: A heritable trait can still change a great deal with the environment.
  • 60% of the adults inherited the trait from a parent: Heritability is not the share of people who have a trait.

3. Which comparison in the table gives the clearest evidence that heredity matters for reading scores?

  1. Adoptive siblings (+0.25) compared with fraternal twins (+0.55)
  2. Identical twins raised together (+0.82) compared with raised apart (+0.72)
  3. Adoptive siblings (+0.25) compared with identical twins apart (+0.72)
  4. Identical (+0.82) versus fraternal twins (+0.55), both raised together
Show the answer

In twin studies, both kinds of twins grow up together. Identical twins share all their genes, fraternal twins about half; when identical twins are more alike, genes are contributing.

  • Adoptive siblings (+0.25) compared with fraternal twins (+0.55): Fraternal twins share both genes and a womb and are the same age, so this mixes heredity and environment.
  • Identical twins raised together (+0.82) compared with raised apart (+0.72): Here genes are the same and the home differs, so this comparison is about environment.
  • Adoptive siblings (+0.25) compared with identical twins apart (+0.72): This changes both genes and home at once, so neither can be singled out.
  • Correct: Identical (+0.82) versus fraternal twins (+0.55), both raised together: Right: the home is shared in both, so the extra likeness tracks the extra genes.

4. Adoptive siblings, who share a home but no genes, correlate at +0.25. What does this suggest?

  1. The shared home has some influence on reading scores
  2. Reading scores are inherited, with no role for the home
  3. Adoptive siblings share half their genes
  4. The home environment lowers children’s reading scores
Show the answer

Because adoptive siblings share no genes, their similarity points to the shared environment: the same home, books and schools.

  • Correct: The shared home has some influence on reading scores: Right: genetically unrelated children raised together are still somewhat alike.
  • Reading scores are inherited, with no role for the home: If they were, unrelated siblings would show about zero correlation.
  • Adoptive siblings share half their genes: Adoptive siblings are not biologically related.
  • The home environment lowers children’s reading scores: A positive correlation means the siblings are alike, not that their scores are low.

5. Identical twins raised apart still correlate at +0.72. Which caution applies to this finding?

  1. A correlation of +0.72 shows that reading is caused by genes in each twin
  2. Twins raised apart often had similar homes, so some likeness is environmental
  3. Twins raised apart do not share any genes, so the result is surprising
  4. The correlation is negative, so separated twins read very differently
Show the answer

Separated twins shared a womb, are the same age and are often placed with similar families. These shared environments can inflate their similarity, which is why researchers combine several designs.

  • A correlation of +0.72 shows that reading is caused by genes in each twin: A correlation shows a relationship, not that each individual’s reading is caused by genes.
  • Correct: Twins raised apart often had similar homes, so some likeness is environmental: Right: separated twins are often placed in similar families and communities.
  • Twins raised apart do not share any genes, so the result is surprising: Identical twins share all their genes, wherever they are raised.
  • The correlation is negative, so separated twins read very differently: The correlation is +0.72, positive and strong.

Study

Phenylketonuria (PKU): an inherited condition shaped by diet

Introduction

Phenylketonuria (PKU) is an inherited condition in which the body cannot break down phenylalanine, a building block of protein found in many foods. Untreated, it builds up and damages the developing brain, causing severe intellectual disability.

Participants

Bickel and colleagues treated a 2-year-old girl in England who had PKU and severe developmental delays.

Method

The researchers gave her a special diet very low in phenylalanine and recorded her behavior and blood levels over several months.

Results

On the diet, her blood levels fell and her alertness and behavior improved; when phenylalanine was added back to her food, her symptoms worsened again.

Discussion

Later work showed that starting the diet in the first weeks of life largely prevents the brain damage, so newborns in many countries are now screened for PKU.

6. A classmate says, "If a trait is inherited, there is no point trying to change it." How does PKU answer this?

  1. Its harm can be largely prevented by changing the diet
  2. PKU shows that inherited traits are usually harmless
  3. PKU shows that inherited traits disappear by adulthood
  4. PKU shows that the environment, not heredity, shapes the brain
Show the answer

Inherited is not the same as fixed. Newborn screening and a special diet let children with PKU develop typically, which is why screening is routine.

  • Correct: Its harm can be largely prevented by changing the diet: Right: inherited does not mean unchangeable.
  • PKU shows that inherited traits are usually harmless: Untreated PKU is very harmful.
  • PKU shows that inherited traits disappear by adulthood: People with PKU keep the condition; the diet manages it.
  • PKU shows that the environment, not heredity, shapes the brain: The condition itself is inherited; both matter.

Part 9 · Summary

Summary

Behavior and mental processes come from heredity and environment working together. Twin, family and adoption studies estimate heritability, the share of variation in a trait within a group that is linked to genetic differences; it does not describe individuals or explain differences between groups. The evolutionary perspective explains shared human traits by natural selection, and its misuse in eugenics shows why claims about heredity must be read carefully.
