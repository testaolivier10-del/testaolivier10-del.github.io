Unit 1 · Topic 1.2

Overview of the Nervous System

The nervous system is made of neurons organized into two parts.

Practice 1: Concept Application

Part 1 · Hook

Why this matters

A ball flies at your face. Before you have time to think, your hands come up, your heart jumps and your breathing catches. A second later you laugh, and within a minute your heart has settled. Several parts of your nervous system did those jobs, some on purpose and some automatically, and the exam expects you to name each one.

Part 4 · Step by step

How it works, step by step

  1. The central nervous system (the brain and spinal cord) takes in information and decides→it sends commands out through the peripheral nervous system
  2. Commands reach the skeletal muscles through the somatic nervous system→you move on purpose: catching, kicking, typing
  3. A threat triggers the sympathetic division of the autonomic nervous system→the heart speeds, pupils widen and digestion slows: the body is ready to act
  4. Once the threat passes, the parasympathetic division takes over→the heart slows and digestion resumes: the body calms and saves energy

Part 5 · Key ideas

Key ideas

  • The central nervous system (CNS) is the brain and spinal cord. The peripheral nervous system (PNS) is every nerve linking the CNS to the rest of the body.
  • The PNS has two parts: the somatic nervous system (voluntary movement of skeletal muscles, plus sensations from the body) and the autonomic nervous system (automatic control of the heart, lungs, glands and gut).
  • The autonomic system has two divisions that work in balance: the sympathetic nervous system arouses; the parasympathetic nervous system calms.
  • The neuron, or nerve cell, is the basic unit that carries information through every part of the system.

Part 6 · Misconception

A common mistake

The wrong idea: The sympathetic nervous system is the "nice", calming one, and the parasympathetic one is for emergencies.

What actually happens: It is the reverse. Sympathetic arouses (heart speeds, pupils widen, digestion slows); parasympathetic calms (heart slows, digestion resumes). Memory hook: parasympathetic, "rest and digest".

Part 7 · Check yourself

Check yourself

Exam-style questions. Anything you miss goes into your review queue.

1. A goalkeeper sees a penalty kick coming and dives to her left. Which division of the nervous system carries the commands that move her arms and legs?

  1. The somatic nervous system
  2. The parasympathetic nervous system
  3. The autonomic nervous system
  4. The sympathetic nervous system
Show the answer

The somatic nervous system, part of the peripheral nervous system, carries commands from the central nervous system to the skeletal muscles and brings in sensations from the body.

  • Correct: The somatic nervous system: Right: it controls voluntary movement of the skeletal muscles.
  • The parasympathetic nervous system: The parasympathetic division calms the body; it does not move skeletal muscles.
  • The autonomic nervous system: The autonomic system runs automatic functions such as heart rate, not deliberate movement.
  • The sympathetic nervous system: The sympathetic division arouses the body’s organs; it does not direct the dive itself.

2. Walking home at night, Leo hears footsteps behind him. His heart pounds, his breathing quickens and his mouth goes dry. Which division is most active?

  1. The parasympathetic nervous system
  2. The sympathetic nervous system
  3. The somatic nervous system
  4. The central nervous system alone
Show the answer

The sympathetic division of the autonomic nervous system speeds the heart and breathing, widens the pupils and slows digestion, preparing the body to act.

  • The parasympathetic nervous system: The parasympathetic division calms the body, slowing the heart.
  • Correct: The sympathetic nervous system: Right: it arouses the body for action.
  • The somatic nervous system: The somatic system moves skeletal muscles; these are automatic changes in organs.
  • The central nervous system alone: The heart and breathing changes are carried out through the peripheral, autonomic system.

3. After a big holiday dinner, Rosa relaxes on the couch. Her heart rate slows and her stomach digests the meal. Which division is most active?

  1. The sympathetic nervous system
  2. The somatic nervous system
  3. The parasympathetic nervous system
  4. The spinal cord alone
Show the answer

The parasympathetic division conserves energy: it slows the heart, lowers blood pressure and lets digestion resume.

  • The sympathetic nervous system: The sympathetic division arouses the body and slows digestion.
  • The somatic nervous system: Digestion and heart rate are automatic, not voluntary movement.
  • Correct: The parasympathetic nervous system: Right: it calms the body and supports digestion.
  • The spinal cord alone: The spinal cord is part of the central nervous system; the calming of organs is autonomic.

The sympathetic division prepares the body for action: faster heart, wider pupils, slower digestion, more sweating.

6. In the diagram’s example, a car horn makes a pedestrian’s heart pound and he jumps back. Which divisions are involved, in order?

  1. Somatic for the pounding heart, sympathetic for the jump
  2. Sympathetic for the pounding heart, somatic for the jump
  3. Parasympathetic for the pounding heart, autonomic for the jump
  4. Central for the pounding heart, parasympathetic for the jump
Show the answer

The heart’s pounding is sympathetic (autonomic) arousal; jumping back moves skeletal muscles through the somatic system.

  • Somatic for the pounding heart, sympathetic for the jump: This swaps the two: the heart is autonomic and the jump uses skeletal muscles.
  • Correct: Sympathetic for the pounding heart, somatic for the jump: Right: an automatic arousal and a voluntary-muscle movement.
  • Parasympathetic for the pounding heart, autonomic for the jump: The parasympathetic division calms the heart rather than speeding it.
  • Central for the pounding heart, parasympathetic for the jump: The jump uses skeletal muscles, moved by the somatic system.

Part 8 · Summary

Summary

The nervous system is made of neurons organized into two parts. The central nervous system, the brain and spinal cord, takes in information and decides. The peripheral nervous system links it to the body: its somatic division moves skeletal muscles on purpose, and its autonomic division runs the organs automatically, with the sympathetic division arousing the body for action and the parasympathetic division calming it again.
