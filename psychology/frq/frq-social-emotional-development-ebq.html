Name: ______________________   Date: ____________   Does early attachment predict later relationships? (7 points)

Study

Source 1: Source 1: Attachment at 1 and friendships at 10

Introduction

Researchers asked whether infants who are securely attached at age 1 get along better with peers in late childhood.

Participants

150 children from one Midwestern city, first studied at 12 months (about half girls; most from low-income families). Parents gave consent, children gave assent at 10, and an IRB approved the study.

Method

At 12 months, each infant was observed in the strange situation and classified as securely or insecurely attached. At 10, the children attended a four-week summer day camp, where counselors who did not know the attachment results rated each child’s social skill with peers on a 1 to 10 scale.

Results

Children classified as securely attached at 12 months had a mean counselor rating of 7.2 at age 10; children classified as insecurely attached had a mean rating of 5.4. The difference was statistically significant.

Discussion

Secure attachment in infancy predicted better relationships with peers nine years later.

Invented study for practice.

Study

Source 2: Source 2: Adults’ attachment styles and romantic relationships

Introduction

Researchers asked whether the way adults describe their feelings in close relationships, using the same categories as infant attachment, relates to how their romantic relationships go.

Participants

480 adults aged 18 to 60 in the United States who were in or had been in a romantic relationship, recruited through a social media survey (61% women). Responses were anonymous.

Method

Participants chose which of three descriptions best fit how they feel in close relationships (secure, avoidant or anxious) and reported the length of their longest romantic relationship in years. They also rated how much they trusted their current or most recent partner on a 1 to 7 scale.

Results

Adults who described themselves as secure reported longer relationships (mean 9 years) than avoidant adults (6 years) or anxious adults (5 years), and higher trust in their partners (mean 5.8, compared with 4.4 and 4.1).

Discussion

Adult relationship patterns resemble infant attachment patterns. Because adults described themselves at one time, the study cannot show that their infant attachment caused these patterns.

Study

Source 3: Source 3: Does attachment stay the same from 1 to 19?

Introduction

Researchers asked how stable attachment is from infancy to early adulthood, and what predicts change.

Participants

60 young adults from middle-income families in one Northeastern US city who had been observed in the strange situation at 12 months. They gave informed consent at 19, and an IRB approved the follow-up.

Method

At 19, each participant completed a structured interview about their childhood relationships, which trained raters, unaware of the infant results, classified as secure or insecure. Participants also reported major stressful life events since childhood, such as a parent’s death, divorce or serious illness.

Results

Overall, 65% of participants (39 of 60) had the same classification at 19 as at 12 months. Among the 36 with no major stressful events, 78% (28) kept the same classification; among the 24 with one or more major events, 46% (11) did. Most changes from secure to insecure followed a major stressful event.

Discussion

Attachment showed moderate stability, and change was more likely after stressful family events.
