Article analysis studio

Read the study, then answer each part the way the readers score it.

About this tool

The article analysis question (AAQ) gives you one study and asks six things about it: the research method, an operational definition, what a statistic shows, an ethical guideline the researchers applied, whether the results generalize, and whether the results support the hypothesis. It is worth 7 points, and each part has its own trap.

Pick a part to see exactly where in the summary its answer is, then what earns the point and what the readers saw students miss. Change the result to see how parts C and F change with it.

How the studio works

Every study here is invented for practice, written in the layout the exam uses: Introduction, Participants, Method and Results with a graph. The numbers are generated each time you run the study and are computed with the course's statistics code, so the graph, the results text and the answers always agree.

The slips under each part come from the College Board's 2025 Chief Reader Reports and the 2025 and 2026 scoring guidelines, in our own words: the wrong or several methods, a definition of the concept instead of how the study measured it, numbers restated instead of interpreted, an ethical guideline the summary never describes, generalizing from sample size, and a claim that a study proves an idea.
