Study

Spaced or massed study of new vocabulary

Introduction

Researchers asked whether spreading study sessions out over time helps students remember vocabulary longer than doing the same amount of study all at once.

Participants

64 tenth graders in Spanish 1 classes at a public high school in Texas. Parents gave written permission and students agreed to take part; the school's review committee approved the study.

Method

Students were randomly assigned to two groups of 32. Both groups studied the same 30 new Spanish words for three 15-minute sessions. The massed group did all three sessions on one Monday, back to back. The spaced group did one session each Monday for three weeks. Four weeks after each group's last session, all students took the same test: they saw each English word and wrote the Spanish word.

Results

On the final test, the spaced group wrote an average of 18 of 30 words correctly (60%); the massed group wrote an average of 10 of 30 (about 33%).

Discussion

The researchers concluded that spacing study sessions improves long-term retention of vocabulary.

Invented study for practice.

1. Which concept do the results support?

  1. The serial position effect
  2. The method of loci
  3. The recency effect
  4. The spacing effect
Show the answer

The spaced group studied for the same total time but remembered more four weeks later. Better long-term retention from spread-out practice is the spacing effect.

  • The serial position effect: The serial position effect is about where an item sits in a list. Both groups studied the same list in the same order.
  • The method of loci: The method of loci links items to places along an imagined route. Neither group was taught a memory strategy.
  • The recency effect: The recency effect is better memory for the last items in a list, not for spaced sessions.
  • Correct: The spacing effect: Right: The spaced group studied for the same total time but remembered more four weeks later. Better long-term retention from spread-out practice is the spacing effect.

2. Which is the independent variable in this study?

  1. Whether the three study sessions were on one day or over three weeks
  2. The number of Spanish words written correctly on the final test
  3. The 30 Spanish words that both groups of tenth graders studied
  4. The four weeks between the last session and the final test
Show the answer

The independent variable is what the researchers changed between groups: the timing of the sessions (massed or spaced).

  • Correct: Whether the three study sessions were on one day or over three weeks: Right: The independent variable is what the researchers changed between groups: the timing of the sessions (massed or spaced).
  • The number of Spanish words written correctly on the final test: That is the dependent variable, what was measured.
  • The 30 Spanish words that both groups of tenth graders studied: The words were the same for both groups, so they were held constant, not varied.
  • The four weeks between the last session and the final test: Both groups waited four weeks, so the delay was held constant.

3. A school board member wants to use these results for adults learning Spanish at night school. Which point about the participants matters most for that generalization?

  1. The study had 64 students, which is too small a sample to generalize from
  2. The participants were tenth graders in one Texas school, unlike adults
  3. The students were randomly assigned, so the results apply to learners of any age
  4. The final test was given four weeks after the last session
Show the answer

Generalizability depends on who was studied. These were teenagers in one school; adults working full time may differ in age, schedule and prior learning, so the result may only partly apply.

  • The study had 64 students, which is too small a sample to generalize from: Generalizing is about how the participants compare with the new population, not about sample size alone.
  • Correct: The participants were tenth graders in one Texas school, unlike adults: Right: Generalizability depends on who was studied. These were teenagers in one school; adults working full time may differ in age, schedule and prior learning, so the result may only partly apply.
  • The students were randomly assigned, so the results apply to learners of any age: Random assignment supports a causal claim within the sample. It does not make the sample like adults.
  • The final test was given four weeks after the last session: The delay is part of the method, the same for both groups. It does not describe who the participants were.

4. Which ethical safeguard does the summary describe for these minors?

  1. Debriefing after a cover story about the true purpose
  2. Payment to each student for each of the study sessions
  3. Parental permission plus each student's own agreement
  4. Random assignment of students to the two study schedules
Show the answer

For minors, researchers need a parent's informed consent and the young person's assent. The summary says both were obtained, and a review committee approved the study.

  • Debriefing after a cover story about the true purpose: The summary describes no deception and no debriefing.
  • Payment to each student for each of the study sessions: No payment is mentioned, and payment is not itself a safeguard.
  • Correct: Parental permission plus each student's own agreement: Right: For minors, researchers need a parent's informed consent and the young person's assent. The summary says both were obtained, and a review committee approved the study.
  • Random assignment of students to the two study schedules: Random assignment is a design feature, not an ethical guideline.

Study

A reminder before a math test

Introduction

Researchers tested whether reminding students of a negative stereotype about their group before a hard math test lowers their scores.

Participants

120 women in their first year at two public universities, all of whom had taken calculus in high school. Each gave informed consent; the universities' review boards approved the study.

Method

A computer randomly assigned each woman to one of two groups of 60. Before the test, the reminder group read: "This test has shown gender differences in past research." The no-reminder group read: "This test has shown no gender differences in past research." Everyone then took the same 20-question math test in 20 minutes. Afterward, every participant was told the true purpose of the two statements and that neither was a real research finding.

Results

The reminder group answered 9.1 questions correctly on average (SD 3.0); the no-reminder group answered 11.4 (SD 3.2). The difference was statistically significant.

Discussion

The researchers concluded that a stereotype reminder lowered math performance in this sample.

Invented study for practice.

5. Which concept do the results best support?

  1. Stereotype lift
  2. Stereotype threat
  3. The Flynn effect
  4. Test-retest reliability
Show the answer

Being reminded of a negative stereotype about one's group lowered test performance. That is stereotype threat.

  • Stereotype lift: Stereotype lift is a small boost from being compared with a group stereotyped as worse. Here performance went down.
  • Correct: Stereotype threat: Right: Being reminded of a negative stereotype about one's group lowered test performance. That is stereotype threat.
  • The Flynn effect: The Flynn effect is the rise in average test scores across generations. This study compared two groups on one day.
  • Test-retest reliability: Reliability is about consistent scores on repeated testing. Each woman took the test once.

6. What does "the difference was statistically significant" mean here?

  1. The difference is large enough to matter in everyday life
  2. Each woman in the reminder group scored lower than each woman in the other group
  3. A difference this large would be unlikely if chance alone were at work
  4. The result will be found again by any lab that repeats the study
Show the answer

Statistical significance means the difference between the groups is unlikely to be due to chance (random variation in who was in each group). It does not say the difference is large.

  • The difference is large enough to matter in everyday life: Significance is not size. How big a difference is, is a separate question (effect size).
  • Each woman in the reminder group scored lower than each woman in the other group: The groups overlap a lot (the SDs are about 3 questions). Significance is about the group averages.
  • Correct: A difference this large would be unlikely if chance alone were at work: Right: Statistical significance means the difference between the groups is unlikely to be due to chance (random variation in who was in each group). It does not say the difference is large.
  • The result will be found again by any lab that repeats the study: A significant result can still fail to replicate; only repeated studies show that.

7. The participants were given a false statement about past research. Which safeguard in the summary addressed this?

  1. Random assignment: a computer placed each woman in a group
  2. Confidentiality: the test scores were stored without any names
  3. Protection from harm: the math test lasted twenty minutes
  4. Debriefing: afterward each woman learned the statements were false
Show the answer

When a study uses deception, researchers must explain the truth afterward. The summary says every participant was told the true purpose and that neither statement was a real finding.

  • Random assignment: a computer placed each woman in a group: Random assignment is a design step for causal claims, not an ethical safeguard.
  • Confidentiality: the test scores were stored without any names: The summary does not describe how scores were stored.
  • Protection from harm: the math test lasted twenty minutes: The test length is part of the method, not a protection described as such.
  • Correct: Debriefing: afterward each woman learned the statements were false: Right: When a study uses deception, researchers must explain the truth afterward. The summary says every participant was told the true purpose and that neither statement was a real finding.

8. A reader wants to apply this result to all high school girls taking math tests. Which statement about generalizing is best supported?

  1. Partly: they were female math students, but older and in college
  2. Fully, because 120 women is a large enough sample
  3. No, because the study was a true experiment
  4. Fully, because the women were randomly assigned to groups
Show the answer

Generalizing is judged by participant characteristics. Like the target group, they were female math students; unlike it, they were college students who had all taken calculus, at two universities.

  • Correct: Partly: they were female math students, but older and in college: Right: Generalizing is judged by participant characteristics. Like the target group, they were female math students; unlike it, they were college students who had all taken calculus, at two universities.
  • Fully, because 120 women is a large enough sample: Sample size alone does not show the participants resemble high school girls.
  • No, because the study was a true experiment: Being an experiment supports a causal claim; it does not by itself prevent generalizing.
  • Fully, because the women were randomly assigned to groups: Random assignment supports cause and effect within the sample. It does not make the sample like high school girls.

9. A film editor swaps an actor's red scarf for a blue one between two shots. Most viewers do not notice. Which concept explains this?

  1. The cocktail party effect
  2. Figure-ground
  3. Perceptual constancy
  4. Change blindness
Show the answer

Viewers failed to notice a change from one view of a scene to the next. That is change blindness.

  • The cocktail party effect: The cocktail party effect is an important sound, like your name, catching attention. Here nothing caught attention.
  • Figure-ground: Figure-ground separates an object from its background. It does not explain missing a change.
  • Perceptual constancy: Color constancy keeps a color looking stable under different light. Here the color really changed and was missed.
  • Correct: Change blindness: Right: Viewers failed to notice a change from one view of a scene to the next. That is change blindness.

10. Asked to picture a "bird", most people imagine something like a robin, not a penguin. A robin is the bird category's what?

  1. Algorithm
  2. Heuristic
  3. Prototype
  4. Mental set
Show the answer

A prototype is the best, most typical example of a concept. People judge membership by comparing new cases with it, which is why a penguin feels like a less "birdy" bird.

  • Algorithm: An algorithm is a step-by-step procedure for solving a problem, not an example of a category.
  • Heuristic: A heuristic is a mental shortcut for judging or solving. The robin is the example, not the shortcut.
  • Correct: Prototype: Right: A prototype is the best, most typical example of a concept. People judge membership by comparing new cases with it, which is why a penguin feels like a less "birdy" bird.
  • Mental set: A mental set is a habit of approaching problems one way. It is not the typical example of a category.

11. Ravi knows that Paris is the capital of France but cannot remember when or where he learned it. Which kind of memory holds this fact?

  1. Episodic memory
  2. Implicit memory
  3. Semantic memory
  4. Sensory memory
Show the answer

General knowledge separated from the moment it was learned is semantic memory, a kind of explicit memory.

  • Episodic memory: Episodic memory is tied to a particular personal event. Ravi cannot recall the event of learning it.
  • Implicit memory: Implicit memory works without conscious recall, like a skill. Ravi can consciously state the fact.
  • Correct: Semantic memory: Right: General knowledge separated from the moment it was learned is semantic memory, a kind of explicit memory.
  • Sensory memory: Sensory memory lasts only a moment. This fact has lasted years.

12. A woman can tell you what she did on almost any date in the last 30 years, including the day of the week and what was in the news, without using a diary or memory tricks. Which concept describes her?

  1. Infantile amnesia
  2. Superior autobiographical memory
  3. The method of loci, a mnemonic
  4. Retrograde amnesia
Show the answer

An extremely detailed memory for the events of one's own life, without using strategies, is highly superior autobiographical memory (HSAM). Fewer than a hundred people have been confirmed to have it.

  • Infantile amnesia: Infantile amnesia is the lack of early-childhood memories, the opposite of a detailed life record.
  • Correct: Superior autobiographical memory: Right: An extremely detailed memory for the events of one's own life, without using strategies, is highly superior autobiographical memory (HSAM). Fewer than a hundred people have been confirmed to have it.
  • The method of loci, a mnemonic: The method of loci is a learned mnemonic. She uses no memory tricks.
  • Retrograde amnesia: Retrograde amnesia is loss of past memories. She keeps an unusually large number of them.
